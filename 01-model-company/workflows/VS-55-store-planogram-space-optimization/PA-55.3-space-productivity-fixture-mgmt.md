## W2182. Sales Per Square Meter Analysis

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly analytics cycle — Sales Per Square Meter |
| **Frequency** | Monthly; 200 stores |
| **Volume** | ~1,600 zone-level analyses |
| **Owner** | Analytics Manager |
| **Participants** | Analytics Manager, Category Mgr, VP Merch, Store Ops Dir |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System calculates the monthly sales/sqm per zone per store (the canonical space-productivity metric); benchmarks each against the chain average and the top-quartile store for that format | System | Analytics Manager | Automated |
| 2 | Category Manager drills into the underperformers (a zone/store that's bottom-quartile for its format); VP Store Operations works with the Store Manager on an improvement plan; the analysis feeds the W2166 space-reallocation and the W2171 layout optimization | Category Mgr / Store Ops Dir | VP Merchandising | 3–4 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Analytics/BI (links to VS-28 / W113, W208 retail analytics, W2169 space-productivity) — the productivity dataset
- Planogram/space (links to VS-29 / W314) — the zone and sqm definitions
- Store-performance scorecard (links to VS-07 / W67) — the productivity as a Store Manager KPI

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Format-conflation risk**: comparing a compact store's zone to a flagship's zone unfairly penalizes the compact store; mitigated by the format-tier benchmarking in step 1 (compare within format, not across)
- **Margin-blindness risk**: sales/sqm rewards high-sales-low-margin zones; mitigated by the companion margin/sqm metric (the analysis reports both, not sales alone)

### Automation Opportunity
- Productivity dashboard auto-refreshed monthly with format-tier benchmarking; bottom-quartile auto-flagged for the Category Manager worklist

### Controls
- CTL-396 (ensure controlled execution — Space Productivity & Fixture Management (PA-55.3).)
- Operational: Analytics Manager methodology sign-off; VP Merchandising review of the format-tier benchmarking rules

### Time Estimate
~3.5 hours/month Category Mgr + Store Ops review + system automated = **~40–50 hours/year** (3.5 hours/month × 12) plus the automated calc.

## W2183. Fixture Procurement & Budgeting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual budget cycle or replacement need |
| **Frequency** | Annual; ~PHP 20–30M budget |
| **Volume** | ~10–15 new store packages + replacements/year |
| **Owner** | Facilities Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Facilities Coord, Store Design Mgr, VP Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coordinator compiles the fixture needs (new-store packages, replacements from the W2179 condition inventory, specialty displays from W2168); solicits 3–5 vendor bids per fixture class; recommends the award to VP Finance against the annual budget; manages the delivery schedule | Facilities Coordinator | VP Store Operations | 3–5 days |

### System Touchpoints
- Procurement (links to VS-34 / W136 indirect procurement, VS-15 P2P) — the fixture sourcing and PO
- Fixed-asset register (links to VS-35 / W399) — fixtures as tracked assets
- Capex (links to VS-40 / W21) — new-store fixture packages are capex

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Single-vendor-lock-in risk**: one vendor wins all fixture classes, creating supply and price leverage; mitigated by the 3–5 bid requirement per class and the dual-source preference for repeat classes
- **Budget-overrun risk**: the new-store package + replacements + specialty exceeds the PHP 20–30M budget; mitigated by the prioritized list (replacements below safety threshold first, then ROI-ranked specialty) and the VP Finance gate

### Automation Opportunity
- Needs-list auto-compiled from the W2179 condition inventory + the new-store pipeline; bid-comparison auto-tabulated

### Controls
- CTL-21: capex-approval for new-store packages; CTL-26 (asset-grade fixture tracking); operational: VP Finance sign-off on the award within the annual budget

### Time Estimate
~4 days/year Coordinator procurement + ongoing delivery management absorbed = **~30–40 hours/year** Facilities Coordinator (4 days × ~8h).

## W2184. Shelf Capacity & Stocking Optimization

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly replenishment planning |
| **Frequency** | Monthly; 35,000 SKUs per store |
| **Volume** | 35,000 SKU capacity analyses/store |
| **Owner** | Merchandising Planner |
| **Participants** | Merch Planner, DC Ops, Store Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System calculates the shelf capacity per SKU (facings × units-per-facing vs. weekly velocity); identifies the overfaced SKUs (reduce facings, free space) and the underfaced SKUs (increase facings to match velocity and avoid stockout-gaps) | System / Merch Planner | Category Manager | 2–3 hours |
| 2 | Planner adjusts the minimum shelf quantities and the replenishment triggers per store format; feeds into the automated replenishment per VS-02 so the shelf stays stocked at the optimized capacity | Merchandising Planner | DC Operations | 2–3 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Planogram/capacity (links to VS-29 / W314, W312 replenishment-parameter master) — the facing and capacity master
- Replenishment (links to VS-02 / W4 replenishment, W2A auto-replenishment) — the optimized capacity drives the replenishment trigger
- Item master (links to VS-29 / W252) — the SKU dimensions and velocity

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Overface-waste risk**: a slow SKU is overfaced, occupying prime space and aging on the shelf (spoilage for paint, obsolescence for trends); mitigated by the capacity-optimization reducing the facing to match velocity
- **Underface-stockout risk**: a fast SKU is underfaced, stocking out between replenishments and losing sales; mitigated by the underface flag and the replenishment-trigger adjustment in step 2

### Automation Opportunity
- Capacity-optimization auto-run monthly from the velocity data; overface/underface auto-flagged for the Planner review

### Controls
- CTL-51: capacity-master currency; operational: Category Manager sign-off on the facing changes

### Time Estimate
~5 hours/month Planner = **~60 hours/year**, absorbed into the planning routine.

## W2185. Promotional Space ROI Analysis

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Post-promotional period |
| **Frequency** | 6/year (bi-monthly) |
| **Volume** | 6 analyses/year |
| **Owner** | FP&A Analyst |
| **Participants** | FP&A, Marketing Mgr, Category Mgr |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FP&A calculates the incremental display sales vs. the standard-shelf baseline (the lift attributable to the endcap/power-aisle placement), the display cost vs. the incremental margin; compares the endcap vs. power-aisle vs. inline-display effectiveness to inform the next promo's space allocation | FP&A Analyst | Finance Manager | 3–4 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- FP&A / analytics (links to VS-17 / W26 budget, VS-28 / W113) — the incremental-sales and cost data
- Promo/display (links to VS-14 / W262, VS-55 / W2177) — the display spec and compliance
- Planogram history (links to VS-29 / W314, W2170) — the pre/post display configuration

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Baseline-confound risk**: the "standard shelf" baseline is itself boosted by the promo traffic, understating the true display lift; mitigated by a holdout-cluster comparison (stores without the display) where feasible
- **Cannibalization-risk blindness**: the display lifts its SKU but cannibalizes the same SKU's inline shelf sales (net-zero); mitigated by the SKU-total-sales view (display + inline), not display-only

### Automation Opportunity
- ROI auto-computed from the promo-attributed sales dataset; display-location effectiveness auto-ranked

### Controls
- CTL-396 (ensure controlled execution — Space Productivity & Fixture Management (PA-55.3).)
- CTL-396 (promo-spend accuracy — links to VS-39 co-op, VS-17); operational: Finance Manager sign-off on the ROI methodology

### Time Estimate
~3.5 hours/promo × 6 promos = **~20–25 hours/year** FP&A Analyst.

## W2186. Store Format Segmentation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual strategy review |
| **Frequency** | Annual; 200 stores in 3 formats |
| **Volume** | 200 stores across 3 segments |
| **Owner** | Store Design Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Design Mgr, VP Merch, COO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Manager segments the 200 stores into 3 formats (large flagship ~20, standard ~120, compact ~60) based on sqm, trade area, and historical performance; develops the format-specific planogram templates with curated assortments (the compact format carries a smaller SKU count per the W299 assortment-cluster master) | Store Design Manager | COO | 3–5 days |
| 2 | COO approves the format strategy; annual re-evaluation based on 12-month performance — stores may upgrade or downgrade format if their trade area or performance has shifted materially | COO | CEO | 2–3 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Store-cluster / format master (links to VS-29 / W299 assortment & store-cluster master, W254 location master) — the format assignment per store
- Planogram templates (links to VS-29 / W314) — the per-format planogram variant
- Assortment (links to VS-01 / W1 SKU planning) — the curated assortment per format

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Format-mismatch risk**: a store is mis-segmented (a compact-format store with a flagship trade area, or vice versa), leaving value on the table or overcrowding the floor; mitigated by the annual re-evaluation and the trade-area data input
- **Assortment-strangulation risk**: the compact format's curated assortment drops a SKU that local customers specifically want; mitigated by the local-demand override mechanism (a compact store can carry a non-standard SKU on a documented local-demand case)

### Automation Opportunity
- Format-segmentation auto-suggested from the sqm + trade-area + performance data; format-change-impact auto-modeled before approval

### Controls
- CTL-51: store-cluster/format master currency; operational: COO sign-off on any format reassignment

### Time Estimate
~3 days per store format × 3 formats Store Design Manager (≈ 72 hours) + ~2.5 hours COO review = **~60–80 hours/year**.

## W2187. Seasonal Display Space Rotation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Pre-season planning (6 seasons/year) |
| **Frequency** | 6 rotations/year; 200 stores |
| **Volume** | ~1,200 rotations/year |
| **Owner** | Merchandising Planner |
| **Participants** | Merch Planner, Marketing, Store Ops |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Planner allocates the seasonal display space per format (the flagship gets more seasonal real estate than the compact); Marketing designs the visual package (signage, fixtures, décor); coordinates the DC staging and the 1-week store-changeover window per format wave | Merch Planner / Marketing | VP Marketing | 1–2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Planogram/seasonal (links to VS-55 / W2166 seasonal refresh, W264 seasonal transition, VS-29 / W306 seasonal-calendar master) — the seasonal space allocation
- Marketing/POP (links to VS-14 / W262, W190) — the visual package
- DC staging (links to VS-04 / W3) — the seasonal-display-kit pre-staging

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Wave-synchronization risk**: the DC staging, the POP delivery, and the store-changeover window misalign, leaving stores with merchandise but no POP (or vice versa); mitigated by the single coordinated wave per format
- **Format-fatigue risk**: the same seasonal display in 200 stores feels uniform and loses local relevance; mitigated by a local-accent element within the national template (the core display is national, a small accent zone is store-selected)

### Automation Opportunity
- Seasonal-kit auto-assembled at the DC from the format allocation; wave-synchronization tracker with milestone alerts

### Controls
- CTL-51: seasonal-planogram currency; operational: VP Marketing sign-off on the visual package

### Time Estimate
~1.5 weeks/rotation × 6 rotations + ~3 hours/store changeover × 200 stores = **~4,000–5,000 hours/year** Planner + Marketing + Store teams (changeover labor absorbed into store-ops).

## W2189. Space Capital Investment Analysis

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual capital planning |
| **Frequency** | Annual; 10–20 renovation decisions |
| **Volume** | 10–20 analyses/year |
| **Owner** | FP&A Director |
| **Participants** | FP&A Dir, Store Design Mgr, COO, CFO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FP&A compiles the space-capital requests (a store expansion, a fixture overhaul, a new-department build-out); calculates the ROI (projected sales uplift from the W2171 layout analysis, margin, payback period, NPV over 5 years); COO/CFO prioritize by ROI against the annual capex budget | FP&A Director | CFO | 2–3 days |
| 2 | Post-renovation: tracks the actual vs. projected uplift for 12 months; feeds the learning into the future projection model so the space-capital estimates improve cycle-over-cycle | FP&A Director | CFO | Ongoing |

### System Touchpoints
- FP&A / capex (links to VS-17 / W26 budget, VS-40 / W21 capex, VS-158 / W85) — the cost and ROI model
- Analytics (links to VS-28 / W113, VS-55 / W2171 layout optimization) — the projected-uplift basis
- Fixed-asset (links to VS-35 / W399) — the renovated space as a capitalized asset

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Projection-optimism risk**: the projected uplift is consistently overstated (the same bias as W2173), eroding capex ROI; mitigated by the 12-month post-actual reconciliation and the model recalibration
- **Stranded-capex risk**: a renovation is approved on a projected uplift that depends on a concurrent assortment change that doesn't happen, stranding the capex; mitigated by the cross-dependency check (the assortment/marketing commitments that the ROI assumes must be confirmed before capex approval)

### Automation Opportunity
- ROI model auto-assembled from the layout-uplift + capex-cost datasets; post-actual auto-reconciled with variance flagging

### Controls
- CTL-21: capex-approval — the space-capital gate; operational: CFO sign-off on the ROI methodology and the prioritized list; CEO sign-off on any >PHP 50M space-capital investment

### Time Estimate
~2.5 days/year FP&A + 12-month post-tracking absorbed = **~20 hours/year** FP&A Director (2.5 days), with post-tracking absorbed.
