# VS-131: Human Rights, Modern Slavery & Responsible Supply Chain Due Diligence

> **Plan & Source** · [Value Stream Index](../value-stream-index.md)

---

## Overview

Human Rights & Responsible Supply Chain Due Diligence workflows for BuildRight Depot Corp. —
owning the enterprise discipline of identifying, preventing, mitigating, and accounting for
**adverse human-rights impacts across BuildRight's own operations and its ~800–1,000-vendor,
~40%-import, commodity-intensive global supply chain**. BuildRight's assortment — tools and
electronics (with component minerals), paint/coatings and chemicals, lumber/building materials,
tiles, apparel-grade PPE, and garden/agro products — and its ~40% import share (China, Taiwan,
Indonesia, Malaysia, Japan, Europe) create real exposure to forced/modern-slavery labor, child
labor, unsafe working conditions, irresponsible recruitment/migrant-labor abuse, conflict-
affected and indigenous-peoples/land-rights impacts, and conflict-minerals/responsible-materials
risk. As a PHP 62.3B retailer selling to international B2B customers (developers, multinationals)
increasingly subject to — and imposing downstream — modern-slavery and human-rights due-diligence
requirements, BuildRight faces statutory and commercial exposure (UK Modern Slavery Act, German
Lieferkettensorgfaltspflichtengesetz / EU CSDDD, US UFLPA/tariff, Australian MSA, and emerging
Philippine frameworks).

Today this discipline is **entirely unowned as a program**: VS-25.2 (ESG social-impact) names
'human rights' as one ESG *reporting topic* and 'forced labor' appears as an audit *risk* in
VS-21.3; VS-25.2 W195 (Sustainable Sourcing & Ethical Vendor Audit), W1176 (Green Procurement),
and W1480 (Supplier Diversity) touch adjacent *environmental/diversity* sourcing; VS-94.3 covers
fair-trade for cooperative sourcing — but no value stream owns the **human-rights due-diligence
(HRDD) program**: policy/commitments/governance, salient-risk identification, supply-chain
mapping and traceability, supplier code of conduct and onboarding safeguards, modern-slavery/
forced-labor/child-labor risk modeling, responsible recruitment, supplier risk assessment and
auditing, worker-voice/grievance verification, remediation, responsible disengagement, conflict-
minerals/responsible-materials DD, living-wage assurance, KPIs and statutory reporting, and
stakeholder engagement. 'human rights' and 'modern slavery' each appeared in only a single PA
file (as an audit risk and an ESG topic, respectively).

This is distinct from **VS-25 (ESG & Sustainability)** which reports the ESG footprint and
covers environmental/diversity sourcing (this value stream owns the *human-rights-specific* DD
program, whose results feed ESG reporting in VS-25.3). It is distinct from **VS-03 (Vendor
Management)** which runs the transactional vendor/PO lifecycle (this value stream owns the
*human-rights safeguards* layered onto onboarding and contracts). It is distinct from **VS-122
(Global Sourcing)** which executes strategic import sourcing (this value stream owns the *source-
side human-rights DD* of those vendors/geographies). It is distinct from **VS-31 (Quality)**
and **VS-117 (DTI-BPS)** which address product quality/standards (this value stream addresses
*labor and human-rights* conditions of production). It is distinct from **VS-119 (Ethics/Speak-
Up)** which handles BuildRight's own workforce ethics (this value stream extends worker voice/
grievance to the *supply chain* and own operations). Human-rights & responsible-sourcing DD is
the *people-in-the-supply-chain* discipline — distinct from ESG reporting, vendor operations,
global sourcing, product quality, and internal ethics.

BuildRight's exposure is structural: a commodity-intensive, heavily-imported assortment produced
in geographies with documented labor-risk, sold to B2B customers who increasingly cascade human-
rights requirements down their own supply chains, with reputational, statutory (modern-slavery
statements), market-access (UFLPA/import detentions), and B2B-customer-retention consequences.
Without a dedicated owner, BuildRight risks undetected forced labor in its supply chain,
import detentions, lost B2B accounts, and reputational harm — risks that no existing value
stream owns end-to-end.
