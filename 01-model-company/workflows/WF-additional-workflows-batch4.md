## W983. Customer Roofing Material Calculator & GI Sheet/Insulation Sizing Recommendation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests roofing material quantity calculation at store or via mobile app |
| **Frequency** | ~6,000–8,000 calculations/month chain-wide (~30–40/store/month) |
| **Volume** | 1–2 roof sections per calculation; average 1.4 sections |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Lumber & Building Materials) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Roofing Specialist), Customer, Category Manager (pricing) |

### Background

Roofing is one of the highest-value, most anxiety-inducing purchases in Philippine home improvement. The country's typhoon belt status means roofing material selection directly impacts family safety. The market predominantly uses corrugated GI (galvanized iron) sheets in lengths of 6, 8, 10, and 12 feet, with gauges ranging from 26 (lightest residential) to 22 (commercial/heavy-duty). Customers must account for roof pitch, overhang, ridge cap coverage, and the critical overlapping requirement (1.5–2 corrugations on sides, 15–30 cm on ends depending on pitch). Incorrect calculations lead to shortages mid-project (delaying the critical task of closing the roof before rain) or costly over-purchases of heavy, difficult-to-return materials. Additional items — insulation (foil-backed foam or fiberglass), ridge rolls, flashings, gutters, fascia boards, roofing nails/screws with washers, and sealant — are frequently overlooked by DIY customers. A professional calculator service increases average order value through cross-selling accessories and builds customer confidence. Post-typhoon demand surges (300–500% above baseline in affected areas) make this a revenue-critical service during the June–November typhoon season.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Roof Measurement & Structure Intake**: Customer provides roof details via: (a) physical measurements — length and width of each roof section (in meters or feet), roof pitch/slope angle (or rise-over-run), number of valleys, hips, ridges, and dormers; (b) house floor plan or blueprint — Sales Associate interprets roof plan dimensions; (c) mobile app — customer uses BuildRight app's roof measurement tool with satellite imagery overlay or AR measurement for existing structures; Sales Associate records: roof shape (gable, hip, shed, flat, combination), number of sections, pitch angle, overhang dimensions (typically 0.6–1.2m in PH residential), existing roof condition (new construction vs. re-roofing) | Sales Associate / Customer | Department Supervisor | 8–12 min |
| 2 | **Material Selection & Specification**: Customer selects roofing material with Sales Associate guidance: (a) GI sheet type — plain corrugated (longest life), pre-painted corrugated (colored, aesthetic), ribbed/standing seam (commercial); (b) gauge/thickness — Gauge 26 (0.45mm, economy residential), Gauge 24 (0.50mm, standard), Gauge 22 (0.63mm, premium/typhoon-rated); (c) length — 6ft, 8ft, 10ft, 12ft standard; custom lengths available via W988 welding/fabrication partner; (d) color (pre-painted only) — tile red, brick red, forest green, ocean blue, brown, white, beige; (e) insulation type — reflective foil (economy), foil-backed foam board (standard), fiberglass blanket (premium); (f) accessories — ridge cap, apex cap, valley flashing, gutter system, fascia cover, bargeboard, roofing screws with EPDM washers, butyl tape sealant | Sales Associate / Customer | Department Supervisor | 5–10 min |
| 3 | **Automated Quantity Calculation**: System computes: (a) total roof area per section (plan area ÷ cos(pitch angle) for sloped surfaces); (b) effective coverage per sheet (gross sheet width minus side overlap of 1.5–2 corrugations ≈ 57–61 cm effective width; effective length = sheet length minus end overlap of 15–30 cm based on pitch); (c) sheets required per section = (roof width ÷ effective sheet width) × (roof slope length ÷ effective sheet length), rounded up to full sheets; (d) ridge cap pieces (linear meters of ridge ÷ cap coverage per piece); (e) valley flashing (linear meters of valleys ÷ effective flashing overlap); (f) insulation area = total roof area (same as sheet plan coverage, no overlap reduction); (g) screws = 12–16 per sheet (depending on purlin spacing); (h) gutter length = total eave perimeter; (i) total weight estimate for delivery planning (GI sheet ≈ 4–6 kg/sqm depending on gauge) | System / Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 2–4 min (automated) |
| 4 | **Cross-Sell Accessories & Services**: Sales Associate recommends: (a) roof insulation (most customers skip this but it reduces indoor temperature by 3–5°C in PH tropical climate); (b) gutter system with downspouts (for rainwater collection, increasingly popular in PH); (c) fascia boards and bargeboard (weatherproofing roof edges); (d) roof installation service referral per W138; (e) waterproof roof sealant for nail/screw holes and overlaps; (f) safety equipment rental per W139 (safety harness, roofing brackets) for DIY customers; system updates quotation with all recommended accessories | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 5–8 min |
| 5 | **Customer Review, Order Creation & Delivery Scheduling**: Customer reviews itemized materials list with quantities and prices; Sales Associate creates POS quotation per W542 or direct sale; for large orders (≥20 sheets or ≥200 kg total weight): (a) system schedules delivery via W5D or W980; (b) customer selects delivery date and confirms delivery address; (c) unloading assistance offered (roofing bundles require 2-person carry); (d) for orders exceeding store stock, system initiates inter-store transfer per W22 or DC replenishment per W4; quotation saved to customer's loyalty account per W894 (Project Vault) | Sales Associate / Customer | Department Supervisor | 5–10 min |

- Roofing quantity calculator module (POS and mobile app) with pitch-correct area calculation engine
- Real-time inventory availability with GI sheet gauge/color/length matrix per store
- POS quotation module (W542) for itemized roofing material list
- Customer loyalty account integration for quotation saving (W894)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Measurement accuracy**: Customer roof measurements are frequently inaccurate (±10–15 cm is common); steep-pitch roofs are difficult for customers to measure; Sales Associate must recommend professional measurement per W442 for orders above PHP 50,000; satellite imagery tool in mobile app significantly improves accuracy for existing structures
- **Typhoon demand surges**: Post-typhoon demand for GI sheets, nails, and tarpaulins can spike 300–500% in affected regions within 48 hours; pre-positioned buffer stock per W960 and emergency procurement per W60 are critical; Category Manager must activate emergency pricing controls to prevent gouging perception

- **No incremental headcount**

- **Total per calculation**: 25–44 min of staff time

## W984. Customer Water Tank & Pump System Sizing Recommendation Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests water tank and/or pump sizing recommendation at store or via mobile app |
| **Frequency** | ~3,000–4,000 consultations/month chain-wide (~15–20/store/month) |
| **Volume** | 1 system per consultation; average 1 system |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Plumbing) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Plumbing Specialist), Customer, Category Manager (pricing) |

### Background

Water supply reliability is a critical concern for Philippine households and businesses, particularly in provincial areas where municipal water pressure is low or supply is intermittent. Many BuildRight customers need a complete water storage and distribution system: a water tank (polyethylene or stainless steel, ranging from 200 liters to 10,000 liters), a water pump (shallow well jet pump, deep well submersible, or pressure booster pump), piping connections, float valves, and accessories. Sizing is complex — a tank too small runs dry during supply interruptions; a tank too large wastes money and space. A pump too weak cannot deliver adequate pressure to upper floors; a pump too powerful wastes electricity and shortens pipe life. The Philippine market has additional considerations: NWRB (National Water Resources Board) regulations for well pumps, LGU requirements for water tank installations in commercial buildings, and typhoon preparedness (ensuring water supply when power is interrupted). This service drives high-value sales (a complete system averages PHP 15,000–50,000) and creates long-term customer relationships through pump maintenance and replacement parts.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer Water Demand Assessment**: Sales Associate collects: (a) number of household/establishment occupants; (b) number of bathrooms, kitchen sinks, laundry areas, and garden taps; (c) number of floors in the building (affects pump head pressure requirement); (d) water source — municipal supply (with pressure/volume issues), shallow well (≤25ft depth), deep well (>25ft); (e) current water supply schedule (24/7, specific hours, intermittent); (f) available tank installation space (ground level, roof deck, inside utility room); (g) budget range | Sales Associate / Customer | Department Supervisor | 8–12 min |
| 2 | **Tank Sizing Calculation**: System calculates recommended tank capacity: (a) daily water consumption = occupants × 150 liters/person/day (PH standard residential); commercial/industrial uses higher factors; (b) desired storage days = 1–3 days for municipal supply backup, 1 day minimum for well systems; (c) tank capacity = daily consumption × storage days; (d) system recommends standard tank sizes (200L, 500L, 1000L, 2000L, 3000L, 5000L, 10000L) rounding up to nearest available size; (e) tank material recommendation — polyethylene (lightweight, UV-stabilized, economy), stainless steel (durable, hygienic, premium); (f) installation type — freestanding (ground level), elevated platform (roof deck), horizontal/vertical orientation based on available space | System / Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 3–5 min (automated) |
| 3 | **Pump Sizing & Selection**: System calculates pump specification: (a) required flow rate (liters per minute) based on simultaneous fixture usage (typically 2–3 fixtures simultaneously in residential); (b) total dynamic head (TDH) = vertical lift (suction head + discharge head to highest fixture) + friction loss (pipe length × friction factor per pipe diameter); (c) pump type recommendation — shallow well jet pump (depth ≤25ft, PH most common), deep well submersible pump (depth >25ft), pressure booster pump (municipal supply with low pressure), automatic pump with pressure tank (for consistent pressure without cycling); (d) horsepower rating — 0.5HP (1-story, 1 bathroom), 1HP (2-story, 2–3 bathrooms), 1.5HP (large residential/commercial), 2HP+ (commercial/industrial); (e) power supply consideration — single-phase 220V (residential) vs. three-phase (commercial) | System / Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 5–8 min |
| 4 | **Accessories & Piping Recommendation**: System generates complete bill of materials: (a) PVC pipes (PVC schedule 40) — sizes based on flow rate (typically ¾" to 1¼" for residential); (b) fittings — elbows, tees, couplings, adapters, threaded connectors; (c) float valve (mechanical or electronic) for automatic tank refill; (d) gate valves/ball valves for isolation; (e) check valve (non-return) to prevent backflow; (f) pressure tank (for automatic pump systems — eliminates pump cycling); (g) pressure gauge and pressure switch; (h) pump controller (automatic dry-run protection mandatory for well pumps); (i) electrical wiring, circuit breaker, and waterproof outdoor switch; (j) pipe sealant (Teflon tape, solvent cement); (k) flexible connector hoses; (l) water filter (sediment filter recommended for well systems) | System / Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 5–8 min |
| 5 | **Customer Review, Quotation & Service Referral**: Customer reviews complete system recommendation with itemized pricing; Sales Associate: (a) presents total system cost and per-item breakdown; (b) offers installation service referral per W138 (plumber installation for complete system); (c) offers pipe cutting and threading service per W944 for custom pipe lengths; (d) creates POS quotation per W542 valid for 14 days; (e) quotation saved to customer loyalty account per W894; (f) for well pump installations: advises customer on NWRB well permit requirements and recommends licensed well driller referral | Sales Associate / Customer | Department Supervisor | 5–10 min |

### System Touchpoints
- Water system sizing calculator module (POS and mobile app) with PH-specific consumption factors
- Product catalog with tank dimensions, pump specifications, and compatibility matrix
- POS quotation module (W542) for complete system bill of materials
- Customer loyalty account integration for quotation saving (W894)
- Installation service referral (W138) for plumber installation
- Pipe cutting service (W944) for custom pipe lengths
- Real-time inventory availability per store for bulky items (tanks, pumps)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Oversizing/undersizing risk**: Incorrect sizing wastes customer money (oversized pump/tank) or fails to deliver adequate water supply (undersized); calculator must use conservative PH residential consumption factors and account for peak simultaneous usage; Sales Associate training on PH plumbing standards per W51 mitigates
- **Power outage vulnerability**: Water pumps require electricity; during typhoons when power is frequently interrupted, pumped water systems fail; Sales Associate should recommend: (a) oversized tank for gravity-fed emergency supply; (b) generator compatibility for pump operation during outages per W913; (c) manual pump backup option for critical installations
- **Well water quality**: Shallow well water in PH may contain contaminants (bacteria, iron, manganese); Sales Associate should recommend water testing and filtration; BuildRight does not provide water testing services but can refer customers to accredited testing laboratories
- **Bulky item logistics**: Water tanks (especially 1000L+ sizes) and large pumps are bulky and heavy; store storage space is limited; delivery scheduling per W5D is mandatory for tanks above 500L; loading assistance per W950 required
- **Competitive pricing sensitivity**: Water tanks and pumps are commodity items with price-transparent online competition; BuildRight's value-add is the professional sizing service, complete system recommendation, and installation referral — not lowest price on individual items

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate (Plumbing Specialist)**: Water system consultations consume ~1.5–2.5 hours/day at 15–20 consultations; absorbed by existing Plumbing Department Sales Associate with plumbing product certification per W51
- **Training**: 12-hour water system sizing and pump selection certification per W51; includes TDH calculation, PH residential plumbing standards, and accessory cross-selling
- **No incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Water demand assessment: 8–12 min
- Tank sizing calculation: 3–5 min
- Pump sizing & selection: 5–8 min
- Accessories & piping recommendation: 5–8 min
- Customer review & quotation: 5–10 min
- **Total per consultation**: 26–43 min of staff time

## W985. Store-Level Customer Aircon Sizing & BTU/Horsepower Calculation Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests aircon sizing recommendation at store appliance section or via mobile app |
| **Frequency** | ~8,000–10,000 calculations/month chain-wide (~40–50/store/month); peaks March–May (PH summer) |
| **Volume** | 1–3 rooms per consultation; average 1.6 rooms |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Home Appliances) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Appliance Specialist), Customer, Category Manager (pricing) |

### Background

Air conditioning is the highest-value appliance category for BuildRight Depot and a necessity in the Philippine tropical climate where temperatures regularly exceed 33°C with 70–90% humidity. Correct sizing is critical: an undersized aircon runs continuously without reaching target temperature (wasting electricity and shortening compressor life), while an oversized aircon cycles on/off too frequently (causing temperature swings, poor humidity removal, and wasted energy). Philippine residential rooms present unique sizing challenges: high ceilings (2.7–3.5m common in PH construction), large windows (poor insulation), concrete walls (thermal mass retention), and heat-generating appliances (kitchens). The Philippine market uses both BTU/hr (international) and HP/horsepower (local terminology) ratings interchangeably — 1HP ≈ 9,000 BTU, 1.5HP ≈ 12,000 BTU, 2HP ≈ 18,000 BTU. Inverter technology has transformed the market (30–50% energy savings over conventional units) but adds complexity to sizing recommendations. This service is a major differentiator during the March–May summer peak when aircon sales can account for 15–20% of total store revenue.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Room Assessment & Data Collection**: Sales Associate collects room parameters: (a) room dimensions — length × width × ceiling height (meters); (b) room type — bedroom, living room, kitchen, office, commercial space; (c) window area and orientation — north/south/east/west facing; window type (jalousie, sliding, awning, fixed glass); glass type (clear, tinted, reflective); presence of curtains/blinds; (d) wall material — concrete hollow block (CHB, most common in PH), wood frame, gypsum board partition; (e) insulation — none (typical PH residential), foam panel, fiberglass; (f) room occupancy — number of people typically in room; (g) heat sources — direct sunlight exposure, number of heat-generating appliances (refrigerator, oven, computer, TV); (h) floor level — ground floor, upper floor, top floor (roof heat gain); (i) existing electrical capacity — dedicated circuit available? Amperage of circuit breaker? | Sales Associate / Customer | Department Supervisor | 8–12 min |
| 2 | **BTU/HP Calculation & Unit Recommendation**: System calculates: (a) base cooling load = room area (sqm) × 500 BTU/sqm (PH tropical baseline for typical residential); (b) adjustments — ceiling height > 2.7m (+10%), west-facing window (+10%), large window area > 40% of wall (+10%), kitchen/heat sources (+4,000 BTU), top floor under roof (+15%), poor insulation (+10%); each occupant beyond first (+600 BTU); (c) total required BTU = base load + adjustments; (d) recommended unit size — 0.5HP/5,000BTU (small bedroom ≤10sqm), 0.75HP/7,000BTU (bedroom 10–14sqm), 1HP/9,000–10,000BTU (bedroom 14–18sqm), 1.5HP/12,000BTU (master bedroom/living room 18–25sqm), 2HP/18,000BTU (living room 25–35sqm), 2.5HP/24,000BTU (large living room 35–50sqm), cassette/duct type ≥3HP (commercial); (e) unit type recommendation — window type (economy, easy installation, typical PH), split type (inverter, quieter, more efficient), portable (rental/temporary), floor-mounted (large rooms), cassette/duct (commercial); (f) inverter vs. conventional recommendation — inverter recommended for rooms used ≥8 hours/day (bedrooms, offices); conventional acceptable for intermittent use (living rooms, guest rooms) | System / Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 3–5 min (automated) |
| 3 | **Electrical Compatibility Check**: Sales Associate verifies: (a) required electrical load — unit wattage (typically 700W–2,500W depending on HP rating); (b) customer's circuit breaker amperage — standard PH residential circuit is 20A/30A; (c) dedicated circuit requirement — units ≥1.5HP require dedicated circuit; (d) wiring gauge — units ≥1HP require 2.0mm² AWG14 wire minimum; ≥2HP require 3.5mm² AWG12; (e) Meralco/utility provider demand — some areas have limited residential electrical capacity; (f) system flags if customer's electrical infrastructure is incompatible and recommends licensed electrician referral per W989 | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 3–5 min |
| 4 | **Cross-Sell Accessories & Services**: Sales Associate recommends: (a) aircon installation service per W138 (professional installation with copper piping, drainage hose, and electrical connection); (b) wall bracket/concealed mount for split-type outdoor unit; (c) aircon cover (off-season protection during Nov–Feb when usage drops); (d) air filter replacement schedule (clean monthly, replace quarterly); (e) voltage regulator/AVR (recommended for areas with unstable voltage — common in provincial PH); (f) drain hose and condensate pump (for units without gravity drainage); (g) extended warranty registration per W935; (h) electrical supplies per W991 (wiring, circuit breaker, outlet) if customer needs electrical upgrade | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 5–8 min |
| 5 | **Quotation & Order Processing**: Customer reviews recommendations; Sales Associate: (a) presents total cost including unit, accessories, and installation; (b) creates POS quotation per W542 valid for 7 days (aircon pricing is volatile during summer peak); (c) for split-type units: coordinates installation scheduling per W138 with 2–5 day lead time; (d) for window-type units: customer may self-install or request installation service; (e) processes sale or saves quotation to customer loyalty account per W894; (f) system registers unit serial number at POS for warranty activation per W33 | Sales Associate / Customer | Department Supervisor | 5–10 min |

- Product catalog with aircon specifications (BTU, HP, wattage, EER, dimensions, weight)

## W986. Store-Level Customer Rebar Cutting, Bending & Fabrication Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer (contractor, builder, or homeowner) requests rebar cutting, bending, or custom fabrication at store |
| **Frequency** | ~5,000–7,000 transactions/month chain-wide (~25–35/store/month) |
| **Volume** | Average 50–200 pieces per transaction; 1–3 bar sizes per order |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Lumber & Building Materials) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Steel Specialist), Customer, Receiving Clerk (steel inventory), Category Manager (pricing) |

### Background

Steel reinforcement bars (rebar) are the structural backbone of Philippine construction, used in virtually every residential, commercial, and infrastructure project. Rebar is sold in standard 6-meter (20ft) lengths in sizes ranging from 10mm (Grade 33, light residential) to 25mm (Grade 60, structural/commercial). Contractors frequently need rebar cut to specific lengths (for stirrups, ties, and column dowels) or bent into hooks, L-shapes, U-shapes, and custom profiles for beam and column reinforcement. Manual rebar cutting and bending at construction sites is labor-intensive and imprecise; BuildRight's in-store cutting and bending service saves contractor labor time, ensures dimensional accuracy, and increases store revenue through cutting/bending fees and associated sales (tie wire, chair supports, spacers, concrete, cement). The service is particularly valuable for small-to-medium contractors who do not own rebar fabrication equipment. Post-typhoon reconstruction surges create massive demand spikes for rebar fabrication services.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## W990. Customer Solar Panel System Sizing & ROI Calculator Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer inquires about solar panel system at store or via mobile app |
| **Frequency** | ~1,500–2,500 consultations/month chain-wide (~7–12/store/month); growing 30–40% annually |
| **Volume** | 1 system per consultation |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Electrical) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Electrical Specialist), Customer, Category Manager (pricing) |

### Background

The Philippines has among the highest residential electricity rates in Southeast Asia (PHP 9–12/kWh average, higher than Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia), making solar energy increasingly attractive for homeowners and businesses. The Net Metering program under the Renewable Energy Act (RA 9513) allows grid-tied solar systems to export excess energy back to the utility (Meralco, VECO, Davao Light, etc.) at a feed-in tariff, effectively spinning the meter backward. However, solar system sizing is complex — it requires analyzing the customer's electricity consumption, roof orientation and shading, available mounting area, local solar irradiance (averaging 4.5–5.5 kWh/m²/day in the Philippines), and financial parameters (system cost, electricity rate trajectory, payback period, ROI). BuildRight sells solar panels (mono/poly-crystalline, 400W–550W), inverters (string and micro-inverter), mounting structures, batteries (optional for hybrid systems), and electrical accessories. This consultation service drives high-value sales (residential systems average PHP 80,000–300,000) and positions BuildRight as a solar energy partner rather than just a component supplier.

## W995. Vendor Consignment Inventory Ageing Analysis & Automatic Markdown Trigger

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly consignment inventory ageing analysis run (scheduled); or individual SKU ageing threshold breach |
| **Frequency** | Monthly analysis; continuous threshold monitoring |
| **Volume** | ~300 consignment SKUs from 12 key vendors; ~30–50 SKUs flagged for markdown action per month |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Buyer, Merchandising Planner, Vendor (consignment partner), Store Operations |

### Background

Consignment inventory — where the vendor owns the stock until it sells — represents a strategic advantage for BuildRight (reduced inventory carrying cost) but also creates operational challenges. Consignment SKUs tend to be slower-moving items (major appliances, premium tiles, specialty fixtures) where BuildRight provides shelf space and the vendor bears the inventory risk. However, consignment inventory that sits unsold for extended periods consumes valuable shelf/floor space, makes the store look stale, and creates administrative overhead for periodic vendor reconciliation per W23. Without an automated ageing analysis and markdown trigger, consignment inventory can become "dead stock" that vendors are reluctant to repatriate and BuildRight cannot dispose of (because it doesn't own it). This workflow establishes clear ageing thresholds, automatic markdown escalation protocols, and vendor notification/resolution processes that keep consignment inventory fresh and productive.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly Consignment Inventory Ageing Report Generation**: System generates ageing analysis for all consignment SKUs: (a) age buckets — 0–30 days (fresh), 31–60 days (aging), 61–90 days (slow), 91–180 days (very slow), 181–365 days (stale), >365 days (dead stock); (b) per SKU: date of receipt, total units received, units sold, units remaining, days since receipt, sell-through rate (%); (c) per vendor: total consignment inventory value by age bucket, number of SKUs in each bucket, shelf space occupied (sqm) by stale/dead consignment stock; (d) exception list: SKUs breaching 60-day, 90-day, and 180-day thresholds; (e) system generates report and distributes to Category Manager and Buyer via email/dashboard | System | Category Manager | Automated (5 min to review) |
| 2 | **Ageing Threshold Escalation & Markdown Trigger**: System automatically triggers actions based on ageing thresholds: (a) **61–90 days** (Yellow Alert): System sends automated notification to Buyer and Vendor with SKU details, ageing data, and sell-through rate; Buyer contacts vendor to discuss: promotional markdown (vendor-funded per W161), improved in-store positioning, or vendor-initiated stock rotation; no mandatory action yet — collaborative approach; (b) **91–180 days** (Orange Alert): System triggers mandatory vendor notification with 14-day response deadline; Buyer and Category Manager review; options: (i) vendor-funded markdown of 15–25% off SRP with vendor absorbing margin loss; (ii) vendor repatriates stock (at vendor's logistics cost); (iii) BuildRight converts consignment to owned stock at negotiated discount (50–70% of original cost) and applies W93 markdown; vendor must respond within 14 days or option (iii) auto-activates; (c) **>180 days** (Red Alert): System auto-triggers markdown at 30% off SRP if vendor did not respond to Orange Alert; markdown cost charged to vendor account per consignment agreement terms; unsold units after 30-day markdown period: vendor must repatriate within 14 days or BuildRight disposes per W91 (salvage/scrap) with vendor bearing disposal cost | System / Buyer | Category Manager | 1–2 hours/month (escalation handling) |
| 3 | **Vendor Communication & Resolution**: Buyer contacts vendor per escalation tier: (a) presents ageing data and sell-through analysis; (b) discusses root causes — pricing too high, product not competitive, poor in-store visibility, wrong store cluster allocation; (c) negotiates resolution — promotional pricing, stock swap (replace slow SKU with faster variant), improved display, or stock return; (d) records resolution in vendor collaboration portal per W357 (vendor portal); (e) for vendors with recurring ageing issues (≥3 SKUs in Orange/Red per quarter): escalates to Category Manager for consignment agreement review and potential shelf space reallocation | Buyer / Category Manager | Category Manager | 30–60 min per vendor |

| 5 | **Consignment Settlement Adjustment**: During monthly consignment settlement per W23: (a) system calculates: units sold × original agreed price (vendor revenue), minus markdown cost (charged to vendor per consignment agreement), minus any repatriation logistics cost; (b) settlement report emailed to vendor per W357; (c) vendor disputes resolved per vendor dispute resolution per W359; (d) payment to vendor net of markdown charges per standard payment terms | Finance Department / Buyer | Category Manager | Included in monthly settlement cycle |

### System Touchpoints
- Consignment inventory ageing analysis module (automated monthly run)
- Ageing threshold monitoring with automated escalation triggers
- Vendor portal (W357) for vendor notification and resolution tracking
- Markdown authorization and POS price update per W93
- Real-time event streaming for price synchronization per POS and ECOM
- Shelf label reprinting per W63
- Consignment settlement calculation per W23
- SKU-level sell-through rate tracking and analytics

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Vendor relationship strain**: Automatic markdown triggers may conflict with vendor expectations, especially if consignment agreement terms are ambiguous; clear contractual terms in consignment agreement (signed during vendor onboarding per W36) must specify ageing thresholds, markdown authority, and vendor cost absorption; Legal review per W230 before agreement execution
- **Shelf space opportunity cost**: Consignment inventory that ages beyond 90 days occupies shelf space that could be allocated to faster-moving owned inventory; Category Manager must quantify opportunity cost (revenue per sqm of alternative products) and present to vendor as economic justification for markdown or repatriation
- **Data accuracy**: Ageing analysis depends on accurate receiving dates and inventory counts per W3 and W6; cycle count accuracy ≥97% per store is required for reliable ageing data; stores with low inventory accuracy may generate false ageing alerts

### Staffing Implication
- **Buyer**: Consignment ageing management adds ~2–3 hours/month for 12 consignment vendors; absorbed by existing Buyer role as part of vendor management responsibilities per W44
- **Category Manager**: Escalation review and markdown approval adds ~1–2 hours/month; absorbed by existing Category Manager role
- **No incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Ageing report review: 5 min/month
- Escalation handling: 1–2 hours/month
- Vendor communication: 30–60 min per vendor
- Markdown execution: 30–60 min per markdown event
- Settlement adjustment: included in monthly cycle

## W998. Customer Septic Tank & Wastewater System Sizing Recommendation Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer (homeowner, contractor, or developer) requests septic tank sizing for new construction or renovation |
| **Frequency** | ~1,500–2,500 consultations/month chain-wide (~7–12/store/month) |
| **Volume** | 1 system per consultation |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Plumbing) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Plumbing Specialist), Customer, Category Manager (pricing) |

### Background

Proper wastewater treatment is a critical infrastructure requirement for every Philippine residential and commercial building. The majority of Philippine homes outside centralized sewerage systems (which serve only ~10% of the population, mainly Metro Manila) rely on septic tanks for wastewater treatment. DENR Administrative Order 2005-10 mandates septic tank sizing, construction, and maintenance standards, and LGUs require septic tank plans for building permit approval per W54. Incorrect sizing — too small leads to overflow and groundwater contamination (a serious public health issue in PH); too large wastes construction cost and space — directly impacts customer safety and compliance. BuildRight sells pre-fabricated septic tanks (polyethylene, 1–10 cubic meters) and all materials for CHB-constructed septic tanks (cement, CHB, waterproofing, PVC pipes, fittings). This service integrates with W989 (plumbing layout) and W998 (water system) to provide complete water infrastructure planning.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Wastewater Load Assessment**: Sales Associate collects: (a) number of occupants/users; (b) building type — residential (single-family, multi-family), commercial, institutional; (c) number of bathrooms, kitchens, laundry areas; (d) water source — municipal supply or well pump per W984 (affects water consumption per capita); (e) soil type — clay (poor percolation), sandy (good percolation), loam; (f) available space for septic tank installation; (g) water table depth (high water table in coastal/lowland PH requires waterproof tank construction and may prevent drain field installation); (h) LGU requirements — some LGUs mandate minimum tank size or specific construction standards | Sales Associate / Customer | Department Supervisor | 8–12 min |
| 2 | **Septic Tank Sizing Calculation**: System calculates per DENR AO 2005-10 and NPCP: (a) daily wastewater volume = occupants × water consumption (150 L/person/day residential) × 80% (wastewater generation factor); (b) septic tank capacity = daily volume × 2–3 days retention time (minimum per DENR: 1.0 cubic meters effective capacity for residential); (c) recommended tank size — 1.0 cu.m. (1–3 occupants), 1.5 cu.m. (4–6 occupants), 2.0 cu.m. (7–10 occupants), 3.0 cu.m. ### Background

Tile and flooring is a high-value, visual purchase where color, texture, and appearance under the customer's actual lighting conditions are critical to satisfaction. Showroom lighting in BuildRight stores (fluorescent/LED) differs significantly from residential lighting (warm LED, natural light through windows), making in-store appearance unreliable. ## W1000. Customer Ceiling System Material Calculator & Design Recommendation Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests ceiling material estimation at store or via mobile app |
| **Frequency** | ~3,000–4,000 calculations/month chain-wide (~15–20/store/month) |
| **Volume** | 1–3 rooms per calculation; average 1.8 rooms |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Lumber & Building Materials) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Building Materials Specialist), Customer, Category Manager (pricing) |

### Background

Ceiling installation is a standard component of Philippine residential and commercial construction, serving both aesthetic and functional purposes (concealing structural elements, electrical wiring, plumbing pipes, and providing thermal insulation). The most common ceiling systems in the Philippines are: (1) gypsum board on metal framing (standard residential), (2) acoustic ceiling tiles on T-bar grid (commercial/offices), (3) PVC ceiling panels (economy residential, moisture-resistant for bathrooms), and (4) plywood/hardiflex on wood framing (traditional residential). Correct material estimation is essential because ceiling materials are fragile (gypsum board), bulky (difficult to transport in small vehicles), and require precise framing layout. Customers who underestimate materials face project delays; those who overestimate pay for return logistics of fragile, easily damaged sheets. The service integrates with W991 (electrical circuit design) for ceiling-mounted light fixture planning and W989 (plumbing layout) for concealed pipe routing.

### Steps

## W1001. Store-Level Employee Performance-Based Profit Sharing & Incentive Bonus Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly/quarterly store performance review cycle; achievement of profit sharing or bonus thresholds |
| **Frequency** | Monthly calculation; quarterly payout |
| **Volume** | All store employees (~29 per store × 200 stores = ~5,800 employees) |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, HR Department, Finance Department, CHRO |

### Background

Employee motivation and retention are critical challenges in Philippine retail, where turnover rates average 15–20% annually. BuildRight's store-level staffing model of 29 employees per store must be highly productive to achieve the per-store revenue target of ~PHP 25M/month. A performance-based profit sharing and incentive bonus program aligns employee behavior with store-level P&L objectives (sales growth, margin improvement, shrinkage reduction, customer satisfaction). Unlike corporate-level bonus programs managed centrally, store-level profit sharing distributes a percentage of store profit above target directly to store employees, creating a direct line of sight between individual effort and financial reward. The program must comply with Philippine labor law (DOLE) regarding variable compensation — bonus amounts are discretionary and separate from 13th month pay per RA 10691, and cannot be guaranteed or considered part of regular wage for statutory benefit computation.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly Store Performance Calculation**: System calculates store-level performance: (a) actual sales vs. target sales (per store budget per W26); (b) actual gross margin vs. target gross margin (28–32% target); (c) store operating expenses vs. budget (labor cost, utilities, supplies); (d) shrinkage/loss rate vs. target (<1.5% of sales); (e) customer satisfaction score vs. target (≥85%); (f) net store profit vs. target; (g) system calculates profit sharing pool = 10% of store profit above target (if store exceeds target); maximum pool capped at 20% of store payroll; (h) if store does not exceed target: no profit sharing pool for that quarter | System / Finance Department | Store Manager | Automated |
| 2 | **Individual Performance Scoring**: System calculates individual employee scores based on: (a) attendance and punctuality (biometric data per W34) — weight 15%; (b) sales contribution (individual sales per POS login for Sales Associates, or department sales for Department Supervisors) — weight 25%; (c) customer satisfaction (individual CSAT scores from post-transaction surveys) — weight 20%; (d) shrinkage in assigned area (cycle count variance per W6) — weight 15%; (e) training completion (certifications per W51) — weight 10%; (f) peer and manager evaluation (Store Manager and Department Supervisor subjective assessment) — weight 15%; (g) individual score = weighted average of all components, normalized to 0–100 scale | System / Store Manager | Store Manager | 1–2 hours/quarter per store |
| 3 | **Profit Sharing Distribution Calculation**: System calculates individual payout: (a) employee share = profit sharing pool × (individual score ÷ sum of all employee scores in store); (b) pro-rata adjustment for employees with <3 months tenure or partial-quarter employment; (c) minimum payout threshold — employee must score ≥60/100 to receive any payout; (d) maximum individual payout capped at 1.5× monthly basic salary per quarter (DOLE compliance — ensures bonus remains discretionary and not structurally guaranteed); (e) Store Manager reviews distribution and approves; (f) Finance Department validates calculation and approves total pool | System / Store Manager / Finance Department | CHRO (policy), Finance Department (budget) | 2–3 hours/quarter |
| 4 | **Payout Processing & Communication**: HR processes payout: (a) profit sharing amount added to employee's regular semi-monthly payroll per W10 as a separate line item "Quarterly Profit Share"; (b) tax treatment — profit sharing is taxable compensation income per BIR (not tax-exempt); employer withholding tax per TRAIN law graduated table per W90; (c) profit sharing does NOT count toward 13th month pay calculation (per DOLE advisory, discretionary bonuses are excluded from 13th month base); (d) Store Manager conducts individual or team meeting to communicate: payout amount, performance score breakdown, and areas for improvement; (e) system generates individual performance statement showing score components and payout | HR Department / Finance Department / Store Manager | Store Manager | 30–60 min per payout cycle (team meeting) |
| 5 | **Quarterly Performance Review & Goal Setting**: Store Manager conducts quarterly performance review with each employee: (a) reviews individual score breakdown — strengths, areas for improvement; (b) discusses customer feedback and shrinkage data; (c) sets specific goals for next quarter (e.g., increase individual sales by 10%, complete product knowledge certification per W51, improve attendance); (d) identifies training needs per W51; (e) documents goals in performance management system per W72; (f) for underperformers (score <60): Store Manager implements performance improvement plan per W72 with 30-day review cycle | Store Manager | HR Manager | 15–20 min per employee (quarterly) |

### System Touchpoints
- Store P&L module with automated profit sharing pool calculation
- POS sales tracking by employee login for individual sales attribution
- Biometric attendance system per W34 for attendance scoring
- Customer satisfaction survey system per W65 for individual CSAT scoring
- Cycle count variance reporting per W6 for shrinkage scoring
- Training completion tracking per W51 for certification scoring
- Payroll system per W10 for profit sharing payout and tax withholding
- Performance management system per W72 for goal setting and reviews
- BIR tax compliance per W90 for proper withholding

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Sales attribution accuracy**: POS transactions are attributed to the logged-in cashier, not the Sales Associate who assisted the customer; for stores with 3 POS terminals and 12 Sales Associates, accurate individual sales attribution requires proper POS login rotation; mobile POS per W206 may improve attribution
- **Subjective evaluation bias**: The 15% peer/manager evaluation component introduces subjectivity; Store Manager must use standardized evaluation criteria (rubric) and document justification for scores; HR reviews score distributions for abnormal patterns (e.g., all employees receiving similar scores, suggesting lazy evaluation)
- **Profit volatility**: Store profit can be affected by factors outside employee control (macro-economic conditions, competitor opening nearby, supply chain disruptions); profit sharing based on profit above target can result in zero payout through no fault of employees; consider maintaining minimum token payout for qualifying scores even in low-profit quarters
- **Tax impact**: Profit sharing is fully taxable, reducing net take-home for lower-income employees; communication must clearly show gross and net amounts to avoid disappointment; some employees may prefer smaller tax-advantaged benefits (meal allowance, rice subsidy) over taxable cash bonus
- **Inter-store equity**: Stores in high-traffic, high-revenue locations (Metro Manila) will naturally generate larger profit sharing pools than provincial stores, even with similar employee effort; consider normalization by store revenue tier or geographic cost-of-living adjustment

### Staffing Implication
- **Store Manager**: Performance scoring and reviews consume ~8–10 hours/quarter for 29 employees; absorbed by existing Store Manager role
- **HR Department**: Profit sharing administration adds ~4–6 hours/quarter across all stores; absorbed by existing HR team
- **Finance Department**: Profit sharing pool validation and payroll processing adds ~2–4 hours/quarter; absorbed by existing Finance team
- **No incremental headcount**

- Vendor portal per W357 for sample submission and disposition notification

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Sample quality vs. production batch quality**: Vendor samples are often higher quality than mass-produced batches; QA must evaluate a production sample (not a hand-picked "golden sample") before test batch procurement; if possible, request production-line random sample rather than vendor-selected sample
- **Test batch cost**: Test batches represent inventory investment (50–500 units × unit cost) with uncertain return; Category Manager must budget for test batch inventory risk (typically 20–30% of test batches are rejected, resulting in markdown or return losses); annual test batch budget allocated as part of merchandising department's new product development fund
- **Pilot store bias**: Test results from 3–5 pilot stores may not be representative of chain-wide performance (pilot stores may have unique customer demographics, Store Manager enthusiasm may inflate sell-through, test product gets disproportionate attention vs. regular assortment); Category Manager must apply statistical confidence filters and consider regional variation before rollout approval
- **Vendor relationship management**: Rejecting a vendor's product after test batch can strain the relationship, especially for smaller vendors who invested significantly in sample development; Buyer must communicate rejection constructively with specific improvement recommendations per W44; maintain vendor relationship for future product opportunities
- **Speed to market**: The sample → evaluation → test batch → rollout cycle can take 4–6 months; fast-moving categories (seasonal items, trending products) may miss market opportunity during the evaluation period; Category Manager may fast-track evaluation for clearly superior products (skip test batch, direct to limited rollout) based on market intelligence per W130

### Staffing Implication
- **Buyer**: Sample evaluation and test batch management adds ~3–5 hours/week for active samples and test batches; absorbed by existing Buyer role as part of assortment management per W1
- **QA**: Product quality evaluation adds ~1–2 hours per sample; absorbed by existing quality function (per W150 product quality testing) or Buyer with quality training
- **Merchandising Planner**: Test batch store allocation and monitoring adds ~1–2 hours/week; absorbed by existing Merchandising Planner role
- **Category Manager**: Disposition decisions add ~1–2 hours/week for review and approval; absorbed by existing Category Manager role
- **No incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Sample registration: 15–20 min per sample
- Quality & commercial evaluation: 1–2 hours per sample
- Test batch procurement & deployment: 2–3 hours per test batch
- Test period monitoring: 30 min/week per active test batch
- Disposition & rollout decision: 1–2 hours per disposition
- **Total per product lifecycle**: 8–15 hours over 4–6 month evaluation period
