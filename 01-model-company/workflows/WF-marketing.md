## W83. Marketing Campaign Planning, Execution & Performance Measurement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Campaign calendar milestone; or ad-hoc campaign request from C-suite / Category Manager |
| **Frequency** | 6 major bi-monthly campaigns/year + 12 monthly hot-deal cycles + ad-hoc campaigns (store openings, seasonal pushes, competitive response) |
| **Volume** | ~18–24 planned campaigns/year + ~5–10 ad-hoc campaigns; each campaign involves 2–8 channels and 200–500+ SKUs |
| **Owner** | Marketing Campaign Manager |
| **Participants** | Campaign Manager, Content Manager, CMO, VP Merchandising, Category Managers, Pricing Analysts, Store Ops Director, Ecom Team, external creative / media agencies |

### Background

BuildRight's ~24-person Marketing team operates across brand, promotions, loyalty, ecommerce, digital, content, and customer service. While W13 covers promotional pricing execution and W50 covers product content management, there is no end-to-end operational workflow for marketing campaign management — from strategic brief through creative production, channel execution, budget tracking, and post-campaign ROI measurement. This workflow bridges the gap between merchandising strategy (W1, W13, W32) and customer-facing execution across all channels.

### Campaign Types

| Campaign Type | Frequency | Channels | Typical Budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Major bi-monthly sale event** | 6/year | In-store signage, POS materials, email, SMS, social media, website banners, digital ads | PHP 2–5M/event |
| **Monthly hot deals** | 12/year | Email, SMS, website, in-store shelf talkers | PHP 200–500K/cycle |
| **New store opening** | ~10–15/year | Local radio, flyers, social media geo-targeted ads, SMS blast to nearby loyalty members, PR | PHP 500K–1M/opening |
| **Seasonal push** | 4/year (aligned with W32) | Full multi-channel: TV, digital, print, in-store events, email, SMS, social | PHP 3–8M/season |
| **Loyalty member engagement** | Monthly | Email, app push notification, in-store POS prompts | PHP 100–300K/month |
| **Competitive response** | Ad-hoc | Social media, email, SMS, local radio | PHP 200–500K |
| **Community / CSR event** | 2–4/year | PR, social media, in-store | PHP 100–300K |

### Phase 1: Campaign Planning & Brief (T-8 to T-6 weeks before campaign launch)

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Category Manager or VP Merchandising submits campaign brief request to Campaign Manager: campaign objective (traffic, revenue, margin, clearance, brand awareness), target customer segment, proposed SKU list, desired timing, and rough budget expectation | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1 hour/brief |
| 2 | Campaign Manager reviews brief with CMO; assesses feasibility against campaign calendar (avoid overlapping campaigns), budget availability, and resource capacity (creative team workload, external agency availability) | Campaign Manager | CMO | 30 min/brief |
| 3 | Campaign Manager develops campaign brief document: (a) campaign name and code (for system tracking and cost allocation), (b) objectives with measurable KPIs (target revenue lift, foot traffic increase, ecommerce conversion rate, new loyalty sign-ups), (c) target audience (all customers, loyalty tiers, trade accounts, geographic segments), (d) channel plan — which channels (in-store, email, SMS, social media, digital ads, print, radio, PR), (e) promotional mechanics (percentage off, bundle deals, gift-with-purchase, loyalty points multiplier, clearance pricing), (f) SKU list and pricing per W13, (g) timeline — key dates for creative review, material production, distribution, and go-live, (h) budget allocation by channel with estimated cost per channel, (i) success metrics and measurement plan | Campaign Manager | CMO | 4–8 hours/campaign |
| 4 | CMO reviews and approves campaign brief; VP Merchandising confirms SKU list and pricing strategy; CFO approves budget for campaigns exceeding PHP 1M | CMO / VP Merchandising / CFO | CEO (if budget > PHP 5M) | 1 hour/campaign |
| 5 | Campaign Manager creates campaign record in system: campaign code, objectives, KPIs, budget, timeline, assigned team members, linked promo pricing records (W13), and linked SKU list; system generates campaign cost center for expense tracking | Campaign Manager | CMO | 30 min/campaign |

### Phase 2: Creative Production & Asset Development (T-6 to T-2 weeks)

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Campaign Manager assigns creative deliverables: (a) in-store POS materials (posters, shelf talkers, banners, endcap displays, outdoor signage), (b) digital assets (website banners, social media posts/stories, email templates, digital ad creatives), (c) print materials (flyers, newspaper inserts, direct mailers), (d) SMS/email copy, (e) press releases or PR materials (if applicable) | Campaign Manager | CMO | 1 hour/campaign |
| 7 | Content Manager and Content Specialists produce or coordinate creative assets: (a) in-house for standard materials (email templates, social media posts, SMS copy, shelf talkers using templates), (b) external creative agency for major campaigns (TV/radio spots, high-end print, complex digital campaigns) per W62 service contract; agency briefed with brand guidelines, campaign messaging framework, and deliverable specifications | Content Manager / External Agency | CMO | 1–3 weeks/campaign |
| 7a | Content Manager ensures all campaign product content (images, descriptions, specifications) is complete for campaign SKUs in PIM per W50; prioritizes campaign SKUs in the content queue; escalates incomplete content to Category Manager with T-2 week deadline | Content Manager | CMO | Part of W50 |
| 8 | Campaign Manager reviews all creative assets against brand guidelines: consistent visual identity, correct pricing (match W13/W40 system prices), correct SKU descriptions, legal disclaimers (BIR-compliant pricing disclosures, promo terms and conditions), and inclusive language | Campaign Manager | CMO | 2–4 hours/campaign |
| 9 | CMO approves final creative assets; Campaign Manager archives approved assets in system linked to campaign record | CMO | CEO (for major brand campaigns) | 30 min/campaign |

### Phase 3: Channel Execution & Distribution (T-2 to T-0 weeks)

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | **In-store materials**: Campaign Manager coordinates printing and distribution of POS materials to 200 stores — centralized printing at HQ, shipped to stores via DC delivery trucks (W4) or direct courier for urgent materials; includes installation instructions and display planogram for promotional displays; Store Operations Director confirms receipt with Store Managers | Campaign Manager / Store Ops Director | COO | 1 week distribution |
| 11 | **Digital channels**: (a) Ecom Team uploads website banners and landing pages to ecommerce platform; configures campaign-specific landing page with promo SKUs and pricing per W13, (b) Campaign Manager schedules social media posts via social media management tool (Meta Business Suite, etc.) with approved creatives and copy, (c) Campaign Manager configures email campaign in email marketing platform: segment audience per campaign brief, schedule send dates, set up A/B test variants if applicable, (d) Campaign Manager configures SMS blast via SMS gateway provider for loyalty member segments | Campaign Manager / Ecom Team | CMO | 2–4 hours/channel |
| 12 | **Traditional media**: Campaign Manager coordinates with media buying agency (or direct media placement) for radio spots, print ads, and digital ads; confirms placement schedule, flight dates, and audience targeting parameters; media costs tracked to campaign cost center | Campaign Manager / Media Agency | CMO | Per W62 contract |
| 13 | **Loyalty integration**: Campaign Manager coordinates with Loyalty Manager to configure campaign-specific loyalty mechanics per W17 — e.g., double-points weekends, bonus points for specific categories, tier-exclusive early access, member-only pricing; Loyalty Manager configures in loyalty engine and tests before go-live | Campaign Manager / Loyalty Manager | CMO | 2 hours/campaign |
| 14 | **System readiness check** (T-3 days): Campaign Manager verifies with IT that (a) promotional pricing is loaded in POS per W13.5, (b) ecommerce campaign pages are live and pricing matches, (c) email and SMS campaigns are scheduled and tested, (d) campaign tracking tags/pixels are correctly implemented for digital performance measurement | Campaign Manager / IT | CIO | 2 hours/campaign |

| 24 | Annual: CMO and CFO review annual marketing budget utilization and ROI; propose next year's marketing budget allocation by channel per W26 annual budget cycle | CMO / CFO | CEO | 4 hours/year |

### System Touchpoints
- Campaign record creation with code, objectives, KPIs, budget, timeline, team, linked SKUs and pricing (W83.5)
- Campaign cost center for expense tracking with GL allocation (W83.5, 25–27)
- Creative asset storage linked to campaign record with version control (W83.9)
- Campaign performance dashboard: real-time sell-through, ecommerce metrics, digital engagement, loyalty activity (W83.16)
- Channel execution tracking: material distribution status to stores, digital asset publication status, email/SMS delivery status (W83.10–15)
- Post-campaign analytics: revenue attribution, ROI per channel, customer acquisition cost, store-level performance (W83.19–21)
- Campaign archive with full documentation for future reference (W83.22)
- Integration with W13 (promotional pricing), W17 (loyalty mechanics), W35 (management reporting), W40 (regular pricing), W50 (product content), W58 (corporate account campaigns), W62 (agency contracts), W65 (CSAT measurement)

### Time Estimate
- Campaign brief development: 4–8 hours/campaign (step 3)
- Creative production: 1–3 weeks/campaign depending on complexity (step 7)
- Channel execution and distribution: 2–4 hours/channel (steps 10–14)
- Launch day monitoring: 4 hours (step 15)
- Daily monitoring during campaign: 30 min/day (step 16)
- Post-campaign analysis and report: 8–12 hours/campaign (steps 19–20)
- Total per major campaign: ~40–60 hours for Campaign Manager; ~20–30 hours for monthly hot-deal cycles
- At 18–24 campaigns/year: ~500–800 hours/year for Campaign Manager

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Pricing discrepancy across channels**: If promotional pricing in POS (W13) does not exactly match ecommerce, social media, email, and in-store signage, customer complaints and DTI pricing compliance issues follow; coordinating pricing updates across 6+ channels for 200–500 SKUs per campaign is error-prone
- **Creative production bottleneck**: The Content Manager and 2–3 Content Specialists must produce assets for ~18–24 campaigns/year plus ad-hoc requests; during peak seasons (Q4 holiday, back-to-school), the creative queue overflows, leading to rushed work or delayed campaign launches
- **ROI attribution across channels**: Accurately attributing revenue to specific campaign channels (e.g., did the customer buy because of the email, the social media post, or the in-store signage?) is methodologically challenging; without reliable attribution, CMO cannot optimize channel spend with confidence
- **Store-level execution variability**: With 200 stores, in-store POS material display compliance varies widely; some stores display materials late, incorrectly, or not at all, undermining campaign consistency and making store-level performance comparisons unreliable

### Staffing Implication
- **1 Campaign Manager** (within Marketing team): manages ~18–24 campaigns/year. At ~20 hours/campaign for major events and ~8 hours/campaign for monthly cycles, total effort is ~200–300 hours/year. Absorbed within existing ~24-person Marketing team — this role likely already exists but was not formalized in a workflow.
- **Content Manager + Content Specialists**: creative production absorbed per W50 staffing.
- **No incremental headcount.**

## W104. Loyalty Program Financial Governance & Periodic Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly loyalty program review calendar; or ad-hoc triggered by significant points liability change, competitive loyalty program launch, or program design change request |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly financial review; annual program design review |
| **Volume** | 600,000 members; ~PHP 1.2–1.8B cumulative points liability; ~50M points earned/month |
| **Owner** | Loyalty Manager (operations); Cost Accountant (financial); CMO (strategic) |
| **Participants** | Loyalty Manager, Cost Accountant, Controller, CMO, CFO, Marketing Analytics |

### Background

W17 (Customer Loyalty Program Operations) covers day-to-day loyalty operations — enrollment, earning, redemption, tier management, and fraud detection. However, there is no periodic governance workflow reviewing the financial health of the loyalty program. With a cumulative points liability of PHP 1.2–1.8B on the balance sheet (deferred revenue), 600,000 members, and a program cost of ~1% of revenue, the loyalty program is a material financial obligation requiring regular oversight. This workflow covers financial reporting, liability management, breakage analysis, tier economics, program ROI, and strategic design decisions.

### Steps

### Quarterly Financial Review

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Liability reporting**: Cost Accountant prepares quarterly loyalty liability report: (a) total outstanding points balance and estimated liability (outstanding points × PHP 1.00 redemption value × expected redemption rate ~75–85%), (b) quarterly points earned vs. redeemed vs. expired (waterfall), (c) deferred revenue balance vs. prior quarter — trend analysis, (d) liability as % of trailing 12-month revenue (benchmark: should remain stable, not growing as % of revenue), (e) breakage rate (points expired ÷ points earned) — compare to PFRS 15 assumption used in W17.4 allocation, (f) deferred revenue adequacy — is the recorded deferred revenue sufficient to cover expected redemptions? | Cost Accountant | Controller | 2 hours/quarter |
| 2 | **Program engagement metrics**: Loyalty Manager prepares quarterly engagement report: (a) active member rate (members with ≥ 1 transaction in trailing 90 days), (b) enrollment trend (new sign-ups per month, channel breakdown: in-store vs. online), (c) redemption rate (points redeemed ÷ points available for redemption), (d) tier distribution (Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum member count and revenue contribution per tier), (e) average points balance per member by tier, (f) earn-to-burn ratio (points earned ÷ points redeemed — ratio > 1.0 means liability is growing), (g) member spend lift — do loyalty members spend more per transaction than non-members? (target: ≥ 15% higher ATV) | Loyalty Manager | CMO | 2 hours/quarter |
| 3 | **Program cost analysis**: Cost Accountant calculates quarterly program cost: (a) points redemption cost (value of discounts given at POS), (b) deferred revenue allocation impact on gross revenue recognition, (c) program administration cost ( Loyalty Manager time, system licensing, card printing, communication), (d) promotional loyalty costs (double-points weekends, bonus points per W17.10), (e) total program cost as % of qualifying revenue; benchmark against industry norms (1.0–1.5% for Philippine retail) | Cost Accountant | Controller | 1 hour/quarter |
| 4 | **Tier economics review**: Loyalty Manager and Cost Accountant analyze tier-level economics: (a) revenue per member by tier, (b) margin per member by tier (after loyalty discount, promotional costs, and tier-specific benefits), (c) upgrade/downgrade rates between tiers, (d) tier retention rate (% of members maintaining or upgrading tier year-over-year), (e) Platinum/Gold tier cost-benefit analysis — do high-tier benefits (exclusive pricing, priority service) drive sufficient incremental margin to justify the cost? | Loyalty Manager / Cost Accountant | CMO / Controller | 1 hour/quarter |
| 5 | **CMO and CFO joint review**: CMO (program strategy) and CFO (financial stewardship) review quarterly loyalty program performance: (a) is the liability growing sustainably?, (b) is engagement improving or declining?, (c) is the program cost within budget?, (d) are tier economics viable?, (e) competitive loyalty benchmarking — how does BuildRight's program compare to competitors (earn rate, redemption value, tier benefits)?; agree on action items (e.g., adjust earn rate, change tier thresholds, introduce new redemption options, run re-engagement campaign for dormant members) | CMO / CFO | CEO | 1 hour/quarter |

### Annual Program Design Review

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Loyalty Manager prepares annual program review document: (a) full-year financial summary (liability, cost, breakage, ROI), (b) program engagement trend over 3 years, (c) member segmentation analysis — heavy redeemers, points hoarders, dormant members, new members, (d) competitive program analysis — what are competitors offering?, (e) member satisfaction data from W65 CSAT surveys specific to loyalty, (f) proposed program changes for next year (earn rate, tier thresholds, new benefits, redemption catalog expansion, partnership opportunities) | Loyalty Manager | CMO | 8 hours/year |
| 7 | CMO, CFO, and CEO review annual program design; approve or modify proposed changes; changes to earn rate or redemption value require Board awareness due to balance sheet impact | CMO / CFO | CEO / Board | 2 hours/year |
| 8 | If program changes approved: Loyalty Manager coordinates implementation timeline with IT and Marketing; system configuration changes deployed; member communication plan executed (30-day advance notice for changes affecting earn rate or tier thresholds); updated program terms published on website and in-store | Loyalty Manager / IT | CMO | Per change scope |

### System Touchpoints
- Quarterly loyalty liability report: outstanding points, deferred revenue, breakage waterfall, liability trend (W104.1)
- Program engagement dashboard: active rate, enrollment, redemption, tier distribution, earn-to-burn, member spend lift (W104.2)
- Program cost reporting: redemption cost, deferred revenue impact, admin cost, promotional cost (W104.3)
- Tier economics analysis: revenue, margin, upgrade/downgrade, retention per tier (W104.4)
- Annual program review with competitive benchmarking and member segmentation (W104.6–7)
- Integration with W17 (day-to-day loyalty operations), W65 (CSAT measurement), W83 (marketing campaigns — loyalty mechanics), W35 (management reporting — program KPIs in executive dashboard)

### Time Estimate
- Quarterly liability reporting: 2 hours/quarter for Cost Accountant (step 1)
- Quarterly engagement metrics: 2 hours/quarter for Loyalty Manager (step 2)
- Quarterly cost analysis: 1 hour/quarter for Cost Accountant (step 3)
- Quarterly tier economics review: 1 hour/quarter (step 4)
- CMO/CFO joint quarterly review: 1 hour/quarter (step 5)
- Annual program review preparation: 8 hours/year for Loyalty Manager (step 6)
- Annual program design review meeting: 2 hours/year (step 7)
- Program change implementation: varies per scope (step 8)
- Total: Cost Accountant ~14 hours/year; Loyalty Manager ~16 hours/year; CMO/CFO ~6 hours/year

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Points liability growth on balance sheet**: With ~PHP 1.2–1.8B in deferred revenue and an earn-to-burn ratio potentially > 1.0, the liability grows faster than redemptions; if the CFO and CMO do not actively manage this, the liability can become material enough to concern external auditors and lenders
- **Breakage rate assumption sensitivity**: PFRS 15 revenue recognition for loyalty points depends on an estimated breakage rate (typically assumed at 15–25%); if actual breakage diverges significantly from the assumption, the deferred revenue balance is misstated, requiring retrospective adjustment
- **Tier economics erosion**: Platinum and Gold tier benefits (exclusive pricing, priority service, bonus points) cost more to deliver as the program matures and members accumulate status; if tier upgrade rates exceed projections, the program's cost-to-revenue ratio can deteriorate silently
- **Competitive loyalty escalation**: If a major competitor launches an aggressive loyalty program (e.g., 2× earn rate, lower redemption thresholds), BuildRight faces a dilemma: match the competitive offer (increasing program cost and liability) or risk losing high-value loyalty members to the competitor

### Staffing Implication
- **Cost Accountant**: adds ~3 hours/quarter for liability and cost analysis + ~2 hours/year for annual review = ~14 hours/year. Absorbed.
- **Loyalty Manager**: adds ~2 hours/quarter for engagement analysis + ~8 hours/year for annual review = ~16 hours/year. Absorbed.
- **CMO / CFO**: 1 hour/quarter joint review + 2 hours/year annual review = ~6 hours/year. Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount.**

## W134. Crisis Communication & Brand Reputation Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Brand crisis (e.g., major safety issue, viral complaint, legal scandal) |
| **Frequency** | Ad-hoc |
| **Volume** | ~1–2 major crises managed per year |
| **Owner** | CMO / Public Relations (PR) Manager |
| **Participants** | CEO, Legal (W125), HR, Store Ops (if applicable) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Alert & Verification: Identify and verify the scale of the crisis | PR Manager | CMO | 1 hour |
| 2 | Crisis Team Activation: Convene CEO, Legal, CMO, and relevant Dept Head | CMO | CEO | 2 hours |
| 3 | Fact Finding: Gather details and internal reports on the incident | Dept Head | CEO | 4 hours |
| 4 | Messaging: Develop official statement and FAQ for press/social media | PR Manager | CEO | 4 hours |
| 5 | Channel Execution: Release statement via PR wires, website, and social media | PR Manager | CMO | 1 hour |
| 6 | Monitoring: Track sentiment and response; adjust messaging as needed | PR Manager | CMO | 1–3 days |
| 7 | Post-Crisis Review: Document impact and update crisis protocol | PR Manager | CMO | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints
- Crisis communication management module (or shared inbox/Slack channel) for crisis team coordination
- Social media monitoring and sentiment analysis tool (integrated with W142 social media management)
- Press release distribution and media contact database (W143 PR tools)
- Website CMS for rapid publication of official statements and FAQ pages
- Customer service ticketing system (W41) for monitoring complaint volume spikes
- Integration with W41 (complaints — early crisis detection), W125 (legal case management — legal risk assessment), W142 (social media — monitoring and response), W143 (PR — media relations), W53 (data privacy breach — crisis communication)

### Time Estimate
- Alert and verification: 1 hour (step 1)
- Crisis team activation: 2 hours (step 2)
- Fact finding: 4 hours (step 3)
- Messaging development: 4 hours (step 4)
- Channel execution: 1 hour (step 5)
- Monitoring and sentiment tracking: 1–3 days of continuous attention (step 6)
- Post-crisis review: 1 day (step 7)
- Total per crisis: ~3–5 days of intensive effort from crisis team; major crises may require weeks of ongoing monitoring

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Social media velocity**: A viral complaint or safety incident can escalate from a single post to national media coverage within hours; the 1-hour verification window (step 1) may be too slow if the issue is already trending on Facebook or TikTok before the crisis team is activated
- **Legal and PR messaging conflict**: Legal may want to minimize admissions of fault while PR wants to issue an empathetic, transparent response; reconciling these perspectives under time pressure can delay the official statement, allowing the narrative to be shaped by social media and competitors
- **Crisis team availability**: The crisis team (CEO, Legal, CMO, relevant Dept Head) consists of senior leaders with full schedules; convening all members within 2 hours (step 2) during evenings, weekends, or peak business periods is challenging
- **Post-crisis reputation recovery**: Even after the immediate crisis is resolved, online sentiment and media narratives can persist for weeks or months; BuildRight may need sustained PR investment and community engagement to fully rebuild trust, especially for safety-related incidents

### Staffing Implication
- **PR Manager**: crisis communication is a core responsibility; at 1–2 major crises/year, effort is highly variable (0 hours in quiet periods, 40+ hours during a crisis). Absorbed within existing PR duties (W143).
- **CMO**: serves as crisis team lead; ~3–5 days of intensive involvement per major crisis. Absorbed.
- **CEO**: crisis team convener; must be available within 2 hours for activation. Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount**, but recommend annual crisis simulation exercise (2–3 hours) to maintain readiness.

## W496. Customer Loyalty Fraud Detection & Prevention

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Fraud alert from loyalty system analytics; anomaly detection threshold breach; customer complaint investigation; manual tip-off from store staff |
| **Frequency** | Weekly (automated scanning); event-driven (investigation activation); monthly (reporting) |
| **Volume** | ~50–100 flagged cases/month for review; ~5–15 confirmed fraud cases/month |
| **Owner** | Marketing — Loyalty Program Manager |
| **Participants** | LP Analyst, IT Security, Customer Service, Store Manager, Finance — Payment Settlement |

### Background

With 600,000 loyalty members, a points-based system (1 point per PHP 100 spent, PHP 1.00 per point redemption value), and tiered benefits (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum), BuildRight's loyalty program represents a significant financial liability (deferred revenue per PFRS 15 — W487) and an attractive target for fraud. Common loyalty fraud schemes include: (a) **Account takeover**: unauthorized access to member accounts to redeem points; (b) **Points farming**: creating multiple accounts to accumulate promotional bonus points; (c) **Return fraud for points**: purchasing items, earning points, then returning items — points are not always reversed, creating arbitrage; (d) **Employee-assisted points manipulation**: cashiers manually awarding extra points to their own or friends' accounts; (e) **Promotional abuse**: exploiting system loopholes during double-points events or sign-up bonuses; (f) **Fake account creation for signup bonuses**: automated or manual creation of fake accounts to harvest enrollment bonuses. W104 covers loyalty program financial governance (financial liability, tier economics) but does not address fraud detection and prevention. W466 covers general LP but is focused on merchandise theft, not loyalty program fraud.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Automated Fraud Scanning**: Loyalty system runs automated fraud detection rules weekly: (a) **Velocity rules**: accounts earning or redeeming points at abnormal rates (e.g., >10x average member activity in 24 hours); (b) **Pattern rules**: multiple accounts sharing same device, IP address, or phone number; (c) **Return-points mismatch**: accounts with high return rates but retained points; (d) **Geographic anomaly**: points earned at a store the member has never visited, or at multiple distant stores on the same day; (e) **Employee-linked accounts**: accounts linked to employee IDs (W205) showing unusual point accumulation; (f) flagged cases are queued for manual review | System / Loyalty Program Manager | IT Security | Automated |
| 2 | **Case Triage**: Loyalty Program Manager reviews flagged cases: (a) classify case severity: **High** (employee involvement, organized ring, financial impact >PHP 10,000), **Medium** (individual fraud, PHP 1,000–10,000 impact), **Low** (suspicious but possibly legitimate activity); (b) assign investigation priority; (c) for High severity: escalate to LP Analyst (W466) for cross-functional investigation; (d) for Medium/Low: Loyalty Program Manager investigates directly | Loyalty Program Manager | Marketing Director | 4 hours/week |
| 3 | **Investigation**: Loyalty Program Manager investigates flagged cases: (a) review account activity log: all point earn/redeem transactions, linked devices, store visits, purchase history; (b) cross-reference with POS transaction data (W5B) — verify that purchases generating points actually occurred; (c) cross-reference with returns data (W12A) — verify points reversal on returned items; (d) for employee-linked accounts: cross-reference with employee work schedule (W34) — are they at the store when points are earned?; (e) for account takeover: review login logs (device, IP, location, timestamp); (f) document findings in fraud case file | Loyalty Program Manager / LP Analyst | Marketing Director | 1–3 hours/case |
| 4 | **Fraud Confirmation & Action**: For confirmed fraud cases: (a) **Account takeover**: reset account credentials, freeze points balance, notify affected member via customer service (W258), restore stolen points; (b) **Points farming / fake accounts**: deactivate fraudulent accounts, forfeit all points, block device/IP; (c) **Return-points arbitrage**: correct points balance (reverse unearned points), update system to auto-reverse points on all returns; (d) **Employee-assisted fraud**: escalate to LP (W466) and HR for disciplinary action (W72/W43); recover fraudulent points; (e) **Promotional abuse**: close loophole (coordinate with IT per W132), forfeit fraudulently earned points; (f) for all cases: document action taken and update fraud prevention rules | Loyalty Program Manager | Marketing Director / LP Director | 1–2 hours/case |
| 5 | **System Rule Enhancement**: Based on fraud patterns identified, Loyalty Program Manager updates fraud detection rules: (a) add new rules to automated scanning engine (W496.1); (b) adjust velocity thresholds based on legitimate vs. fraudulent activity patterns; (c) coordinate with IT to implement system-level controls: mandatory points reversal on returns, CAPTCHA for online enrollment (W263), device fingerprinting for account access, employee account tagging (W205); (d) submit rule changes through change management (W132) | Loyalty Program Manager / IT | Marketing Director | Event-driven |
| 6 | **Monthly Fraud Report**: Loyalty Program Manager prepares monthly loyalty fraud report: (a) total flagged cases, confirmed fraud cases, and false positive rate; (b) fraud loss quantification (points fraudulently earned/redeemed × PHP 1.00/point); (c) fraud type breakdown (account takeover, points farming, return arbitrage, employee-assisted, promotional abuse); (d) recovery actions taken; (e) rule enhancements implemented; (f) fraud trend analysis (increasing/decreasing by type); (g) update loyalty program financial liability estimate (W104) to reflect fraud adjustments | Loyalty Program Manager | Marketing Director | 4 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Loyalty Engine: fraud detection rules engine, account activity logs, points transaction history
- POS Transaction Database: purchase verification
- Returns/Exchange System (W12A): return-points cross-reference
- Customer Data Platform (W156): account device/IP/login tracking
- Employee Master (W292): employee account identification
- Helpdesk/Customer Service (W258): member communication for account takeover
- Integration with W17 (loyalty operations), W104 (loyalty financial governance), W156 (CDP), W205 (employee purchases), W258 (customer ticketing), W263 (enrollment), W466 (LPAP), W132 (change management for rule updates)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **False positives damaging customer experience**: Overly aggressive fraud detection rules can flag legitimate customer activity (e.g., a contractor member making bulk purchases at multiple stores on the same day), resulting in account freezes that damage the customer relationship and defeat the loyalty program's purpose
- **Points reversal system gap**: If the ERP does not automatically reverse loyalty points on merchandise returns, the return-points arbitrage scheme is an open vulnerability; implementing automatic points reversal requires configuration changes that may not be available in all ERP systems
- **Scale of monitoring**: With 600,000 members and ~42,900 ecommerce orders/month, manual review of all flagged cases is labor-intensive; automated rules can only detect known fraud patterns, not novel schemes
- **Employee-assisted fraud detection difficulty**: Detecting cashiers who manually award extra points requires correlating POS transaction data with loyalty points earned — a cross-system analysis that may not be available in real-time
- **Financial liability impact**: Loyalty fraud inflates the deferred revenue liability on the balance sheet (W487); if fraud is not detected and corrected, the PFRS 15 liability is overstated, impacting financial statement accuracy

- Weekly automated scanning review: ~4 hours/week
- Individual case investigation: ~1–3 hours/case × ~10 confirmed cases/month = ~10–30 hours/month
- Monthly fraud report: ~4 hours/month
- Rule enhancement: ~4 hours/event (estimated ~6 events/year)
- **Total annual**: ~250–450 hours/year for Loyalty Program Manager

- **Loyalty Program Manager**: ~250–450 hours/year on loyalty fraud management. Significant but absorbed within existing loyalty management role.
- **LP Analyst**: ~20–40 hours/year on cross-functional investigations (High severity cases). Absorbed within existing LP duties (W466).
- **IT Security**: ~20–30 hours/year for account takeover investigation and system controls. Absorbed.

### Background

Philippine marketing and advertising is heavily regulated. The DTI monitors sales promotions per RA 7394 (Consumer Act) and RA 7394 requires truth in advertising. DTI sales promotion permits are required per W427. The Advertising Standards Council (ASC) has self-regulatory guidelines. Specific compliance requirements include: (a) DTI Sales Promotion Permit for contests, raffles, buy-one-take-one per W427, (b) truth in pricing — advertised prices must be available and honored per Consumer Act, (c) Senior Citizen/PWD discount compliance in advertising per W170, (d) product claims must be substantiated (especially for tools, chemicals, building materials), (e) social media advertising must include required disclaimers, (f) bank partnership promotions must comply with BSP regulations per W149, (g) data privacy consent for marketing per RA 10173. ## W902. Customer Loyalty Partner Reward Catalog Management & Fulfillment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Loyalty member redeems accumulated points for a partner reward (non-BuildRight merchandise or service — e.g., airline miles, hotel discount, home insurance premium discount, restaurant voucher, partner retailer gift card); or Marketing reviews/updates the partner reward catalog |
| **Frequency** | ~15,000–20,000 partner reward redemptions/month; catalog review quarterly |
| **Volume** | Avg 1–2 reward items per redemption; avg 500–1,500 points per reward |
| **Owner** | Loyalty Program Manager |
| **Participants** | Customer (Loyalty Member), Loyalty Program Manager, Marketing Coordinator, Partner Vendor, Finance (AP), IT (system integration) |

### Background

BuildRight's loyalty program (600,000 members per model company profile) operates on a points-based system: 1 point per PHP 100 spent; redemption value PHP 1.00 per point; four tiers (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum). While W17 manages core loyalty operations, W550 handles points-as-payment at POS, and W570 manages points expiry, the partner reward catalog — where members redeem points for non-BuildRight rewards provided by external partners — is a distinct operational workflow. Partner rewards are a critical loyalty program tool: they increase the perceived value of points (beyond the PHP 1.00/point in-store value), differentiate the program from competitors, and drive partnerships with complementary brands (airlines for OFW families, hotels for business customers, home insurance for new homeowners, appliance brands for renovation customers). This workflow manages the full lifecycle: partner reward catalog curation, digital fulfillment, partner settlement, catalog performance analytics, and quarterly catalog refresh.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Partner Reward Catalog Curation**: (a) Loyalty Program Manager curates partner reward catalog based on: (i) member demographic analysis from CDP (W156) — BuildRight's customers are homeowners, DIYers, and contractors; relevant partners include home insurance, furniture stores, airlines/hotels (for contractor travel), restaurants, fuel stations, appliance brands, garden centers; (ii) partner ROI analysis — cost of reward to BuildRight vs. incremental engagement/revenue driven; (iii) partner willingness to offer exclusive rates to BuildRight members; (iv) seasonal relevance — aircon service vouchers pre-summer, flood insurance pre-rainy season; (b) each partner reward defined in system: (i) reward description and image; (ii) points required for redemption; (iii) BuildRight's cost per reward (wholesale rate negotiated with partner); (iv) reward validity period after issuance (typically 90 days); (v) fulfillment method (digital voucher code, physical card mail-out, partner API direct redemption); (vi) partner settlement terms (monthly, per-redemption, or prepaid block); (c) catalog structured in tiers: Bronze-accessible (100–500 points), Silver-accessible (501–1,500), Gold-accessible (1,501–5,000), Platinum-exclusive (5,001+) | Loyalty Program Manager | Marketing Manager | 20–30 hours/quarter |
| 2 | **Member Redemption**: (a) loyalty member browses partner reward catalog in: (i) mobile app "Rewards" section (W615); (ii) website loyalty page; (iii) quarterly rewards catalog mailer (physical, for Gold/Platinum members); (b) member selects reward and confirms redemption; (c) system validates: (i) sufficient points balance; (ii) reward availability (not out of stock); (iii) member tier eligibility; (d) system deducts points from member balance; (e) system generates reward fulfillment per type: (i) **digital voucher code**: system generates unique code + barcode; delivered via email and app notification; code activated in partner system via API or manual upload; (ii) **physical card/certificate**: system creates fulfillment order to Marketing Coordinator; card/certificate mailed to member's registered address via LBC/2GO; (iii) **direct partner redemption**: system sends member's loyalty ID to partner via API; member presents loyalty ID at partner outlet for instant redemption | Customer / System | — | 3–5 min (digital); 5–7 days (physical mail) |
| 3 | **Partner Fulfillment Verification**: (a) for digital vouchers: system tracks voucher status — Issued, Redeemed (partner confirms usage), Expired (validity period passed); (b) for physical cards: system tracks delivery status via courier integration; (c) monthly: system reconciles issued rewards vs. partner-confirmed redemptions; unredeemed/expired rewards flagged; (d) if digital voucher not received: member contacts call center (W259); agent resends voucher code; (e) if physical card not delivered within 10 days: agent initiates replacement shipment | System / Call Center Agent | Loyalty Program Manager | ~2 hours/month reconciliation |
| 4 | **Partner Settlement**: (a) monthly: Finance processes partner settlement per agreement terms: (i) **per-redemption**: system calculates total rewards redeemed × BuildRight cost per reward; AP issues payment or offset against partner's BuildRight purchases (if partner is also a vendor); (ii) **prepaid block**: BuildRight prepurchases block of rewards at wholesale rate; system deducts from prepaid balance per redemption; replenishment when balance < 20%; (iii) **contra arrangement**: partner provides rewards at no cost in exchange for BuildRight marketing exposure (in-store signage, app placement); no financial settlement; (b) Finance reconciles: (i) rewards issued vs. partner invoices; (ii) unredeemed rewards — liability treatment: unredeemed digital vouchers are not a liability (points already deducted); expired physical cards are breakage; (iii) settlement accuracy — discrepancies > 5% investigated with partner | Finance (AP) / Loyalty Program Manager | Finance Manager | 4–6 hours/month |
| 5 | **Quarterly Catalog Performance Review & Refresh**: (a) quarterly: Loyalty Program Manager reviews catalog performance: (i) redemption rate by reward — top 10 most redeemed rewards, bottom 10 (candidates for removal); (ii) points consumption vs. program liability — are partner rewards accelerating points redemption (reducing BuildRight's loyalty liability) or cannibalizing in-store redemption (W550, which drives incremental sales)?; (iii) partner settlement cost vs. incremental member engagement (purchases by members who redeemed partner rewards vs. those who didn't); (iv) fulfillment success rate — digital delivery success, physical delivery timeliness; (v) member satisfaction — CSAT score on partner rewards from post-redemption survey per W65; (b) catalog refresh actions: (i) remove rewards with < 10 redemptions/quarter; (ii) adjust points requirements based on cost changes; (iii) add new partners based on member demographic trends; (iv) negotiate better rates with high-volume partners; (v) introduce seasonal/limited-time rewards to create urgency; (c) updated catalog published in app and website | Loyalty Program Manager | Marketing Manager | 10–15 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints

- Loyalty points engine (W17) for balance deduction and redemption processing
- Customer CDP (W156) for member demographic analysis and segmentation
- Mobile app (W615) for catalog browsing and redemption
- Ecommerce platform for web-based catalog access
- Partner API integration for digital voucher generation and direct redemption
- Email/SMS notification system (W708) for reward delivery
- Courier integration for physical reward fulfillment tracking
- AP settlement module for partner payment processing
- BI dashboard for catalog performance analytics (redemption rates, cost per reward, member engagement lift)
- Loyalty financial governance module (W104) for liability tracking

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Partner redemption friction**: if partner's system doesn't recognize BuildRight voucher codes (API failure, partner staff unawareness), members have poor experience and blame BuildRight, not the partner; SLA with partners for issue resolution < 48 hours is essential
- **Points liability management**: partner rewards redeem at PHP 1.00/point face value but cost BuildRight less (wholesale rate); however, if too many members shift from in-store redemption (W550, which drives PHP 1.00 in sales) to partner rewards (which costs BuildRight PHP 0.50–0.80 net), program economics shift — must monitor mix quarterly
- **Physical card fulfillment delays**: Philippine postal/courier reliability in provincial areas (Visayas, Mindanao) means physical rewards may take 10–14 days; digital-first catalog strategy mitigates
- **Partner dropping out mid-period**: if a partner ceases operations or withdraws from the program, already-issued rewards become unfulfillable; system must have partner financial health monitoring (similar to W491 for suppliers) and backup fulfillment plan
- **Reward catalog staleness**: if catalog isn't refreshed quarterly, members lose interest in earning points ("nothing worth redeeming"); catalog refresh discipline is critical
- **Digital voucher fraud**: unique voucher codes could be shared, screenshotted, or resold; single-use enforcement via partner API and barcode scanning at partner outlet mitigates

- **Loyalty Program Manager**: ~20–30 hours/quarter on catalog curation and performance review + ~2–4 hours/month on settlement oversight and issue resolution; absorbed by existing role
- **Marketing Coordinator**: ~5–8 hours/month on catalog content management and physical fulfillment; absorbed by existing role
- **Finance (AP)**: ~4–6 hours/month on partner settlement processing; absorbed by existing AP team
- **No incremental headcount**.

- Catalog curation: 20–30 hours/quarter
- Per-member redemption: 3–5 min (digital); 5–7 days (physical)
- Monthly reconciliation: 2 hours
- Monthly partner settlement: 4–6 hours
- Quarterly review and refresh: 10–15 hours
- **Total ongoing**: ~40–60 hours/quarter + 6–8 hours/month
