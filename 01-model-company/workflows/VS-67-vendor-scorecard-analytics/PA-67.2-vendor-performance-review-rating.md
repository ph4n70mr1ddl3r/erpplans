## W2462. Monthly Vendor Performance Report

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly reporting cycle |
| **Frequency** | Monthly; ~800–1,000 vendors |
| **Volume** | ~800–1,000 vendor performance reports/month |
| **Owner** | Procurement Analyst |
| **Participants** | Procurement Analyst, Procurement Mgr, VP Supply Chain |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Generates the monthly report package from the W2458 scorecard output: top-20 performers, bottom-20 performers, biggest movers (±15 points month-over-month), new additions (first-scored vendors from W2460), KPI trend lines per dimension; distributes to Category Managers and Buyers with action-prompt annotations (each bottom-20 / biggest-mover entry carries a "review required by" tag) | Procurement Analyst | Procurement Manager | 4–6 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Analytics/BI (links to VS-28 / W113) — the monthly report is a curated view over the W2458 supplier-performance fact table; the same dashboard Buyers self-serve during the month
- Supplier-performance module (W2458) — source of the composite scores and per-dimension drilldowns
- Distribution/notification (links to VS-63 store-comms pattern, applied HQ-side) — report delivered to each Category Manager's queue with the action-prompt tags

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Report-fatigue risk**: a 1,000-vendor report is noise unless the bottom-20 and biggest-movers are foregrounded; mitigated by the "review required by" tagging that turns the report into a worklist, not a reference document
- **Lagging-signal risk**: a monthly report surfaces a problem 30 days after the transactions that caused it; mitigated by the W2460 new-vendor weekly cadence and the exception-driven W2455 daily monitoring for high-volume vendors

### Automation Opportunity
- Report auto-generated from the W2458 calc output (no manual assembly); action-prompt tags auto-derived from the mover/threshold rules

### Controls
- CTL-48 (rebate-dispute frequency surfaced as a scorecard input — visible in the monthly report); operational: Procurement Manager sign-off on the published report

### Time Estimate
~4–6 hours/month Procurement Analyst (curation + annotation + distribution) = **~48–72 hours/year**.

## W2463. Quarterly Strategic Vendor Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly review cycle |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly; top 50 strategic vendors |
| **Volume** | ~50 vendor reviews/quarter |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Mgr, Procurement Mgr, Vendor, VP Supply Chain |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Category Manager conducts a structured review with the strategic vendor: presents the W2458 scorecard with the W2461 benchmark percentile, discusses strengths and improvement areas, agrees 90-day improvement targets documented in a joint action plan; vendor presents their roadmap (new products, capacity, sustainability initiatives) | Category Manager / Vendor | VP Supply Chain | 1–2 hours/vendor/quarter |
| 2 | Category Manager records the agreed targets in the supplier-performance module so the next quarter's review opens with a scorecard-vs-target view; escalates any target miss to the W2465 PIP workflow if a threshold breach accompanies it | Category Manager | Procurement Manager | 30 min/vendor/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Supplier-performance module (W2458) — the scorecard artifact presented in the review, and where the 90-day targets are recorded
- CRM / supplier-relationship artifact (links to VS-03 / W36 vendor-master, VS-100 legal for joint-development agreements) — the joint action plan and vendor roadmap stored against the vendor record
- Benchmark dataset (W2461) — the market-percentile framing that makes "improvement" concrete

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Soft-target risk**: 90-day targets agreed in the room are vague ("improve delivery") and unmeasurable at the next review; mitigated by the target being recorded as a specific KPI threshold (e.g., "OTD 92%→95% by Q-end") in the system, not just meeting notes
- **Asymmetry risk**: the vendor sees BuildRight's scorecard but BuildRight doesn't see the vendor's view of BuildRight as a customer (payment behavior, forecast accuracy); mitigated by an annual two-way scorecard exchange for the top 20 strategic vendors

### Automation Opportunity
- Scorecard-vs-target view auto-prepared for each review (opens with last quarter's agreed targets and current actuals); joint-action-plan template auto-populated from the discussion

### Controls
- Operational: VP Supply Chain reviews any strategic-vendor target miss before it escalates to W2465 (prevents a single Category Manager from unilaterally PIP-ing a strategic relationship)

### Time Estimate
~1.5 hours/vendor/quarter × 50 strategic vendors + ~30 min/target-recording = **~300–375 hours/quarter** distributed across ~5 Category Managers (≈ 60–75 hours each/quarter, ~5–6 hours/week — core vendor-management duty).

## W2464. Annual Vendor Tier Classification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual classification review |
| **Frequency** | Annual; ~800–1,000 vendors classified |
| **Volume** | ~800–1,000 classifications/year |
| **Owner** | VP Supply Chain |
| **Participants** | VP Supply Chain, Procurement Mgr, Category Mgrs, CFO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | VP Supply Chain classifies vendors into five tiers from the annual scorecard rollup: (a) Strategic/Platinum — top 20 vendors (~45% of COGS), (b) Preferred/Gold — next 50, (c) Approved/Silver — standard, (d) Probation/Bronze — underperforming, (e) Watch/At-Risk — potential for delisting per W2466 | VP Supply Chain | CFO | 5–10 days/year |
| 2 | Tier determines commercial treatment: PO allocation priority, payment terms, co-op marketing budget (links to VS-39 rebates), strategic-collaboration level (joint business planning), vendor-development investment (links to VS-67.3) | VP Supply Chain / CFO | CEO | 1–2 days |

### System Touchpoints
- Supplier-performance module (W2458) — the annual scorecard rollup (12-month composite + per-dimension averages) is the classification input
- Vendor-master (links to VS-29 / W287) — the tier tag is written to the vendor master, where it drives PO-allocation logic, payment-term selection, and co-op-budget eligibility
- Co-op/rebate accrual (links to VS-39 / W27) — tier drives the rebate-tier rate in the rebate agreement master

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Tier-inertia risk**: vendors stay in a tier by precedent rather than current performance (a formerly-strategic vendor now mid-pack keeps Platinum); mitigated by the annual forced re-classification against current scorecard, with any tier-change documented and communicated
- **Concentration risk**: 45% of COGS in the top 20 vendors means a single tier-down or delisting has outsized supply impact; mitigated by the dual-source requirement for Strategic-tier items (links to VS-02 supply planning) flagged at classification

### Automation Opportunity
- Draft tier assignment auto-generated from the annual scorecard rollup + the prior-year tier (VP Supply Chain reviews the diff, not the full list); tier-change communications auto-drafted

### Controls
- CTL-06 (vendor-master integrity — the tier tag must be current, since it drives payment terms and allocation); operational: CFO approval on tier changes affecting Strategic/Platinum vendors (commercial-materiality control)

### Time Estimate
~5–10 days/year VP Supply Chain classification + ~1–2 days CFO/CEO commercial-terms sign-off = **~50–100 hours/year**.

## W2465. Vendor Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor falls below performance threshold |
| **Frequency** | ~20–30 PIPs/year |
| **Volume** | ~20–30/year |
| **Owner** | Procurement Manager |
| **Participants** | Procurement Mgr, Category Mgr, Vendor, VP Supply Chain |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Procurement Manager issues a formal PIP to the underperforming vendor: cites the specific KPIs below threshold (from W2458), the required improvement levels, the 90-day timeline, and the consequences if not met (volume reduction or escalation to W2466 delisting); documents in the vendor record | Procurement Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2–3 hours per PIP |
| 2 | Monthly check-ins during the PIP window: vendor reports corrective actions taken, Procurement validates improvement against the W2458 monthly scorecard; if targets met → graduate from PIP with the improved baseline locked; if not → escalate to the W2466 delisting review | Procurement Manager / Vendor | VP Supply Chain | 1 hour/month per PIP |

### System Touchpoints
- Supplier-performance module (W2458) — the PIP is tracked as a workflow state on the vendor record; the monthly check-in reads the live scorecard against the PIP targets
- Vendor-master (links to VS-29 / W287) — "PIP active" flag blocks new Strategic-tier benefits until graduation
- Document control (links to VS-88) — the formal PIP letter and the monthly check-in notes are versioned artifacts (audit trail)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **PIP-as-theatre risk**: the plan is issued but the consequence is never enforced, training vendors that underperformance has no cost; mitigated by the documented escalation path (PIP miss → W2466) and the quarterly W2459 audit checking PIP-outcome follow-through
- **Moving-goalpost risk**: the PIP targets are redefined mid-window to let the vendor pass; mitigated by the targets being locked in the system at PIP issuance and change-controlled (VP Supply Chain approval to modify)

### Automation Opportunity
- PIP auto-triggered when a vendor breaches threshold for N consecutive months (not waiting for manual detection); graduation/escalation auto-suggested from the scorecard-vs-target state at day 90

### Controls
- CTL-48 (rebate-dispute pattern is one PIP trigger — commercial and performance signals converge here); operational: PIP-outcome follow-through sampled in the W2459 quarterly audit

### Time Estimate
~2.5 hours/PIP issuance + ~3 hours/PIP monitoring (1 hour × 3 monthly check-ins) × ~25 PIPs/year = **~140–160 hours/year** Procurement Manager.

## W2466. Vendor Delisting Decision & Execution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor consistently underperforms despite PIP |
| **Frequency** | ~5–10 delistings/year |
| **Volume** | ~5–10/year |
| **Owner** | VP Supply Chain |
| **Participants** | VP Supply Chain, Category Mgr, Procurement, Finance, Legal |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | VP Supply Chain initiates the delisting: documents the performance history, the W2465 PIP results, the quantified business impact (stockouts, quality costs, expedite fees); confirms an alternative vendor is qualified and can absorb the volume (links to VS-02 supply planning); presents the recommendation to the Category Manager and CFO | VP Supply Chain | CFO | 3–5 days per delisting |
| 2 | Category Manager approves the alternative sourcing; Procurement transitions the volume over a defined ramp-down; Finance settles final payments and any open rebates/consignment (links to VS-39, VS-45); Legal reviews contractual obligations and termination notice; vendor-portal access revoked (links to VS-28/W593) and the vendor-master set to "inactive — do not pay" (CTL-06) | Category Mgr / Procurement / Finance / Legal | VP Supply Chain | 30–60 days |

### System Touchpoints
- Vendor-master (links to VS-29 / W287, CTL-06) — the "delisted" status blocks new POs and triggers the AP-do-not-pay check; stale-delisted-vendor cleanup links to CTL-51
- Supplier portal (links to VS-28 / W593) — access revoked; open ASN/invoice workflow closed out
- Contract repository (links to VS-100 / W230 legal contract review) — termination-notice period and any early-termination penalty verified before the ramp-down starts

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Supply-gap risk**: delisting before the alternative vendor can absorb volume causes stockouts on the affected items; mitigated by the prerequisite "alternative qualified + volume-confirmed" gate in step 1, enforced via the dual-source flag from W2464
- **Retaliation/transition-disruption risk**: a delisted vendor delays final shipments, withholds repair parts, or disputes the final settlement; mitigated by the contractual termination-notice check in step 2 and Finance escrowing the final payment pending clean handover

### Automation Opportunity
- Delisting-checklist workflow (legal notice → portal revocation → master status → AP block) sequenced with sign-offs; the alternative-vendor capacity check drawn from VS-02 supply planning

### Controls
- CTL-06 (vendor-master status — the "do not pay" block is the key fraud-prevention control on a delisted vendor); CTL-42 (bank-account-change guard — prevents a delisted vendor's last-payment reroute); operational: CFO + Legal sign-off on every delisting

### Time Estimate
~4 days/delisting for step 1 (VP Supply Chain + Category Manager) + ~45-day ramp-down in step 2 (mostly monitoring, ~10–15 hours of active work spread across Finance/Procurement/Legal) × ~7 delistings/year = **~200–280 hours/year** total across functions.

## W2467. Vendor Recognition & Award Program

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual vendor awards |
| **Frequency** | Annual; top performers recognized |
| **Volume** | 10–15 vendors recognized/year |
| **Owner** | VP Supply Chain |
| **Participants** | VP Supply Chain, Procurement Mgr, Category Mgrs, CEO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | VP Supply Chain identifies award recipients from the annual W2464 tier classification and W2458 scorecard: Vendor of the Year (highest composite), Quality Excellence (best quality dimension), Delivery Reliability (best OTD/fill), Innovation Partner (best innovation dimension / new-SKU contribution), Sustainability Champion (best ESG/sustainable-sourcing evidence per VS-25/VS-131); coordinates a recognition event (annual supplier summit or virtual) | VP Supply Chain | CEO | 3–5 days/year |
| 2 | Awards carry commercial weight: increased PO allocation priority, preferred payment terms, co-marketing opportunities, and a strategic-partnership discussion (joint business planning); reinforces the performance culture and signals to the vendor base what excellence looks like | VP Supply Chain / Procurement Mgr | Category Mgr | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints
- Supplier-performance module (W2458) + annual classification (W2464) — the objective basis for award selection (awards are not subjective; they trace to the scorecard)
- Vendor-master / co-marketing budget (links to VS-39 / VS-14 marketing) — award-tier benefits recorded against the vendor record and the marketing co-op budget
- Event logistics (links to VS-139 event-marketing pattern, applied supplier-side) — summit coordination

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Perception-of-favoritism risk**: an award without a visible scorecard basis looks like cronyism to non-recipients; mitigated by awards tracing to the W2458 objective scorecard and the selection criteria published to the vendor base
- **Award-without-reward risk**: a plaque with no commercial benefit becomes meaningless; mitigated by the tangible benefits in step 2 (allocation, terms, co-marketing) that make the award materially valuable

### Automation Opportunity
- Award-eligibility auto-shortlisted from the annual scorecard (VP Supply Chain curates the shortlist, doesn't assemble it); recipient communications and benefit-activation auto-drafted

### Controls
- Operational: CEO sign-off on Vendor of the Year (reputational-materiality control); award selection criteria documented and retained (audit trail for any "why not us" vendor query)

### Time Estimate
~4 days/year VP Supply Chain selection + event + 1 day benefit-activation = **~40 hours/year**, plus event logistics absorbed into VS-139.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly system review |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly; continuous improvement |
| **Volume** | Scorecard system serving ~800–1,000 vendors |
| **Owner** | IT Business Analyst |
| **Participants** | IT Business Analyst, Procurement Mgr, VP Supply Chain |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | IT reviews the scorecard system end-to-end: reporting capabilities, data-visualization usability, integration completeness (are all W2455 source feeds flowing?), user feedback from Buyers/Category Managers; implements prioritized enhancements — new KPIs (e.g., sustainability dimension), better dashboards (mobile access for Buyers on the floor), faster calc-cycle latency | IT Business Analyst | CIO | 5–10 days/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Supplier-performance module (W2458) and the W2455 data pipeline — the systems under enhancement
- Analytics/BI platform (links to VS-28 / W113) — the dashboard layer; enhancements follow the BI governance standard
- IT change management (links to VS-27 / W132 IT change management) — enhancements released through the standard change pipeline, not ad-hoc

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Enhancement-without-adoption risk**: IT ships a new dashboard nobody uses because Buyers weren't consulted on the design; mitigated by the user-feedback input in step 1 and a pilot with 2–3 Category Managers before full release
- **Calc-drift risk**: an enhancement to the calc engine silently changes historical scores (a retroactive re-tier); mitigated by versioned calc logic and a "recompute historical?" decision gate requiring VP Supply Chain approval

### Automation Opportunity
- Usage telemetry on the dashboards (which views, which users, how often) to prioritize enhancements by actual adoption rather than anecdote

### Controls
- CTL-51 (master-data/stale-data checks — applies to the scorecard fact table the enhancements touch); operational: enhancements released via W132 change management with Procurement Manager UAT sign-off

### Time Estimate
~5–10 days/quarter IT Business Analyst = **~80–160 hours/year**, absorbed into the IT procurement-systems roadmap.

## W2469. Cross-Functional Vendor Feedback Integration

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly cross-functional input |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly; 5 functions (Procurement, Quality, Finance, Store Ops, Merchandising) |
| **Volume** | 5 functional inputs/quarter |
| **Owner** | Procurement Manager |
| **Participants** | Procurement Mgr, Quality Mgr, Finance Mgr, Store Ops Dir, Category Mgrs |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Procurement collects structured cross-functional feedback per strategic vendor: Quality (inspection trends from W110/W681), Finance (invoice-dispute frequency and payment-terms compliance from W7/W100), Store Ops (delivery-quality / receiving experience from W3/W681), Merchandising (product quality and trend-responsiveness from VS-01/VS-14); integrates the weighted signals into the qualitative dimension of the W2458 composite | Procurement Manager | VP Supply Chain | 4–6 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Source modules per function: Quality (VS-31), Finance/AP (VS-15/W7), Store Ops receiving (VS-04/W3), Merchandising (VS-01) — each provides a structured extract; the integration is Procurement's rollup
- Supplier-performance module (W2458) — the destination; cross-functional signals feed the qualitative dimension alongside the W2457 internal survey
- Collaboration tooling (cross-functional workspace or the procurement module's feedback sub-form) — structured capture rather than email

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Anecdote-over-data risk**: Store Ops submits "deliveries are always late lately" without the underlying receipt data; mitigated by the structured extract requirement (each function submits its module's data, not just impressions)
- **Dominant-function risk**: Finance's invoice-dispute view dominates because it's the most quantified, drowning out Quality's softer signals; mitigated by the pre-agreed dimension weights and Procurement Manager judgment in the integration

### Automation Opportunity
- Each function's extract auto-pulled from its source module (no manual submission); the integration rollup auto-computed with the Procurement Manager reviewing exceptions only

### Controls
- CTL-24 (receiving-fraud detection — Store Ops feedback that surfaces anomalous receiving patterns feeds back into CTL-24's monitoring); operational: cross-functional inputs retained as audit trail for any subsequent W2465 PIP or W2466 delisting decision

### Time Estimate
~5 hours/quarter Procurement Manager integration + ~1 hour/quarter × 4 other functions (extract prep) = **~25 hours/quarter ≈ ~100 hours/year** total across functions.
