# VS-170: Inventory Pledge, Asset-Based Lending & Trust-Receipt (Warehouse-Receipt) Financing

> **Finance** · [Value Stream Index](../value-stream-index.md)

---

## Overview

Inventory-pledge, asset-based lending (ABL) and trust-receipt (warehouse-receipt) financing
workflows for BuildRight Depot Corp. — owning the **collateral-based working-capital-financing
operating discipline** beneath the group's revolving credit facilities: the borrowing-base
certificate and eligibility analysis (eligible inventory + eligible AR × advance rates −
reserves), the inventory pledge and field-exam cycle, the **trust-receipt financing lifecycle
(PD 115, the Trust Receipts Law — the dominant Philippine import-financing instrument)**, and
warehouse-receipt / field-warehousing collateral operations.

This matters at BuildRight's scale and capital structure. The group carries ~PHP 37B of
merchandise inventory at cost (its single largest balance-sheet asset), ~40% of COGS imported
(~PHP 17–18B/yr; ~400–600 import TEUs/month; ~PHP 1.4B/month import value), ~5,200 trade
accounts + ~200 corporate accounts + a COD-heavy ecommerce AR base, and operates across 5 legal
entities. Inventory + AR are the natural collateral for the revolving facilities and import
lines that fund the cash-conversion cycle (45–90-day import lead times mean months of cash tied
up in ocean/DC/pipeline inventory). The *collateral operations* that sustain those facilities —
the borrowing-base certificate, field examinations, eligible-inventory/AR analysis, advance-rate
application, trust-receipt release-and-remittance against import documents, warehouse-receipt
collateral, and collateral release/substitution — directly govern availability (how much cash
BuildRight can draw), cost (the margin/rate tiers tied to utilization and excess-availability),
covenant compliance, and the risk of an over-advance, a field-exam true-down, a frozen
borrowing base, or a PD 115 trust-receipt violation (a criminal matter under Philippine law).
Mis-management distorts liquidity, breaches covenants, triggers a bank default, or creates
personal/criminal officer liability (PD 115 Sec. 13–14 for trust-receipt misuse).

Today this discipline is **unowned as a unified program**. The defining terms — "borrowing
base", "asset-based lending", "trust receipt", "trust-receipt financing", "field warehouse",
"warehouse receipt" (as collateral), and "notional pool" — each appear in **zero** PA files.
Adjacent coverage exists only as scattered single workflows: **W319** (debt facility & covenant
compliance management, in VS-18.1) manages the *facility contract and covenants* at the treasury
level but not the *collateral operations* beneath it; **W232** (LC and bank guarantees, VS-15.1)
issues import LCs; **W249** (demurrage & detention charge settlement, VS-02.2) settles import
charges; **W239** (customs duty recon, VS-15.1) settles duties; and "collateral" appears across
22 PA files (mostly surety/bond collateral in VS-116, not inventory/AR pledge) and "encumbrance"
across 10. No value stream owns the **inventory-pledge / ABL borrowing-base / trust-receipt /
warehouse-receipt collateral-operations discipline**. It is **not** covered by **VS-18 (Treasury
& Cash)**, which owns cash, bank accounts, FX, forecasting, and the facility at the contract
level (W319) — this owns the *collateral mechanics* that determine availability under it. It is
**not** covered by **VS-105 (Supply Chain Finance & Working Capital)**, which is *supplier-side*
(reverse factoring / dynamic discounting / DPO) — this is *BuildRight's own* borrowing against
its own inventory/AR. It is **not** covered by **VS-16 (Order-to-Cash)**, which collects AR — this
pledges AR as collateral and reconciles pledged vs collected. It is **not** covered by **VS-87 /
VS-02.2 (Customs / Import)**, which clears goods — this finances them post-clearance under trust
receipt. And it is **not** covered by **VS-17 (Record-to-Report)**, which accounts for debt — this
owns the *collateral and borrowing-base operations* feeding debt accounting.

This value stream owns the end-to-end program: facility structuring & the borrowing-base model;
borrowing-base certificate preparation & submission; eligible-inventory & eligible-AR analysis
with advance rates and reserves; the inventory pledge and field-examination cycle; **trust-receipt
financing lifecycle (PD 115)** — import-document release against trust receipt, goods held in
trust, sell-through, proceeds segregation and remittance, and trust-receipt release; warehouse-
receipt and field-warehousing collateral; collateral release, substitution, and true-down;
borrowing-base reconciliation against inventory/AR ledgers; PFRS/encumbrance disclosure and
covenant monitoring (links VS-18.1 W319); integration with import LC settlement (links VS-15.1
W232), customs duty (VS-15.1 W239), inventory ownership across DCs/stores (links VS-05/VS-29),
and AR (links VS-16); multi-entity facility governance (links VS-72/VS-18); fraud/over-advance
controls; field-exam/bank-audit response; and financing-cost analytics. It integrates tightly
with VS-18 (treasury/facility), VS-105 (supplier-side SCF), VS-16 (AR), VS-15 (P2P/import
settlement), VS-02.2/VS-87 (import/customs), VS-05/VS-29 (inventory/master data), VS-17 (R2R),
VS-72 (intercompany), VS-100 (legal — PD 115 trust-receipt law), VS-21 (audit), and VS-86
(financial-crime — proceeds diversion).
