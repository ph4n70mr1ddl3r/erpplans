## W1878. Trade Professional Account Application & Onboarding

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Contractor or trade professional applies for trade account at store or online |
| **Frequency** | ~500–800 new trade account applications/year (net of closures) |
| **Volume** | ~5,200 active trade accounts; ~5–8% annual growth |
| **Owner** | Account Manager |
| **Participants** | Account Manager, Credit Analyst, Trade Professional, Store Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trade professional submits application: (a) full name, business name (if applicable), TIN, business address, contact information, trade type (electrical, plumbing, general construction, interior design, architecture, HVAC, other), (b) supporting documents: DTI/SEC registration (if business), PCAB license (Philippine Contractors Accreditation Board — for licensed contractors), PRC license (for architects and engineers), valid government ID, proof of business address (utility bill or lease contract), (c) applicant self-declares estimated annual purchase volume | Trade Professional | — | 15–30 min |
| 2 | Account Manager reviews application: (a) verify documents are complete and authentic, (b) check for duplicate accounts (same TIN or business name already in system), (c) classify trade type and assign segmentation: (i) solo professional (electrician, plumber): expected PHP 100K–500K/year, (ii) small contractor (1–5 employees): PHP 500K–3M/year, (iii) medium contractor (6–50 employees): PHP 3M–20M/year, (iv) large contractor / developer: PHP 20M+/year, (v) architect / designer: PHP 200K–2M/year (specification influence but lower direct purchase volume) | Account Manager | Store Manager | 15–30 min |
| 3 | Credit Analyst processes credit application per W24 (credit application & scoring): (a) credit check: verify no adverse BIR, bank, or supplier references, (b) set initial credit limit per segmentation: solo PHP 50K, small PHP 200K, medium PHP 1M, large PHP 5M+ (subject to credit scoring), (c) set payment terms: Net 30 for solo/small; Net 30–60 for medium; Net 60–90 for large, (d) Credit Manager approves per tiered authority | Credit Analyst | Credit Manager | Per W24 |
| 4 | Account Manager completes onboarding: (a) create trade account in CRM/ERP with full profile and segmentation, (b) assign dedicated Account Manager based on geographic coverage and account size, (c) issue trade account card (physical + digital) with trade pricing access, (d) provide welcome kit: (i) trade pricing guide (5–15% discount structure by category), (ii) credit terms summary, (iii) dedicated Account Manager contact information, (iv) trade program benefits overview (W1549 contractor annual tier review), (v) buildright.com.ph trade portal login, (e) schedule introductory meeting at account's preferred store for store team introduction | Account Manager | VP Store Operations | 1–2 hours |
| 5 | Store Manager and Department Supervisors introduced to new trade account: (a) recognize the contractor at their primary store, (b) brief Sales Associates on contractor's trade type and typical purchase needs, (c) ensure trade pricing is active in POS for account card scan | Store Manager | Account Manager | 15–30 min |

### System Touchpoints
- Trade account application module (online and in-store) (W1878.1)
- CRM trade account creation with segmentation and Account Manager assignment (W1878.4a–b)
- Trade account card issuance (physical + digital) linked to POS pricing (W1878.4c)
- Credit application integration with W24 (credit scoring) (W1878.3)
- Integration with W24 (credit application), W460 (corporate & trade account onboarding), W13.3 (customer data & CRM)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Application abandonment**: Lengthy application process with document requirements deters smaller trade professionals; offer instant provisional trade account (with limited credit) pending full documentation to capture more applicants
- **Document fraud**: PCAB licenses and PRC licenses may be falsified; verify through PCAB/PRC online verification portals during application review


### Automation Opportunity
- auto-document ((b) supporting documents: dti/sec registration (if)
- auto-review (account manager reviews application: (a) verify)
- auto-validate ((a) credit check: verify no adverse)

### Controls
operational: Account Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **Account Manager**: Onboarding adds ~1–2 hours × 500–800/year = ~500–1,600 hours/year; with ~5 Account Managers, this is ~100–320 hours/AM/year — significant but absorbed as core function

### Time Estimate
- Application submission: 15–30 min
- Application review: 15–30 min
- Credit processing: per W24
- Onboarding completion: 1–2 hours
- Total per account: ~2–3 hours active time; 1–2 weeks elapsed
- Aggregate: ~1,000–2,400 hours/year across all Account Managers

## W1879. Trade Account Annual Recertification & Tier Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual recertification cycle (anniversary of account creation) |
| **Frequency** | ~5,200 recertifications/year (~100/week) |
| **Volume** | All active trade accounts |
| **Owner** | Account Manager |
| **Participants** | Account Manager, Credit Analyst, Trade Professional |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System triggers annual recertification 30 days before anniversary: (a) Account Manager reviews account activity: (i) trailing 12-month purchase volume, (ii) payment history: on-time payment rate, aging incidents, bounced checks, (iii) credit utilization: current balance vs. credit limit, (iv) purchase frequency: how often does the account purchase?, (v) return rate and dispute history | System / Account Manager | VP Store Operations | 15–30 min/account |
| 2 | Account Manager determines tier classification: (a) review segmentation per W1878.2c: has the contractor grown into a higher tier? (b) adjust credit limit per W1549 (contractor annual spend tier review): increase if utilization > 80% and payment history clean; decrease if utilization < 20% or payment issues, (c) review trade pricing eligibility: higher-volume accounts may qualify for deeper trade discounts per W1549 | Account Manager | VP Store Operations | 15 min/account |
| 3 | Credit Analyst reviews creditworthiness: (a) for accounts with payment issues: reassess credit limit and terms — may reduce limit, tighten terms to Net 30, or convert to COD, (b) for accounts requesting credit limit increase: process per W24 credit scoring, (c) for dormant accounts: flag for W1882 (dormancy reactivation) or W1884 (offboarding) | Credit Analyst | Credit Manager | 10–15 min/account |
| 4 | Account Manager contacts trade professional: (a) confirm current contact and business information is accurate, (b) communicate any changes to credit limit, terms, or pricing tier, (c) discuss upcoming project pipeline and anticipated purchase needs, (d) confirm Account Manager assignment (may change if contractor's primary store has changed), (e) update CRM record with recertification outcome | Account Manager | — | 15–30 min/account |

### System Touchpoints
- Automated recertification triggers (W1879.1)
- Account activity analytics dashboard: volume, payment, utilization, frequency (W1879.1a)
- Tier classification and pricing adjustment per W1549 (W1879.2)
- CRM update with recertification outcome (W1879.4e)
- Integration with W24 (credit), W1549 (contractor tier review), W8 (AR processing)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Recertification backlog**: 5,200 accounts/year = ~100/week; if Account Managers fall behind, accounts operate on stale credit and pricing terms


### Automation Opportunity
- auto-review (account manager reviews account activity: (i))
- auto-count (account manager determines tier)
- auto-flag (dormant accounts: flag for w1882 (dormancy)

### Controls
operational: Account Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by Account Managers and Credit Analysts as core annual responsibility

### Time Estimate
- Per account: ~45–75 min active time
- Aggregate: ~5,200 × 60 min = ~5,200 hours/year (~1,040 hours per Account Manager if 5 AMs)

## W1880. Trade Account Manager Assignment & Coverage Planning

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual territory planning; Account Manager turnover; account growth requiring reassignment |
| **Frequency** | Annual territory planning; ad-hoc reassignments |
| **Volume** | ~5,200 trade accounts ÷ ~5 Account Managers = ~1,040 accounts/AM |
| **Owner** | VP Store Operations |
| **Participants** | VP Store Operations, Account Managers, Regional Store Managers |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Annual: VP Store Operations reviews Account Manager coverage: (a) current account distribution by AM: number of accounts, total managed revenue, geographic coverage, (b) identify imbalances: AMs with > 1,200 accounts or > PHP 5B managed revenue are overloaded; AMs with < 800 accounts may have capacity for more, (c) consider geographic alignment: AMs should cover accounts clustered by region to minimize travel time and maximize store-level coordination, (d) consider specialization: should certain AMs specialize in large contractor accounts (higher touch, fewer accounts) vs. small trade accounts (lower touch, more accounts)? | VP Store Operations | COO | 1–2 days |
| 2 | VP Store Operations reassigns accounts per coverage plan: (a) reassign accounts between AMs to balance workload and revenue coverage, (b) ensure each AM has a mix of account sizes (not all small or all large), (c) update CRM with new AM assignments, (d) notify affected trade professionals of their new AM with introduction communication | VP Store Operations | COO | 1 day |
| 3 | AM develops annual coverage plan for assigned accounts: (a) classify accounts by engagement frequency: (i) key accounts (top 50 by revenue): monthly in-person visit + weekly call/email, (ii) growth accounts (next 200): quarterly in-person visit + monthly call, (iii) maintained accounts (remaining): semi-annual visit + quarterly call/email, (b) plan store visit schedule to coincide with trade account visits, (c) identify upsell/cross-sell opportunities per account | Account Manager | VP Store Operations | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints
- AM coverage analytics dashboard: accounts, revenue, geography per AM (W1880.1)
- CRM account assignment management (W1880.2c)
- Visit scheduling and activity logging in CRM (W1880.3)
- Integration with W13.3 (customer data & CRM)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **AM burnout**: 1,040 accounts per AM with varying engagement frequencies is unsustainable if all accounts are treated equally; tiered engagement model (step 3) is essential


### Automation Opportunity
- auto-review (store operations reviews account manager coverage:)
- auto-count (operations reassigns accounts per coverage plan:)
- auto-schedule (store visit schedule to coincide with)

### Controls
operational: VP Store Operations review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **Account Managers**: If 5 AMs at ~1,040 accounts each proves insufficient (based on revenue coverage and engagement quality), consider adding 1–2 AMs; the 30% B2B revenue share (PHP 18.7B) justifies adequate AM staffing

### Time Estimate
- Annual coverage planning: 1–2 days
- Account reassignment: 1 day
- Individual coverage plans: 1 week per AM
- Total: ~2 weeks/year

## W1881. Trade Account Quarterly Business Review & Relationship Health Assessment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly review cycle |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly for key accounts (top 50); semi-annual for growth accounts |
| **Volume** | ~50 key account QBRs/quarter; ~100 growth account reviews semi-annually |
| **Owner** | Account Manager |
| **Participants** | Account Manager, Trade Professional, VP Store Operations, Category Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Account Manager prepares QBR package for each key account: (a) trailing 12-month purchase summary: total spend, spend by category, spend vs. prior year, (b) pricing analysis: is the account receiving competitive pricing? Compare to market and peer accounts, (c) product penetration: what % of BuildRight categories does the account purchase from? Identify categories where account buys from competitors, (d) service metrics: delivery performance, order accuracy, fill rate, complaint resolution time, (e) account health score: composite of spend trend, payment behavior, engagement frequency, satisfaction indicators | Account Manager | VP Store Operations | 1–2 hours/account |
| 2 | Account Manager conducts QBR meeting with trade professional: (a) present purchase analysis and trends, (b) discuss upcoming projects and anticipated material needs, (c) identify opportunities: (i) categories where BuildRight can capture more of the contractor's spend, (ii) new products or services relevant to contractor's business, (iii) project pipeline that BuildRight can support with quantity pricing and dedicated fulfillment, (d) address any service issues or complaints from the contractor, (e) discuss credit and pricing adjustments if warranted | Account Manager | — | 1–2 hours |
| 3 | Post-QBR: Account Manager updates CRM with: (a) meeting notes and action items, (b) updated project pipeline information, (c) any pricing or credit change requests (route to appropriate approver), (d) updated account health score and risk assessment, (e) follow-up actions with due dates | Account Manager | — | 30 min |

### System Touchpoints
- QBR analytics package generation from sales and service data (W1881.1)
- CRM meeting logging with action items and follow-up tracking (W1881.3)
- Account health score model with automated calculation (W1881.1e)
- Integration with W67 (store performance), W108 (collections), W1549 (contractor tier review)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **QBR preparation time**: 50 QBRs × 2 hours preparation = 100 hours/quarter per AM; without automated analytics package generation, AMs spend more time preparing than engaging


### Automation Opportunity
- auto-count (account manager prepares qbr)
- auto-identify (needs, (c) identify opportunities: (i) categories)
- auto-route (change requests (route to appropriate approver),)

### Controls
operational: Account Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — QBRs are a core Account Manager responsibility

### Time Estimate
- Preparation: 1–2 hours/account
- Meeting: 1–2 hours/account
- Follow-up: 30 min/account
- Total: ~150–300 hours/quarter across all AMs for key accounts

## W1882. Trade Account Dormancy Detection & Reactivation Campaign

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | System detects no purchase activity for 90+ days |
| **Frequency** | Monthly dormancy scan |
| **Volume** | ~200–400 dormant accounts identified/year |
| **Owner** | Account Manager |
| **Participants** | Account Manager, Marketing Team, Trade Professional |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monthly: system generates dormancy report: (a) accounts with no purchases in 90 days: "at-risk", (b) accounts with no purchases in 180 days: "dormant", (c) accounts with no purchases in 365 days: "churned", (d) for each account: last purchase date, trailing 12-month spend, assigned AM, store of last purchase | System | Account Manager | Automated |
| 2 | AM investigates dormancy reason: (a) call trade professional: is the business still active? Did they switch to a competitor? Was there a service issue? (b) common reasons: (i) project completed — between projects, (ii) competitor offered better pricing, (iii) service complaint unresolved, (iv) business slowdown or closure, (v) moved to area not served by BuildRight | Account Manager | — | 15–30 min/account |
| 3 | Reactivation action based on reason: (a) between projects: offer project pipeline support, credit limit pre-approval for upcoming project, (b) competitor pricing: review pricing competitiveness per W1881.1b; if pricing is genuinely uncompetitive, request Category Manager approval for matched or preferential pricing, (c) service issue: escalate unresolved complaint per W13.1; offer service recovery gesture (discount, priority delivery), (d) business slowdown: offer flexible terms, smaller minimum orders, (e) relocation: update primary store assignment; offer online ordering with delivery | Account Manager / Marketing Team | VP Store Operations | 30–60 min/account |
| 4 | Quarterly: Marketing Team analyzes dormancy patterns: (a) dormancy rate trend: improving or worsening?, (b) dormancy by segment: which contractor types are most likely to go dormant?, (c) dormancy by store: are certain stores losing trade accounts at higher rates?, (d) competitive intelligence: which competitors are winning dormant accounts?, (e) reactivation success rate: % of dormant accounts successfully reactivated | Marketing Team | VP Store Operations | 1 day/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Automated dormancy detection with configurable thresholds (W1882.1)
- CRM activity tracking for reactivation outreach (W1882.2)
- Dormancy analytics with segmentation and trend analysis (W1882.4)
- Integration with W13.1 (customer complaints), W1883 (churn prediction), W1549 (tier review)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Reactivation cost**: Offering pricing concessions to reactivate dormant accounts may erode margin and train accounts to go dormant periodically to negotiate better pricing


### Automation Opportunity
- auto-count (report: (a) accounts with no purchases)
- auto-flag for investigation (am investigates dormancy reason: (a))
- auto-review (competitor pricing: review pricing competitiveness per)

### Controls
operational: Account Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by Account Managers as part of ongoing account management

### Time Estimate
- Monthly dormancy scan: automated
- Investigation and reactivation: ~30–60 min × 200–400 accounts/year = ~100–400 hours/year
- Quarterly analysis: 1 day/quarter
- Total: ~200–500 hours/year

## W1883. Trade Account Churn Risk Prediction & Retention Intervention

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly churn risk model scoring |
| **Frequency** | Monthly model refresh; weekly review of high-risk accounts |
| **Volume** | ~5,200 active trade accounts scored monthly |
| **Owner** | FP&A / Account Manager |
| **Participants** | FP&A, Account Manager, VP Store Operations, Category Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monthly: FP&A runs churn risk prediction model: (a) input features: (i) purchase frequency trend (declining?), (ii) spend trend (decreasing?), (iii) category breadth (narrowing?), (iv) payment behavior changes (slowing payments?), (v) return rate (increasing?), (vi) complaint frequency, (vii) engagement recency (last AM contact date), (viii) competitor activity in account's trade area, (b) model outputs: churn risk score per account (high/medium/low), top contributing factors, predicted next-90-day spend | FP&A | VP Store Operations | 1 day/month |
| 2 | Account Manager reviews high-risk accounts weekly: (a) accounts flagged "high churn risk": AM must contact within 5 business days, (b) AM investigates risk factors and develops retention plan: (i) if pricing-related: request Category Manager review for competitive pricing match, (ii) if service-related: escalate service issue with operations team, (iii) if relationship-related: schedule face-to-face meeting to rebuild rapport, (iv) if project-related: offer project support services (dedicated fulfillment, job-site delivery) | Account Manager | VP Store Operations | 30–60 min/account |
| 3 | Monthly: VP Store Operations reviews retention metrics: (a) trade account churn rate (target: < 10% annual), (b) retention intervention success rate, (c) revenue at risk: total spend of high-risk accounts, (d) model accuracy: prediction vs. actual churn (calibrate model quarterly) | VP Store Operations | COO | 1 hour/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Churn risk prediction model with automated monthly scoring (W1883.1)
- CRM risk flag integration with AM task assignment (W1883.2)
- Retention metrics dashboard (W1883.3)
- Integration with W1881 (QBR), W1882 (dormancy), W1549 (tier review)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Model accuracy**: Early-stage churn models may have high false positive rates, wasting AM time on accounts not actually at risk; model must be calibrated with actual churn data over time


### Automation Opportunity
- auto-count (activity in account's trade area, (b))
- auto-review (account manager reviews high-risk accounts weekly:)
- auto-predict (model accuracy: prediction vs. actual churn)

### Controls
operational: FP&A / Account Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **FP&A**: Churn model adds ~1 day/month — absorbed
- No incremental headcount

### Time Estimate
- Model execution: 1 day/month
- High-risk account review: ~4–8 hours/week across all AMs
- Monthly review: 1 hour
- Total: ~80–120 hours/month

## W1884. Trade Account Offboarding & Account Closure

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Trade professional requests closure; account dormant > 365 days; account terminated for payment default |
| **Frequency** | ~200–400 account closures/year |
| **Volume** | Per account |
| **Owner** | Account Manager |
| **Participants** | Account Manager, Credit Analyst, Finance, Trade Professional |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Offboarding trigger: (a) voluntary: trade professional requests account closure (business closure, relocation, switching to competitor), (b) involuntary — dormancy: account dormant > 365 days per W1882, (c) involuntary — payment default: account written off per W81 (bad debt) or consistently late payments despite interventions | Account Manager / Credit Analyst | VP Store Operations | 15 min |
| 2 | Finance checks account financial status: (a) outstanding AR balance: must be PHP 0 before closure (or converted to COD for remaining balance), (b) outstanding PDCs: return or destroy per W424, (c) security deposit refund (if applicable), (d) unresolved disputes: document and transfer to general AR for collection | Finance | Controller | 15–30 min |
| 3 | Account Manager closes account in CRM: (a) set account status to "Closed" with reason code, (b) document closure reason and any exit interview insights, (c) retain account history for 10 years per BIR retention requirement, (d) remove trade pricing access, (e) if voluntary closure: ask for exit feedback and permission to re-contact if contractor returns to the area | Account Manager | — | 15–30 min |
| 4 | Quarterly: VP Store Operations reviews closure patterns: (a) closure rate by reason, (b) closure rate by segment and region, (c) competitive win/loss analysis from exit interviews, (d) systemic issues identified from closure reasons | VP Store Operations | COO | 1 day/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Account closure workflow with financial clearance check (W1884.2)
- CRM account status management with reason code tracking (W1884.3)
- Closure analytics dashboard (W1884.4)
- Integration with W81 (bad debt), W24 (credit), W1882 (dormancy), W108 (collections)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **AR balance on closed accounts**: Trade accounts with outstanding AR cannot be fully closed; Finance must continue collection activity even after account is operationally closed


### Automation Opportunity
- auto-count (professional requests account closure (business closure,)
- auto-document (unresolved disputes: document and transfer to)
- workflow notification (permission to re-contact if contractor returns)

### Controls
operational: Account Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by Account Manager and Finance

### Time Estimate
- Per closure: ~45–75 min active time
- Aggregate: ~200–400 × 60 min = ~200–400 hours/year

## W1885. Trade Account Master Data Quality & Segmentation Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Semi-annual data quality audit |
| **Frequency** | Semi-annual |
| **Volume** | ~5,200 trade accounts |
| **Owner** | CRM Data Analyst |
| **Participants** | CRM Data Analyst, Account Managers, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CRM Data Analyst runs data quality checks: (a) completeness: % of accounts with all required fields populated (contact info, trade type, TIN, segmentation), (b) accuracy: sample 100 accounts for phone verification — are contact numbers still valid?, (c) duplication: detect duplicate accounts (same TIN, similar name, same address), (d) segmentation accuracy: do account segmentation labels match actual purchase behavior? (e.g., account labeled "small contractor" but spending PHP 5M/year should be "medium contractor") | CRM Data Analyst | VP Store Operations | 2–3 days |
| 2 | Account Managers action data quality issues: (a) update incorrect or missing contact information, (b) merge duplicate accounts, (c) correct segmentation mismatches, (d) update dormant or closed accounts | Account Manager | VP Store Operations | 1–2 days/cycle |
| 3 | Semi-annual: CRM Data Analyst presents data quality report to VP Store Operations: (a) data quality score (% of accounts with complete, accurate data), (b) improvement trend vs. previous audit, (c) specific issues requiring process changes (e.g., Account Managers not updating records after visits) | CRM Data Analyst | VP Store Operations | 1 day/cycle |

### System Touchpoints
- Automated data quality scoring dashboard (W1885.1)
- Duplicate detection algorithm (W1885.1c)
- Segmentation accuracy analytics (W1885.1d)
- Integration with W29 (master data management), W13.3 (customer data & CRM)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Data decay**: Trade account contact information decays at ~15–20% per year (phone number changes, business address changes, personnel turnover); without regular updates, AM outreach to wrong contacts wastes time


### Automation Opportunity
- auto-validate (data quality checks: (a) completeness: %)
- auto-count (account managers action data)
- auto-record (not updating records after visits))

### Controls
operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- **CRM Data Analyst**: Semi-annual audit adds ~5–8 days/year — absorbed if CRM analyst exists; otherwise Account Managers absorb (~2–3 days each)

### Time Estimate
- Data quality checks: 2–3 days/cycle
- AM updates: 1–2 days/cycle across all AMs
- Reporting: 1 day/cycle
- Total: ~8–12 days/cycle × 2 cycles = ~16–24 days/year
