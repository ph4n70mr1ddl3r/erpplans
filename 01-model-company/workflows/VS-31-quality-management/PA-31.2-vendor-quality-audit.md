## W1608. Vendor Quality Scorecard & Performance Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly quality data aggregation cycle |
| **Frequency** | Monthly scorecard generation; quarterly review with vendors |
| **Volume** | ~800–1,000 active vendors |
| **Owner** | Quality Manager |
| **Participants** | Quality Manager, Category Managers, Buyers, VP Merchandising |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System auto-calculates vendor quality score monthly based on weighted metrics: (a) GR inspection pass rate (weight 30%) — from W1600/W1601 data, (b) defect rate per million items received (weight 25%), (c) customer complaint rate for vendor's products (weight 20%) — from VS-13 data, (d) return-to-vendor rate (weight 15%) — from W88/W1280 data, (e) certification compliance rate (weight 10%) — from W1612 data | System | Quality Manager | Automated |
| 2 | Quality Manager reviews auto-generated scorecard; validates anomalies (e.g., low score driven by one large defective lot vs. chronic quality issues); adds qualitative notes from DC Quality Inspectors | Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain | 4 hours/month |
| 3 | Vendors scored: (a) **A (≥ 90)**: Preferred vendor — eligible for increased volume, relaxed inspection (skip-lot sampling), (b) **B (75–89)**: Acceptable — standard inspection and monitoring, (c) **C (60–74)**: Watch list — enhanced inspection, quarterly review, corrective action plan required, (d) **D (< 60)**: Probation — 90-day improvement window; if no improvement, vendor discontinuation recommended to Category Manager | Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2 hours/month |
| 4 | Quarterly: Quality Manager and Category Manager conduct joint quality review with C/D vendors: (a) present scorecard data, (b) discuss specific quality issues, (c) agree on corrective actions and timeline, (d) document in Vendor Corrective Action Request per W1610 | Quality Manager / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1 hour/vendor |
| 5 | Quarterly: Quality Manager presents top-level vendor quality summary to VP Merchandising: (a) overall vendor quality trend, (b) top 10 quality problem vendors, (c) correlation between vendor quality improvements and customer complaint reduction, (d) recommended vendor additions/removals based on quality performance | Quality Manager | VP Merchandising | 30 min/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Automated vendor quality scoring from GR inspection, customer complaint, RTV, and certification data (W1608.1)
- Vendor quality scorecard with trend visualization (W1608.2–3)
- Corrective action tracking integration (W1610)
- Vendor classification (A/B/C/D) with downstream inspection rule changes (W1608.3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Score manipulation**: Vendors may dispute defect classifications to improve scores; requires clear defect taxonomy and photo evidence standards
- **Small volume vendor noise**: Vendors with few shipments per month have statistically unreliable scores; minimum shipment threshold needed for scoring (suggest 5+ shipments/quarter)
- **Quality vs. price tradeoff**: Category Managers may resist downgrading low-quality vendors who offer the best prices; requires VP-level escalation path

- Step 2: 4 hours/month
- Step 3: 2 hours/month
- Step 4: 1 hour/vendor

## W1609. Annual Vendor Quality Audit Program

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual audit calendar (Q1 planning) or triggered by vendor quality score drop to D-level |
| **Frequency** | ~30–40 on-site audits/year (top 20 vendors annually + triggered audits) |
| **Volume** | Top 20 vendors (45% of COGS) + ~10–20 triggered audits |
| **Owner** | Quality Manager |
| **Participants** | Quality Manager, Buyer, Category Manager, vendor quality team |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Quality Manager develops annual audit plan: (a) mandatory audits for top 20 vendors, (b) triggered audits for D-scored vendors, (c) new vendor audits (first year), (d) focus areas per vendor based on quality scorecard trends (e.g., if paint vendor has high defect rate, focus on colorant mixing and batch consistency) | Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain | 8 hours |
| 2 | Schedule audit with vendor (2–4 weeks lead time); share audit scope and checklist; for import vendors: coordinate with buying team for scheduled visit or third-party audit arrangement | Quality Manager | — | 2 hours/audit |
| 3 | On-site audit execution: (a) facility tour (production floor, raw material storage, finished goods warehouse, QC lab), (b) quality management system review (ISO 9001 certification or equivalent, incoming material QC, in-process QC, final QC, non-conformance handling), (c) product-specific inspection (review testing protocols, calibration records, defect tracking), (d) documentation review (certifications, test reports, traceability systems), (e) worker training and competency records | Quality Manager / Buyer | VP Supply Chain | 1 day/vendor (local); 2 days (international) |
| 4 | Audit report: (a) findings categorized as Critical (immediate action required), Major (corrective action within 30 days), Minor (improvement opportunity), Observation (best practice), (b) evidence photos and documentation, (c) overall audit score, (d) comparison to previous audit score | Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain | 4 hours/audit |
| 5 | Follow-up: (a) Critical findings — immediate meeting with vendor; supply may be suspended pending resolution, (b) Major findings — Vendor Corrective Action Request per W1610 with 30-day deadline, (c) Minor/Observation — tracked for next audit review | Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2 hours/finding |

### System Touchpoints
- Audit scheduling and calendar management (W1609.2)
- Audit checklist template per product category (W1609.3)
- Findings database with lifecycle tracking (open → corrective action → verification → closed) (W1609.4–5)
- Integration with vendor scorecard (W1608)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **International vendor access**: ~40% of vendors are international (China, Taiwan, Indonesia); on-site audits require travel budget and logistics; third-party audit firms (SGS, Bureau Veritas, TÜV) may be needed at ~PHP 50–100K per audit
- **Audit fatigue**: Vendors audited by multiple customers may experience audit fatigue; leverage mutual recognition of ISO 9001 or GFSI-type certifications where possible
- **Corrective action follow-through**: Vendor promises corrective actions but implementation may lag without follow-up verification

### Time Estimate

- Step 2: 2 hours/audit
- Step 3: 1 day/vendor (local); 2 days (international)
- Step 4: 4 hours/audit
- Step 5: 2 hours/finding

## W1610. Vendor Corrective Action Request (VCAR) Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quality inspection rejection (W1600), vendor audit finding (W1609), or chronic quality issue identified in scorecard (W1608) |
| **Frequency** | ~15–25 VCARs/month |
| **Volume** | ~200–300 active VCARs/year |
| **Owner** | Quality Manager |
| **Participants** | Quality Manager, Buyer, Category Manager, vendor quality representative |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Quality Manager creates VCAR: (a) references source (inspection report, audit finding, complaint analysis), (b) describes non-conformance with evidence (photos, defect data, customer complaint details), (c) specifies severity (Critical/Major/Minor), (d) assigns corrective action deadline (Critical: 7 days; Major: 30 days; Minor: 60 days), (e) sends to vendor via vendor portal (per VPP-001) or email | Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain | 30 min/VCAR |
| 2 | Vendor acknowledges VCAR within 3 business days; commits to investigation and corrective action plan | Vendor | — | — |
| 3 | Vendor submits corrective action plan: (a) root cause analysis (5-Why, fishbone diagram, or equivalent), (b) immediate containment action (what's done now to prevent recurrence), (c) permanent corrective action (process/system changes), (d) prevention of recurrence (controls, monitoring), (e) timeline for implementation | Vendor | Quality Manager | Vendor-dependent |
| 4 | Quality Manager reviews and approves corrective action plan; if inadequate, requests revision with specific guidance | Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain | 30 min/VCAR |
| 5 | At corrective action deadline: Quality Manager verifies implementation — (a) review vendor-submitted evidence of implementation (updated procedures, test results, training records), (b) for Critical/Major: schedule verification inspection of next shipment, (c) update vendor quality scorecard | Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain | 30 min/VCAR |
| 6 | **Close**: Quality Manager closes VCAR if corrective action verified as effective; VCAR record retained for 3 years; open VCAR count reflected in vendor quality score | Quality Manager | — | 10 min |
| 7 | **Escalation**: If vendor fails to respond within deadline or corrective action is ineffective after 2 attempts: (a) escalate to Category Manager and VP Merchandising, (b) consider enhanced inspection (100% inspection on all shipments), (c) consider vendor probation or discontinuation | Quality Manager / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | As needed |

### System Touchpoints
- VCAR creation with source document linking (W1610.1)
- Vendor portal integration for VCAR acknowledgment and response (W1610.2–3)
- VCAR lifecycle tracking with SLA deadlines and escalation alerts (W1610.4–7)
- Integration with vendor quality scorecard (W1608)


### Pain Points / Risks
- **Recurrence risk**: VCARs closed on containment without a verified root cause let chronic vendor issues persist across ~200–300 active VCARs/year; mitigated by mandatory vendor RCA (5-Why/fishbone) with permanent corrective action reviewed and approved by the Quality Manager before closure
- **Relationship-strain risk**: chargeback-heavy VCAR handling triggers vendor defensiveness and supply risk; mitigated by corrective-plan dialogue with specific guidance before any commercial action, with VP Supply Chain as the escalation tier (steps 3–4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-inspect of "inspection report, audit finding, complaint analysis" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-submit of "submits corrective action plan" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-review of "reviews and approves corrective action plan" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-229 (ensure supplier quality & product-safety verification.)
operational: Quality Manager review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
- Step 1: 30 min/VCAR
- Step 2: —
- Step 3: Vendor-dependent
- Step 4: 30 min/VCAR
- Step 5: 30 min/VCAR
- Step 6: 10 min
- Step 7: As needed

## W1611. New Vendor Quality Qualification & First Article Inspection

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New vendor onboarding per W42 (Vendor Sourcing & Onboarding in VS-03) |
| **Frequency** | ~50–80 new vendors/year |
| **Volume** | ~4–7 new vendor qualifications per month |
| **Owner** | Quality Manager |
| **Participants** | Quality Manager, Buyer, Category Manager, DC Quality Inspector |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Buyer initiates new vendor quality qualification as part of onboarding (W42); Quality Manager receives notification with vendor profile, product categories, and sample request | Buyer / Quality Manager | Category Manager | 10 min |
| 2 | Quality Manager requests product samples from vendor (minimum 3 units per SKU for first article inspection); for import vendors: request pre-shipment sample or third-party inspection report | Quality Manager | — | 30 min |
| 3 | DC Quality Inspector performs First Article Inspection (FAI): (a) dimensional verification against product specification, (b) material/finish quality assessment, (c) functional testing per category checklist, (d) packaging and labeling review, (e) certification documentation verification (PS/ICC/FPA per category), (f) comparison to existing product benchmarks (if replacing incumbent vendor) | DC Quality Inspector | Quality Manager | 30–60 min/SKU |
| 4 | Quality Manager reviews FAI results; classifies: (a) **Approved** — vendor product meets quality standards; vendor cleared for commercial orders with standard inspection (10% sampling per W1600), (b) **Conditional** — product acceptable with noted deviations; vendor cleared for trial order with enhanced inspection (100% sampling on first 3 shipments), (c) **Rejected** — product does not meet quality standards; vendor not cleared; Buyer to source alternative or negotiate vendor quality improvements | Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain | 30 min |
| 5 | FAI record attached to vendor master; initial quality classification (A/B/C/D) set based on FAI results; sampling plan set per classification | Quality Manager | — | 10 min |

### System Touchpoints
- FAI request and tracking linked to vendor onboarding workflow (W1611.1)
- FAI checklist per product category (W1611.3)
- Vendor master quality classification update (W1611.5)
- Sampling plan initial configuration per vendor classification (W1604)


### Pain Points / Risks
- **Defect-inflow risk**: an unqualified vendor ships defect-rate product straight to 200 stores; mitigated by First Article Inspection (≥3 units per SKU, dimensional/material/functional) gating commercial clearance, stepping down to 10% sampling only after approval (steps 2–3)
- **Golden-sample risk**: hand-picked samples misrepresent mass-production quality; mitigated for imports by pre-shipment samples or third-party inspection reports, with post-approval scorecard monitoring (W1600) triggering re-qualification on drift (step 2)

- System auto-inspect of "inspection" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "documentation verification" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls

operational: Quality Manager review and approval gate

- Step 3: 30–60 min/SKU

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |

| 3 | Vendor submits renewed certification; Quality Manager reviews and updates record; system unblocks PO creation if previously blocked | Quality Manager | — | 10 min/certification |

### System Touchpoints

## W1613. Import Vendor Quality & Compliance Pre-Shipment Verification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|

| **Frequency** | ~100–150 import POs/month (~40% of procurement by vendor count) |
| **Volume** | ~50–75 pre-shipment verifications/month |
| **Owner** | Import Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Import Coordinator, Quality Manager, third-party inspection agency, Buyer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Import Coordinator identifies import POs approaching ship-ready date; determines pre-shipment verification requirement based on: (a) vendor quality score (C/D vendors require mandatory PSI), (b) PO value (> PHP 1M requires PSI), (c) product category risk (electrical, chemical categories require PSI), (d) new vendor (first 3 shipments require PSI) | Import Coordinator | Supply Chain Manager | 30 min/week |
| 2 | For shipments requiring PSI: Import Coordinator engages third-party inspection agency (SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek) at vendor's location; provides PO specification, quality requirements, and inspection checklist | Import Coordinator | Supply Chain Manager | 1 hour/PO |

| 4 | PSI report reviewed by Quality Manager: (a) **Pass** — authorize shipment; vendor books freight, (b) **Conditional Pass** — authorize shipment with rework conditions; vendor corrects minor issues before shipping, (c) **Fail** — shipment withheld; vendor must correct and resubmit for re-inspection; Buyer notified of delay | Quality Manager | Supply Chain Manager | 30 min/PO |
| 5 | PSI report linked to import PO and container tracking; feeds into PDI at Philippine port per W1602 | Import Coordinator | — | 10 min |

### System Touchpoints

## W1614. Quality Cost Analysis & Vendor Chargeback Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly quality cost aggregation or vendor-caused quality event |
| **Frequency** | Monthly analysis; ad-hoc chargeback processing |
| **Volume** | ~30–50 chargebacks/month |
| **Owner** | Quality Manager |
| **Participants** | Quality Manager, Controller, Buyer, Category Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monthly: System aggregates quality costs across 4 categories: (a) **Prevention costs** — quality inspection labor, vendor audits, certification management, training, (b) **Appraisal costs** — inspection equipment, third-party testing, lab supplies, (c) **Internal failure costs** — scrap, rework, re-inspection, Quality Hold carrying cost, (d) **External failure costs** — customer returns due to quality, warranty claims, recall costs, lost sales, brand damage (estimated) | System / Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain | 4 hours/month |
| 2 | Quality Manager analyzes cost of quality (COQ) as percentage of COGS and revenue; compares to industry benchmarks (target: COQ < 2% of COGS); identifies top cost drivers | Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2 hours/month |
| 3 | For vendor-caused quality events: Quality Manager creates chargeback request — (a) documents vendor-caused failure with evidence, (b) calculates chargeback amount: direct cost (product cost + handling + inspection) + consequential cost (lost margin from stockout if applicable), (c) routes to Buyer for vendor negotiation and Controller for GL posting | Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain / Controller | 30 min/event |
| 4 | Buyer negotiates chargeback with vendor — options: (a) direct credit note on next payment, (b) product replacement at no charge, (c) future order price concession, (d) combination | Buyer | Category Manager | As needed |
| 5 | Controller processes chargeback settlement: (a) debit vendor AP, (b) credit quality cost recovery account, (c) track chargeback recovery rate (target: > 80% of chargeback amount recovered) | Controller | CFO | 15 min/event |
| 6 | Quarterly: Quality Manager presents COQ trend to executive team with recommendations for quality investment (e.g., additional inspector, testing equipment) and vendor portfolio optimization | Quality Manager | VP Supply Chain / CFO | 2 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Quality cost aggregation from inspection, return, scrap, and warranty data (W1614.1)
- Chargeback request creation with vendor AP linkage (W1614.3)
- Chargeback settlement tracking and recovery rate dashboard (W1614.4–5)
- COQ trend reporting and benchmarking (W1614.6)


### Pain Points / Risks
- **Unrecovered-cost risk**: vendor-caused failures (~30–50 events/month) absorbed as shrink instead of charged back; mitigated by monthly quality-cost aggregation with documented chargeback calculation (direct + handling cost) and Buyer-led negotiation (steps 1, 3–4)
- **Metric-distortion risk**: cost-of-quality categories mixed (prevention vs. appraisal vs. failure) hide the true drivers against the <2%-of-COGS target; mitigated by the standard four-category aggregation and benchmark comparison (steps 1–2)

### Automation Opportunity

- System auto-analyze of "analyzes cost of quality" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "documents vendor-caused failure with evidence," (replaces manual Step 3).

operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

- Step 2: 2 hours/month
- Step 3: 30 min/event
- Step 4: As needed
- Step 5: 15 min/event
