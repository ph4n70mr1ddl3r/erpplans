## W879. Daily Report Distribution & Automated Dashboard Refresh

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — daily at 06:00 AM (before store opening) for morning reports; continuous refresh for real-time dashboards |
| **Frequency** | Daily (365 days/year — automated with exception monitoring) |
| **Volume** | 15–20 automated report distributions per day; 5 real-time dashboards refreshed continuously |
| **Owner** | BI Operations Analyst / IT Operations |
| **Participants** | BI Manager, Store Managers (report recipients), Regional Managers, Finance Controller, VP Operations |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ETL batch completion verification — confirm overnight data warehouse refresh completed successfully (sales, inventory, finance, HR feeds); check for errors or data latency; if failed, initiate W881 exception handling | BI Operations Analyst | BI Manager | 30 min |
| 2 | Automated report generation and distribution — system generates: (a) Daily Sales Flash (by store, region, chain), (b) Daily Inventory Alert (stock-outs, overstock, aging), (c) Daily Cash Reconciliation Summary, (d) Daily POS Performance Report, (e) Daily ecommerce Dashboard; distribute via email and portal per recipient distribution lists | BI System (automated) | BI Manager | 15 min (automated) |
| 3 | Dashboard refresh validation — verify real-time dashboards reflect current data: (a) Executive KPI Dashboard, (b) Store Operations Dashboard, (c) Supply Chain Dashboard, (d) Finance Dashboard, (e) Customer Experience Dashboard; check timestamp and data freshness | BI Operations Analyst | BI Manager | 15 min |
| 4 | Investigate and resolve data discrepancies — if daily report numbers don't match source system (e.g., POS sales don't match finance posting), trace data lineage, identify root cause (ETL error, timing difference, data quality issue), and issue corrected report if material | BI Operations Analyst | BI Manager | 30–60 min per discrepancy |
| 5 | Monitor report delivery success — check email delivery logs; re-send failed distributions; follow up with recipients who report access issues; maintain distribution list accuracy | BI Operations Analyst | BI Manager | 15 min |
| 6 | End-of-day data snapshot — at store close (varies by region, 8–10 PM), capture final daily snapshot for next morning reports; verify POS close-of-day batch completion feeds | BI System (automated) | BI Operations Analyst | 15 min (automated) |

### System Touchpoints
- **BI Platform** — report generation, dashboard hosting, scheduling engine
- **Data Warehouse** — dimensional model, fact tables, data refresh
- **ETL Tool** — data pipeline monitoring, error alerting
- **Email System** — report distribution, delivery tracking
- **ERP Portal** — self-service report access
- **POS System** — daily sales data source

### Pain Points / Risks
- Overnight ETL failures result in stale morning reports — executives make decisions on outdated data
- Report distribution list maintenance is ongoing — employee role changes create incorrect recipients
- Dashboard performance degrades as data volume grows — query optimization required quarterly
- Time zone differences across Philippine regions are minimal but store closing times vary — data completeness timing
- Email report attachments may be blocked by spam filters — PDF attachment size limits


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "check for errors or data latency" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-generate of "generates" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-match of "match source system" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-222 (ensure BI data governance & reporting integrity.)
- operational: quarterly

### Staffing Implication
- 1 BI Operations Analyst (early shift: 5:30 AM start for morning report verification)
- BI Manager: escalation point for data discrepancies
- IT Operations: ETL infrastructure support

### Time Estimate
- Daily monitoring: 2–3 hours per analyst
- **Annual estimate: ~900 person-hours/year (daily 2.5 hours × 365 days)**

## W1214. Store Performance Scorecard Weekly Review & Regional Benchmarking

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — weekly (every Monday); system auto-generates scorecard from prior week's data |
| **Frequency** | Weekly |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × 52 weeks = 10,400 scorecards/year; ~6 Regional Manager reviews/week (one per RM, per profile §12.1) |
| **Owner** | Regional Manager |
| **Participants** | Regional Manager, Store Managers (in region), BI Analyst (dashboard maintenance), VP Store Operations (monthly consolidation) |

### Background

With 200 stores across the Philippine archipelago, BuildRight Depot's VP Store Operations and ~6 Regional Managers need a standardized, data-driven view of store performance to identify top performers, underperformers, and emerging trends. Weekly scorecards — automatically generated from POS, inventory, workforce, and customer data — provide a consistent performance snapshot that enables: (a) early intervention for declining stores before monthly P&L reveals the problem, (b) recognition and best-practice sharing from top-performing stores, (c) regional benchmarking to account for local market differences, (d) resource allocation (capital investment, staffing adjustments, merchandising support) based on performance data. This workflow complements W522 (daily cashier performance) and W67 (monthly store performance review) by adding a structured weekly cadence with standardized KPIs and regional comparison.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System auto-generates **Weekly Store Performance Scorecard** overnight Sunday/Monday for each store with prior week metrics: (a) **Revenue**: weekly revenue vs. target, year-over-year growth, revenue per sqm, average transaction value; (b) **Productivity**: revenue per employee, transactions per labor hour; (c) **Inventory**: inventory turns, out-of-stock rate (from W42 cycle count data), shrinkage estimate; (d) **Customer**: CSAT score (from W65 surveys), loyalty scan rate, BOPIS fulfillment SLA compliance; (e) **Operations**: on-time store opening rate, receiving accuracy, planogram compliance per W86 | System | — | Automated |
| 2 | System calculates **regional rankings**: stores ranked within their region on each KPI; color-coded: green (top quartile), yellow (middle 50%), red (bottom quartile); stores that moved > 10 percentile positions week-over-week flagged as "movers" | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | System identifies **exception alerts**: (a) revenue decline > 10% week-over-week without seasonal explanation, (b) out-of-stock rate > 5% for any major category, (c) shrinkage estimate > 2% of revenue, (d) CSAT score < 75%, (e) loyalty scan rate < 40%, (f) any KPI in bottom quartile for 3+ consecutive weeks (chronic underperformer) | System | — | Automated |
| 4 | Regional Manager opens scorecard dashboard Monday morning; reviews: (a) region-level summary: total revenue vs. target, store count in green/yellow/red, key movers; (b) each store's scorecard with exception alerts; (c) identifies 3–5 stores requiring attention (new red stores, declining trends, chronic underperformers) | Regional Manager | VP Store Operations | 30–60 min |
| 5 | Regional Manager contacts flagged Store Managers: (a) **coaching call** for declining stores — understand root cause (new competitor, staff turnover, supply issues, local event), agree on corrective actions; (b) **recognition call** for top movers — acknowledge improvement, ask what's working; (c) documents call notes and action items in store operations module | Regional Manager | — | 15–30 min per flagged store |
| 6 | **Weekly regional team huddle** (Monday afternoon): Regional Manager shares: (a) region performance vs. other regions (VP Store Operations shares inter-regional comparison), (b) top-performing stores and their best practices, (c) common challenges and cross-store solutions, (d) upcoming initiatives and priorities for the week | Regional Manager | VP Store Operations | 30 min |
| 7 | **Monthly consolidation**: VP Store Operations reviews aggregated scorecard data: (a) compare 10 regions on same KPIs, (b) identify best- and worst-performing regions, (c) analyze trends: are chain-wide KPIs improving or declining?, (d) present monthly summary to CEO/COO with recommendations for capital allocation, new store prioritization, or operational interventions | VP Store Operations | COO | 2–3 hours/month |
| 8 | **Quarterly KPI review**: BI Analyst and VP Store Operations review scorecard KPIs: (a) are targets still appropriate?, (b) should new KPIs be added (e.g., ecommerce conversion rate, marketplace seller rating)?, (c) adjust regional grouping if store count changes with new store openings | BI Analyst / VP Store Operations | CIO | 2 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Weekly Store Performance Scorecard: auto-generated from POS transactions, inventory movements, workforce scheduling, CSAT surveys, and BOPIS fulfillment data
- Regional ranking engine: percentile calculation within region groups
- Exception alert engine: configurable thresholds for each KPI
- Store operations module: call notes, action items, follow-up tracking
- BI dashboard: interactive drill-down from region → store → KPI → contributing factors
- Integration with W522 (daily cashier performance), W67 (monthly store review), W879 (daily report distribution), W86 (planogram compliance)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Ranking gaming** — Store Managers may focus on ranked KPIs at the expense of unmeasured aspects; balanced scorecard with counter-metrics mitigates (e.g., pair revenue with margin)
- **Regional comparison unfairness** — stores in different markets (Metro Manila vs. provincial) have inherently different performance ceilings; within-region ranking and year-over-year comparison mitigates
- **Dashboard fatigue** — weekly review adds to Regional Manager workload; exception-based alerting and concise dashboard design keep review time manageable
- **Data lag** — CSAT survey responses and shrinkage estimates may lag real-time; scorecard should clearly label data freshness


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-scan of "scan rate, BOPIS fulfillment SLA compliance" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-calculate of "calculates regional rankings" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-alert of "alerts" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-325 (ensure controlled execution — BI Platform & Reporting (PA-28.1).)
- operational: Regional Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- Regional Manager: ~1.5–2.5 hours/week on scorecard review and coaching calls; absorbed by existing role
- BI Analyst: ~2 hours/quarter on KPI review and dashboard maintenance
- VP Store Operations: ~2–3 hours/month on consolidation
- No incremental headcount

### Time Estimate
- Regional Manager: 30–60 min review + 30–90 min coaching calls + 30 min huddle = ~1.5–3 hours/week
- VP Store Operations: 2–3 hours/month for consolidation
- BI Analyst: 2 hours/quarter
- **Annual estimate: ~1,300 hours (Regional Managers) + ~30 hours (VP) + 8 hours (BI)**

## W1250. Store Foot Traffic Analytics, Conversion Rate Monitoring & Sales Floor Productivity Insights

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — weekly foot traffic analytics refresh; monthly productivity deep-dive; ad-hoc triggered by store conversion rate decline or regional benchmarking request |
| **Frequency** | Weekly analytics refresh; monthly deep-dive per store; quarterly regional benchmarking |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × weekly analytics; monthly deep-dive for bottom-quartile stores (50 stores); quarterly benchmarking for all stores by region |
| **Owner** | BI Analyst (Retail Analytics) |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Regional Manager, VP Store Operations, Category Manager, Facilities Manager |

### Background

BuildRight Depot operates 200 big-box stores with 8,000–15,000 sqm selling areas, generating PHP 25M in-store revenue per store per month. Despite significant investment in physical retail space, the company lacks systematic measurement of how effectively store traffic converts to revenue. Key questions include: (a) how many customers enter each store daily and how does this vary by day-of-week, season, and promotional event? (b) what is the conversion rate (transactions ÷ foot traffic) and how does it compare across stores? (c) which zones within the store (lumber yard, tool wall, tile gallery, appliance section) drive the highest engagement? (d) does foot traffic correlate with staffing levels — are stores understaffed during peak hours? This workflow establishes the analytics framework for foot traffic measurement, conversion rate monitoring, and sales floor productivity insights that drive operational decisions including staffing optimization, planogram refinement, and capital allocation for store layout improvements.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Weekly foot traffic data collection & processing**: BI Analyst extracts and processes foot traffic data from store-level people counters (door-mounted infrared or video-based counters): (a) aggregate daily entry counts by store; (b) calculate conversion rate: POS transactions ÷ foot traffic entries; (c) calculate ATV (average transaction value) and items per basket; (d) compute sales per sqm using store selling area from location master; (e) flag stores where conversion rate dropped > 10% week-over-week | BI Analyst | BI Manager | 3 hours/week |
| 2 | **Conversion rate variance analysis**: For flagged stores, investigate conversion rate decline: (a) cross-reference with promotional calendar — was the store running an event that drove traffic without conversion? (b) check staffing levels during peak traffic hours — understaffing reduces conversion; (c) review out-of-stock rate for top-selling SKUs — stock-outs reduce conversion; (d) check weather data — heavy rain in provincial locations depresses walk-in traffic; (e) compile findings for Regional Manager review | BI Analyst | Regional Manager | 2 hours/week |
| 3 | **Monthly sales floor productivity deep-dive**: For bottom-quartile stores (50 stores by conversion rate), conduct detailed analysis: (a) zone-level traffic heat map (if zone-level sensors available) showing dwell time in lumber yard, tool wall, tile gallery, appliance section, checkout; (b) category-level conversion — which departments have high traffic but low sales; (c) staffing-to-traffic ratio — sales associates per 100 visitors by hour; (d) queue wait time correlation — does checkout queue length correlate with abandoned purchases; (e) generate store-specific productivity improvement recommendations | BI Analyst / Regional Manager | VP Store Operations | 4 hours/month |
| 4 | **Quarterly regional benchmarking report**: BI Analyst produces quarterly benchmarking report comparing all 200 stores across: (a) foot traffic growth rate (YoY, QoQ); (b) conversion rate ranking by region (Mindanao, Visayas, Luzon, NCR); (c) ATV trends by store maturity (new stores < 1 year vs. mature stores > 3 years); (d) sales per sqm vs. foot traffic per sqm — identifying over/under-performing space; (e) present to VP Store Operations and CMO for strategic decisions on marketing spend allocation, store hours optimization, and layout renovation priorities | BI Analyst / BI Manager | VP Store Operations | 8 hours/quarter |
| 5 | **Action plan tracking**: Track implementation and impact of productivity improvement actions: (a) for each recommended action (staffing adjustment, layout change, category refresh), measure before/after conversion rate and ATV; (b) report ROI of layout changes and capital improvements; (c) feed results into annual store capital budget process | BI Analyst | Regional Manager | 2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- **People Counter System** — infrared/video door counters capturing entry counts per store
- **POS System** — transaction count, ATV, items per basket per store per day
- **BI Platform** — foot traffic dashboard, conversion rate analytics, store ranking
- **Location Master** — store selling area (sqm) for productivity calculations
- **HR / Scheduling Module** — staffing levels by hour for staffing-to-traffic ratio
- **Promotional Calendar** — campaign schedule for traffic/conversion correlation

### Pain Points / Risks
- People counter accuracy varies by technology — infrared counters overcount (children, shopping carts) while video-based counters are more accurate but more expensive
- Provincial stores may have unreliable power affecting counter uptime — estimated 85–90% data capture rate
- Conversion rate is influenced by many factors (weather, payday cycle, local events) making week-over-week comparison noisy
- Zone-level analytics require additional sensor investment — not all 200 stores have zone-level coverage
- Data privacy considerations — people counters capture customer movement patterns that may require NPC compliance review


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "counters" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "review out-of-stock rate for top-selling SKUs — stock-outs reduce conversion" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "checkout" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-325 (ensure controlled execution — BI Platform & Reporting (PA-28.1).)
- operational: BI Analyst (Retail Analytics) review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- ~20% of 1 BI Analyst (~364 hours/year) on foot traffic and store productivity analytics — a standing part-time allocation, not a dedicated FTE
- Store Managers: 15 min/week reviewing store-level conversion dashboard
- Regional Managers: 1 hour/month reviewing regional conversion trends

### Time Estimate
- Weekly analytics: 3 hours/week = 156 hours/year
- Monthly deep-dive: 4 hours/month = 48 hours/year
- Quarterly benchmarking: 8 hours/quarter = 32 hours/year
- Action plan tracking: 2 hours/month = 24 hours/year
- Conversion-rate variance analysis (step 2): 2 hours/week = 104 hours/year
- **Annual estimate: ~364 person-hours/year**

## W1451. Merchandise Category Performance Analytics, Gross Margin Optimization & Assortment Rationalization Insights

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly category performance reporting cycle; or quarterly assortment review; or ad-hoc request from VP Merchandising or Category Manager |
| **Frequency** | Monthly reporting; quarterly deep-dive analysis; annual assortment rationalization review |
| **Volume** | 12 product categories; 35,000 active SKUs; 200 stores; ~PHP 62.3B annual revenue across all categories |
| **Owner** | BI Analytics Manager |
| **Participants** | BI Analytics Manager, Data Analyst, VP Merchandising, Category Managers, Pricing Analyst, Finance Controller, VP Store Operations |

### Background

BuildRight Depot's 35,000 active SKUs are organized across 13 product categories (profile §6.2) (Lumber, Tiles, Plumbing, Electrical, Paint, Hand Tools, Power Tools, Hardware/Fasteners, Appliances, Home Décor, Garden, and Safety/PPE). Effective category management requires data-driven insights on: (a) which categories and sub-categories are growing vs. declining, (b) gross margin by category and SKU (target: 28–32% blended), (c) inventory productivity (GMROI — Gross Margin Return on Inventory Investment) by category, (d) space productivity (sales per sqm by department), and (e) assortment rationalization opportunities (identify SKUs that should be added, maintained, or discontinued). This workflow produces the analytical insights that drive merchandising decisions.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly category performance dashboard**: Data Analyst produces monthly category scorecard: (a) revenue by category: (i) actual vs. plan vs. prior year, (ii) revenue share (% of total), (iii) same-store sales growth, (b) gross margin by category: (i) gross margin %, (ii) gross margin PHP, (iii) margin variance drivers (mix, pricing, cost changes), (c) inventory productivity: (i) GMROI (gross margin ÷ average inventory cost), (ii) inventory turns by category, (iii) weeks of supply, (d) space productivity: (i) sales per sqm by department, (ii) gross margin per sqm — identifies departments where space reallocation would improve store profitability | Data Analyst | BI Analytics Manager | 8 hours/month |
| 2 | **SKU-level margin analysis** (quarterly): Data Analyst conducts deep-dive margin analysis: (a) for each category, rank all SKUs by gross margin contribution (top 20% of SKUs typically drive 80% of margin), (b) identify "margin leakers": high-velocity SKUs with below-target margin — evaluate: (i) is the cost increasing? (vendor price increase, landed cost change per W85), (ii) is pricing competitive? (competitor pricing data), (iii) recommended action: negotiate vendor cost reduction, increase SRP, or discontinue, (c) identify "margin gems": low-velocity SKUs with high margin — evaluate: (i) can velocity be increased through better placement/promotion?, (ii) are these niche products worth keeping in assortment?, (d) identify "dead weight": low-velocity, low-margin SKUs — candidates for discontinuation per W1 | Data Analyst / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 16 hours/quarter |
| 3 | **Assortment rationalization recommendation** (annual): BI Analytics Manager produces annual assortment rationalization report: (a) SKU lifecycle analysis: (i) new SKU performance vs. forecast (first 90 days), (ii) active SKU performance (sales velocity, margin, GMROI), (iii) declining SKU trend (12-month sales trend — is the SKU dying?), (iv) dead SKU identification (zero sales in 90 days), (b) market gap analysis: (i) competitor assortment comparison — categories where BuildRight has fewer SKUs than competitors, (ii) customer search queries on buildright.com.ph with zero results — products customers want but BuildRight doesn't carry, (c) recommendation: (i) SKUs to discontinue (target: 2,000–3,000/year), (ii) SKUs to add (target: 2,000–4,000/year for net 5–8% SKU growth), (iii) space reallocation recommendations by department, (d) present to VP Merchandising and Category Managers for action planning | BI Analytics Manager | VP Merchandising | 40 hours/year |

## W1452. Customer Segmentation & Lifetime Value Analytics: Trade Professional vs. Retail vs. Corporate Account Profiling

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly customer analytics cycle; or annual marketing strategy planning; or loyalty program review per W104 |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly segmentation refresh; annual deep-dive with lifetime value modeling; monthly monitoring dashboards |
| **Volume** | ~600,000 loyalty members (B2C); ~5,200 trade accounts (B2B); ~200 corporate accounts (B2B); ~42,900 ecommerce customers/month |
| **Owner** | BI Analytics Manager |
| **Participants** | BI Analytics Manager, Data Scientist, VP Marketing, CMO, Trade Account Manager, CRM Manager, VP Merchandising |

### Background

BuildRight Depot serves three distinct customer segments: (a) walk-in retail (B2C) — ~60% of revenue from ~600,000 loyalty members, (b) trade professionals (B2B) — 30% of revenue from ~5,000 contractors/builders, and (c) corporate/institutional accounts (B2B) — 10% of revenue from ~200 large accounts. Each segment has fundamentally different purchasing behavior, margin profile, service requirements, and lifetime value. Understanding these differences is critical for: marketing investment allocation (where to spend marketing pesos), merchandising strategy (which products to prioritize for which segment), and service model design (in-store services, delivery, credit terms). This workflow produces the customer segmentation analytics and lifetime value models that inform BuildRight's customer strategy.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Quarterly segmentation refresh**: Data Scientist refreshes customer segmentation model: (a) RFM analysis (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) for all 605,200+ customers: (i) Recency: days since last purchase, (ii) Frequency: number of transactions in trailing 12 months, (iii) Monetary: total spend in trailing 12 months, (b) segment classification: (i) Retail Champions: top 10% by spend (loyalty members who shop frequently and spend heavily), (ii) Retail Regulars: middle 50% (steady but moderate shoppers), (iii) Retail At-Risk: previously active but declining (trigger win-back per loyalty program), (iv) Trade Active: trade accounts with purchases in last 90 days, (v) Trade Dormant: trade accounts with no purchases in 90+ days (trigger re-engagement), (vi) Corporate Active: corporate accounts with active projects, (vii) Corporate Pipeline: corporate accounts with upcoming project bids, (c) update customer master records with segment tags for targeted marketing per W83 and W1443 | Data Scientist | BI Analytics Manager | 16 hours/quarter |
| 2 | **Lifetime value modeling** (annual): Data Scientist builds/updates customer lifetime value (CLV) model: (a) historical CLV by segment: (i) average annual spend × average customer tenure × gross margin %, (ii) Trade professionals: avg PHP 300K/year × 5-year avg tenure × 25% margin = PHP 375K lifetime value, (iii) Retail Champions: avg PHP 50K/year × 3-year avg tenure × 32% margin = PHP 48K lifetime value, (iv) Corporate accounts: avg PHP 5M/year × 3-year avg tenure × 20% margin = PHP 3M lifetime value, (b) predictive CLV: machine learning model predicts future value based on purchasing patterns, demographics, and engagement signals, (c) customer acquisition cost (CAC) by segment: marketing spend ÷ new customers acquired, (d) CLV:CAC ratio by segment — guides marketing budget allocation (target: CLV:CAC > 3:1) | Data Scientist | CMO | 40 hours/year |
| 3 | **Segment-specific insight generation**: BI Analytics Manager produces segment-specific insights for stakeholders: (a) for VP Marketing: (i) which segment should receive increased marketing investment (highest CLV:CAC ratio), (ii) win-back campaign targets (At-Risk and Dormant segments), (iii) cross-sell/upsell opportunities (retail customers buying project materials who could be trade account candidates), (b) for VP Merchandising: (i) basket analysis by segment (what trade pros buy vs. retail customers — different category affinities), (ii) private label penetration by segment, (c) for VP Store Operations: (i) trade pro visit frequency and preferred store hours (inform staffing), (ii) BOPIS adoption by segment, (d) for VP Finance: (i) credit risk by segment (corporate account aging), (ii) margin by segment | BI Analytics Manager | CMO / VP Merchandising | 16 hours/quarter |
| 4 | **Monthly segment monitoring dashboard**: Data Analyst maintains real-time segment dashboard: (a) active customer count by segment, (b) monthly spend by segment vs. plan, (c) new customer acquisition by segment, (d) churn rate by segment (customers who haven't purchased in 90 days), (e) segment migration (customers moving between segments — e.g., Retail Regular → Trade Account upgrade), (f) alert on significant segment shifts: (i) trade account churn > 5% in a month, (ii) retail active customer decline > 2% month-over-month | Data Analyst | BI Analytics Manager | 8 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints

### Background

BuildRight's 200 Store Managers are responsible for the daily performance of their stores (sales, customer service, inventory accuracy, staff productivity, safety). A self-service analytics dashboard accessible on the Store Manager's tablet or mobile phone provides real-time and near-real-time KPIs with actionable context: "Your paint department sales are 15% below today's target — here's why (foot traffic is normal but conversion rate is down; check staffing in the paint section)."
