## W883. Ad-hoc Analytics Request Fulfillment & SLA Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Business user submits analytics request that cannot be answered by existing dashboards or reports |
| **Frequency** | ~30–50 ad-hoc requests per month |
| **Volume** | Average request: 4–8 hours to fulfill; complex requests: 20–40 hours |
| **Owner** | BI Analyst / BI Manager |
| **Participants** | Business Requestor, BI Analyst, Data Engineer (for complex data extraction), BI Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Receive and triage request — log request in analytics queue: (a) business question, (b) urgency (standard: 5 business days, urgent: 2 business days, executive: same day), (c) data requirements, (d) output format (report, visualization, data extract, presentation); assign to BI Analyst based on expertise and workload | BI Manager | BI Analyst | 15 min |
| 2 | Clarify requirements — BI Analyst meets with requestor to: (a) understand decision context and how analysis will be used, (b) define scope (time period, geography, product categories), (c) agree on methodology and output format, (d) set delivery timeline | BI Analyst | BI Manager | 30 min |
| 3 | Extract and prepare data — (a) identify data sources, (b) build SQL queries or ETL pipeline, (c) validate data quality and completeness, (d) transform data for analysis | BI Analyst | Data Engineer | 1–4 hours |
| 4 | Conduct analysis — apply appropriate methods: (a) descriptive statistics and trend analysis, (b) comparison (same-store, year-over-year, plan vs. actual), (c) segmentation (customer, product, geography), (d) correlation analysis (if applicable); document methodology and assumptions | BI Analyst | BI Manager | 2–8 hours |
| 5 | Create deliverable — prepare output: (a) executive summary with key findings, (b) supporting visualizations, (c) data tables with source notes, (d) methodology documentation, (e) limitations and caveats; format per request (PDF, PPT, interactive dashboard, Excel) | BI Analyst | BI Manager | 1–4 hours |
| 6 | Review and deliver — BI Manager reviews for accuracy and clarity; requestor reviews for completeness; iterate if needed; deliver final output | BI Manager | Business Requestor | 30 min |
| 7 | Evaluate for reusability — if request is recurring: propose dashboard or automated report development (per W880); add to development backlog with priority ranking | BI Analyst | BI Manager | 15 min |

### System Touchpoints
- **BI Platform** — ad-hoc query tool, visualization development
- **Data Warehouse** — data extraction, SQL analysis
- **ITSM** — request ticketing, SLA tracking, satisfaction survey
- **Document Management** — deliverable storage, version control
- **BI Development Backlog** — reusable request pipeline

### Pain Points / Risks
- Request volume can exceed BI team capacity — backlog management and prioritization essential
- Executive "urgent" requests disrupt planned work — requires flexible resource allocation
- Scope creep — initial request expands during analysis ("while you're at it, can you also..."); scope management needed
- Data availability limitations — some requests require data not captured in current systems
- Analysis quality depends on BI Analyst's business knowledge — cross-training is essential
- Output may be misinterpreted — clear caveats and methodology documentation required

### Staffing Implication
- 2 BI Analysts (ad-hoc analytics fulfillment)
- BI Manager: triage and quality review (25% of time)
- Data Engineer: complex data extraction support (as needed)

### Time Estimate
- Per standard request: 4–8 hours
- Per complex request: 20–40 hours
- **Annual estimate: 480 requests × 8 hours average = 3,840 person-hours/year**

## W1178. Predictive Analytics Model Development, Deployment & Monitoring

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Business need for predictive capability; or annual analytics roadmap planning; or pilot success triggering production deployment |
| **Frequency** | ~4–6 new model development projects per year; continuous monitoring of production models |
| **Volume** | 3–5 production models in operation at any time; serving 200 stores and 4 DCs |
| **Owner** | Data Scientist / BI Manager |
| **Participants** | Data Scientist, BI Manager, Business Domain Owner (Category Manager, Supply Chain Director, HR Director), Data Engineer, IT Operations |

### Background

BuildRight's 33.6M annual transactions, 35,000 active SKUs, 200 stores, and ~800 vendors generate rich data sets suitable for predictive analytics. Key use cases for a big-box home improvement retailer include: (a) **Demand sensing**: improve forecast accuracy beyond W31 statistical forecasting by incorporating weather data (PAGASA), local events (fiestas, construction booms), and social media signals; (b) **Customer churn prediction**: identify loyalty members per W17 at risk of lapsing and trigger targeted retention per W660; (c) **Inventory risk scoring**: predict which SKUs are likely to become slow-moving or obsolete per W587, enabling proactive markdown; (d) **Vendor reliability prediction**: forecast which vendors are likely to miss OTIF targets per W44, enabling proactive sourcing alternatives; (e) **Store performance prediction**: identify stores likely to miss quarterly revenue targets, enabling early intervention. This workflow governs the lifecycle of predictive models from ideation through deployment and monitoring.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Use case identification & prioritization**: Annual planning + ad-hoc: (a) BI Manager and business domain owners identify predictive analytics use cases; (b) score each use case on: business impact (revenue, cost, risk), data availability and quality, technical feasibility, and implementation effort; (c) prioritize top 4–6 use cases for annual roadmap; (d) CIO approves roadmap and budget; (e) quick wins (high impact, low effort) scheduled for H1 | BI Manager / Business Domain Owner | CIO | 2 days |
| 2 | **Data exploration & preparation**: For each approved use case: (a) Data Scientist explores available data: historical sales (POS), inventory movements, customer transactions, vendor performance, weather, economic indicators; (b) assess data quality per W1177 governance standards; (c) build feature engineering pipeline: derive predictive variables (lagged sales, rolling averages, seasonality indicators, store demographic features); (d) prepare training data set with appropriate time splits; (e) document data sources, transformations, and assumptions | Data Scientist / Data Engineer | BI Manager | 2–4 weeks |
| 3 | **Model development & validation**: (a) Data Scientist trains multiple model types: time series (ARIMA, Prophet), regression (XGBoost, LightGBM), neural networks (for complex patterns); (b) cross-validate using walk-forward validation for time series; (c) compare model performance vs. baseline (current W31 forecast, random baseline, naive seasonal baseline); (d) select best model based on: accuracy (MAPE, RMSE), interpretability, computational cost, robustness; (e) document model architecture, hyperparameters, and performance metrics; (f) Business Domain Owner reviews model logic for business plausibility | Data Scientist | BI Manager | 2–6 weeks |
| 4 | **Pilot deployment**: (a) deploy model in pilot scope: 1–2 product categories for demand sensing, or 1 region for churn prediction; (b) run model alongside existing process for 4–8 weeks; (c) compare model predictions to actual outcomes; (d) measure business impact: forecast accuracy improvement, churn reduction, inventory risk identification lead time; (e) iterate on model based on pilot findings; (f) Business Domain Owner approves for production based on pilot results | Data Scientist / Data Engineer / BI Manager | Business Domain Owner | 4–8 weeks |
| 5 | **Production deployment**: (a) Data Engineer integrates model into production data pipeline: scheduled batch scoring (nightly for demand sensing, weekly for churn, monthly for inventory risk); (b) IT Operations deploys model infrastructure (cloud compute, API endpoints); (c) integrate model outputs into existing workflows: demand forecast feed to W31 S&OP, churn scores to W660 retention program, inventory risk scores to W587 obsolescence review; (d) build monitoring dashboard showing model predictions, accuracy, and drift metrics | Data Engineer / IT Operations | BI Manager | 2–4 weeks |
| 6 | **Ongoing model monitoring**: Continuous monitoring: (a) **Prediction accuracy**: track MAPE/RMSE weekly; alert if accuracy degrades > 20% from baseline; (b) **Data drift**: monitor input feature distributions for shifts (e.g., sales pattern change after new competitor enters market); (c) **Concept drift**: monitor if relationship between features and target changes (e.g., economic downturn changes price sensitivity); (d) **Business KPI impact**: track if model-driven decisions actually improve business outcomes; (e) monthly model health report to BI Manager | Data Scientist / System | BI Manager | 2 hours/week |
| 7 | **Model retraining & retirement**: (a) quarterly: retrain models with latest data to incorporate new patterns; (b) if retrained model significantly outperforms production model: promote to production; (c) if model consistently underperforms baseline: retire model and revert to existing process; (d) document model version history and performance trajectory | Data Scientist | BI Manager | 1 day/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Data warehouse per W881 for training data extraction
- ML platform (cloud-based: AWS SageMaker, Azure ML, or GCP Vertex AI) for model training, deployment, and monitoring
- Feature store for reusable predictive variables
- Integration with W31 demand forecasting, W660 retention, W587 obsolescence, W44 vendor scorecard
- Model monitoring dashboard with drift detection
- BI platform for presenting model insights to business users

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Data quality dependency**: models are only as good as their training data; data quality issues per W884 directly impact model accuracy; demand sensing for provincial stores may be unreliable due to sparse transaction data
- **Black box perception**: business users may distrust model recommendations they don't understand; interpretability features (SHAP values, feature importance) essential for adoption
- **Overfitting risk**: complex models may fit historical patterns that don't repeat; walk-forward validation mitigates but doesn't eliminate
- **Change management**: replacing intuitive merchandising decisions with model-driven recommendations requires cultural shift; Category Manager buy-in critical
- **Computational cost**: production models require cloud compute resources; monthly cost can reach PHP 200,000–500,000 for complex models at BuildRight's scale

### Staffing Implication
- **Data Scientist**: 1 FTE dedicated to predictive analytics development and monitoring; ~80% of time on model lifecycle
- **Data Engineer**: model deployment and pipeline integration ~20% of time; absorbed by existing role
- **BI Manager**: use case prioritization and business stakeholder management ~10% of time; absorbed by existing role

### Time Estimate
- Per model: ideation 1 day + data prep 2–4 weeks + development 2–6 weeks + pilot 4–8 weeks + production 2–4 weeks = **3–6 months**
- Ongoing monitoring: 2 hours/week per production model
- Quarterly retraining: 1 day/model
- **Annual estimate: ~2,000–3,000 hours/year for 4–6 model projects + monitoring**
