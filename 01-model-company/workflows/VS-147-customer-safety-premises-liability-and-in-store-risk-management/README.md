# VS-147: Customer Safety, Premises Liability & In-Store Risk Management

> **Governance & Assurance** · [Value Stream Index](../value-stream-index.md)

---

## Overview

Customer Safety, Premises Liability & In-Store Risk Management workflows for BuildRight Depot
Corp. — owning the **customer-facing safety and premises-liability discipline** across BuildRight's
~205 high-traffic public sites: 200 big-box stores (8,000–15,000 sqm each, selling-area + lumber
yard + tile gallery + parking lot), 4 DCs with customer will-call counters, and ecommerce/COD
doorstep contact. At 2.8M monthly POS transactions and ~5,800 store staff + customers + trade
pros + contractors sharing the floor with **forklifts, reach trucks, lumber/tile/cement loads,
overhead racking, paint/chemical aisles, and a lumber/building-materials yard**, the customer
premises-liability exposure (slip/trip/fall, falling/shifted merchandise, powered-equipment
contact in the sales area, parking-lot incidents, chemical exposure, crowd/queue crush at sale
events, child/PWD harm) is structurally one of BuildRight's largest and most litigation-exposed
operational risks.

Today this discipline is **unowned as a program**. "Premises liability", "customer safety",
"falling merchandise", "aisle safety", and "forklift-in-sales-area" appear in **zero** PA files as
dedicated workflow headers (only scattered single-step mentions). The capability is sprinkled
inside VS-24 (Health, Safety & Environment — which owns **employee/occupational** OSH, not the
*customer*-side liability program), VS-23 (Loss Prevention — owns shrink/theft, not bodily-injury
liability), VS-07 (Store Operations — owns self-reported housekeeping, not the premises-liability
*program*), VS-138 (Integrated Facilities Management — owns the *building*, not the
customer-safety *operating discipline*), and VS-20/VS-109 (site design as a *project*, not the
in-life safety *program*). No value stream owns the end-to-end customer-safety & premises-
liability program: the hazard register & risk assessment, the zone-specific safety standards
(sales floor, lumber/tile yard, parking lot, chemical aisle), powered-equipment-in-sales-area
controls, customer incident response & investigation, injury/claims handling, corrective action,
regulatory notification (BFP Fire Code, DOLE, LGU, Consumer Act RA 7394), and safety analytics.

This is distinct from **VS-24 (HSE)** which owns *employee* occupational health & safety — this
value stream owns the *customer/non-employee* on-premises safety and the premises-liability
exposure that follows. It is distinct from **VS-23 (Loss Prevention)** which targets *asset
shrink/theft* — this value stream targets *customer bodily-injury and property-damage liability*.
It is distinct from **VS-07 (Store Operations)** which executes *self-reported* housekeeping
walks — this value stream owns the *risk-assessed, audited, claims-managed* safety program. It is
distinct from **VS-138 (IFM)** which maintains the *building/equipment* — this value stream owns
the *operating discipline* that keeps customers safe *around* those buildings, the powered
equipment moving through selling aisles, and the merchandise stacked overhead. It is distinct from
**VS-26 (BCP & Insurance)** which provides *insurance/risk-financing* — this value stream owns the
*loss-prevention-by-design* program that keeps the loss from happening (and feeds VS-26 the
incident data).

BuildRight's exposure is structural: a single documented customer injury at a lumber yard or a
falling-stock incident can cost PHP hundreds of thousands to millions in a premises-liability
claim, drive Consumer Act / BFP / LGU scrutiny, and damage the "Your Home Building Partner"
reputation. The program also carries the powered-equipment-in-aisle risk unique to home-
improvement big-box retail (contractors and customers sharing aisles with forklifts moving
cement/tile/lumber pallets during trading hours) and the chemical-exposure dimension (paint,
solvents, thinners, adhesives accessible to customers).
