## W1862. Monthly Rent Payment Processing & Scheduling

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly rent due date per lease agreement (typically 1st or 5th of month) |
| **Frequency** | ~205 rent payments/month |
| **Volume** | ~205 active leases × 12 months = ~2,460 payments/year |
| **Owner** | AP Clerk |
| **Participants** | AP Clerk, Lease Administrator, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System generates monthly rent payment schedule: (a) list of all active leases with rent amount due, payment due date, landlord payment details, (b) system compares rent amount to lease schedule (verifies no missed escalations), (c) AP Clerk reviews payment schedule for accuracy | System / AP Clerk | Finance Manager | 1–2 hours/month |
| 2 | Landlord issues billing/invoice: (a) some landlords send monthly invoices; others rely on auto-payment per lease terms, (b) AP Clerk matches landlord invoice to lease schedule: (i) base rent matches scheduled amount (including current escalation), (ii) CAM charges match agreed rates or reconciliation, (iii) no unauthorized charges, (c) if discrepancy: AP Clerk flags to Lease Administrator for investigation | AP Clerk | Lease Administrator | 5–10 min/lease |
| 3 | AP Clerk processes rent payment: (a) payment method per lease: post-dated check (PDC), bank transfer, or auto-debit, (b) for PDC: AP Clerk issues check per W424 (PDC management); PDCs may be issued in batches quarterly or annually, (c) for bank transfer: AP Clerk creates payment in payment run per W7.9, (d) Finance approves payment batch, (e) system posts payment: Dr. Rent Expense (BuildRight Property Management, Inc.) / Cr. Cash | AP Clerk / Finance | Finance Manager | Per W7 payment run |
| 4 | Monthly: Finance reconciles rent payments against lease schedule: (a) total rent paid vs. total rent scheduled, (b) identify any missed payments (risk of late fees or lease default), (c) verify PDC clearing per W1380 (AR PDC) / W424 (AP PDC) | Finance | CFO | 2–4 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Automated rent payment scheduling from lease register (W1862.1)
- Landlord invoice matching to lease schedule (W1862.2)
- Payment processing per W7 (AP) with PDC management per W424 (W1862.3)
- Rent payment reconciliation dashboard (W1862.4)
- Integration with W7 (AP processing), W424 (AP PDC), W89 (bank reconciliation)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **PDC management complexity**: With ~205 leases using PDCs, managing 2,460+ checks per year requires disciplined tracking; uncashed PDCs (landlord forgets to deposit) create reconciliation discrepancies
- **Late payment penalties**: Lease agreements typically include 5–10% late payment penalties; missing a rent due date across even a few leases incurs significant unnecessary cost
- **Unauthorized landlord charges**: Landlords may include unauthorized charges (additional CAM, marketing fund contributions) in invoices; AP Clerk must verify each line item against the lease agreement


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-review of "reviews payment schedule for accuracy" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-send of "send monthly invoices" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "check" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-220 (ensure lease-administration & PFRS 16 integrity.)
- operational: AP Clerk review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Staffing Implication
- **AP Clerk**: Rent payment processing adds ~20–30 hours/month (5–10 min × 205 leases + reconciliation); absorbed by existing AP team
- No incremental headcount

### Time Estimate
- Payment schedule review: 1–2 hours/month
- Invoice matching: ~20–30 hours/month
- Payment processing: per W7 payment run
- Reconciliation: 2–4 hours/month
- Total: ~40–50 hours/month

## W1863. Annual Rent Escalation Execution & Verification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Rent escalation effective date per lease (typically anniversary of lease commencement) |
| **Frequency** | ~150–180 escalations/year (most leases have annual escalation; some biennial) |
| **Volume** | 1 escalation per lease per occurrence |
| **Owner** | Lease Administrator |
| **Participants** | Lease Administrator, AP Clerk, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System alerts Lease Administrator 90 days before escalation effective date: (a) lease number, current rent, escalation method (fixed %, CPI-linked, or fixed amount), new rent amount (system-calculated per escalation formula in lease), (b) Lease Administrator verifies system calculation against lease agreement: (i) fixed % escalation: verify percentage matches lease, (ii) CPI-linked: verify CPI index used (Philippine Statistics Authority PSA CPI), base period, and calculation, (iii) fixed amount: verify step-up schedule, (c) if system calculation matches lease: approve escalation in system | Lease Administrator | Finance Manager | 15–30 min/escalation |
| 2 | If landlord disputes escalation amount: (a) Lease Administrator reviews lease escalation clause with Legal Counsel, (b) if CPI-linked escalation: provide landlord with PSA CPI documentation supporting BuildRight's calculation, (c) if fixed % escalation: reference lease agreement, (d) if discrepancy from landlord error: formally dispute in writing per W1858, (e) escalate to Legal Counsel if unresolved within 30 days | Lease Administrator / Legal | VP Real Estate | 1–2 hours/dispute |
| 3 | Finance updates PFRS 16 lease liability for revised rent schedule: (a) remeasure lease liability using revised cash flows and current discount rate, (b) adjust ROU asset for difference, (c) post journal entry for PFRS 16 remeasurement | Finance | CFO | Per lease remeasurement |
| 4 | AP Clerk updates monthly rent payment schedule with new escalated amount effective from escalation date | AP Clerk | Finance Manager | 5 min/escalation |

### System Touchpoints
- Automated escalation alerting with 90-day advance notice (W1863.1)
- Escalation calculation engine: fixed %, CPI-linked, or fixed amount (W1863.1a)
- PFRS 16 lease liability remeasurement upon escalation (W1863.3)
- Rent payment schedule auto-update upon escalation approval (W1863.4)
- Integration with W275 (PFRS 16), W1862 (rent payment processing)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **CPI calculation disputes**: CPI-linked escalations can be disputed if landlord uses a different CPI base period or index; clear escalation clause drafting at lease signing (W1854) prevents disputes
- **Missed escalations**: If the system alert is not actioned, BuildRight continues paying old rent amount, underpaying the landlord and creating a catch-up liability; automated escalation with mandatory review mitigates this


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "verify percentage matches lease" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "documentation supporting BuildRight's calculation" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-count of "discount rate" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-368 (ensure controlled execution — Rent Payment, Escalation & CAM Reconciliation (PA-42.2).)
- operational: Lease Administrator review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **Lease Administrator**: Escalation verification adds ~15–30 min × 150–180/year = ~40–90 hours/year — absorbed

### Time Estimate
- Verification: 15–30 min/escalation
- Dispute resolution: 1–2 hours/dispute (occasional)
- PFRS 16 update: per accounting process
- Total: ~60–120 hours/year

## W1864. Common Area Maintenance (CAM) Charge Reconciliation & Dispute Resolution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual CAM reconciliation statement received from landlord; monthly CAM charges in rent invoice |
| **Frequency** | Annual reconciliation per lease; monthly CAM charges in ~100–150 leases (properties in multi-tenant developments) |
| **Volume** | ~100–150 annual CAM reconciliation reviews |
| **Owner** | Lease Administrator |
| **Participants** | Lease Administrator, Finance, VP Real Estate, Legal Counsel |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monthly: AP Clerk pays CAM charges as billed by landlord: (a) monthly CAM charges are typically estimated amounts based on pro-rata share of common area costs, (b) AP Clerk verifies monthly charge matches lease terms: (i) pro-rata share percentage, (ii) agreed cap on annual CAM increase (if negotiated), (iii) excluded charges (capital improvements, landlord's own administrative overhead) | AP Clerk | Lease Administrator | 5 min/lease |
| 2 | Annual: Landlord provides CAM reconciliation statement: (a) actual CAM expenses for the year vs. estimated charges billed monthly, (b) pro-rata allocation to each tenant based on leased area, (c) supporting documentation: invoices for common area expenses (electricity, water, cleaning, security, elevator maintenance, parking maintenance, property management fee), (d) reconciliation result: (i) if actual < estimated: landlord refunds overpayment or credits against future rent, (ii) if actual > estimated: tenant pays shortfall | Landlord / Lease Administrator | VP Real Estate | 1–2 hours/lease |
| 3 | Lease Administrator reviews CAM reconciliation: (a) verify pro-rata share calculation against lease terms and actual leased area, (b) review expense categories: are all charges covered under the lease definition of "common area maintenance"? Flag any capital improvement charges that should be excluded (landlord's responsibility, not tenants'), (c) compare CAM cost per sqm to market benchmarks: is the landlord overcharging relative to comparable properties? (d) verify landlord's property management fee is within lease-specified cap (typically 5–10% of CAM costs), (e) if reconciliation appears reasonable: approve for payment/credit, (f) if discrepancies found: document and dispute per step 4 | Lease Administrator | VP Real Estate | 1–2 hours/lease |
| 4 | CAM dispute resolution: (a) Lease Administrator submits written dispute to landlord with specific line-item objections and supporting documentation, (b) negotiate resolution: landlord may agree to adjust, provide additional documentation, or maintain original charges, (c) if dispute unresolved within 30 days: escalate to Legal Counsel for formal demand letter, (d) if landlord refuses reasonable adjustment: VP Real Estate considers at lease renewal (W1856) — unresolved CAM disputes are leverage for rent negotiation | Lease Administrator / Legal | VP Real Estate | 2–8 hours/dispute |

### System Touchpoints
- CAM charge tracking per lease with pro-rata share calculation (W1864.1)
- CAM reconciliation review module with line-item verification (W1864.3)
- CAM dispute tracking with escalation workflow (W1864.4)
- Integration with W1862 (rent payment), W1858 (landlord communication)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **CAM overcharging**: Landlords in multi-tenant developments may inflate CAM charges by including capital improvements, landlord administrative overhead, or costs for vacant units; without detailed annual reconciliation review, BuildRight systematically overpays
- **Lack of documentation**: Some landlords provide minimal documentation for CAM expenses (lump-sum amounts without invoices); insist on supporting documentation per lease terms


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-match of "matches lease terms" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "documentation" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews CAM reconciliation" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-220 (ensure lease-administration & PFRS 16 integrity.)
- operational: Lease Administrator review and approval gate; operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Staffing Implication
- **Lease Administrator**: CAM reconciliation adds ~100–150 reviews × 1–2 hours = ~100–300 hours concentrated in 2–3 months after year-end — this is a significant workload spike; may require temporary support during reconciliation season

### Time Estimate
- Monthly verification: ~5 min/lease × 100–150 = ~10–15 hours/month
- Annual reconciliation: ~1–2 hours/lease × 100–150 = ~100–300 hours
- Dispute resolution: ~2–8 hours × 20–30 disputed reconciliations = ~40–240 hours
- Total: ~300–600 hours/year (concentrated in Q1–Q2)

## W1865. Percentage Rent Calculation & Payment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual sales threshold exceeded per percentage rent clause in lease agreement |
| **Frequency** | Annual calculation per lease with percentage rent clause |
| **Volume** | ~50–80 leases include percentage rent clauses (common in mall-based and mixed-use developments) |
| **Owner** | FP&A |
| **Participants** | FP&A, Lease Administrator, Finance, VP Real Estate |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FP&A calculates annual store sales per location: (a) total gross sales from POS and ecommerce data for the store location, (b) apply exclusions per lease: (i) sales tax (VAT) excluded, (ii) returns and refunds excluded, (iii) employee discount sales may be excluded (per lease terms), (iv) gift card sales excluded until redemption, (c) calculate adjusted gross sales for percentage rent purposes | FP&A | Finance Manager | 30 min/store |
| 2 | FP&A calculates percentage rent liability per lease: (a) compare adjusted gross sales to breakpoint (sales threshold above which percentage rent applies): natural breakpoint (where % rent = base rent) or artificial breakpoint (specified dollar amount), (b) if sales exceed breakpoint: percentage rent = (adjusted gross sales − breakpoint) × percentage rate (typically 2–6% for retail leases), (c) if sales below breakpoint: no additional percentage rent owed, (d) FP&A prepares percentage rent calculation with supporting sales data for each applicable lease | FP&A | CFO | 1–2 days |
| 3 | Lease Administrator submits sales certification to landlord: (a) some leases require BuildRight to certify annual sales to landlord, (b) if percentage rent owed: Lease Administrator coordinates payment with Finance, (c) if landlord disputes sales figure: provide detailed POS transaction data as supporting documentation | Lease Administrator | VP Real Estate | 1–2 days |
| 4 | Finance processes percentage rent payment: (a) accrue percentage rent liability monthly based on sales-to-date trend (avoid large year-end surprise), (b) actual payment made after annual calculation completed, (c) Dr. Rent Expense / Cr. Cash | Finance | CFO | Per AP process |

### System Touchpoints
- Store sales analytics module with percentage rent calculation engine (W1865.1)
- Breakpoint and percentage rate configuration per lease (W1865.2)
- Sales certification report generation for landlord submission (W1865.3)
- Percentage rent accrual automation (W1865.4a)
- Integration with W35 (financial reporting), W67 (store performance), W275 (PFRS 16 — variable lease payments)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Sales reporting disputes**: Landlords may dispute BuildRight's sales certification if they believe actual sales are higher (e.g., observed high foot traffic); clear lease language on sales definition and audit rights prevents disputes
- **Accrual timing**: Without monthly accruals, year-end percentage rent payments can be unexpectedly large (PHP 500K–2M per store for high-performing locations); monthly accrual based on YTD sales trend smooths P&L impact


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "discount sales may be excluded" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-calculate of "calculates percentage rent liability per lease" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "documentation" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-220 (ensure lease-administration & PFRS 16 integrity.)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- **FP&A**: Percentage rent calculation adds ~1–2 days/year — absorbed
- No incremental headcount

### Time Estimate
- Sales calculation: 30 min/store × 50–80 = ~25–40 hours
- Percentage rent calculation: 1–2 days
- Landlord submission: 1–2 days
- Total: ~60–80 hours/year concentrated in January–February

## W1866. Rent Abatement & Concession Tracking

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Lease clause providing rent abatement (e.g., first 3 months rent-free during fit-out); force majeure event (typhoon damage); negotiated concession |
| **Frequency** | Occasional — ~5–10 abatement events/year |
| **Volume** | Per lease |
| **Owner** | Lease Administrator |
| **Participants** | Lease Administrator, VP Real Estate, Finance, Legal Counsel |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lease Administrator identifies abatement eligibility: (a) rent-free period during tenant fit-out (typically 3–6 months for new leases), (b) rent reduction during building damage or landlord-caused disruption (e.g., roof repair blocking sales floor), (c) force majeure abatement per lease clause (typhoon, earthquake rendering premises unusable), (d) negotiated concession (e.g., rent reduction agreed during COVID-19 or economic downturn) | Lease Administrator | VP Real Estate | 1–2 hours |
| 2 | Lease Administrator confirms abatement with landlord in writing: (a) specify abatement period, amount, and conditions, (b) obtain landlord written acknowledgment, (c) if force majeure: document event impact with photographs, insurance claims, and store closure records | Lease Administrator | Legal Counsel | 1–2 days |
| 3 | Finance processes abatement: (a) skip or reduce rent payment for abatement period, (b) if already paid: process rent credit from landlord against future rent, (c) PFRS 16 impact: remeasure lease liability if abatement changes lease cash flows, (d) track abatement value for lease negotiation leverage at renewal | Finance | CFO | Per accounting process |

### System Touchpoints
- Abatement tracking per lease with period and amount (W1866.1)
- Rent payment adjustment for abatement periods (W1866.3a)
- PFRS 16 remeasurement for changed cash flows (W1866.3c)
- Integration with W1862 (rent payment), W275 (PFRS 16), W26.2 (crisis response — force majeure)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Abatement documentation**: If abatement is not documented in writing and acknowledged by landlord, landlord may later claim full rent was owed; all abatements must be confirmed in writing
- **PFRS 16 complexity**: Rent abatements that change the pattern of lease payments trigger remeasurement under PFRS 16; Finance must account for the revised lease liability


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "records" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-track of "track abatement value for lease negotiation leverage at renewal" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-368 (ensure controlled execution — Rent Payment, Escalation & CAM Reconciliation (PA-42.2).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by existing Lease Administrator and Finance roles

### Time Estimate
- Per abatement event: ~3–8 hours active time; ~1–2 weeks elapsed for documentation

## W1867. Security Deposit Management & Return Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New lease execution (deposit collection); lease termination (deposit return) |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 new deposits/year; ~2–5 deposit returns/year |
| **Volume** | ~205 active security deposits on file |
| **Owner** | Lease Administrator |
| **Participants** | Lease Administrator, Finance, Legal Counsel, Landlord |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | At lease execution: Finance processes security deposit payment to landlord: (a) amount per lease terms (typically 2–3 months' rent), (b) payment method: cash, check, or bank guarantee, (c) record deposit in security deposit tracking module: lease number, amount, payment date, expected return conditions | Finance | CFO | Per payment process |
| 2 | Ongoing: Lease Administrator monitors security deposit adequacy: (a) some leases require deposit top-up if rent escalates and deposit is expressed as months' rent (deposit = 3 months' rent; after escalation, deposit should increase to reflect new rent level), (b) if top-up required: Lease Administrator coordinates with Finance | Lease Administrator | Finance | Annual review |
| 3 | At lease termination: (a) VP Real Estate coordinates property handover with landlord per W1857; document property condition with photographs, (b) Lease Administrator formally requests security deposit return from landlord within 30 days of lease termination per lease terms, (c) landlord may deduct: (i) unpaid rent or CAM charges, (ii) property restoration costs beyond fair wear and tear, (iii) other lease violations, (d) Lease Administrator reviews landlord's deduction claim: (i) verify each deduction against lease terms, (ii) if deductions are excessive or unjustified: dispute per W1858 landlord communication, (e) Finance processes returned deposit: Dr. Cash / Cr. Security Deposit Receivable | Lease Administrator / Finance | VP Real Estate | 2–4 weeks |
| 4 | If landlord fails to return deposit within agreed period (typically 30–60 days post-termination): (a) Lease Administrator sends formal demand letter, (b) if no response: Legal Counsel issues legal demand, (c) legal action as last resort for significant deposit amounts | Legal Counsel | VP Legal | Variable |

### System Touchpoints
- Security deposit tracking register: lease, amount, payment date, return conditions (W1867.1c)
- Deposit adequacy monitoring linked to rent escalation schedule (W1867.2)
- Deposit return request and deduction review workflow (W1867.3)
- Integration with W1857 (lease termination), W1858 (landlord communication)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Deposit retention disputes**: Landlords frequently claim excessive restoration costs to retain deposits; photographic evidence at move-in and move-out is the best defense
- **Deposit amount risk**: At PHP 500K–3M per lease (2–3 months' rent), total security deposits across 205 leases = PHP 100M–600M; any landlord default on deposit return is a significant financial loss; mitigated by the Lease Administrator review and approval gate


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "record deposit in security deposit tracking module" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-escalate of "escalates and deposit is expressed as months' rent" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "document property condition with photographs" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-368 (ensure controlled execution — Rent Payment, Escalation & CAM Reconciliation (PA-42.2).)
- operational: Lease Administrator review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by Lease Administrator and Finance

### Time Estimate
- New deposit processing: ~30 min per lease
- Annual adequacy review: ~2 hours/year
- Return processing: ~2–4 hours per lease termination
- Total: ~30–50 hours/year

## W1868. Multi-Entity Rent Settlement & Intercompany Billing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly rent payment cycle; intercompany settlement per IC agreement |
| **Frequency** | Monthly |
| **Volume** | ~205 leases paid by BuildRight Property Management, Inc.; recharged to BuildRight Depot, Inc. |
| **Owner** | Finance |
| **Participants** | Finance (Property Management entity), Finance (Depot entity), Lease Administrator |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | BuildRight Property Management, Inc. pays rent to landlords per W1862: (a) all lease payments flow through Property Management entity's bank accounts, (b) Property Management entity records rent expense and cash outflow | Finance (Property Mgmt) | CFO (Property Mgmt) | Per W1862 |
| 2 | Monthly: Finance generates intercompany rent recharge invoice: (a) total rent paid by Property Management entity on behalf of Depot entity: base rent + CAM + percentage rent + other lease costs, (b) apply management fee markup per intercompany agreement (if applicable — arm's-length transfer pricing per W235), (c) generate IC invoice from Property Management to Depot, (d) Depot records IC rent expense; Property Management records IC revenue | Finance | Controller | 1 day/month |
| 3 | IC settlement: (a) Property Management and Depot net IC balances monthly per W14 (IC settlement), (b) any markup charged by Property Management is eliminated in consolidation per W9A (month-end close), (c) consolidated financial statements reflect actual rent expense without intercompany margin | Finance | Controller | Per W14 + W9A |
| 4 | Quarterly: Finance reviews IC rent recharge pricing: (a) ensure transfer pricing methodology is documented and defensible per W235, (b) verify markup (if any) is at arm's length and consistent with transfer pricing policy, (c) adjust if BIR audit risk identified | Finance / Tax | CFO | 1 day/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- IC rent recharge invoice generation (W1868.2)
- IC settlement and elimination per W14, W9A (W1868.3)
- Transfer pricing documentation module (W1868.4)
- Integration with W14 (IC transactions), W235 (transfer pricing), W234 (IC elimination), W9A (month-end close), W486 (PAS 24 related party disclosure)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **BIR transfer pricing scrutiny**: IC rent recharges between BuildRight entities are subject to BIR transfer pricing rules; any markup on rent must be justified and documented
- **Cash flow timing mismatch**: Property Management entity pays landlords on due dates (1st of month) but Depot entity may not settle IC recharge until mid-month; Property Management needs adequate working capital or IC cash advance arrangement


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "records rent expense and cash outflow" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-generate of "generates intercompany rent recharge invoice" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "methodology is documented and defensible per W235" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-368 (ensure controlled execution — Rent Payment, Escalation & CAM Reconciliation (PA-42.2).)
- operational: Finance review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by existing Finance team

### Time Estimate
- Monthly recharge processing: 1 day/month
- Quarterly pricing review: 1 day/quarter
- Total: ~16 days/year (12 monthly + 4 quarterly)

## W1869. Landlord Invoice Verification & Payment Exception Handling

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Landlord invoice received with amount different from expected rent schedule |
| **Frequency** | ~20–30 invoice exceptions/month |
| **Volume** | Exceptions include: unauthorized charges, incorrect escalation amounts, duplicate billing, late fees |
| **Owner** | AP Clerk |
| **Participants** | AP Clerk, Lease Administrator, VP Real Estate, Legal Counsel |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AP Clerk identifies invoice exception during rent payment processing: (a) total amount exceeds expected rent schedule by > PHP 1,000 or 2%, (b) new charge line items not in previous months, (c) duplicate invoice for same period, (d) late fee or penalty charge | AP Clerk | Lease Administrator | 5 min/exception |
| 2 | Lease Administrator investigates exception: (a) compare invoice to lease agreement: is the charge authorized?, (b) common unauthorized charges: (i) capital improvement levy, (ii) marketing fund contribution, (iii) property insurance premium increase without notice, (iv) late fee for previously disputed charges, (c) if charge is authorized per lease: approve for payment, (d) if charge is unauthorized: dispute per step 3 | Lease Administrator | VP Real Estate | 15–30 min/exception |
| 3 | Dispute resolution: (a) Lease Administrator sends written dispute to landlord referencing specific lease clause that does not authorize the charge, (b) pay only undisputed portion of invoice on time (avoid late fees on undisputed rent), (c) if landlord insists on payment: escalate to VP Real Estate for negotiation, (d) Legal Counsel involved if landlord threatens lease default over disputed charges | Lease Administrator / Legal | VP Real Estate | 1–2 hours/dispute |
| 4 | Monthly: Lease Administrator reports exception and dispute summary to VP Real Estate: (a) exception frequency by landlord, (b) total disputed amount, (c) resolution rate and average resolution time, (d) chronic offenders flagged for lease renegotiation consideration | Lease Administrator | VP Real Estate | 1 hour/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Invoice matching with configurable tolerance thresholds (W1869.1)
- Exception logging and tracking with SLA (W1869.2)
- Dispute workflow with landlord communication integration (W1869.3)
- Exception analytics by landlord (W1869.4)
- Integration with W1862 (rent payment), W1858 (landlord communication)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Partial payment risk**: Paying only the undisputed portion may still trigger late fee claims from landlords who consider full payment an obligation; clearly document partial payment reason in writing; mitigated by CTL-368 (controlled execution — Rent Payment, Escalation & CAM Reconciliation (PA-42.2))
- **Exception backlog**: 20–30 exceptions/month × 15–30 min = ~5–15 hours/month; if Lease Administrator is overloaded, exceptions accumulate and disputes age


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-schedule of "schedule by > PHP 1,000 or 2%" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approve for payment" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-send of "sends written dispute to landlord referencing specific lease clause that does…" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-368 (ensure controlled execution — Rent Payment, Escalation & CAM Reconciliation (PA-42.2).)
- operational: AP Clerk review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by AP Clerk and Lease Administrator

### Time Estimate
- Exception identification: ~5 min × 20–30 = ~2 hours/month
- Investigation: ~15–30 min × 20–30 = ~5–15 hours/month
- Dispute resolution: ~1–2 hours × 10–15 = ~10–30 hours/month
- Monthly reporting: 1 hour/month
- Total: ~20–50 hours/month
