## W857. Store & DC Property Insurance Claim Filing & Documentation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Property damage event at any store, DC, or HQ — fire, break-in, water damage, equipment failure, or vandalism |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 30–50 property claims per year across 205 locations |
| **Volume** | Average claim value: PHP 50,000–500,000 (minor to moderate damage); major claims (typhoon, fire): PHP 1–10 million |
| **Owner** | Insurance Coordinator / Finance Controller |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, LP Officer, Finance Controller, Insurance Broker, External Adjuster, Legal Counsel |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incident notification — Store Manager reports property damage to Insurance Coordinator within 24 hours; include initial damage description, estimated value, photos, and whether emergency repairs needed | Store Manager | Insurance Coordinator | 1 hour |
| 2 | Emergency mitigation authorization — for time-sensitive damage (water ingress, structural compromise), Insurance Coordinator authorizes emergency repairs up to PHP 100,000 without adjuster inspection; document with photos before and after | Insurance Coordinator | Finance VP | 30 min |
| 3 | Compile claim documentation — (a) detailed damage description with cause, (b) photographic evidence (before/after where available), (c) repair estimates from 2–3 contractors, (d) police report (if theft/vandalism), (e) BFP fire investigation report (if fire), (f) inventory damage assessment with replacement cost | Store Manager / LP Officer | Insurance Coordinator | 4–8 hours |
| 4 | Submit insurance claim to broker — complete insurer claim form, attach all documentation, provide loss amount breakdown (building, equipment, inventory, cleanup); submit within insurer's required timeframe (typically 30 days) | Insurance Coordinator | Finance Controller | 2 hours |
| 5 | Coordinate adjuster inspection — schedule insurance adjuster visit, ensure Store Manager availability, provide access to damaged areas and documentation | Insurance Coordinator | Insurance Broker | 2 hours (scheduling) |
| 6 | Negotiate settlement — review adjuster report, dispute undervaluation if applicable, provide supplementary evidence, and agree on settlement amount with insurer | Insurance Coordinator | Finance Controller | 4–8 hours (over 2–4 weeks) |
| 7 | Process settlement — record insurance recovery in finance module, offset against damage loss, and close claim file | Finance Controller | Finance VP | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- **Finance Module** — claim expense recording, insurance recovery posting, GL impact
- **ERP LP Module** — incident report linking, evidence management
- **Document Management** — claim file storage, photo archive, contractor estimates
- **Inventory Module** — damaged inventory valuation, write-off processing
- **Fixed Asset Module** — building/equipment damage assessment, impairment recording

### Pain Points / Risks
- Claim documentation quality varies significantly by store — provincial stores may lack photographic evidence
- Adjuster availability after major typhoons is limited — claims may take 2–3 months for catastrophic events
- Insurer may dispute cause (wear-and-tear vs. covered peril) — Legal Counsel needed for complex claims
- Emergency repairs must be pre-authorized but delay increases secondary damage — judgment call required
- PHP 100,000 emergency threshold may be insufficient for major water damage at large stores

### Staffing Implication
- 1 FTE Insurance Coordinator (reports to Finance Controller)
- Store Managers: 4–8 hours per claim for documentation
- External: insurance broker + adjuster per claim

### Time Estimate
- Per claim: 15–25 person-hours over 4–8 weeks
- **Annual estimate: 40 claims × 20 hours = 800 person-hours/year**

## W858. Typhoon, Flood & Natural Disaster Damage Assessment & Insurance Claim

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Natural disaster event (typhoon, flood, earthquake, volcanic eruption) affecting BuildRight locations; triggered by W848 post-disaster rapid assessment |
| **Frequency** | 2–3 catastrophic events per year affecting multiple locations simultaneously |
| **Volume** | Each event may generate 10–50 property claims; catastrophic event (Signal No. 4+) may affect 30–80 stores |
| **Owner** | Insurance Coordinator / BC Manager |
| **Participants** | VP Operations, Finance VP, Store Managers (affected), LP Officers, External Adjuster Team, Insurance Broker, Legal Counsel, Structural Engineer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Aggregate damage reports from all affected stores — compile W848 rapid assessment results into consolidated damage register; categorize by severity (minor: operational within 24h, moderate: 1–7 days, major: > 7 days or structural concern) | BC Manager | VP Operations | 4–8 hours |
| 2 | Prioritize claim filing order — major structural damage first, then moderate inventory damage, then minor; estimate aggregate claim value and notify insurer of catastrophic event | Insurance Coordinator | Finance VP | 2 hours |
| 3 | Deploy damage assessment teams — for major claims: engage external structural engineer + insurance adjuster; for moderate claims: LP Officer + Store Manager conduct detailed assessment; for minor: Store Manager self-assessment with photo documentation | BC Manager | VP Operations | 1–3 days (field deployment) |
| 4 | Compile catastrophic event claim package — consolidated claim submission to insurer with: (a) event summary (PAGASA data, affected area map), (b) location-by-location damage detail, (c) aggregate loss estimate with supporting valuation, (d) business interruption calculation (per W860), (e) emergency repair documentation | Insurance Coordinator | Finance VP | 8–16 hours |
| 5 | Engage loss adjuster for catastrophic event — insurer appoints catastrophic loss adjuster; coordinate multi-site inspection schedule over 2–4 weeks; provide logistics support (transport, access) | Insurance Broker | Insurance Coordinator | 2–4 weeks (elapsed) |
| 6 | Negotiate catastrophic settlement — typically involves interim payments for emergency repairs + final settlement after full assessment; may require mediation if insurer disputes scope or value | Finance VP | CEO | 4–8 weeks (elapsed) |
| 7 | Process recovery and close claims — record all insurance recoveries, reconcile against actual repair costs, document unrecovered losses, and update property valuations for next renewal | Finance Controller | Finance VP | 2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- **Finance Module** — catastrophic loss tracking, interim recovery posting, GL impact
- **ERP LP Module** — consolidated incident register, evidence repository
- **BI Dashboard** — damage heat map, claim status tracker, financial impact summary
- **Fixed Asset Module** — property impairment recording, revaluation
- **Inventory Module** — mass inventory write-off, replacement cost calculation

### Pain Points / Risks
- Catastrophic events overwhelm insurance adjuster capacity — claim resolution may take 3–6 months
- Business interruption calculation requires detailed sales data comparison (current year vs. prior year) — complex documentation
- Aggregate claim value may exceed policy limits — underinsurance risk for catastrophic events
- Multiple simultaneous claim submissions increase documentation errors and omissions
- Provincial stores may lack contractor availability for repair estimates — delays claim filing
- Insurer may invoke policy deductibles per-location, significantly reducing net recovery

### Staffing Implication
- Insurance Coordinator: full-time during catastrophic event (4–8 weeks)
- Finance VP: 20% of time during catastrophic event settlement
- BC Manager: coordinates damage assessment teams
- External: structural engineer + loss adjuster + broker (all engaged per event)

### Time Estimate
- Per catastrophic event: 200–400 person-hours over 2–6 months
- **Annual estimate: 2.5 events × 300 hours = 750 person-hours/year**

## W859. Vehicle & Fleet Insurance Claim Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vehicle accident, theft, or damage involving company-owned fleet vehicles (delivery trucks, service vehicles, management vehicles) |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 20–30 vehicle claims per year for fleet of ~80 vehicles |
| **Volume** | Average claim value: PHP 50,000–300,000 (repair); total loss: PHP 500,000–3,000,000 |
| **Owner** | Fleet Manager / Insurance Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Driver, Fleet Manager, Insurance Coordinator, Finance Controller, External Repair Shop, LTO (Land Transportation Office) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incident report — driver reports vehicle incident to Fleet Manager within 4 hours; include police report (if third-party involved), photos of damage, location, and circumstances | Driver | Fleet Manager | 1 hour |
| 2 | Vehicle assessment — Fleet Manager arranges vehicle inspection at authorized repair shop; obtain repair estimate; classify as repairable or total loss | Fleet Manager | Insurance Coordinator | 1–3 days (elapsed) |
| 3 | Submit insurance claim — complete vehicle claim form with: (a) police report or barangay blotter, (b) driver's license and vehicle registration, (c) repair estimate or total loss valuation, (d) photos, (e) third-party information (if applicable) | Insurance Coordinator | Fleet Manager | 2 hours |
| 4 | Coordinate repair authorization — insurer approves repair scope and cost; Fleet Manager authorizes repair shop to proceed; arrange replacement vehicle if needed for delivery schedule | Fleet Manager | Insurance Coordinator | 3–5 days (elapsed) |
| 5 | Third-party liability handling — if company vehicle at fault, coordinate with insurer for third-party claim settlement; if third-party at fault, pursue recovery through their insurer | Insurance Coordinator | Legal Counsel | Variable (2–12 weeks) |
| 6 | Process claim settlement — record insurance recovery, post repair expense, and close claim file; update vehicle asset record | Finance Controller | Insurance Coordinator | 2 hours |
| 7 | Driver incident review — Fleet Manager reviews incident with driver; determine preventability; if preventable, apply Fleet safety program consequence (retraining, warning, or suspension) | Fleet Manager | Supply Chain Director | 1 hour |

### System Touchpoints
- **Fleet Management Module** — vehicle record, incident log, maintenance tracking
- **Finance Module** — claim expense, insurance recovery, vehicle asset adjustment
- **Insurance Module** — claim filing, status tracking, settlement recording
- **HR System** — driver incident record, disciplinary action (if applicable)
- **WMS/Delivery Module** — delivery rescheduling during vehicle downtime

### Pain Points / Risks
- Vehicle downtime affects delivery schedule — replacement vehicle cost is often unrecoverable
- Third-party claims in Philippines are slow — court system backlog means 1–3 year resolution
- Driver may be unlicensed or under the influence — policy may not cover; pre-employment verification critical
- Provincial repair shops may not meet insurer's authorized network — requires exception approval
- Total loss settlement may be below vehicle replacement cost — gap coverage recommended

### Staffing Implication
- Fleet Manager: 2–4 hours per claim
- Insurance Coordinator: 2 hours per claim
- Finance Controller: 1 hour per claim

### Time Estimate
- Per claim: 8–15 person-hours over 2–8 weeks
- **Annual estimate: 25 claims × 12 hours = 300 person-hours/year**

## W860. Business Interruption Insurance Claim & Loss Documentation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Store or DC closure exceeding 72 consecutive hours due to insured peril; typically triggered alongside W858 (catastrophic property claim) |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 3–5 business interruption claims per year |
| **Volume** | Average claim: PHP 500,000–5,000,000; catastrophic: PHP 10–50 million (multi-store closure) |
| **Owner** | Finance Controller / Insurance Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Finance VP, BC Manager, Store Manager, External Accountant, Insurance Broker, Loss Adjuster |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify BI-qualifying events — track all store/DC closures exceeding 72 hours; calculate estimated daily sales loss per location using rolling 12-month average; flag for BI claim consideration | Finance Analyst | Finance Controller | 2 hours per event |
| 2 | Compile BI documentation package — (a) daily sales comparison (closure period vs. same period prior year), (b) payroll expense during closure (continuing costs), (c) fixed costs (rent, utilities, depreciation), (d) mitigation actions taken (BOPIS, curbside pickup), (e) saved variable costs (reduced COGS, lower utility), (f) projected recovery period sales ramp-up curve | Finance Analyst | Finance Controller | 8–16 hours |
| 3 | Engage forensic accountant (for claims > PHP 5 million) — independent verification of loss calculation; adjust for trends, seasonality, and market conditions; prepare accountants' report for insurer | External Accountant | Finance VP | 1–2 weeks (elapsed) |
| 4 | Submit BI claim to insurer — include property claim reference (W858), detailed loss calculation, supporting financial statements, and projected recovery timeline | Insurance Coordinator | Finance Controller | 4 hours |
| 5 | Negotiate BI settlement — insurer reviews loss calculation methodology, may dispute: (a) base period selection, (b) trend adjustments, (c) saved expense calculations, (d) recovery period assumption; typically requires 2–3 rounds of negotiation | Finance Controller | Finance VP | 4–8 weeks (elapsed) |
| 6 | Process BI recovery — record insurance recovery as other income; reconcile against actual lost profit; close BI claim file | Finance Controller | Finance VP | 2 hours |
| 7 | Update BI loss history — maintain claims database for actuarial analysis at next policy renewal; identify patterns (geographic, seasonal) for risk management | Insurance Coordinator | Finance VP | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- **Finance Module** — sales history, expense analysis, BI loss calculation
- **BI Analytics** — daily sales trend, same-store comparison, recovery curve modeling
- **ERP Communication Module** — closure duration tracking, reopening date confirmation
- **Document Management** — claim file archive, forensic accountant report
- **Insurance Module** — claim tracking, settlement recording

### Pain Points / Risks
- BI claims are the most disputed type — insurers challenge loss calculation methodology extensively
- Saved expense calculation is inherently judgmental — requires forensic accountant for larger claims
- Recovery period assumption is subjective — insurer assumes faster recovery than business projects
- BI policy waiting period (typically 72 hours) means first 3 days are uninsured
- Claims > PHP 10 million require CFO and potentially Board involvement in settlement negotiation
- Policy may have aggregate annual limit — multiple BI claims may exhaust coverage

### Staffing Implication
- Finance Controller: 30% of time during active BI claim
- Finance Analyst: dedicated to BI calculation for duration
- External forensic accountant: engaged per claim (PHP 200,000–500,000 fee)

### Time Estimate
- Per claim: 40–80 person-hours over 2–4 months
- **Annual estimate: 4 claims × 60 hours = 240 person-hours/year**

## W861. Employee Injury Insurance Claim Coordination & SSS/ECC Filing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Workplace injury or occupational illness affecting any BuildRight employee; triggered by HSE incident report |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 100–150 reportable injuries per year across 6,715 employees |
| **Volume** | ~30–50 claims filed with SSS/ECC per year (lost-time injuries); minor injuries treated on-site without claim |
| **Owner** | HR Manager / Insurance Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Injured Employee, Store Manager, HR Manager, Insurance Coordinator, Company Nurse, SSS Representative, DOLE (for serious incidents) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Injury incident documentation — Store Manager completes incident report within 24 hours: nature of injury, body part, cause, witness statements, first aid administered, and medical referral if needed | Store Manager | HR Manager | 1 hour |
| 2 | Determine reportability — HR Manager classifies as: (a) first aid only (no claim), (b) medical treatment (clinic/hospital visit, possible claim), (c) lost-time injury (claim required), (d) serious incident (DOLE notification required within 24 hours per DO 198-18) | HR Manager | HR Director | 30 min |
| 3 | Facilitate medical treatment — arrange company-designated physician evaluation; ensure Employee's Compensation (SSS/ECC) medical requirements are met from initial visit; document all treatment and costs | Company Nurse | HR Manager | Variable (same day) |
| 4 | File SSS sickness/injury notification — submit SSS Form B-302 (Sickness Notification) within employer deadline; for EC claims, submit SSS Form ECC-1.1 (Employees' Compensation Claim) with medical certificates | HR Benefits Specialist | HR Manager | 2 hours |
| 5 | Track employee recovery and return-to-work — monitor medical status, coordinate with HSE (W805) for return-to-work assessment, arrange light duty if applicable, and maintain contact with employee during absence | HR Manager | Store Manager | Ongoing (1–12 weeks) |
| 6 | Process SSS/ECC benefit reimbursement — upon claim approval, verify benefit amount, coordinate with payroll for salary differential (if company pays above SSS benefit), and record in HR system | HR Benefits Specialist | Finance Controller | 2 hours |
| 7 | Claim closure and record update — close incident file, update OSH (Occupational Safety and Health) log, contribute to annual HSE statistics, and identify preventive measures | HR Manager | HSE Officer | 1 hour |

### System Touchpoints
- **HR System** — incident reporting, injury tracking, SSS/ECC claim status
- **HSE Module** — workplace injury log, OSH statistics, root cause analysis
- **Finance Module** — medical expense tracking, salary differential calculation
- **Payroll System** — SSS sickness benefit integration, salary continuation processing
- **SSS Portal** — online claim submission, status inquiry

### Pain Points / Risks
- DOLE notification within 24 hours for serious incidents is a strict requirement — non-compliance results in penalties
- Company-designated physician requirement may conflict with employee's preferred doctor
- SSS/ECC claim processing time is 2–6 months — employee may face financial hardship during wait
- Salary differential calculation (company pays 100% for first X days, then SSS benefit + company differential) is complex
- Workplace injury under-reporting by Store Managers to avoid incident rate KPI impact
- Provincial stores may lack nearby company-accredited medical facilities

### Staffing Implication
- HR Benefits Specialist: 3 hours per claim
- HR Manager: 1 hour per claim
- Company Nurse: on-site at HQ, on-call for provincial stores
- Store Manager: 1 hour per incident for initial documentation

### Time Estimate
- Per claim: 8–12 person-hours over 2–6 months (including waiting time)
- **Annual estimate: 40 claims × 10 hours = 400 person-hours/year**

## W862. Insurance Policy Annual Renewal & Coverage Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — annual renewal cycle (policies typically renewed annually, aligned to company fiscal year) |
| **Frequency** | Annual comprehensive review; quarterly interim adjustments for material changes |
| **Volume** | 1 annual renewal covering ~15–20 insurance policies across all 5 legal entities |
| **Owner** | Insurance Coordinator / Finance VP |
| **Participants** | Finance VP, VP Operations, LP Director, Fleet Manager, HR Director, Insurance Broker, External Actuary |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Compile renewal data package — (a) prior year claims history (frequency, severity, recovery rate), (b) current asset register (buildings, equipment, vehicles), (c) payroll data (for workers' comp), (d) revenue data (for BI and liability limits), (e) any new locations or operations | Insurance Coordinator | Finance VP | 8 hours |
| 2 | Conduct coverage gap analysis — compare current coverage against: (a) new locations opened during year, (b) new vehicle acquisitions, (c) changes in inventory values, (d) new business activities (ecommerce warehouse, installation services), (e) updated risk assessment from BC Manager | Insurance Coordinator | Finance VP | 4 hours |
| 3 | Obtain competitive quotations — engage insurance broker to obtain quotes from 3–5 insurers; compare coverage terms, premiums, deductibles, and exclusions; prepare comparison matrix | Insurance Broker | Finance VP | 2 weeks (elapsed) |
| 4 | Evaluate and negotiate — review quotes with Finance VP and LP Director; negotiate terms with preferred insurer; consider higher deductibles for premium savings on low-frequency risks | Insurance Coordinator | Finance VP | 1 week |
| 5 | Present renewal recommendation to CFO — summarize coverage changes, premium impact (vs. prior year), claims history analysis, and recommendation for policy selection | Finance VP | CFO | 2 hours |
| 6 | Execute renewal — obtain CEO/CFO authorization, sign policy documents, process premium payment, distribute certificates of insurance to all locations | Insurance Coordinator | Finance VP | 4 hours |
| 7 | Update insurance register — record all policy details in insurance tracking system; set renewal reminders; distribute policy summary to all Store Managers and DC Managers | Insurance Coordinator | Finance VP | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- **Finance Module** — premium payment processing, insurance expense tracking
- **Fixed Asset Module** — asset register for coverage verification
- **ERP LP Module** — claims history extraction
- **HR System** — payroll data for workers' comp calculation
- **Document Management** — policy document storage, certificate distribution

### Pain Points / Risks
- Philippine insurance market has limited capacity for large retail risks — few competitive options
- Premium increases of 10–20% per year are common for property insurance in typhoon-prone areas
- Underinsurance is a chronic risk — inventory values fluctuate seasonally; annual valuation may be stale
- New store openings mid-policy require endorsement — coverage gap if not reported promptly
- Flood and earthquake coverage may require separate policies with high deductibles or exclusions

### Staffing Implication
- Insurance Coordinator: 30 hours for annual renewal
- Finance VP: 8 hours for review and approval
- Insurance Broker: manages quotation process
- External Actuary: engaged every 3 years for independent coverage adequacy review

### Time Estimate
- **Annual renewal: ~60 person-hours over 4–6 weeks**

## W863. Third-Party Liability Claim & Customer Incident Insurance Response

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer or third-party injury/property damage on BuildRight premises; or claim/lawsuit received from third party |
| **Frequency** | Estimated 20–30 third-party incidents per year; 5–10 result in formal claims |
| **Volume** | Average claim value: PHP 20,000–200,000; major injury lawsuit: PHP 500,000–5,000,000 |
| **Owner** | Insurance Coordinator / Legal Counsel |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, LP Officer, Insurance Coordinator, Legal Counsel, Insurance Broker, External Lawyer (for litigation) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incident documentation — Store Manager completes third-party incident report within 24 hours: injured party details, incident description, witness statements, photos of scene, first aid provided, and CCTV footage preservation request | Store Manager | LP Manager | 1–2 hours |
| 2 | Notify insurer of potential claim — even if formal claim not yet filed, notify insurer of incident for reservation of rights; include incident report, photos, and CCTV footage reference | Insurance Coordinator | Legal Counsel | 1 hour |
| 3 | Customer follow-up — HR or Store Manager contacts injured party within 48 hours to express concern, offer assistance, and document injury progression; do NOT admit liability or make settlement offers without Legal approval | Store Manager / HR | Legal Counsel | 1 hour |
| 4 | Legal assessment — Legal Counsel reviews incident for liability exposure; determine defense strategy; engage external lawyer if litigation anticipated | Legal Counsel | Insurance Coordinator | 2–4 hours |
| 5 | Formal claim response — if demand letter received, Legal Counsel drafts response (deny liability, offer mediation, or negotiate settlement); coordinate with insurer for settlement authority | Legal Counsel | Finance VP | 4–8 hours |
| 6 | Settlement or litigation — negotiate settlement within insurer's authority (typically < PHP 500,000); for larger claims, defend through litigation; keep insurer informed at every stage | Legal Counsel / External Lawyer | Finance VP | Variable (1–24 months) |
| 7 | Claim closure — record settlement or judgment in finance module; update premises safety measures to prevent recurrence; close incident file | Insurance Coordinator | Finance VP | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- **ERP LP Module** — incident report, evidence management, CCTV footage preservation
- **Finance Module** — settlement payment, legal expense tracking
- **Insurance Module** — claim notification, status tracking, settlement recording
- **Legal Matter Management** — case tracking, deadline management, document repository
- **HSE Module** — premises safety improvement tracking

### Pain Points / Risks
- Social media amplification of customer injury incidents — reputation risk management required
- Store employees may inadvertently admit liability at scene — front-line training critical
- Philippine courts are sympathetic to consumer-plaintiffs — settlement is often more cost-effective than litigation
- Medical cost documentation from claimants may be inflated — requires verification
- Slipping hazard claims (wet floors during rainy season) are the most common and are largely preventable with proper mat placement and signage
- Premises liability is a Store Manager KPI — may discourage incident reporting

### Staffing Implication
- Insurance Coordinator: 4 hours per claim
- Legal Counsel: 8–20 hours per claim (more for litigation)
- External Lawyer: retained for litigation cases
- Store Manager: 2 hours per incident

### Time Estimate
- Per claim: 15–40 person-hours over 1–12 months
- **Annual estimate: 7 claims × 25 hours = 175 person-hours/year**

## W864. Insurance Claim Recovery, Settlement & Accounting Entry

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Insurance claim settlement received from insurer; or final claim denial |
| **Frequency** | 40–60 settlement events per year across all claim types |
| **Volume** | Average settlement: PHP 50,000–500,000; catastrophic: PHP 5–50 million |
| **Owner** | Finance Controller |
| **Participants** | Insurance Coordinator, Finance VP, External Auditor (for material settlements) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Receive settlement offer — Insurance Coordinator receives insurer's settlement offer letter; review against claim amount for acceptance or dispute | Insurance Coordinator | Finance Controller | 1 hour |
| 2 | Verify settlement calculation — Finance Controller reconciles: (a) claim amount vs. offer, (b) deductible application, (c) co-insurance adjustment, (d) underinsurance penalty (average clause), (e) salvage credit (if applicable); calculate net recovery | Finance Controller | Finance VP | 2–4 hours |
| 3 | Approve settlement — for amounts < PHP 500,000: Finance Controller approves; PHP 500,000–5,000,000: Finance VP approves; > PHP 5,000,000: CEO/CFO approves | Finance Controller | Finance VP / CEO | 1 hour |
| 4 | Process settlement payment — record insurance recovery in GL: debit cash/bank, credit insurance recovery (other income) or receivable; offset against original loss entry; process any deductible write-off | Finance Controller | Finance VP | 1 hour |
| 5 | Reconcile claim file — ensure total claim costs (repairs, replacements, lost inventory) equal insurance recovery + deductible + unrecovered balance; post unrecovered balance to appropriate expense account | Finance Controller | Finance VP | 1 hour |
| 6 | Close claim file — update insurance register, archive claim documentation per retention policy (7 years for BIR), and update claims history database for actuarial analysis | Insurance Coordinator | Finance Controller | 1 hour |
| 7 | Report to management — include settlement results in monthly finance report; flag unrecovered amounts; provide input for annual insurance renewal negotiation (W862) | Finance Controller | Finance VP | 30 min |

### System Touchpoints
- **Finance Module** — GL posting, insurance recovery accounting, deductible tracking
- **Insurance Module** — claim status update, settlement recording, claims history
- **Document Management** — settlement letter archive, claim file closure
- **BI Dashboard** — claims recovery rate, unrecovered loss tracking, actuarial data

### Pain Points / Risks
- Settlement may be significantly below claim amount (50–70% recovery is typical for property claims)
- Accounting for underinsurance penalty (average clause) is complex and often disputed with insurer
- Catastrophic event settlements may span multiple fiscal years — accrual accounting required
- Unrecovered losses must be clearly documented for tax deduction purposes (BIR scrutiny on insurance losses)
- External auditor may challenge insurance recovery estimates at year-end — documentation is critical

### Staffing Implication
- Finance Controller: 2–4 hours per settlement
- Insurance Coordinator: 1 hour per settlement
- External Auditor: reviews material settlements during annual audit

### Time Estimate
- Per settlement: 4–8 person-hours
- **Annual estimate: 50 settlements × 6 hours = 300 person-hours/year**
