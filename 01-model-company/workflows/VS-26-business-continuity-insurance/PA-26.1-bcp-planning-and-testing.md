## W847. Business Continuity Plan Annual Review & Update

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — annual (Q1 each year); or ad-hoc triggered by material organizational change (new store format, DC addition, IT platform migration) |
| **Frequency** | Annual formal review; quarterly interim updates for material changes |
| **Volume** | 1 comprehensive plan revision per year covering all 205 locations + HQ |
| **Owner** | Business Continuity Manager / VP Operations |
| **Participants** | IT Director, Finance VP, HR Director, Supply Chain Director, LP Director, Legal Counsel, Department Heads |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Distribute BCP self-assessment questionnaire to all department heads — assess current recovery capabilities, identify new single points of failure, validate RTO/RPO targets, and confirm contact lists | BC Manager | VP Operations | 1 week (elapsed) |
| 2 | Conduct business impact analysis (BIA) update — reassess critical business processes, maximum tolerable downtime, financial impact per hour of outage, and interdependency map for all 5 legal entities | BC Manager | VP Operations | 2 weeks (elapsed) |
| 3 | Review and update IT disaster recovery plan — verify DR site readiness (Clark DC for Luzon, Cebu DC for Visayas, Davao DC for Mindanao), test data replication integrity, update system inventory and recovery sequences | IT Director | VP Operations | 1 week |
| 4 | Update store emergency response procedures — incorporate lessons from prior year incidents, update emergency contact directories (PNP, BFP, Red Cross, LGU), verify emergency supply stock levels | BC Manager | VP Operations | 1 week |
| 5 | Validate communication tree — update all-staff contact database, test SMS blast system, verify satellite phone inventory for areas with unreliable cellular coverage | HR Director | VP Operations | 3 days |
| 6 | Present updated BCP to Executive Committee for approval — key changes, cost implications, identified gaps, and investment recommendations | BC Manager | CEO | 2 hours |
| 7 | Distribute updated BCP materials — digital copies to all Store Managers, physical emergency binders to all 200 stores and 4 DCs, and updated intranet portal | BC Manager | VP Operations | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints
- **ERP Document Management** — BCP version control, distribution tracking
- **HR System** — employee contact database, communication tree
- **IT DR Platform** — replication status dashboard, DR site health monitoring
- **Communication Platform** — SMS blast system, satellite phone inventory
- **BI Dashboard** — prior year incident summary, recovery time actuals vs. targets

### Pain Points / Risks
- BCP review is often deprioritized during peak business periods (Q4 holiday season)
- Store Manager turnover means emergency binder holders may not be current
- IT DR testing is expensive and requires weekend downtime; may be skipped to avoid sales impact
- Philippine telecom infrastructure is vulnerable during disasters — backup channels essential
- Insurance coverage gaps may emerge between annual reviews as operations expand

### Staffing Implication
- 1 FTE Business Continuity Manager (reports to VP Operations)
- Department Heads: ~20 hours each for annual self-assessment
- IT Team: ~40 hours for DR plan update and testing
- External consultant: engaged every 2 years for independent BCP assessment

### Time Estimate
- BC Manager: ~160 hours over 6 weeks (annual)
- Department Heads: ~20 hours each × 15 departments = 300 hours
- IT Team: ~40 hours for DR review
- **Total annual effort: ~500 person-hours**

## W853. Business Continuity Plan Tabletop Exercise & Drill Execution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — annual full exercise; semi-annual tabletop; quarterly IT DR test |
| **Frequency** | 1 full exercise/year, 2 tabletops/year, 4 IT DR tests/year |
| **Volume** | 1 full exercise involving 10–20 key personnel; 2 tabletops involving 8–12 participants each; 4 IT DR tests involving IT team |
| **Owner** | BC Manager |
| **Participants** | Executive Committee, IT Director, VP Operations, HR Director, Finance VP, Supply Chain Director, LP Director, Store Manager representatives |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Design exercise scenario — based on realistic threat (typhoon Signal No. 4 hitting Visayas, 7.2 magnitude earthquake in Mindanao, ransomware attack on ERP, or pandemic resurgence); define inject points and expected responses | BC Manager | VP Operations | 8 hours |
| 2 | Prepare exercise materials — scenario briefing document, inject cards (escalating events), evaluation rubric, participant roles, and communication simulation plan | BC Manager | VP Operations | 4 hours |
| 3 | Conduct tabletop exercise — walk through scenario with Executive Committee and department heads; discuss decision points, communication protocols, resource allocation, and escalation procedures | BC Manager | CEO | 3–4 hours |
| 4 | For full exercise: simulate operational response — activate communication tree (simulated), conduct mock store closure/reopening at 2 volunteer stores, test IT DR failover, and simulate supply chain rerouting | BC Manager | VP Operations | 1 full day |
| 5 | Evaluate exercise performance — assess decision speed, communication effectiveness, plan adequacy, resource availability, and interdepartmental coordination | BC Manager | VP Operations | 2 hours |
| 6 | Document findings and recommendations — identify plan gaps, training needs, resource shortfalls, and system improvements; assign corrective action owners and deadlines | BC Manager | VP Operations | 4 hours |
| 7 | Track corrective action completion — follow up on all identified improvements; verify implementation before next exercise cycle | BC Manager | VP Operations | Ongoing (quarterly review) |

### System Touchpoints
- **ERP Document Management** — exercise materials, findings documentation
- **IT DR Platform** — test failover execution and validation
- **Communication Platform** — simulated notification testing
- **HR System** — staff availability simulation
- **BI Dashboard** — exercise performance metrics

### Pain Points / Risks
- Executive time is scarce — tabletop scheduling requires 3–4 weeks advance notice
- Full exercises are expensive (PHP 1–2 million for operational disruption, IT testing, external evaluators)
- Exercise findings may not be acted on if not tied to accountability and budget
- IT DR testing during business hours risks impacting operations; weekend testing requires overtime budget
- Scenarios may be too theoretical — participants may not take them seriously

### Staffing Implication
- BC Manager: 80 hours per year for exercise design, execution, and follow-up
- Executive Committee: 4 hours per tabletop, 8 hours per full exercise
- IT Team: 16 hours per DR test × 4 tests = 64 hours
- External evaluator: engaged for full exercise (1 day)

### Time Estimate
- Per tabletop: ~40 person-hours total
- Per full exercise: ~200 person-hours total
- Per IT DR test: ~30 person-hours
- **Annual estimate: 2 × 40 + 200 + 4 × 30 = 400 person-hours/year**

## W1204. Store-Level Business Continuity Plan (BCP) Annual Update, Tabletop Exercise & Certification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual BCP review cycle (Q1 each year); or triggered by major incident at any store, new store opening, or significant operational change |
| **Frequency** | Annual per store; 200 stores reviewed on rolling schedule (~40/month over 5 months in Q1–Q2) |
| **Volume** | 200 store-level BCPs + 4 DC-level BCPs + 1 HQ BCP = 205 total |
| **Owner** | Business Continuity Manager |
| **Participants** | BC Manager, Store Manager, VP Store Operations, IT Disaster Recovery Lead, Insurance Coordinator, Regional Manager |

### Background

Each of BuildRight Depot's 200 stores, 4 DCs, and HQ must have an actionable Business Continuity Plan (BCP) that enables rapid response and recovery from disruptions including typhoons, earthquakes, flooding, fire, extended power outage, system failure, and supply chain disruption. While W49 and W158 cover corporate-level disaster response and BCP testing, this workflow governs the annual update and certification of individual location-level BCPs, ensuring each Store Manager knows their specific response procedures, communication chains, and recovery priorities.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **BCP template update**: BC Manager reviews and updates the standardized store-level BCP template: (a) updates contact lists (regional management, emergency services, insurance hotline, ACS contact, bank branch), (b) updates escalation procedures, (c) updates recovery time objectives (RTO): POS operations ≤ 4 hours, full store operations ≤ 24 hours, normal operations ≤ 72 hours, (d) incorporates lessons learned from incidents in the past year, (e) distributes updated template to all Store Managers | BC Manager | VP Store Operations | 8 hours (template update) |
| 2 | **Store Manager BCP review**: Each Store Manager reviews and customizes the BCP template for their specific location: (a) identifies location-specific risks (flood-prone area, earthquake zone, high-crime area), (b) lists local emergency contacts (barangay captain, nearest fire station, nearest hospital, police station), (c) documents alternative operating procedures (reduced POS terminals, manual operations per W580, limited product categories), (d) identifies pre-approved local contractors for emergency repairs, (e) documents cash float emergency procedure if armored car cannot access store | Store Manager | Regional Manager | 2 hours/store |
| 3 | **Tabletop exercise execution**: BC Manager facilitates annual tabletop exercise for each store (can be conducted in batches of 10–15 stores via video conference): (a) presents a scenario (typhoon Signal No. 4, fire in backroom, 48-hour system outage, flooding), (b) Store Manager walks through their BCP response step-by-step, (c) BC Manager evaluates response quality: communication timeliness, escalation appropriateness, resource awareness, customer/safety prioritization, (d) identifies gaps and assigns corrective actions | BC Manager / Store Manager | VP Store Operations | 1 hour/store |
| 4 | **Gap remediation**: For gaps identified in Step 3: (a) Store Manager updates BCP to address gaps within 2 weeks, (b) BC Manager reviews updated BCP and confirms closure, (c) critical gaps (e.g., no emergency power plan, no communication backup) require immediate remediation within 48 hours | Store Manager / BC Manager | Regional Manager | 1 hour/store |
| 5 | **BCP certification**: BC Manager issues annual BCP certification for each location that: (a) BCP is current and location-specific, (b) Store Manager has completed tabletop exercise, (c) all gaps from tabletop exercise are remediated, (d) emergency contact lists are verified; certification status tracked in BCP dashboard; uncertified stores flagged for VP Store Operations attention | BC Manager | VP Store Operations | 15 min/store |
| 6 | **New store BCP onboarding**: For each new store opening (10–15/year): BC Manager conducts dedicated BCP onboarding session with the new Store Manager during the pre-opening period (per W16/W702); tabletop exercise conducted within 30 days of opening; initial BCP certification issued upon successful tabletop completion | BC Manager | VP Store Operations | 3 hours/new store |

### System Touchpoints
- BCP document management system with template distribution and version control (W1204.1)
- Location-specific risk mapping with GIS data (flood zones, fault lines, crime data) (W1204.2)
- Tabletop exercise evaluation scoring and gap tracking (W1204.3–4)
- BCP certification tracking dashboard with status per location (W1204.5)
- New store BCP onboarding checklist integrated with W16/W702 (W1204.6)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Store Manager turnover impact**: with ~15–20% annual turnover, trained Store Managers leave and new ones may not be familiar with their location's BCP; mitigated by BCP onboarding for new Store Managers (W16) and requiring BCP review as part of new SM orientation
- **Tabletop exercise fatigue**: conducting tabletop exercises for 200 stores annually is resource-intensive; mitigated by batch video conference sessions (10–15 stores at a time) and alternating detailed vs. abbreviated exercises for low-risk locations
- **BCP document staleness**: contact lists, procedures, and risk assessments become outdated quickly; mitigated by mandatory annual update in Step 2 and trigger-based updates (staff turnover, phone number changes)
- **Execution gap between plan and reality**: a well-documented BCP does not guarantee effective execution under stress; mitigated by realistic scenario-based tabletop exercises and periodic live drills (W158)

### Staffing Implication
- **BC Manager**: ~20 hours/week during Q1–Q2 (BCP review season) = ~400 hours/year. Absorbed within existing role.
- **Store Managers**: ~3 hours/year each for BCP review and tabletop exercise = ~600 hours/year total across 200 stores. Absorbed within existing duties.
- **VP Store Operations**: ~2 hours/week during BCP review season for certification oversight. Absorbed within existing role.
- **No permanent incremental headcount**.

## W1322. IT Disaster Recovery (DR) Failover Test Execution, Validation & Recovery Time Assessment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly IT DR testing calendar; or ad-hoc triggered by major system incident, IT infrastructure change, or regulatory audit requirement |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly full DR failover test (4×/year); monthly component-level tests for critical modules (POS, inventory) |
| **Volume** | 4 full failover tests + 12 component tests per year |
| **Owner** | IT Director |
| **Participants** | IT Director, Infrastructure Team, Applications Team, IT Security, BC Manager, VP Operations (observer for quarterly tests) |

### Background

BuildRight's 200 stores, 4 DCs, 600 POS terminals, ecommerce platform, and 6,715 users depend on continuous ERP system availability. The model company profile specifies 99.9% uptime for POS and 99.5% for back office. The BCP (W847) includes IT DR planning and W853/158 cover general BCP drills. However, IT disaster recovery testing is a specialized technical exercise distinct from business-level tabletop exercises: it requires actually failing over production systems to the DR site, validating data integrity, measuring recovery time against RTO targets, and failing back. For a retailer processing 2.8M POS transactions/month, even a 4-hour ERP outage during business hours represents ~PHP 700K in lost revenue. This workflow governs the quarterly IT DR failover test — a critical technical assurance activity that validates BuildRight's ability to recover from catastrophic system failure.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pre-test planning**: IT Director prepares DR test plan: (a) selects test scope: full failover (all systems) or component failover (POS only, inventory only, ecommerce only, financials only); (b) defines test window: typically Saturday midnight to Sunday 6 AM to minimize business impact; (c) identifies RTO targets to validate: POS ≤ 4 hours, inventory ≤ 6 hours, ecommerce ≤ 4 hours, financials ≤ 8 hours; (d) defines RPO targets: data loss ≤ 15 minutes (near-real-time replication); (e) notifies all stakeholders: Store Managers, DC Supervisors, ecommerce team, Finance — "system maintenance window" communication; (f) prepares rollback plan in case DR failover fails | IT Director | VP Operations | 4–6 hours |
| 2 | **Pre-failover data snapshot**: Infrastructure Team captures baseline data: (a) record current transaction volumes (POS, ecommerce, AP/AR); (b) take database timestamp snapshot for data integrity comparison; (c) document current system performance metrics (response times, concurrent users, batch job status); (d) verify DR site replication is current: replication lag must be < 5 minutes before proceeding | Infrastructure Team | IT Director | 1 hour |
| 3 | **Failover execution**: Infrastructure Team executes failover: (a) primary site: gracefully stop all application services; (b) activate DR site: (i) promote DR database to primary, (ii) start application services on DR infrastructure, (iii) update DNS/load balancer to route traffic to DR site, (iv) verify network connectivity from stores and DCs to DR site; (c) start timer: record exact failover start time for RTO measurement; (d) monitor system health: CPU, memory, disk I/O, database connections, replication status | Infrastructure Team | IT Director | 2–4 hours |
| 4 | **Functional validation**: Applications Team validates all critical functions at DR site: (a) **POS**: process test transactions at 2–3 volunteer stores (cash sale, card payment, return, price lookup, inventory check); verify near-real-time inventory sync; (b) **Ecommerce**: place test BOPIS order and test delivery order; verify inventory reservation and order confirmation; (c) **Inventory**: verify real-time stock levels match pre-failover snapshot; process test goods receipt and transfer order; (d) **Procurement**: verify PO creation and approval workflow; check vendor portal accessibility; (e) **Finance**: verify GL posting, AP invoice processing, AR statement generation; (f) **HR/Payroll**: verify employee self-service portal and time attendance access; (g) **DC/WMS**: verify RF gun connectivity and pick/pack/ship processing; (h) record any functional failures or degraded performance | Applications Team | IT Director | 2–3 hours |
| 5 | **Performance validation**: Infrastructure Team measures DR site performance against production baselines: (a) POS transaction processing time: target < 3 seconds per transaction per model company profile; (b) report generation: target < 30 seconds for standard reports; (c) concurrent user capacity: verify DR site supports ≥ 500 concurrent users (50% of peak per NFR-028); (d) batch job processing: verify scheduled batch jobs (nightly inventory sync, daily sales posting) execute within normal time windows; (e) database query performance: compare average query response time to production baseline | Infrastructure Team | IT Director | 1 hour |
| 6 | **Data integrity verification**: Applications Team compares post-failover data to pre-failover snapshot: (a) record count comparison for critical tables (inventory, orders, customers, GL entries); (b) verify no data loss: all transactions recorded between last replication and failover are present at DR site; (c) verify referential integrity: no orphaned records or broken cross-references; (d) calculate actual RPO: time difference between last replicated transaction and failover time | Applications Team | IT Director | 1 hour |
| 7 | **Failback execution**: After validation: (a) Infrastructure Team executes failback to primary site: (i) replicate DR site data back to primary, (ii) stop DR site services, (iii) promote primary back to active, (iv) update DNS/load balancer, (v) verify connectivity; (b) verify all systems operational at primary site; (c) resume normal operations; (d) verify replication from primary to DR site resumes normally | Infrastructure Team | IT Director | 2–3 hours |
| 8 | **Post-test report**: IT Director prepares DR test report: (a) test scope and objectives; (b) timeline: planned vs. actual failover and failback times; (c) RTO achievement: actual vs. target for each system; (d) RPO achievement: actual data loss (if any) vs. target; (e) functional test results: pass/fail by system; (f) performance test results: DR vs. production baseline; (g) data integrity verification results; (h) issues encountered: any failures, errors, or unexpected behavior; (i) recommendations: improvements to DR architecture, procedures, or training; (j) report submitted to VP Operations and BC Manager for integration into BCP per W847 | IT Director | VP Operations | 4 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- DR site infrastructure: server compute, storage replication, networking, DNS management (W1322.3, 7)
- Production monitoring dashboard extended to DR site for performance comparison (W1322.5)
- Automated data integrity comparison tool: pre/post-failover record count and transaction verification (W1322.6)
- POS test transaction module for store-level DR validation (W1322.4)
- DR test report template with RTO/RPO tracking and trend analysis (W1322.8)
- Integration with W847 (BCP review), W853 (BCP drill), W695 (IT incident management), W376 (capacity planning)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Failover during test causing actual outage**: if failover fails, stores cannot process transactions until failback completes; mitigated by Saturday midnight test window and rollback plan; worst-case scenario: stores switch to offline POS mode per W580 for up to 4 hours
- **Data replication lag**: if replication lag exceeds RPO target at time of failover, data loss occurs; mitigated by near-real-time synchronous replication for critical databases and pre-failover replication lag check (Step 2)
- **Test fatigue**: quarterly tests at midnight require IT team overtime; mitigated by rotating test responsibilities across Infrastructure Team members
- **Production-like performance at DR**: DR site may be sized for emergency operation (lower spec) but not production-equivalent performance; performance degradation during DR operation is acceptable for short periods but must be documented and communicated to users
- **Failback risk**: replicating data from DR back to primary introduces a second failover risk; mitigated by thorough pre-failback verification and staged approach

### Staffing Implication
- **IT Director**: ~10–12 hours per quarterly test (planning + execution + reporting). Absorbed.
- **Infrastructure Team**: ~8–10 hours per test (evening/weekend). Compensated per overtime policy per W10.
- **Applications Team**: ~4–6 hours per test (functional validation). Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount.**

### Time Estimate
- Pre-test planning: 4–6 hours
- Pre-failover snapshot: 1 hour
- Failover execution: 2–4 hours
- Functional validation: 2–3 hours
- Performance validation: 1 hour
- Data integrity verification: 1 hour
- Failback: 2–3 hours
- Post-test report: 4 hours
- **Per test**: ~17–22 hours total effort; elapsed time: 8–12 hours (midnight to morning)
- **Annual**: ~70–90 hours across 4 quarterly tests + 12 component tests (~24 hours) = ~95–115 hours

## W1323. Supply Chain Disruption Simulation & Alternate Sourcing Activation Drill

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual BCP testing cycle (aligned with W847/W853); or triggered by actual near-miss disruption event (port congestion, supplier bankruptcy, typhoon-induced logistics failure) |
| **Frequency** | Annual full simulation; semi-annual tabletop scenario for supply chain team |
| **Volume** | 1 full simulation + 1 tabletop per year |
| **Owner** | Supply Planning Manager |
| **Participants** | Supply Planning Manager, Category Managers (5), Buyers (10–12), DC Supervisors (4), Logistics Manager, VP Merchandising, VP Operations, Procurement Manager |

### Background

W729 covers actual supply chain disruption rapid response and W852 covers disruption business impact assessment. W847/853 cover general BCP review and tabletop exercises. However, BuildRight's supply chain is uniquely vulnerable due to: (a) Philippine archipelago geography requiring inter-island shipping, (b) 40% import dependency from Asia-Pacific suppliers, (c) 400–600 TEU/month import volume, (d) typhoon season (Jun–Nov) disrupting both port operations and inter-island logistics, (e) concentration risk from top 20 vendors accounting for 45% of COGS. A supply chain disruption simulation tests the organization's ability to activate alternate sourcing, reroute logistics, and maintain store replenishment when primary supply paths fail. Unlike a general BCP tabletop, this simulation involves detailed operational decision-making: which vendors to activate, which DCs to reroute through, how to prioritize limited inventory across 200 stores, and how to communicate with customers about availability.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Scenario design**: Supply Planning Manager designs simulation scenario based on realistic Philippine supply chain risks: (a) **Scenario A — Port congestion**: Manila port congested for 30 days (as occurred in 2014, 2018); import containers delayed; DC3 (Laguna) and DC4 (Clark) unable to receive import shipments; (b) **Scenario B — Primary vendor failure**: top vendor (accounting for 8% of COGS) suddenly ceases operations due to factory fire or financial collapse; 300+ SKUs affected; (c) **Scenario C — Typhoon logistics disruption**: Signal No. 5 typhoon closes Visayas ports for 7 days; DC2 (Cebu) unable to receive or dispatch; 40 Visayas stores cut off from supply; (d) **Scenario D — Raw material shortage**: global cement or steel shortage causes 30% price increase and 50% availability reduction for key construction materials; scenario selected annually by rotation; Supply Planning Manager prepares inject cards with escalating developments over simulated 30-day period | Supply Planning Manager | VP Merchandising | 2 days |
| 2 | **Simulation kickoff**: Supply Planning Manager briefs participants: (a) presents initial scenario inject (e.g., "Day 1: News reports Manila port congestion; 200 BuildRight containers stuck at anchorage; ETA unknown"); (b) participants organized by function: Merchandising/Buying (sourcing), Supply Planning (allocation), Logistics (rerouting), DC Operations (receiving/dispatch), Finance (cost impact); (c) each function has access to their normal ERP data and tools; (d) simulation runs in real-time compressed format: each simulated "day" = 30 minutes of real time; total simulation: 15 simulated days in ~8 hours | Supply Planning Manager | VP Operations | 30 min briefing |
| 3 | **Alternate vendor activation drill**: Buyers must identify and activate alternate vendors for affected categories: (a) search vendor master for pre-qualified alternate vendors per W224; (b) contact alternate vendors for emergency pricing and availability (simulated via role-play or scripted responses); (c) Buyers who have maintained vendor relationships per W36 get faster response times; (d) Category Manager approves alternate vendor selection based on price, quality, and lead time tradeoffs; (e) Procurement issues emergency POs per W60 expedited order process; (f) Finance confirms budget authorization per W489 for emergency procurement above normal thresholds | Buyers / Category Manager / Procurement | VP Merchandising | 2–3 simulated days |
| 4 | **Logistics rerouting drill**: Logistics Manager must reroute shipments around disrupted nodes: (a) if port congested: identify alternate ports (Subic, Batangas) and arrange drayage to DCs; (b) if DC inaccessible: redirect shipments to alternate DC and plan inter-DC transfer per W22; (c) if inter-island routes closed: assess air freight for critical items; evaluate inventory redistribution from unaffected stores; (d) coordinate with 3PL carriers per W1207 for capacity; (e) Logistics Manager documents rerouting decisions and estimated cost impact vs. normal logistics cost | Logistics Manager | VP Operations | 3–4 simulated days |
| 5 | **Store allocation prioritization**: Supply Planning Manager must allocate limited inventory across 200 stores: (a) identify stores with lowest weeks-of-supply in affected categories; (b) prioritize: (i) high-volume flagship stores, (ii) stores with active B2B project commitments (W58), (iii) stores in high-competition trade areas where stock-outs drive customer defection; (c) activate store-to-store transfers per W22 for stores with excess inventory in affected categories; (d) communicate allocation decisions to Regional Managers; (e) adjust replenishment parameters per W31 to stretch available inventory | Supply Planning Manager | VP Merchandising | 3–4 simulated days |
| 6 | **Customer communication coordination**: Marketing and Store Operations coordinate customer messaging: (a) update ecommerce inventory availability to reflect real-time constraints; (b) prepare store signage for affected categories ("Temporarily unavailable — arriving [date]"); (c) enable substitution suggestions per W279 for out-of-stock items; (d) B2B Sales Manager contacts affected trade account customers with revised delivery timelines; (e) Customer Service updated with FAQ script for availability inquiries | Marketing / Store Ops / B2B Sales | VP Merchandising | 2 simulated days |
| 7 | **Financial impact assessment**: Finance tracks simulated financial impact: (a) lost revenue from stock-outs (estimated); (b) incremental logistics cost from rerouting; (c) incremental procurement cost from alternate vendors (price premiums, expedited shipping); (d) margin impact by category; (e) Finance provides cost-benefit analysis to VP Operations for decision support | Finance / VP Finance | — | Continuous during simulation |
| 8 | **Simulation debrief & lessons learned**: After simulation: (a) Supply Planning Manager facilitates debrief: (i) what worked well — pre-qualified alternate vendors, clear escalation protocols, rapid logistics rerouting; (ii) what needs improvement — vendor contact information outdated, DC capacity insufficient for rerouted volume, store allocation prioritization criteria unclear; (b) each function documents their top 3 lessons learned and recommended improvements; (c) Supply Planning Manager compiles simulation report with findings and corrective action plan; (d) corrective actions tracked to completion before next simulation; (e) findings integrated into BCP per W847 and communicated to BC Manager | Supply Planning Manager / All participants | VP Operations | 4 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- ERP system with live data (used in simulation for realistic decision-making)
- Vendor master with alternate vendor pre-qualification status (W1323.3)
- Logistics planning module with alternate route visualization (W1323.4)
- Inventory allocation and replenishment parameter tools (W1323.5)
- Ecommerce inventory availability management (W1323.6)
- Financial impact modeling tool for disruption cost estimation (W1323.7)
- Simulation scenario library with reusable inject cards (W1323.1)
- Integration with W729 (disruption response), W852 (impact assessment), W847 (BCP review), W60 (emergency orders), W22 (transfers), W31 (replenishment)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Operational disruption from simulation**: running a realistic simulation requires participant focus for a full day; scheduling 20+ key people simultaneously is difficult during normal operations; mitigated by scheduling on designated BCP testing days per W853 calendar
- **Unrealistic simplification**: compressed-time simulation may not capture real-world complexity (vendor negotiation takes days, not 30 minutes); mitigated by role-play realism and scripted vendor responses reflecting realistic negotiation timelines
- **Alternate vendor data staleness**: pre-qualified alternate vendors may have changed pricing, capacity, or contact information since last review; simulation reveals data quality issues that must be corrected in vendor master per W252
- **Dominant personality bias**: senior participants may dominate simulation decisions, suppressing input from operational staff with ground-level knowledge; mitigated by structured decision-making process and function-specific decision authority
- **No consequences**: unlike a real disruption, simulation participants know the scenario is artificial, reducing urgency and stress-based decision quality; mitigated by having VP Operations evaluate decision quality and include performance notes in annual reviews

### Staffing Implication
- **Supply Planning Manager**: ~4 days/year (2 days scenario design + 1 day simulation + 1 day debrief/reporting). Absorbed.
- **Participants (20–25 people)**: ~1 day each for simulation + 2 hours debrief = ~200 hours/year total. Absorbed as part of BCP testing.
- **No incremental headcount.**

### Time Estimate
- Scenario design: 2 days
- Simulation execution: 1 full day (8 hours)
- Debrief: 4 hours
- Report and corrective actions: 4 hours
- **Total per simulation**: ~4 days of Supply Planning Manager time + 1 day × 20–25 participants
- **Annual**: ~4 days (full simulation) + ~2 days (tabletop) = ~6 days of Supply Planning Manager time
