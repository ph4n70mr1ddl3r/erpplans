## W134. Crisis Communication & Brand Reputation Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Brand crisis (e.g., major safety issue, viral complaint, legal scandal) |
| **Frequency** | Ad-hoc |
| **Volume** | ~1–2 major crises managed per year |
| **Owner** | CMO / Public Relations (PR) Manager |
| **Participants** | CEO, Legal (W125), HR, Store Ops (if applicable) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Alert & Verification: Identify and verify the scale of the crisis | PR Manager | CMO | 1 hour |
| 2 | Crisis Team Activation: Convene CEO, Legal, CMO, and relevant Dept Head | CMO | CEO | 2 hours |
| 3 | Fact Finding: Gather details and internal reports on the incident | Dept Head | CEO | 4 hours |
| 4 | Messaging: Develop official statement and FAQ for press/social media | PR Manager | CEO | 4 hours |
| 5 | Channel Execution: Release statement via PR wires, website, and social media | PR Manager | CMO | 1 hour |
| 6 | Monitoring: Track sentiment and response; adjust messaging as needed | PR Manager | CMO | 1–3 days |
| 7 | Post-Crisis Review: Document impact and update crisis protocol | PR Manager | CMO | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints
- Crisis communication management module (or shared inbox/Slack channel) for crisis team coordination
- Social media monitoring and sentiment analysis tool (integrated with W142 social media management)
- Press release distribution and media contact database (W143 PR tools)
- Website CMS for rapid publication of official statements and FAQ pages
- Customer service ticketing system (W41) for monitoring complaint volume spikes
- Integration with W41 (complaints — early crisis detection), W125 (legal case management — legal risk assessment), W142 (social media — monitoring and response), W143 (PR — media relations), W53 (data privacy breach — crisis communication)

### Time Estimate
- Alert and verification: 1 hour (step 1)
- Crisis team activation: 2 hours (step 2)
- Fact finding: 4 hours (step 3)
- Messaging development: 4 hours (step 4)
- Channel execution: 1 hour (step 5)
- Monitoring and sentiment tracking: 1–3 days of continuous attention (step 6)
- Post-crisis review: 1 day (step 7)
- Total per crisis: ~3–5 days of intensive effort from crisis team; major crises may require weeks of ongoing monitoring

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Social media velocity**: A viral complaint or safety incident can escalate from a single post to national media coverage within hours; the 1-hour verification window (step 1) may be too slow if the issue is already trending on Facebook or TikTok before the crisis team is activated
- **Legal and PR messaging conflict**: Legal may want to minimize admissions of fault while PR wants to issue an empathetic, transparent response; reconciling these perspectives under time pressure can delay the official statement, allowing the narrative to be shaped by social media and competitors
- **Crisis team availability**: The crisis team (CEO, Legal, CMO, relevant Dept Head) consists of senior leaders with full schedules; convening all members within 2 hours (step 2) during evenings, weekends, or peak business periods is challenging
- **Post-crisis reputation recovery**: Even after the immediate crisis is resolved, online sentiment and media narratives can persist for weeks or months; BuildRight may need sustained PR investment and community engagement to fully rebuild trust, especially for safety-related incidents

### Staffing Implication
- **PR Manager**: crisis communication is a core responsibility; at 1–2 major crises/year, effort is highly variable (0 hours in quiet periods, 40+ hours during a crisis). Absorbed within existing PR duties (W143).
- **CMO**: serves as crisis team lead; ~3–5 days of intensive involvement per major crisis. Absorbed.
- **CEO**: crisis team convener; must be available within 2 hours for activation. Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount**, but recommend annual crisis simulation exercise (2–3 hours) to maintain readiness.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |

| 3 | Procurement: Source materials (lumber, tools, supplies) at cost from stores | Buyer | CSR Coord | 1 week |
| 4 | Implementation: Execute event (e.g., building a community center, relief distribution) | Store Staff | CSR Coord | 1–3 days |
| 5 | Documentation: Capture photos/videos and prepare impact report | PR Manager | CMO | 3 days |
| 6 | Publicity: Share CSR stories on social media and annual report (W114) | PR Manager | CMO | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints
- CSR project management module for project selection, budget tracking, and milestone management
- Budget and fund allocation system integrated with W21 (capex/budget approval)
- Procurement module (W2) for sourcing CSR materials at cost from store inventory
- Photo/video asset storage linked to campaign record for documentation
- PR and social media management tools (W142/W143) for CSR story publication
- Integration with W21 (budget approval — CSR fund allocation), W83 (marketing campaigns — CSR event promotion), W114 (sustainability reporting — CSR impact data), W135 (CSR impact measurement), W142 (social media — CSR publicity), W143 (PR — media coverage)

### Time Estimate
- Project selection and beneficiary identification: 2 weeks/project (step 1)
- Budget approval: 1 week/project (step 2)
- Material procurement: 1 week/project (step 3)
- On-site implementation event: 1–3 days/project (step 4)
- Documentation (photos, video, impact report): 3 days/project (step 5)
- Publicity and storytelling: 1 week/project (step 6)
- Total per CSR project: ~4–6 weeks elapsed time; CSR Coordinator spends ~20–30 hours/project
- At 4–6 projects/year: ~80–180 hours/year for CSR Coordinator

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Budget justification pressure**: With PHP 10–20M annual CSR budget, there is internal pressure to demonstrate measurable community impact; if impact metrics (W151) are weak or anecdotal, the CFO and CEO may reduce CSR allocation in subsequent annual budgets (W26)
- **Material procurement at cost from stores**: Sourcing lumber, tools, and supplies from BuildRight's own stores at cost reduces project expenses but complicates inventory management and store-level margin reporting; stores may resist diverting sellable inventory to CSR projects
- **Employee volunteer coordination**: CSR events require employee volunteers from nearby stores or HQ; scheduling volunteer time without disrupting store operations (especially during peak weekends) is logistically challenging and may incur hidden overtime costs
- **Typhoon relief timing conflicts**: Emergency CSR requests (typhoon relief) coincide with business continuity response (W49); the same stores and staff needed for community relief distribution are simultaneously managing store cleanup and reopening, creating resource conflicts

### Staffing Implication
- **CSR Coordinator**: manages 4–6 projects/year × ~20–30 hours/project = ~80–180 hours/year. This is a dedicated role within Marketing. Absorbed.
- **PR Manager**: documentation and publicity adds ~4 days/project × 4–6 projects = ~16–24 days/year. Absorbed within W143 duties.
- **Store Staff**: on-site implementation events require 1–3 days of volunteer time per project. Absorbed with scheduling coordination.
- **No incremental headcount.**

| 4 | **Distribution**: Send releases via wire services and direct media outreach | PR Manager | — | 1 hour |

### System Touchpoints
- Media contact database (CRM or dedicated PR tool) for reporter/editor/blogger relationship management
- Press release distribution platform (wire service) for mass media outreach
- Website CMS for press release publication and media kit hosting
- Executive briefing document repository with talking points and Q&A preparation
- Integration with W134 (crisis communication — press release for crisis response), W83 (campaigns — campaign PR support), W135 (CSR — CSR event publicity), W16 (new store opening — local media outreach)

### Time Estimate
- Media list maintenance: ongoing, ~2 hours/month
- Press release drafting: 4 hours/release (step 2)
- Executive briefing preparation: 2 hours/interview or press conference (step 3)
- Distribution: 1 hour/release (step 4)
- At ~12–15 press releases/year: ~80–100 hours/year for PR Manager

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Media relationship dependency**: Philippine media coverage often depends on personal relationships between PR managers and reporters; if the PR Manager leaves, media access and goodwill must be rebuilt from scratch, creating continuity risk
- **Negative media spin**: Even a well-crafted press release can be taken out of context by media looking for a controversy angle; BuildRight has limited control over how the story is framed once distributed, especially during sensitive events (product recalls, layoffs, safety incidents)
- **Executive media readiness**: C-suite executives may not be comfortable with media interviews or may deviate from approved talking points under pressure; without regular media training, a single interview misstep can generate a PR crisis (W134)
- **Limited PR resources for 200 stores**: With ~15 press releases/year and one PR Manager, most store-level events (community partnerships, minor CSR activities, new store openings in smaller markets) receive no PR support; local media opportunities are missed

### Staffing Implication
- **PR Manager**: ~80–100 hours/year for press releases, media relations, and executive briefing preparation. This is a core part of the role alongside W134 crisis communication. Absorbed.
- **CMO**: ~2 hours/month for PR strategy review and media interview preparation. Absorbed.
- **External PR agency**: retainer for major campaigns and crisis support per W62 contract. Budgeted within Marketing spend.
- **No incremental headcount.**

## W151. Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) Impact Measurement & Reporting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|

| **Frequency** | Monthly reporting; Annual impact summary |
| **Volume** | ~10–15 major initiatives/year |
| **Owner** | CSR Manager |
| **Participants** | Marketing, HR, Finance, external NGOs |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Data Collection**: Gather quantitative data: number of beneficiaries, tons of waste diverted, trees planted, scholarship hours funded | CSR Team | CSR Manager | 1 week |
| 2 | **Financial Reconciliation**: Match actual CSR spend against budget and donations collected from customers | Finance | Controller | 2 days |
| 3 | **Qualitative Stories**: Interview beneficiaries and employee volunteers for "impact stories" | Marketing / CSR | CMO | 3 days |
| 4 | **Impact Report**: Produce "BuildRight Impact" dashboard; integrate into Annual ESG Report (W114) | CSR Manager | CEO | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints
- CSR project database with beneficiary count, waste diversion weight, tree count, scholarship hours, and volunteer hours tracking
- Finance module for CSR budget vs. actual reconciliation and donation tracking
- Customer donation tracking (if round-up or checkout donation programs exist) linked to POS
- Impact dashboard visualization tool integrated with BI platform (W113)
- Annual ESG report template integrated with W114 sustainability reporting module
- Integration with W114 (sustainability reporting — CSR impact data feeds), W135 (CSR execution — project completion triggers measurement), W83 (campaigns — CSR impact publicity), W113 (BI — impact dashboard)

### Time Estimate
- Data collection per initiative: 1 week (step 1); ongoing across 10–15 initiatives/year
- Financial reconciliation: 2 days per initiative (step 2)
- Qualitative story gathering: 3 days per initiative (step 3)
- Annual impact report compilation: 1 week (step 4)
- Overall: CSR Manager spends ~30–50 hours/year on measurement and reporting activities

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Quantitative data reliability**: Metrics like "tons of waste diverted" or "number of beneficiaries" depend on field-level data collection by store staff or project partners; without standardized measurement protocols, data quality varies and undermines report credibility
- **Financial vs. impact disconnect**: Finance tracks CSR spend accurately, but connecting spend to actual community impact is methodologically difficult; PHP 10–20M in annual CSR spending without compelling impact evidence risks budget cuts during cost-reduction periods
- **Attribution of outcomes to BuildRight**: For collaborative CSR projects (e.g., school repairs involving LGU and NGO partners), attributing the outcome solely to BuildRight is neither fair nor credible; shared-credit reporting requires nuanced storytelling
- **ESG report burden on small team**: The CSR Coordinator (within Marketing) must balance executing CSR projects (W135) with measuring and reporting their impact (W151); during peak reporting periods, measurement takes time away from active project management

### Staffing Implication
- **CSR Manager**: ~30–50 hours/year for data collection, reporting, and annual impact report compilation. Absorbed within existing CSR duties alongside W135.
- **Finance**: ~2 days per initiative for financial reconciliation = ~20–30 days/year. Absorbed within existing Finance team.
- **Marketing**: ~3 days per initiative for qualitative story gathering = ~30–45 days/year. Absorbed within content team.
- **No incremental headcount.**

## W153. Retail Media Network (RMN) Operations

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor request to purchase premium advertising space on BuildRight digital/physical assets |
| **Frequency** | Ongoing; Monthly campaign cycles |
| **Volume** | ~20–30 vendors participating; ~50 active ad placements |
| **Owner** | Retail Media Manager |
| **Participants** | Merchandising, Digital Marketing, Ecommerce, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Inventory Mapping**: Catalog all "ad assets": website banners, app home screen, email newsletters, in-store digital screens, gondola end-caps | RMN Manager | CMO | 1 week |
| 2 | **Rate Card**: Define pricing for ad placements (CPM, CPC, or flat fee per week) | RMN Manager | CMO | 3 days |
| 3 | **Media Booking**: Vendor selects and books ad space; Category Manager approves for brand alignment | Vendor / Buyer | RMN Manager | 2 days |
| 4 | **Execution**: Upload ad creative to PIM (W50) or digital signage system; ensure tracking links are active | Digital Mktg | Ecommerce Mgr | 1 day |
| 5 | **Performance Reporting**: Provide vendor with "Closed-Loop" report: ad impressions vs. actual sales lift at POS | System / RMN Mgr | RMN Manager | Monthly |
| 6 | **Billing**: Invoice vendor for media services (distinct from product rebates W27) | Finance | Controller | Monthly |

### System Touchpoints
- Retail Media Network (RMN) platform for ad inventory management, booking, and performance tracking
- PIM/DAM system (W50) for ad creative storage and digital signage content management
- POS and ecommerce analytics for closed-loop sales attribution (ad impressions vs. actual purchase lift)
- AR module for vendor media services billing and invoice generation (distinct from product rebates W27)
- Digital signage content management system for in-store screen scheduling
- Integration with W50 (product content — ad creative management), W83 (campaigns — RMN campaigns aligned with promotional calendar), W113 (BI — closed-loop performance reporting), W27 (vendor rebates — RMN revenue is separate from product rebates), W7 (AP — vendor billing)

### Time Estimate
- Ad inventory mapping: 1 week (initial setup; updated quarterly)
- Rate card development: 3 days (initial; reviewed semi-annually)
- Per-vendor media booking: 2 days (step 3)
- Ad creative upload and activation: 1 day/campaign (step 4)
- Monthly performance reporting per vendor: 2–4 hours/vendor (step 5)
- Monthly vendor billing: 2 hours/vendor (step 6)
- At 20–30 vendors with monthly cycles: ~40–60 hours/month for RMN Manager

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Closed-loop attribution complexity**: Proving that a vendor's ad on BuildRight's website or in-store screen directly drove incremental POS sales is technically challenging; if attribution methodology is weak, vendors will dispute value and reduce media spend
- **Vendor conflict of interest**: Vendors who are both BuildRight suppliers (negotiating product margins via W27 rebates) and RMN advertisers (paying for ad placement) may conflate the two, using media spend as leverage in product negotiations; Finance must keep RMN revenue separate from product rebates
- **In-store digital signage maintenance**: Ad content on in-store digital screens requires functioning hardware and network connectivity across 200 stores; if screens are down or displaying outdated content, vendors paying for placement will demand credits or cancellations
- **Limited vendor participation at scale**: With ~20–30 participating vendors, the RMN generates modest incremental revenue; scaling to 50–100+ vendors requires significant sales effort and may not justify the infrastructure investment for a Philippine hardware retailer

### Staffing Implication
- **Retail Media Manager**: ~40–60 hours/month for ad inventory management, vendor coordination, booking, and performance reporting across 20–30 vendors. This is a dedicated role within Marketing. Absorbed.
- **Digital Marketing**: ~1 day per campaign for ad creative upload and activation. Absorbed.
- **Finance**: ~2 hours/vendor/month for billing = ~40–60 hours/month. Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount**, but if RMN scales beyond 50 vendors, consider a dedicated RMN Operations Coordinator.

## W189. Referral Program & Brand Ambassador Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Launch of referral campaign or onboarding of new Brand Ambassador |
| **Frequency** | Ongoing |
| **Volume** | ~1,000–5,000 active referrers |
| **Owner** | Loyalty Manager |
| **Participants** | Marketing, Customer Service, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Program Setup**: Define referral rewards (e.g., PHP 500 voucher for both referrer and referee) | Loyalty Mgr | CMO | 1 week |
| 2 | **Link Generation**: System generates unique referral codes/links for all loyalty members (W17) | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | **Ambassador Onboarding**: Sign agreements with "Power Referrers" (vetted high-engagement customers) | Loyalty Mgr | CMO | 2 days |
| 4 | **Referral Tracking**: Referee uses code at POS or online; system validates eligibility (new customer only) | System | — | Real-time |
| 5 | **Reward Fulfillment**: System auto-issues vouchers or loyalty points upon successful referee purchase | System | — | Real-time |
| 6 | **Fraud Monitoring**: Audit for self-referrals or "referral mills"; block suspicious accounts | Loyalty Mgr | — | Weekly |
| 7 | **Performance Analysis**: Calculate Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) via referral vs. other channels | Analytics | CMO | Monthly |

### System Touchpoints
- Referral tracking engine integrated with Loyalty module (W17) and POS/ecommerce for referral code validation
- Automated voucher issuance system for reward fulfillment (PHP 500 voucher or loyalty points)
- Brand Ambassador agreement management module (W62 vendor/contract tracking)
- Fraud detection rules engine for self-referral and referral mill pattern identification
- CAC analytics dashboard comparing referral channel vs. paid advertising vs. organic acquisition
- Integration with W17 (loyalty — referral code/link generation and points/voucher issuance), W83 (campaigns — referral campaign promotion), W65 (CSAT — referee satisfaction measurement), W113 (BI — CAC analytics)

### Time Estimate
- Program setup (one-time): 1 week for defining rewards, rules, and system configuration (step 1)
- Ambassador onboarding: 2 days per batch of ambassadors (step 3)
- Weekly fraud monitoring: 2–4 hours/week (step 6)
- Monthly performance analysis: 4–8 hours/month (step 7)
- Ongoing program management: ~8–12 hours/month for Loyalty Manager

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Referral fraud at scale**: With 1,000–5,000 active referrers and financial incentives (PHP 500 voucher per referral), fraud risk is significant; self-referrals (creating fake accounts), referral mills (paying people to sign up and purchase), and referee churn (making a minimum purchase to earn the reward then never returning) can erode program ROI
- **Reward cost vs. customer lifetime value**: PHP 500 for referrer + PHP 500 for referee = PHP 1,000 per successful referral; if referred customers have low repeat purchase rates, the CAC via referral may exceed the actual customer lifetime value, making the program financially unsustainable
- **Ambassador program governance**: "Power Referrers" with financial incentives may use aggressive or misleading sales tactics (e.g., overpromising BuildRight product capabilities) that damage brand reputation; monitoring ambassador communications across thousands of interactions is impractical
- **Program fatigue**: Referral programs tend to generate strong initial results that decline as the easily reachable network is exhausted; without periodic program refreshes (new rewards, seasonal campaigns), referral volume drops and the program becomes a fixed cost without proportional benefit

### Staffing Implication
- **Loyalty Manager**: ~8–12 hours/month for program management, ambassador onboarding, fraud monitoring, and performance analysis. Absorbed within existing W17 loyalty duties.
- **Analytics**: ~4–8 hours/month for CAC reporting. Absorbed.
- **Marketing**: periodic campaign promotion support per W83. Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount.**

## W286. Retail Media Network (RMN) Vendor Billing & Yield Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | End of month billing cycle for vendors who purchased ad space (digital screens, e-commerce banners, physical endcaps) in the retailer's network. |
| **Frequency** | Monthly |
| **Volume** | 50-100 vendor invoices per month |
| **Owner** | Marketing Finance / RMN Director |
| **Participants** | Vendors/Brands, Marketing, AR, Sales Ops |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | RMN platform aggregates impression data, click-through rates, or physical placement durations for the month; Marketing Finance validates data completeness against ad placement schedule. | Marketing Finance | RMN Platform Admin | 2 hours |
| 2 | System calculates the yield and total cost owed by the vendor based on their media contract; RMN Platform Admin reviews calculations for anomalies (zero-impression placements, CPM spikes, contract term mismatches). | RMN Platform Admin | Marketing Finance | 1 hour |
| 3 | ERP auto-generates a sales invoice (or debit memo offsetting AP) to the vendor for the advertising services rendered; Sales Ops reviews invoice details and routes to AR for processing. | Sales Ops | Marketing Finance | 2 hours |
| 4 | Vendor receives a performance dashboard alongside the invoice; Sales Ops fields vendor inquiries on impression metrics and provides supporting data for disputed line items. | Sales Ops | RMN Director | 1 hour |
| 5 | Finance monitors collection and recognizes the high-margin RMN revenue; AR tracks aging and escalates overdue invoices beyond 30 days. | Marketing Finance | Controller | 30 min |

### System Touchpoints
- Marketing Module (RMN) for impression aggregation, yield calculation, and vendor contract terms
- AR / AP (Debit Memo) for automated invoice generation and vendor account management
- BI Dashboard for vendor performance reporting, yield analytics, and revenue recognition tracking
- CRM for vendor communication and dispute resolution tracking
- Integration with W153 (RMN Operations — ad placement and booking data), W27 (vendor rebates — separate from RMN billing), W7 (AP — debit memo offset), W113 (BI — revenue analytics)

### Time Estimate
- Impression data aggregation and validation: 2 hours/month (step 1)
- Yield calculation review: 1 hour/month (step 2)
- Invoice generation and routing: 2 hours/month (step 3)
- Vendor dashboard distribution and inquiry handling: 1 hour/month (step 4)
- Collection monitoring and revenue recognition: 30 min/month (step 5)
- Total: ~6.5 hours/month for the billing run cycle; additional ~2 hours/month for vendor dispute resolution as needed

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Vendor impression metric disputes**: vendors frequently challenge reported impression counts, click-through rates, or physical placement durations; without third-party-validated audit trails, disputes can delay payment by 30–60 days and strain vendor relationships
- **Revenue leakage from missed invoicing**: with 50–100 vendor invoices per month across multiple ad formats (digital screens, e-commerce banners, physical endcaps), placement changes or mid-month additions can be missed during the billing run, resulting in un-invoiced services and lost revenue
- **Yield optimization complexity**: calculating optimal pricing across CPM, CPC, and flat-fee models for different ad placements (high-traffic vs. low-traffic stores, homepage banner vs. category page) requires ongoing analysis; underpricing premium placements leaves revenue on the table
- **AR collection delays for advertising services**: vendors may deprioritize payment for advertising invoices relative to product invoices, leading to longer DSO for RMN revenue; AR escalation processes for advertising services may need different cadence than standard product AR

### Staffing Implication
- **Marketing Finance**: ~2 days/month for RMN billing run, yield analysis, and collection monitoring. Absorbed within existing Finance team.
- **RMN Platform Admin**: ~4 hours/month for impression data aggregation and dashboard distribution to vendors. Absorbed within existing RMN Manager duties (W153).
- **Sales Ops**: ~2 hours/month for vendor communication and invoice follow-up. Absorbed.
- **No incremental headcount.**

## W833. Marketing Campaign Compliance Review & Regulatory Approval

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Marketing campaign creative and copy completed per W83; ready for launch |
| **Frequency** | Bi-monthly for major campaigns; weekly for digital campaigns |
| **Volume** | ~6 major catalog campaigns/year + ~50 digital/social media campaigns/month requiring compliance review |
| **Owner** | Marketing Manager |
| **Participants** | Marketing Manager, Legal Counsel, Compliance Officer, Brand Manager, External Agency |

### Background

Philippine marketing and advertising is heavily regulated. The DTI monitors sales promotions per RA 7394 (Consumer Act) and RA 7394 requires truth in advertising. DTI sales promotion permits are required per W427. The Advertising Standards Council (ASC) has self-regulatory guidelines. Specific compliance requirements include: (a) DTI Sales Promotion Permit for contests, raffles, buy-one-take-one per W427, (b) truth in pricing — advertised prices must be available and honored per Consumer Act, (c) Senior Citizen/PWD discount compliance in advertising per W170, (d) product claims must be substantiated (especially for tools, chemicals, building materials), (e) social media advertising must include required disclaimers, (f) bank partnership promotions must comply with BSP regulations per W149, (g) data privacy consent for marketing per RA 10173. Launching a campaign without compliance review risks DTI fines (PHP 10K-500K), consumer lawsuits, and brand damage.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Marketing Manager submits completed campaign materials** for compliance review: creative assets, copy, landing pages, social media posts, in-store signage, promotional mechanics | Marketing Manager | — | 15-30 min |
| 2 | **Legal Counsel reviews**: (a) advertising claims accuracy and substantiation, (b) promotional mechanics compliance (raffle, contest, giveaway rules per DTI guidelines), (c) terms and conditions completeness, (d) intellectual property clearance (images, music, fonts per W126 IP management), (e) data privacy consent wording per W834 RA 10173 | Legal Counsel | — | 2-4 hours |
| 3 | **Compliance Officer reviews**: (a) DTI Sales Promotion Permit secured per W427, (b) price accuracy vs. ERP pricing master per W289, (c) Senior Citizen/PWD discount applicability per W170, (d) product safety claims per W185 product liability, (e) environmental claims substantiation per W114 sustainability compliance, (f) competitor comparison accuracy per W130 competitor intelligence | Compliance Officer | — | 1-2 hours |
| 4 | **If campaign involves bank partnership**: Legal verifies BSP compliance per W149 bank partnerships | Legal Counsel | — | 30-60 min |
| 5 | **Marketing Manager addresses review feedback** and resubmits if required | Marketing Manager | — | 1-2 hours |
| 6 | **Legal Counsel and Compliance Officer approve** via digital sign-off per W686 document approval routing | Legal Counsel / Compliance Officer | — | 10 min |
| 7 | **Campaign cleared for launch** per W83 execution timeline | Marketing Manager | — | 5 min |
| 8 | **During campaign**: Compliance monitors for consumer complaints related to advertising per W41; escalates to Legal if issues arise | Compliance Officer | Legal Counsel | Ongoing |
| 9 | **Post-campaign**: compliance review retrospect included in W565 campaign ROI analysis — any regulatory issues documented for future prevention | Marketing Manager | Legal Counsel | 30-60 min |

### System Touchpoints

- W83 campaign planning & execution — campaign creative and timeline
- W427 DTI sales promotion permit — permit requirement and status
- W170 senior/PWD discount — discount compliance in advertising
- W149 bank partnerships — BSP regulatory compliance
- W126 IP management — intellectual property clearance
- W185 product liability — product safety claims review
- W114 sustainability compliance — environmental claims substantiation
- W130 competitor intelligence — competitive comparison accuracy
- W289 pricing master — advertised price accuracy verification
- W41 complaint resolution — consumer complaint monitoring
- W565 campaign ROI — post-campaign compliance retrospect
- W686 document approval — digital sign-off routing
- W834 data privacy compliance — RA 10173 consent requirements
- W271 data subject requests — data privacy issue management

### Pain Points / Risks

## W909. Customer Trade Account Co-Branded Credit Card Program Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual bank partnership review cycle; or new bank partner opportunity identified; or existing co-branded card program requires modification |
| **Frequency** | Annual program review; ~500–800 new card applications/month; ~10,000–15,000 active cardholders |
| **Volume** | ~PHP 200M–400M annual co-branded card spend |
| **Owner** | Marketing Director (Partnership) |
| **Participants** | Bank Partner, Marketing Director, Finance Manager (settlement), Legal Counsel, VP Sales, Credit Manager (W572 integration) |

### Background

BuildRight's trade customers (contractors, builders, interior designers — ~5,200 trade accounts contributing ~30% of revenue) require purchasing flexibility. Many operate on project-based cash flow: they buy materials on credit, complete the project, receive payment, then settle their accounts. A co-branded credit card with a partner bank offers extended payment terms (30–60 days vs. standard Net 30), BuildRight-specific rewards (2x loyalty points on BuildRight purchases, 1x on other spend), and a revolving credit facility that smooths project cash flow. For BuildRight, a co-branded card increases average transaction value (cardholders spend 15–25% more per transaction per industry benchmarks), improves customer retention (cardholders have higher lifetime value), and generates interchange revenue sharing from the bank. For the bank, BuildRight's trade customer base represents a lucrative segment with predictable spend patterns. This workflow manages the co-branded credit card program lifecycle, from bank partnership negotiation through card issuance, transaction processing, settlement, and program optimization.

### System Touchpoints

- Bank API for card application processing and real-time approval
- POS system for co-branded card identification and promotional trigger (POS-071)
- Loyalty points engine (W550) for 2x earning on BuildRight purchases
- Mobile app (W615) for digital card display and application
- Trade account system for pre-filled application data (W508)
- Settlement reconciliation system (W99) for bank revenue share
- Customer communication module (W708) for cardholder communications
- Credit monitoring system (W572) for trade account credit status (independent)
- Vendor portal (W705) for vendor co-branded promotional coordination
- BI dashboard for program analytics and ROI tracking
- CRM system for cardholder lifecycle management

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Credit risk concentration**: co-branded card extends credit to trade customers independently of BuildRight's credit assessment (W572); a cardholder may default on their bank card while maintaining good standing with BuildRight or vice versa; clear separation of credit decisions between bank and BuildRight; data sharing agreement for mutual risk alerting
- **Low cardholder activation**: approved customers may not activate or use the card; first-purchase bonus (1,000 points) and in-store activation campaign drive activation; 30-day activation follow-up per W708
- **Bank partnership dependency**: program success depends on bank partner's service quality (approval speed, customer service, statement accuracy); contractual SLAs with penalty clauses per W688; annual competitive benchmarking with alternative bank partners
- **Cannibalization of existing payment methods**: co-branded card may cannibalize trade account credit (Net 30) which has lower cost for BuildRight (no interchange fee); monitor trade account vs. co-branded card payment mix; position co-branded card as supplement (for extended terms beyond Net 30 or for personal spending by trade account owners)
- **Data privacy**: bank requires customer transaction data for credit assessment; RA 10173-compliant data sharing agreement with clear scope, consent, and retention terms required; customer consent at application
- **Promotional cost sharing**: 0% installment promotional interest subsidies shared between BuildRight and bank per W750; clear cost allocation rules in partnership agreement with monthly reconciliation

- **Sales Associate (Pro Desk)**: ~10–15 min per card application; ~5–10 applications/store/month; absorbed by existing Pro Desk duties
- **No incremental headcount**

- Card application: 10–15 min per application
