## W2350. Sample Program Cost Tracking

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly financial analysis |
| **Frequency** | Monthly; all sample programs |
| **Volume** | All sample categories |
| **Owner** | Finance Analyst |
| **Participants** | Finance Analyst, Category Mgr, VP Merchandising |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finance Analyst tracks the full sample-program cost per category: sample procurement (W2334–W2336), distribution (DC freight allocation), display maintenance (W2343/W2344 labor + materials), digital tool cost (W2339/W2346); computes the total sample-program cost per category per quarter, split by BuildRight-funded vs. vendor-funded (from W2338) | Finance Analyst | Finance Manager | 4–6 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Cost accounting (links to VS-158 / W85 product costing & margin, VS-17 / W26 budget) — the sample-program cost capture
- Co-op/rebate (links to VS-39 / W27) — the vendor-funded portion netted against the BuildRight cost
- Analytics (links to VS-28 / W113) — the cost dataset feeding W2353 ROI

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Hidden-cost risk**: store-labor cost (the W2344 cleaning, the W2343 replacement handling) isn't captured because it's absorbed into store-ops labor, understating the true program cost; mitigated by an allocated-labor estimate (minutes/week × loaded labor rate) applied per store
- **Vendor-funding-opacity risk**: the vendor funds the samples but the cost shows as BuildRight spend until the co-op credit lands, distorting the month-over-month view; mitigated by the net-cost presentation (gross less vendor co-op) in the monthly report

### Automation Opportunity
- Cost auto-aggregated from the GL + co-op master; net-cost view auto-computed

### Controls
- CTL-39: co-op/rebate accuracy — the vendor-funded portion validated; operational: Finance Manager monthly sign-off on the cost report

### Time Estimate
~5 hours/month Finance Analyst = **~60 hours/year**, absorbed into the VS-17 monthly close.

## W2351. Sample-to-Order Conversion Rate Analysis

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly analysis cycle — Sample-to-Order Conversion Rate |
| **Frequency** | Monthly; 200 stores |
| **Volume** | ~200 stores/month |
| **Owner** | Analytics Manager |
| **Participants** | Analytics Manager, Category Mgr, Sales Associates |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Analytics Manager computes the sample-to-order conversion rate: % of sample interactions (W2340 sample-assisted sales ÷ estimated sample interactions, where interaction is captured by sample-SKU scan or zone-traffic sensor); by category, by sample-SKU, by store; identifies the highest-converting samples and the underperformers; recommends display changes (add/expand the winners, retire the losers) | Analytics Manager | Category Manager | 4–6 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Analytics/BI (links to VS-28 / W113, W208 retail analytics) — the conversion dataset
- Sample-to-order data (links to VS-62 / W2340) — the numerator (sample-assisted sales)
- Zone-traffic sensor (links to VS-28 / W208 where sensor-equipped) — the denominator (interactions)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Denominator-weakness risk**: without zone-traffic sensors, the "interaction" denominator is estimated, making the conversion rate imprecise; mitigated by prioritizing the sample-SKU scan as the cleaner numerator and treating the rate as directional (rank-order) rather than absolute
- **Conversion-without-stock risk**: a sample converts but the SKU is out of stock, so the "conversion" doesn't become a sale; mitigated by the in-stock filter on the conversion metric (conversion-to-available-SKU, not conversion-to-any-SKU)

### Automation Opportunity
- Conversion dashboard auto-refreshed monthly with rank-order by sample/store; underperformer auto-flagged

### Controls
- CTL-417 (ensure controlled execution — Sample & Display ROI Analytics (PA-62.3).)
- Operational: Analytics Manager methodology sign-off (numerator/denominator definitions documented)

### Time Estimate
~5 hours/month Analytics Manager = **~60 hours/year**, absorbed into the VS-28 cadence.

## W2352. Display Zone Sales Attribution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly deep analysis |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly; display zones across 200 stores |
| **Volume** | ~1,600 zones analyzed |
| **Owner** | Analytics Manager |
| **Participants** | Analytics Manager, Category Mgr, VP Merchandising |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Analytics Manager attributes sales to the display zones: compares sales per sqm in the sample-display zones vs. comparable non-display zones in the same category (a quasi-controlled comparison); measures the uplift attributable to the sample presence to justify the space allocation | Analytics Manager | VP Merchandising | 3–5 days/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Analytics/BI (links to VS-28 / W113) — the zone-level sales dataset
- Planogram/space (links to VS-55 / W314 planogram, space-allocation) — the zone definitions and sqm
- Sample-to-order (links to VS-62 / W2340) — the cleaner attribution signal where captured

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Attribution-confound risk**: a sample-display zone sells more not because of the samples but because of better location (end-cap, high-traffic aisle); mitigated by the comparable-zone selection in step 1 (same category, similar traffic) rather than a random comparison
- **Cannibalization risk**: the display zone's uplift comes at the expense of another zone's sales (net-zero for the store); mitigated by the store-total sales view alongside the zone view

### Automation Opportunity
- Zone-attribution model auto-run quarterly with comparable-zone matching

### Controls
- CTL-417 (ensure controlled execution — Sample & Display ROI Analytics (PA-62.3).)
- Operational: Analytics Manager methodology sign-off (comparable-zone selection documented); VP Merchandising review of the attribution before it feeds space decisions

### Time Estimate
~4 days/quarter Analytics Manager = **~130–160 hours/year**.

## W2353. Sample Program Annual ROI

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual comprehensive review |
| **Frequency** | Annual |
| **Volume** | Full sample program |
| **Owner** | FP&A Analyst |
| **Participants** | FP&A, Category Mgrs, VP Merchandising |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FP&A Analyst computes the annual sample-program ROI: total program cost (from W2350) vs. the incremental revenue attributed to sample displays (from W2352 uplift × category margin); ROI by category; recommends next-year budget allocation across categories and across physical vs. digital (W2355) | FP&A Analyst | CFO | 3–5 days |

### System Touchpoints
- FP&A / cost accounting (links to VS-17 / W26 budget, VS-158 / W85) — the cost and revenue inputs
- Analytics (links to VS-28 / W113, VS-62 / W2352) — the incremental-revenue attribution
- Budget (links to VS-17 / W26, VS-101 merchandise financial planning) — the next-year allocation

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Incremental-revenue-attribution risk**: the W2352 uplift is itself an estimate, so the ROI inherits its uncertainty; mitigated by the ROI presented as a range (low/high uplift scenario) rather than a point estimate, and the directional use (rank-order categories) rather than absolute
- **Brand-value-blindness risk**: the ROI captures direct sales uplift but not the brand-experience value (a best-in-class sample program differentiates BuildRight from competitors), understating the program's strategic value; mitigated by a qualitative brand-value input alongside the financial ROI

### Automation Opportunity
- ROI model auto-assembled from the W2350 cost + W2352 uplift datasets; scenario (low/high) auto-computed

### Controls
- CTL-417 (ensure controlled execution — Sample & Display ROI Analytics (PA-62.3).)
- CTL-417 (budget-allocation accuracy — links to VS-17 / W26); operational: CFO sign-off on the ROI methodology and budget recommendation

### Time Estimate
~4 days/year FP&A Analyst = **~30–40 hours/year**, absorbed into the annual planning cycle.

## W2354. Customer Feedback on Sample Experience

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly survey cycle — Customer Feedback Sample Experience |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly; ~500 respondents |
| **Volume** | ~500 respondents/quarter |
| **Owner** | Customer Experience Manager |
| **Participants** | CX Manager, Category Mgr, Marketing Research |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CX Manager fields a sample-experience survey: customer satisfaction with the sample displays, usefulness of physical samples (which categories most need a tactile sample?), interest in the digital/AR tools (W2339), suggestions for improvement; the findings feed the display strategy (W2357 roadmap) and the per-category display decisions | CX Manager | VP Marketing | 2–3 days/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- CX/CSAT module (links to VS-13 / W65 CSAT/NPS, W87 feedback loop) — the survey instrument and response dataset
- CRM (links to VS-13) — survey linked to the customer's purchase history (sample-buyers vs. non-sample-buyers)
- Market research (links to VS-44 / consumer insights) — the deeper qualitative research counterpart

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Self-selection-bias risk**: survey respondents are disproportionately engaged customers (the disengaged don't respond), overstating satisfaction; mitigated by the response-rate monitoring and the incentive structure to broaden response
- **Stated-vs-revealed-preference risk**: customers say they want a feature (more samples, better AR) but their behavior doesn't change when it's delivered; mitigated by the behavioral data (W2351 conversion, W2355 digital usage) as the ground truth alongside the stated preference

### Automation Opportunity
- Survey distribution and response-rate dashboard; sentiment-analysis auto-tagging of free-text suggestions

### Controls
- CTL-417 (ensure controlled execution — Sample & Display ROI Analytics (PA-62.3).)
- Operational: CX Manager methodology sign-off; Category Manager review of category-specific findings

### Time Estimate
~2.5 days/quarter CX Manager = **~80–100 hours/year**, absorbed into the VS-13 CX cadence.

## W2355. Digital vs. Physical Sample Effectiveness

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Semi-annual comparison |
| **Frequency** | Semi-annual |
| **Volume** | Digital tool users vs. in-store sample users |
| **Owner** | Digital Product Manager |
| **Participants** | Digital PM, Analytics Manager, Category Mgr |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Digital PM compares the digital tool (AR visualizer, W2339) vs. the physical sample: usage → conversion rate, cost-per-conversion (digital tool cost vs. physical sample cost from W2350), customer preference (from W2354); recommends the channel-investment split (which categories benefit more from a physical sample vs. a digital tool) | Digital PM / Analytics Manager | VP Marketing | 3–5 days/semi-annual |

### System Touchpoints
- Analytics (links to VS-28 / W113) — the digital-tool usage and conversion data
- Digital engagement (links to VS-75 / digital engagement app) — the digital-tool surface
- Cost accounting (links to VS-62 / W2350) — the cost-per-conversion comparison

### Pain Points / Risks
- **False-dichotomy risk**: framing digital vs. physical as either/or when they're complementary (a customer browses digital at home, then confirms with a physical sample in-store); mitigated by measuring the combined journey, not just each channel in isolation
- **Channel-self-selection risk**: digital-tool users are a different segment (younger, more digital) than physical-sample users, so the conversion comparison conflates channel-effect with segment-effect; mitigated by segment-controlled comparison where possible

### Automation Opportunity
- Channel-comparison dashboard auto-refreshed semi-annually; combined-journey attribution where the customer-ID links the digital and in-store interaction

### Controls
- CTL-417 (ensure controlled execution — Sample & Display ROI Analytics (PA-62.3).)
- Operational: Digital PM methodology sign-off; VP Marketing review of the investment-split recommendation

### Time Estimate
~4 days/semi-annual Digital PM + Analytics = **~130–160 hours/year**.

## W2356. Competitor Sample Display Benchmarking

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual competitive review |
| **Frequency** | Annual; 4–5 competitors |
| **Volume** | 4–5 competitor display programs |
| **Owner** | Marketing Analyst |
| **Participants** | Marketing Analyst, Category Mgr, VP Merch |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Marketing Analyst mystery-shops the 4–5 competitor stores (the big-box home-improvement competitors in BuildRight's markets): evaluates their sample-display quality, variety, digital integration, customer experience; compares to BuildRight's program; identifies gaps and best practices to adopt | Marketing Analyst | VP Merchandising | 3–5 days |

### System Touchpoints
- Competitive intelligence (links to VS-33 / competitive intelligence, VS-57 competitor price intelligence, VS-44 consumer insights) — the benchmark dataset
- Mystery-shopping (links to VS-146 customer mystery shopping) — the methodology

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Anecdotal-benchmark risk**: a single mystery-shop visit per competitor is anecdotal; mitigated by multiple visits across multiple competitor stores and a structured scoring rubric
- **Copy-without-fit risk**: a competitor practice is copied without adapting to BuildRight's format/customer; mitigated by the fit-assessment in the recommendation (not blind adoption)

### Automation Opportunity
- Benchmark dataset structured for year-over-year tracking; competitor-display photos cataloged for reference

### Controls
- CTL-417 (ensure controlled execution — Sample & Display ROI Analytics (PA-62.3).)
- CTL-417 (competition-law conduct — the mystery-shopping is observational, not anti-competitive; links to W2683); operational: VP Merchandising review of the benchmark findings

### Time Estimate
~4 days/year Marketing Analyst = **~30–40 hours/year**, absorbed into the VS-33 competitive-intelligence cadence.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual strategy review |
| **Frequency** | Annual |
| **Volume** | Full sample program strategy |
| **Owner** | VP Merchandising |
| **Participants** | VP Merch, CIO, Category Mgrs, COO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | VP Merchandising reviews the emerging display technologies (VR showroom, 3D-printed samples on demand, AI color matching from a phone photo) and the program's current performance (from W2353 ROI); evaluates the technology investments against the ROI and customer-demand evidence (W2354); recommends the technology roadmap; presents to the Executive Committee | VP Merchandising / CIO | CEO | 3–5 days |

### System Touchpoints
- Innovation/PoC (links to VS-30 / W398 emerging-tech PoC lifecycle, VS-128 AI/ML governance) — the evaluation of emerging technologies
- Roadmap/portfolio (links to VS-112 PMO, VS-113 enterprise architecture) — the technology roadmap as a portfolio artifact
- Analytics (links to VS-28 / W113, VS-62 / W2353) — the performance basis

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Shiny-object risk**: a technology (VR showroom) is adopted for novelty rather than demonstrated customer value; mitigated by the ROI-and-demand-evidence gate in step 1 and a PoC-before-scale discipline (links to VS-30)
- **Capability-prematurity risk**: a technology is adopted before the org can support it (3D-printed samples need design + maintenance capability BuildRight lacks); mitigated by the capability assessment alongside the technology assessment

### Automation Opportunity
- Roadmap auto-populated from the PoC portfolio status; investment-scenario model auto-generated

### Controls
- CTL-417 (ensure controlled execution — Sample & Display ROI Analytics (PA-62.3).)
- operational: AI/ML governance — links to VS-128 if the innovation involves AI; operational: ExComm sign-off on the roadmap and any >PHP 5M technology investment

### Time Estimate
~4 days/year VP Merchandising + CIO = **~60–80 hours/year**, absorbed into the annual strategy cycle.
