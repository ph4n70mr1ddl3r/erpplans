## W2334. Tile & Flooring Sample Procurement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly sample refresh or new product launch |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly; ~200 stores × 8–10 tile sample boards |
| **Volume** | ~1,600–2,000 tile sample boards/quarter |
| **Owner** | Category Manager (Tiles) |
| **Participants** | Category Mgr, Vendor, Merch Planner, DC Ops |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Category Manager selects the tile/flooring SKUs requiring physical sample displays (the A/B movers where a tactile sample lifts conversion); coordinates with the vendor for sample production — physical tile pieces mounted on display boards with the SKU, price, and spec card | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 2–3 days |
| 2 | Vendor delivers the sample boards to the DC; DC distributes to the 200 stores via the regular replenishment cycle (samples ride alongside merchandise POs to avoid a separate freight line); Store Manager merchandises the samples in the Tile Gallery zone per the VS-55 planogram | Vendor / DC / Store Manager | Category Manager | 1–2 weeks |
| 3 | System tags each sample board with a sample-SKU (distinct from the sellable SKU) so sample inventory is tracked separately from sellable inventory and the W2340 sample-to-order conversion can attribute the sale | Merch Coordinator | Category Manager | Per refresh |

### System Touchpoints
- Item/sample master (links to VS-29 / W252, W296 service & non-stock item master) — the sample-SKU as a distinct tracked item tied to its sellable parent SKU
- Planogram/space (links to VS-55 / W86 planogram compliance, W314 planogram template master) — the Tile Gallery zone placement
- DC distribution (links to VS-04 / W3, W106 dispatch) — samples ride the regular replenishment

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Sample-shrinkage risk**: physical tile samples walk (customers pocket a tile piece) or get damaged on the display board, leaving gaps that hurt conversion; mitigated by the W2337 minimum-threshold tracking and the durable-board/glued-sample construction spec
- **Sample-sellable-decoupling risk**: the sample is mounted at a price that doesn't match the current sellable SKU price (after a price change), misleading the customer; mitigated by the sample-SKU→parent-SKU link that flags the sample for re-labeling when the parent price changes

### Automation Opportunity
- Sample-replenishment auto-triggered from the W2337 minimum-threshold flag; sample-label auto-regenerated when the parent SKU price changes

### Controls
- CTL-415 (ensure controlled execution — Sample Inventory Procurement & Distribution (PA-62.1).)
- operational: sample inventory tracked separately — prevents a sample-SKU transaction from corrupting sellable-inventory counts; operational: Store Manager display-condition check in the VS-07 daily walk

### Time Estimate
~2–3 days Category Manager selection per quarter + ~1.5 weeks distribution + ~30 min/store merchandising × 200 stores = **~120–160 hours/quarter** distributed across Category Manager, DC, and Store Managers.

## W2335. Paint Color Card & Swatch Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Seasonal refresh or new paint line introduction |
| **Frequency** | Semi-annual; ~200 stores × 200 color cards |
| **Volume** | ~40,000 color cards per refresh cycle |
| **Owner** | Category Manager (Paint) |
| **Participants** | Category Mgr, Paint Vendor, Store Ops |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Category Manager orders the updated color-card sets from the paint vendor: all available tints with BuildRight's current pricing printed on each card; vendor prints and delivers to the DC | Category Manager | Vendor | 1–2 days |
| 2 | DC distributes to stores; Paint Station staff replaces the old cards with the new set (old cards recycled per VS-73 waste streams); system tracks the distribution and confirms receipt per store | DC / Store Staff | Store Manager | 1 week |
| 3 | Category Manager validates the color-card accuracy against the actual tinted paint (the card is a representation; the mixed paint must match) via a sample-verification at a few stores each cycle | Category Manager | Vendor | Per cycle |

### System Touchpoints
- Item/sample master (links to VS-29 / W296) — color cards as sample-SKUs tied to the paint SKU and tint code
- Paint-mixing module (links to VS-09 / W168 custom paint mixing) — the tint code on the card drives the mix; color-card accuracy is the customer's expectation of the mix result
- Planogram (links to VS-55) — Paint Station card-display placement

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Color-mismatch risk**: the printed card doesn't match the actual tinted paint (card stock absorbs color differently, or the vendor's card and BuildRight's tint formula diverge), leading to customer complaints and returns; mitigated by the sample-verification in step 3 and the tint-formula governance in W168
- **Card-walk risk**: color cards are pocketable and high-walk; mitigated by displaying one reference set and keeping backups behind the counter, plus the W2337 replenishment

### Automation Opportunity
- Card-distribution tracking with per-store receipt confirmation; tint-code→card QR code so a customer can scan the card to see the digital swatch (links to W2339)

### Controls
- CTL-37: warranty/return integrity — a color-mismatch return traces back to the card accuracy; operational: Category Manager cycle-verification sign-off

### Time Estimate
~1.5 days Category Manager ordering + ~1 week distribution + ~1 hour/store replacement × 200 stores = **~250–300 hours per cycle ≈ ~500–600 hours/year** (2 cycles).

## W2336. Countertop & Surface Material Sample Distribution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New product launch or annual refresh |
| **Frequency** | Annual + new launches; ~200 stores × 5–8 samples |
| **Volume** | ~1,000–1,600 countertop samples/cycle |
| **Owner** | Category Manager (Building Materials) |
| **Participants** | Category Mgr, Vendor, Merch Planner, DC Ops |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Manager selects the countertop SKUs for sample display (granite, quartz, laminate — the materials where the tactile/visual sample is decisive); vendor produces the sample chips; distributed via the DC; stores display in the dedicated countertop zone | Category Manager / Vendor | VP Merchandising | 2–3 days + 1–2 weeks |
| 2 | System tags the countertop sample chips with sample-SKUs tied to the sellable parent (the full-slab SKU), enabling the W2340 sample-to-order attribution | Merch Coordinator | Category Manager | Per cycle |

### System Touchpoints
- Item/sample master (links to VS-29 / W252, W296) — countertop sample-SKU tied to the full-slab parent
- Planogram (links to VS-55) — countertop display zone placement
- Special-order module (links to VS-09 / W38 special order) — countertops are typically special-order (full slab cut to spec), so the sample→quote→special-order chain is the conversion path

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Sample-vs-slab-variation risk**: a granite sample chip doesn't represent the full-slab variation (granite is naturally variable), so the customer's slab differs from the sample they selected; mitigated by the customer-viewing-the-actual-slab step in the special-order process (W38) and a clear "natural variation" disclosure on the sample
- **Chip-damage risk**: laminate/quartz chips crack or the label peels, making the sample unreadable; mitigated by the durable-chip spec and the W2337 replenishment

### Automation Opportunity
- Sample→special-order quote auto-populated from the sample-SKU scan (the Sales Associate scans the chip and the quote opens pre-filled with the parent slab SKU and current price)

### Controls
- CTL-37: warranty/return integrity — a variation-dispute return; operational: "natural variation" disclosure standard on every stone sample

### Time Estimate
~3 days Category Manager selection + ~1.5 weeks distribution + ~30 min/store merchandising × 200 stores = **~140–180 hours/cycle**.

## W2337. Sample Inventory Tracking & Replenishment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly inventory review of sample stock |
| **Frequency** | Monthly; 200 stores |
| **Volume** | ~200 store sample inventories/month |
| **Owner** | Merchandising Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Merch Coordinator, Store Manager, DC Ops |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System tracks the sample inventory per store: tile boards, color cards, countertop chips, fabric swatches — each as a tracked sample-SKU; flags stores with damaged/missing samples below the minimum threshold (e.g., <6 of 10 tile boards present) | System / Merch Coordinator | Merchandising Planner | 2–3 hours/month |
| 2 | Coordinator generates replenishment orders for the below-threshold stores; samples picked at the DC and shipped with the regular store delivery; Store Manager confirms receipt and re-displays | Merch Coordinator / DC | Store Manager | 1–2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Inventory module (links to VS-05 / W6 cycle counting, W97 sample/demo inventory status) — sample inventory as a tracked class with its own minimum thresholds
- DC distribution (links to VS-04 / W3) — samples ride regular replenishment
- Analytics (links to VS-27 / W113) — sample-presence rate per store (a planogram-compliance metric)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Hidden-gap risk**: a sample is missing but not flagged because the threshold is set too low or the cycle-count misses the sample class; mitigated by the W2337 monthly sample-specific review (separate from the sellable cycle count) and the W86 planogram-compliance audit that visually catches gaps
- **Replenishment-latency risk**: a below-threshold store waits 1–2 weeks for the next delivery, losing conversion the whole time; mitigated by samples riding every regular replenishment (not a separate monthly sample-only delivery)

### Automation Opportunity
- Below-threshold auto-detection from the cycle-count result; replenishment auto-added to the next regular store delivery

### Controls
- CTL-51: stale/incomplete sample master — the sample-SKU must be current; operational: Merch Coordinator monthly review of the sample-presence rate per store

### Time Estimate
~2.5 hours/month Coordinator tracking + DC pick/pack absorbed into regular ops + ~15 min/store re-display × ~60 below-threshold stores/month = **~180–250 hours/year** Merch Coordinator + Store Managers.

## W2338. Sample Vendor Negotiation & Cost Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual vendor review for sample costs |
| **Frequency** | Annual; ~20 sample vendors |
| **Volume** | ~20 vendor negotiations/year |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Mgr, Procurement, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Category Manager negotiates the sample costs with each of the ~20 sample vendors: tile boards, color cards, countertop chips; samples are typically vendor-funded (the vendor absorbs the cost as a co-marketing investment) or cost-shared; the negotiated terms feed the vendor co-op budget per VS-39 | Category Manager / Procurement | Finance Manager | 1–2 days/year |

### System Touchpoints
- Co-op/rebate agreement master (links to VS-39 / W27, VS-14 marketing co-op) — the sample-cost-sharing terms recorded in the vendor agreement
- Vendor scorecard (links to VS-67 / W2458) — sample-cost cooperation as a qualitative dimension
- AP (links to VS-15 / W7) — where BuildRight shares the cost, the sample invoice is processed against the co-op accrual

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Cost-creep risk**: the vendor funds samples year 1 then quietly passes the cost to BuildRight year 2 (buried in a price increase); mitigated by the annual sample-cost line-item review against the prior-year terms
- **Free-rider risk**: a vendor refuses to fund samples while benefiting from the display-driven sales; mitigated by the sample-funding expectation built into the Strategic-tier vendor commercial terms (W2464)

### Automation Opportunity
- Sample-cost-per-vendor dashboard (BuildRight-funded vs. vendor-funded) surfaced at the annual negotiation; year-over-year delta auto-flagged

### Controls
- CTL-39 (rebate/co-op accuracy — the sample cost-sharing validated against the agreement); CTL-48 (rebate-dispute resolution if the vendor disputes the cost-share)

### Time Estimate
~1.5 days/year Category Manager + Procurement negotiation × ~20 vendors (distributed, ~1 hour each) = **~40–60 hours/year**.

| **Frequency** | Quarterly app updates; continuous availability |
| **Volume** | ~42,900 ecommerce customers + in-store tablet users |
| **Owner** | Digital Product Manager |
| **Participants** | Digital PM, IT, Category Mgr, Vendor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Digital PM manages the AR/3D visualization tools in the BuildRight app and in-store tablets: tile visualizer (see tile on your room floor), paint color visualizer (see the wall color), countertop preview; updates the product catalog quarterly with new SKUs enabled for visualization | Digital PM / IT | CIO | Ongoing |
| 2 | Category Manager validates the color/texture accuracy of the digital samples vs. the physical (the digital must match the physical sample which must match the actual product); coordinates with the vendor for the digital assets (3D models, high-res textures) | Category Manager / Vendor | Digital PM | Quarterly |

### System Touchpoints
- PIM/DAM (links to VS-137 / W50 PIM, the digital-asset master) — the 3D models and textures as governed digital assets
- Ecommerce/app platform (links to VS-10 / W50 PIM, VS-75 digital engagement app) — the AR tool surface
- Item master (links to VS-29 / W252) — SKU enabled-for-visualization flag

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Digital-vs-physical-mismatch risk**: the AR render looks different from the actual product (lighting, texture resolution), generating "not as shown" returns; mitigated by the Category Manager accuracy validation in step 2 and a "render is indicative" disclosure
- **Asset-staleness risk**: a SKU is discontinued but its AR asset remains, letting customers visualize a product they can't buy; mitigated by the SKU-enabled flag tied to the item-master lifecycle status (W68 discontinuation)

### Automation Opportunity
- AR-asset auto-disabled when the SKU is discontinued; digital-asset accuracy flagged for re-validation when the physical sample changes (W2334/W2335 refresh)

### Controls
- CTL-51: digital-asset/SKU master currency; operational: Category Manager quarterly accuracy sign-off

### Time Estimate
~4 hours/week Digital PM management + ~1 day/quarter Category Manager validation = **~250–300 hours/year** Digital PM + Category Manager.

## W2340. Sample-to-Order Conversion Process

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer selects product based on sample display |
| **Frequency** | Continuous; ~2,000–3,000 sample-assisted sales/month |
| **Volume** | ~2K–3K/month across tile, flooring, countertop categories |
| **Owner** | Sales Associate |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate, Customer, CRM |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sales Associate assists the customer at the sample display; identifies the selected sample; scans the sample barcode or looks up the SKU in the system; checks sellable inventory and current pricing; creates a quote or sales order (special order for countertops per W38) | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 15–30 min |
| 2 | System logs the sample-assisted sale: sample-ID → parent-SKU → transaction; tracks the conversion rate per sample per store; feeds the sample-ROI analytics that justify the W2338 sample-program spend | System | Category Manager | Automated |

### System Touchpoints
- POS / quotation (links to VS-08 / W5B in-store selling, W542 quotation & estimate) — the sample-scan → quote/sale path
- Item/sample master (links to VS-29 / W296) — the sample-SKU→parent-SKU link that attributes the sale
- Special order (links to VS-09 / W38) — countertop sample sales flow to special order
- CRM (links to VS-13) — sample-assisted sale tagged on the customer record for follow-up

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Scan-failure risk**: the sample barcode is damaged/unreadable, so the Associate looks up the SKU manually and the sample-attribution is lost (understating the sample ROI); mitigated by the sample-SKU lookup fallback that still captures the attribution
- **Inventory-disappointment risk**: the sample sells the customer on a SKU that's out of stock, converting interest into a lost sale; mitigated by the real-time inventory check in step 1 (POS ATP per the integration architecture) before the quote closes

### Automation Opportunity
- Sample-conversion dashboard auto-populated from the tagged transactions; sample-ROI auto-computed (sample-program cost ÷ sample-assisted margin)

### Controls
- CTL-415 (ensure controlled execution — Sample Inventory Procurement & Distribution (PA-62.1).)
- operational: sample-SKU transaction integrity — the attribution link must not corrupt sellable-inventory financials; operational: Department Supervisor visibility on sample-assisted sale rate

### Time Estimate
~20 min average per sample-assisted sale × ~2,500/month = **~800–1,000 staff-hours/month** Sales Associate (absorbed into the selling role, not incremental headcount); system logging is automated.

## W2341. Seasonal Sample Refresh Execution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Seasonal transition (6 rotations/year) |
| **Frequency** | 6/year; 200 stores |
| **Volume** | ~1,200 seasonal sample refreshes/year |
| **Owner** | Merchandising Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Merch Coordinator, Store Managers, DC Ops |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coordinator issues the seasonal sample-refresh instructions: remove the off-season samples (return-to-vendor or recycle per VS-73), install the new-season samples; DC pre-stages the seasonal sample kits; stores execute during the planogram rotation per VS-55 (samples rotate with the merchandise reset) | Merch Coordinator / DC / Store Manager | Merchandising Planner | 1–2 weeks per rotation |

### System Touchpoints
- Planogram/reset (links to VS-55 / W86, W264 seasonal transition, W314 planogram template) — the seasonal sample refresh sequenced with the merchandise reset
- DC pre-staging (links to VS-04 / W3) — seasonal sample kits kitted ahead of the reset wave
- Inventory disposition (links to VS-05 / W91 damaged/obsolete, VS-73 waste) — off-season sample disposition

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Mis-timed-refresh risk**: the samples rotate before or after the merchandise, leaving the display incoherent (new merchandise, old samples) for a window; mitigated by the refresh sequenced into the VS-55 planogram rotation as a single workstream
- **Off-season-sample-waste risk**: off-season samples are discarded when they could be reused next season or returned to the vendor for credit; mitigated by the disposition decision (return-to-vendor where the vendor accepts, recycle where not) in step 1

### Automation Opportunity
- Refresh-kit auto-assembled at the DC from the seasonal planogram; off-season-sample disposition auto-suggested per vendor agreement

### Controls
- CTL-26: asset-grade sample disposition; operational: Merch Coordinator sign-off on the rotation completion per store cluster

### Time Estimate
~1.5 weeks/rotation × 6 rotations + ~1 hour/store reset work × 200 stores = **~250–350 hours/rotation ≈ ~1,500–2,100 hours/year** distributed across Merch Coordinator, DC, and Store Managers.
