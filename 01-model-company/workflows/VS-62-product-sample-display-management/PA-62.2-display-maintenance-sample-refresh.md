## W2342. Monthly Display Condition Inspection

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly inspection cycle |
| **Frequency** | Monthly; 200 stores × 8 zones |
| **Volume** | ~1,600 zone inspections/month |
| **Owner** | Regional Operations Manager |
| **Participants** | Regional Ops Mgr, Store Manager, Merch Coord |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Regional Ops Manager (or designated field auditor) inspects the sample displays across ~8 zones per store: condition, cleanliness, completeness (samples present vs. the W2337 minimum threshold), pricing-label accuracy (links to W2345), visual-merchandising standard adherence (links to VS-55 planogram); scores each zone on a standard rubric | Regional Ops Mgr | VP Store Operations | 2–3 hours/store |
| 2 | Store Manager remediates the findings within 48 hours; damaged samples replaced from the store reserve or ordered from the DC (W2343); persistent issues escalated to the Merchandising Coordinator for a program-level fix | Store Manager | Regional Ops Mgr | 2–4 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Planogram/audit (links to VS-55 / W86 planogram compliance audit, VS-177 field-retail-standards audit) — the inspection rubric and scoring
- Sample inventory (links to VS-62 / W2337) — completeness check against the sample-presence threshold
- Field-audit tooling (links to VS-133 / VS-177) — the mobile inspection app the Regional Ops Mgr uses

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Inspection-decoupled-from-action risk**: the inspection generates findings but the Store Manager doesn't remediate within 48 hours; mitigated by the finding-age tracker and Regional Ops Manager follow-up on open findings >48 hours
- **Rubric-drift risk**: different inspectors score the same display differently (subjectivity); mitigated by the photo-evidence requirement per finding and the rubric calibration sessions for the Regional Ops team

### Automation Opportunity
- Inspection findings with photos auto-routed to the Store Manager's worklist with the 48-hour SLA; finding-closure auto-verified on the next cycle

### Controls
- CTL-195: display integrity — a display that misrepresents product is a consumer-protection risk per W2682; operational: Regional Ops Manager finding-closure sign-off

### Time Estimate
~2.5 hours/store inspection × 200 stores + ~3 hours remediation per store = **~700–900 hours/month** Regional Ops + Store Managers (Regional Ops Managers cover ~40 stores each).

## W2343. Damaged Sample Replacement Process

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Store reports damaged or missing sample |
| **Frequency** | Ad-hoc; ~500–800 replacements/month |
| **Volume** | ~500–800/month across 200 stores |
| **Owner** | Store Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Dept Supervisor, Merch Coordinator, DC Ops |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Supervisor reports the damaged sample in the system: sample-ID, damage type, photo evidence; system generates the replacement order; Merch Coordinator approves (or auto-approved if below threshold); replacement shipped with the next DC delivery | Dept Supervisor / System | Merch Coordinator | 10 min + 3–5 days delivery |

### System Touchpoints
- Inventory/replenishment (links to VS-62 / W2337, VS-05 / W6) — the sample-SKU replenishment
- DC distribution (links to VS-04 / W3) — replacement rides the next regular delivery
- Disposition (links to VS-05 / W91 damaged-goods, VS-73 waste) — the damaged sample's disposition (return-to-vendor, recycle, dispose)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Replacement-latency risk**: the 3–5 day delivery window leaves the display incomplete, losing conversion; mitigated by the store reserve stock (a small on-hand buffer of high-damage samples) for immediate swap
- **Damage-pattern blindness risk**: the same sample (e.g., a fragile laminate chip) damages repeatedly across stores but the pattern isn't seen because each replacement is handled as a one-off; mitigated by the W2348 quarterly analytics aggregating damage frequency per sample-SKU to flag a durability redesign

### Automation Opportunity
- Below-threshold auto-approval for low-value replacements; damage-frequency dashboard auto-aggregated for the W2348 review

### Controls
- CTL-416 (ensure controlled execution — Display Maintenance & Sample Refresh (PA-62.2).)
- operational: sample-SKU transaction integrity; operational: photo-evidence requirement per damage report

### Time Estimate
~10 min/report × ~650 replacements/month + DC handling absorbed = **~150–200 hours/month** Dept Supervisors (distributed across stores).

## W2344. Sample Display Area Cleaning & Maintenance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Weekly scheduled maintenance |
| **Frequency** | Weekly; 200 stores |
| **Volume** | ~200 stores/week |
| **Owner** | Store Staff |
| **Participants** | Store Staff, Department Supervisor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Designated staff cleans the sample displays weekly: dust tile boards, wipe countertop chips, replace faded color cards, clean the display fixtures, organize the sample order; logs completion in the VS-07 daily-ops checklist | Store Staff | Department Supervisor | 30–45 min/week |

### System Touchpoints
- Store daily-ops (links to VS-07 / W5 daily store operations, W47 maintenance) — the cleaning task in the weekly store-ops cycle
- Planogram (links to VS-55) — the sample-order organization standard

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Skip-risk**: the weekly clean is skipped during busy periods, and the display degrades (dusty samples depress conversion); mitigated by the task in the VS-07 weekly checklist with Department Supervisor sign-off
- **Cleaning-product-damage risk**: the wrong cleaner damages a sample (abrasive on a polished stone, solvent on a laminate); mitigated by the per-material cleaning spec in the task instructions

### Automation Opportunity
- Cleaning-task auto-scheduled in the VS-07 weekly checklist; completion auto-tracked via the task system

### Controls
- CTL-416 (ensure controlled execution — Display Maintenance & Sample Refresh (PA-62.2).)
- Operational: Department Supervisor weekly sign-off on the cleaning checklist

### Time Estimate
~30–45 min/week/store × 200 stores ≈ ~6,000–9,000 min ≈ **~100–150 hours/week ≈ ~5,200–7,800 hours/year** Store Staff (absorbed into the store-ops routine, not incremental headcount).

## W2345. Sample Display Signage & Pricing Update

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Price change or new sample added |
| **Frequency** | ~200–300 updates/month |
| **Volume** | ~200–300/month |
| **Owner** | Store Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Dept Supervisor, Pricing Analyst, Merch Coord |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System generates the sample pricing labels when the parent SKU's SRP changes (the sample-SKU→parent-SKU link from W2334 surfaces the price-change impact); Supervisor prints and applies the labels to the sample display; ensures the sample SKU, product name, price, and order-availability match the current data | Dept Supervisor / System | Store Manager | 1–2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Price-tag/labeling (links to VS-08 / W181 price-tag printing, VS-55 / W63 shelf-label generation) — the sample-label generation
- Pricing master (links to VS-29 / W289 pricing master) — the SRP change that triggers the label refresh
- Item/sample master (links to VS-29 / W296) — the sample→parent link that flags the sample for re-labeling

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Stale-price risk**: the sample shows the old price after an SRP change, misleading the customer and creating a DTI price-complaint exposure (links to W2682 consumer protection); mitigated by the auto-generated label list triggered by the parent price change
- **Mismatched-availability risk**: the sample displays a SKU that's discontinued or out of stock, frustrating the sample-to-order conversion; mitigated by the availability check on the label-refresh trigger

### Automation Opportunity
- Sample-label list auto-generated on every parent-SKU price change; label-refresh auto-verified on the W2342 inspection

### Controls
- CTL-416 (ensure controlled execution — Display Maintenance & Sample Refresh (PA-62.2).)
- CTL-416 (price-tag accuracy — links to VS-08 / W69 price-compliance audit, DTI price-freeze compliance); operational: Department Supervisor label-application sign-off

### Time Estimate
~1.5 hours/month × 200 stores = **~300 hours/month ≈ ~3,600 hours/year** Dept Supervisors (absorbed into the store-ops pricing routine).

## W2346. Interactive Display Technology Maintenance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly maintenance or malfunction |
| **Frequency** | Monthly; ~50 stores with tablet/kiosk displays |
| **Volume** | ~50 stores/month |
| **Owner** | IT Support |
| **Participants** | IT Support, Store Manager, Digital PM |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | IT Support maintains the interactive sample displays: in-store tablets running the AR visualizer (W2339), digital kiosks with the product catalog; updates the software, replaces damaged tablets, troubleshoots connectivity (links to VS-27 IT ops) | IT Support | CIO | 2–3 hours/store/month |

### System Touchpoints
- IT asset/MDM (links to VS-27 / W48 helpdesk, W265 POS-terminal/peripheral management, VS-99 IT-asset lifecycle) — the tablet/kiosk as a managed device
- AR/catalog app (links to VS-62 / W2339, VS-75 digital engagement app) — the software under maintenance
- Network/connectivity (links to VS-27 / W366) — the in-store connectivity the displays depend on

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Display-downtime risk**: a malfunctioning tablet sits broken for weeks because no one reports it (the store team assumes IT knows); mitigated by the device-health telemetry (MDM reports offline devices) and a "report a broken display" quick-path for Store staff
- **Theft/vandalism risk**: in-store tablets are theft targets and vandalism-prone; mitigated by the secure-mounting spec and the device-tracking (MDM locates/wipes a stolen device)

### Automation Opportunity
- Device-health telemetry auto-ticketing (an offline device auto-opens an IT ticket without waiting for a store report)

### Controls
- CTL-416 (ensure controlled execution — Display Maintenance & Sample Refresh (PA-62.2).)
- CTL-416 (IT-asset tracking — links to VS-99); operational: IT Support monthly maintenance log per device

### Time Estimate
~2.5 hours/store/month × 50 stores = **~125 hours/month ≈ ~1,500 hours/year** IT Support.

## W2347. Vendor-Funded Display Program Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor offers branded display fixture |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 vendor display programs/year |
| **Volume** | ~10–15 programs/year |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Mgr, Marketing, Vendor, Store Ops |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Vendor proposes a branded display (e.g., a Bosch power-tool wall, a Boysen paint station); Category Manager evaluates: brand alignment, customer experience, space requirement (against the VS-55 space-allocation plan), vendor funding level (is the vendor paying for the fixture and installation?) | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 2–3 days/program |
| 2 | Approved displays: vendor provides the fixture and installation; Store Ops coordinates the placement; Category Manager monitors the performance (traffic, conversion, sales lift) vs. the standard display baseline to validate the program | Vendor / Store Ops / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 weeks install + ongoing |

### System Touchpoints
- Planogram/space (links to VS-55 / W314 planogram template, space-allocation) — the fixture's footprint allocated against the store's space plan
- Co-op/rebate (links to VS-39 / W27, VS-14 marketing co-op) — the vendor-funding terms recorded in the vendor agreement
- Analytics (links to VS-27 / W113) — the performance-vs-baseline measurement

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Clutter risk**: too many vendor-funded displays crowd the floor and dilute brand coherence; mitigated by the space-allocation gate in step 1 (a vendor display displaces standard merchandise, so the opportunity cost must be justified)
- **Funding-withdrawal risk**: the vendor funds the fixture year 1 then withdraws funding, leaving BuildRight with an orphan fixture; mitigated by the multi-year funding term in the agreement and a "funding ends → re-evaluate or decommission" clause

### Automation Opportunity
- Performance-vs-baseline dashboard auto-populated post-install; funding-term-expiry tracker

### Controls
- CTL-39: co-op/rebate accuracy — the vendor funding validated; CTL-48 (rebate-dispute resolution if the vendor disputes the funding obligation); operational: VP Merchandising sign-off on any vendor display >PHP 500K fixture value

### Time Estimate
~2.5 days/program × ~12 programs/year + ongoing performance monitoring absorbed = **~250–350 hours/year** Category Manager + Store Ops.

## W2348. Sample Display Performance Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly analytics review |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly; 200 stores |
| **Volume** | ~200 stores analyzed quarterly |
| **Owner** | Analytics Manager |
| **Participants** | Analytics Manager, Category Mgr, VP Merchandising |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Analytics Manager measures display performance: sales per display zone, sample-to-order conversion rate (from W2340), dwell time at displays (where sensor-equipped), customer feedback on displays; identifies the top- and bottom-performing displays and the damage-frequency patterns (from W2343) | Analytics Manager | Category Manager | 2–3 days/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Analytics/BI (links to VS-27 / W113, W208 retail analytics) — the performance dataset
- Sample-to-order data (links to VS-62 / W2340) — the conversion-rate input
- Customer feedback (links to VS-13 / W65 CSAT/NPS) — the customer-experience input

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Attribution-weakness risk**: sales-per-zone is hard to attribute cleanly (a customer may browse the tile display then buy at the counter, not at the display); mitigated by the W2340 sample-scan attribution as the cleaner signal where captured
- **Vanity-metric risk**: dwell time looks good but doesn't convert; mitigated by the conversion-rate (not dwell) as the primary KPI

### Automation Opportunity
- Performance dashboard auto-refreshed quarterly; bottom-performer auto-flagged for the Category Manager's review

### Controls
- CTL-416 (ensure controlled execution — Display Maintenance & Sample Refresh (PA-62.2).)
- Operational: Analytics Manager methodology sign-off (attribution rules documented); Category Manager review of any display with conversion below threshold

### Time Estimate
~2.5 days/quarter Analytics Manager = **~80–100 hours/year**, absorbed into the VS-28 analytics cadence.

## W2349. Sample Display Decommissioning

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Product delisted or display program ended |
| **Frequency** | ~50–100 decommissions/year |
| **Volume** | ~50–100/year |
| **Owner** | Merchandising Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Merch Coordinator, Store Manager, Facilities |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coordinator issues the decommission: remove the samples, dismantle the fixture, return the vendor-owned displays (per the W2347 agreement terms), dispose/recycle the BuildRight-owned materials (per VS-73 waste streams); restore the zone to the standard planogram | Merch Coordinator / Store Manager / Facilities | Category Manager | 1–2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Item/sample master (links to VS-29 / W296, W68 discontinuation) — the sample-SKU status set to decommissioned
- Disposition (links to VS-05 / W91, VS-73 waste, W443 salvage/scrap) — the display-material disposition
- Vendor agreement (links to VS-39 / W27, VS-100 / W230) — the return obligation for vendor-funded displays

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Lingering-display risk**: the product is delisted but the display stays up, letting customers sample a product they can't buy; mitigated by the decommission triggered by the W68 discontinuation, not waiting for a manual catch
- **Asset-loss risk**: a vendor-owned display is scrapped instead of returned, forfeiting the deposit/credit; mitigated by the return-obligation check in step 1 against the W2347 agreement

### Automation Opportunity
- Decommission auto-triggered by the W68 product discontinuation; return-obligation auto-surfaced from the agreement master

### Controls
- CTL-198: asset-grade display disposition; operational: Merch Coordinator decommission sign-off per program

### Time Estimate
~1.5 weeks/decommission × ~75 decommissions/year + ~4 hours/store removal ≈ ~300 hours = **~200–400 hours/year** Merch Coordinator + Facilities + Store Managers.
