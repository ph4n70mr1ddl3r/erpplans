## W1083. Store-Level Customer Plumbing Fixture & Fitting Compatibility Verification Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer brings a plumbing fixture, fitting, valve, or pipe sample to the store for matching, or requests help designing a plumbing layout for a residential or small commercial project |
| **Frequency** | ~8,000–12,000 customer interactions/month across all stores (plumbing is 12% of SKUs, ~4,200 items) |
| **Volume** | 1–5 fixture compatibility checks per customer visit |
| **Owner** | Plumbing Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Plumbing Section), Customer |

### Background

Plumbing fixture compatibility is one of the most confusing aspects of DIY home improvement in the Philippines. The Philippine plumbing market has a mix of standards: (a) BSP (British Standard Pipe) threaded fittings — the dominant standard for galvanized iron (GI) pipes and brass fittings in the Philippines, available in ½", ¾", 1", 1¼", 1½", and 2" nominal sizes; (b) NPT (National Pipe Thread) — found on some imported fixtures, particularly from US-branded products; (c) compression fittings for PPR (polypropylene random copolymer) pipes — increasingly popular for new construction, using heat-fusion joints rather than threaded connections; (d) push-fit connectors (like SharkBite) — gaining market share for repair work; (e) PVC solvent-weld fittings — for DWV (drain-waste-vent) systems in sizes 2", 3", 4". Filipino homeowners frequently encounter mismatched threads, incompatible pipe materials (joining GI to PPR to PVC), faucet-to-valve incompatibilities (wall-mounted vs. deck-mounted, single-hole vs. widespread), and drain assembly mismatches (Philippine sinks often use different overflow and waste fitting dimensions than imported ones). The resulting return rate on plumbing fixtures is the highest of any category at BuildRight (~12–15%), largely driven by incompatibility rather than defect. A structured compatibility verification service at the plumbing counter reduces returns, increases basket size through correct companion product sales, and builds customer trust. This workflow complements W989 (plumbing layout design) which focuses on the overall pipe routing and material takeoff; this workflow specifically addresses the point-of-selection compatibility verification at the fixture and fitting level.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer Project & Fixture Context Gathering**: Sales Associate identifies the customer's need: (a) replacement part matching — customer brings existing fixture/fitting for replacement; (b) new installation — customer is building or renovating and needs a complete plumbing fixture set; (c) repair — customer has a leak or break and needs the correct repair part; (d) upgrade — customer wants to replace an older fixture with a modern equivalent. Associate records: fixture location (bathroom, kitchen, laundry, outdoor), fixture type (faucet, valve, drain, toilet, shower head, water heater connection), existing pipe material visible at installation point (GI, PPR, PVC, copper, PE), and approximate pipe diameter if known | Sales Associate | Plumbing Department Supervisor | 3–5 min |
| 2 | **Physical Sample Inspection & Thread/Material Identification**: For customers bringing samples: (a) Associate measures thread diameter using a thread pitch gauge (BSP threads have ½" BSP ≈ 21mm OD, ¾" BSP ≈ 26.5mm OD, 1" BSP ≈ 33.3mm OD); (b) identifies thread type: male vs. female, tapered (BSPT) vs. parallel (BSPP); (c) identifies material: GI (silver-gray, magnetic), brass (gold/yellow, non-magnetic), PPR (green or white plastic), PVC (white or gray plastic, lighter than PPR), copper (reddish-brown), PE (black flexible tubing); (d) checks seal type required: Teflon tape, pipe sealant (locktite), rubber gasket, O-ring, or solvent cement; (e) for faucets: identifies mounting type (wall-mount, deck-mount, single-hole, centerset, widespread), handle configuration (single-lever, dual-handle), and cartridge type (ceramic disc, compression, ball); (f) for drains: measures drain hole diameter (standard Philippine wash basin: 1¼" or 1½"), checks overflow presence/absence, identifies trap type (P-trap, S-trap, bottle trap) | Sales Associate | Plumbing Department Supervisor | 3–5 min |
| 3 | **System-Assisted Compatibility Lookup**: System performs compatibility matching: (a) system matches the identified thread type, size, and material against compatible products in inventory; (b) cross-reference table maintained in product attribute master (W298) links every faucet model to compatible valves, supply hoses, drain assemblies, and mounting hardware; (c) system flags incompatibilities: e.g., "Selected faucet requires ½" BSP female inlet. Customer's existing supply line is ⅜" compression. Adapter fitting needed: ⅜" compression × ½" BSP male (SKU #XXXX)"; (d) system recommends transition fittings for cross-material joins: GI-to-PPR transition union, PVC-to-GI threaded adapter, PPR-to-copper compression fitting; (e) for toilets: system verifies rough-in distance (standard Philippine: 12" or 10" from wall to closet flange center), bowl shape (round vs. elongated), flush type (gravity, pressure-assisted, dual-flush), and water supply connection type; (f) system displays compatibility status for each selected item: ✓ Compatible, ⚠ Adapter Required (with specific SKU), ✗ Not Compatible (with alternative recommendation) | System | Sales Associate | 2–3 min (automated) |
| 4 | **Complete Fixture Set Assembly & Companion Products**: System generates a complete fixture package with all required accessories: (a) for a faucet installation: faucet body + supply hoses (hot/cold, correct length) + angle stop valves (matching pipe type) + Teflon tape + mounting hardware + escutcheon/deck plate; (b) for a toilet installation: toilet bowl + tank + closet flange + wax ring/sponge gasket + toilet bolts + supply valve + flexible supply hose + toilet seat cover; (c) for a shower installation: shower valve (concealed vs. exposed) + shower head + arm + diverter (if combined with tub spout) + hot/cold supply connections + rough-in valve body; (d) recommended tools: adjustable wrench, basin wrench, pipe cutter (for material type), Teflon tape, plumber's putty, silicone sealant; (e) for trade account customers, system applies contracted pricing per W163 and offers bulk quantity breaks | System / Sales Associate | Plumbing Department Supervisor | 3–5 min |
| 5 | **Estimate Generation & Installation Advisory**: (a) system generates itemized estimate with compatibility notes for each item; (b) installation advisory printed on estimate: "Plumbing installations in the Philippines must comply with the National Plumbing Code (NPC) and local LGU plumbing permit requirements. BuildRight Depot recommends engaging a licensed Master Plumber for installations. This compatibility verification is for material selection assistance only and does not constitute a plumbing design."; (c) for customers needing installation, system offers referral to licensed plumber partners per W904 (contractor referral service); (d) estimate saved to customer loyalty account; (e) system offers to schedule a store-level plumbing consultation appointment for complex projects requiring W989 (plumbing layout design) | Sales Associate | Plumbing Department Supervisor | 2–3 min |

### System Touchpoints
- Product attribute master (W298) — fixture compatibility cross-reference database
- Real-time inventory availability per store (W533)
- POS quotation module (W542) — estimate-to-sale conversion
- Loyalty account integration (W17)
- Return rate analytics dashboard (category-level return tracking by SKU)
- Contractor referral service module (W904)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Thread standard confusion**: BSP vs. NPT mismatch is the #1 cause of plumbing returns; Associates must be trained to measure and identify thread types accurately
- **Mixed-material plumbing systems**: Many Filipino homes have a patchwork of GI, PVC, and PPR pipes from decades of incremental repairs; transition fittings must cover every combination
- **Imported fixture incompatibility**: Imported faucets and fixtures (particularly from China and the US) may use non-BSP threads or non-standard drain sizes; system must flag these and show required adapters
- **Water pressure variation**: Philippine water pressure varies dramatically (from gravity-fed systems at ~15 PSI to pressurized systems at 40–80 PSI); fixture selection (particularly shower valves) must account for local pressure conditions

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate (Plumbing Section)**: Compatibility verification adds 5–10 minutes per consultation; at ~40–60 consultations per store per week, this represents ~5–8 hours/week — absorbed by existing Plumbing Department staff (12 Sales Associates per store covering all departments)
- **Plumbing Department Supervisor**: Handles escalations for unusual compatibility questions; ~1 hour/week
- **No permanent incremental headcount** — the service reduces return processing labor (estimated 2–3 hours/week recovered per store)

### Time Estimate
- Context gathering: 3–5 min
- Sample inspection: 3–5 min
- System compatibility lookup: 2–3 min
- Fixture set assembly: 3–5 min
- Estimate & advisory: 2–3 min
- **Total per consultation**: 13–21 min

## W1084. Customer Home Security & CCTV System Design & Product Recommendation Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests a home security system design, CCTV camera layout, or burglar alarm system recommendation for residential or small commercial property |
| **Frequency** | ~2,000–3,500 customer interactions/month across all stores (growing segment driven by Philippine crime rates and affordable IP camera technology) |
| **Volume** | 1 system design per customer visit |
| **Owner** | Electrical Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Electrical Section), Customer |

### Background

Home security is a rapidly growing category for Philippine hardware retailers. BuildRight carries CCTV cameras (IP and analog), digital video recorders (DVR/NVR), burglar alarm systems, smart locks, motion sensors, door/window contact sensors, and accessories. The Philippine residential security market is driven by: (a) high urban crime rates (break-ins, theft) especially in Metro Manila and growing cities like Davao and Cebu; (b) the proliferation of affordable IP cameras and WiFi-enabled smart security devices from brands like Dahua, Hikvision, TP-Link Tapo, Imou, and EZVIZ; (c) increasing OFW (Overseas Filipino Worker) families who want remote monitoring of their Philippine homes; (d) small business owners (sari-sari stores, workshops, warehouses) who need affordable surveillance; (e) barangay and community security initiatives. The typical Filipino homeowner is not technically trained and struggles with camera selection (resolution, lens type, night vision range), storage sizing (HDD capacity for NVR), network requirements (bandwidth for remote viewing), and power supply planning (PoE vs. separate power adapters). This is distinct from W1071 (smart home system design) which covers the broader smart home ecosystem; this workflow focuses specifically on the security and surveillance function.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Property Assessment & Security Requirements**: Sales Associate gathers property and security details: (a) property type: single-detached house, townhouse, condominium, apartment, small commercial (store, workshop, office); (b) property size: lot area (sqm), number of floors, perimeter length (meters); (c) entry points: number of doors (main, back, side, garage), number of ground-floor windows, garage/carport presence; (d) security priorities: perimeter monitoring, entry point detection, indoor monitoring, vehicle monitoring, nanny/caregiver monitoring; (e) remote access requirement: customer wants to view cameras from mobile phone (yes/no), frequency of remote viewing, number of household members who need access; (f) internet connectivity: WiFi available (speed if known), wired Ethernet availability at planned camera locations, cellular signal strength (for 4G cameras in remote areas); (g) power availability: outlets near planned camera locations, willingness to run new electrical wiring; (h) budget range: entry-level (PHP 3,000–8,000), mid-range (PHP 8,000–20,000), premium (PHP 20,000–50,000+) | Sales Associate | Electrical Department Supervisor | 5–8 min |
| 2 | **CCTV Camera Layout & Coverage Design**: System generates camera placement plan: (a) perimeter cameras: one camera per 8–10 meters of boundary wall/fence coverage, mounted at 2.5–3m height, wide-angle lens (2.8mm for 100° FOV or 3.6mm for 85° FOV); (b) entry point cameras: dedicated camera for each main door and vehicle gate; (c) outdoor cameras: IP67 weather-rated minimum, IR night vision range ≥ 20m, true WDR (wide dynamic range) for backlit entrances; (d) indoor cameras: for monitoring main living areas, choose dome-style (less obtrusive) with 2.8mm lens; (e) camera resolution recommendation based on budget: 2MP (1080p) minimum for general monitoring, 4MP/5MP for license plate recognition at gates, 8MP (4K) for premium detail; (f) special cameras: PTZ (pan-tilt-zoom) for large open areas, doorbell camera (WiFi) for main entrance, 4G solar camera for areas without power/WiFi; (g) system outputs a camera count and a recommended placement floor plan sketch | System | Sales Associate | 3–5 min (automated) |
| 3 | **Recording, Storage & Network Design**: System specifies recording infrastructure: (a) NVR (network video recorder) channel count = camera count + 20% spare capacity for future expansion; (b) storage calculation: recording days desired × cameras × bitrate × hours/day ÷ 8 ÷ 1024 = TB required (typical: 4 cameras at 2Mbps × 24hrs × 30 days = ~630GB → 1TB HDD minimum); (c) HDD selection: surveillance-rated HDD (WD Purple, Seagate SkyHawk) required (standard desktop HDDs fail under 24/7 write load); (d) network design: for IP cameras on existing WiFi — max 2–3 cameras per WiFi access point to avoid bandwidth congestion; for 4+ cameras — recommend PoE (Power over Ethernet) NVR with built-in PoE switch for reliability; (e) remote viewing: system confirms mobile app compatibility (iOS/Android), cloud storage options (optional subscription), and router configuration requirements (port forwarding or P2P cloud access) | System | Electrical Department Supervisor | 3–4 min (automated) |
| 4 | **Alarm & Accessory Selection**: System recommends complementary security components: (a) door/window magnetic contact sensors for entry point detection; (b) PIR (passive infrared) motion sensors for indoor zones; (c) siren/strobe alarm (120dB minimum for audible deterrence); (d) smart lock for main door (fingerprint, PIN, RFID, or Bluetooth); (e) UPS (uninterruptible power supply) for NVR and network — critical in Philippines where power outages are common and burglars may cut power; (f) cable accessories: Cat6 Ethernet cable (for PoE cameras), power extension cables, cable conduits, junction boxes; (g) mounting hardware: camera brackets, pole mounts, cable clips; (h) system recommends installation tools: drill, Ethernet crimper, fish tape, cable tester | System / Sales Associate | Electrical Department Supervisor | 3–4 min |
| 5 | **Complete System BOM, Estimate & Advisory**: (a) system generates complete BOM with itemized pricing: cameras, NVR, HDD, cables, sensors, accessories, UPS, tools; (b) for trade/B2B customers, applies contract pricing per W163; (c) estimate includes: camera count summary, storage capacity confirmation, network requirement summary, estimated installation time (DIY: 4–8 hours for 4-camera system; professional: 1–2 days); (d) advisory printed: "CCTV installation is not regulated in the Philippines for residential use. However, cameras must not be pointed at neighboring properties' private areas. Audio recording without consent may violate RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act). BuildRight Depot recommends professional installation for optimal camera positioning and cable management."; (e) system offers referral to licensed security system installers per W904; (f) estimate saved to customer loyalty account | Sales Associate | Electrical Department Supervisor | 3–5 min |

### System Touchpoints
- Product attribute master (W298) — camera specifications, NVR compatibility, HDD compatibility matrix
- Real-time inventory availability per store (W533)
- POS quotation module (W542)
- Loyalty account integration (W17)
- Contractor referral service module (W904)
- BOM/kit assembly module (W46)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **WiFi reliability**: Many customers underestimate WiFi limitations; cameras on WiFi may experience dropouts, especially in Philippine concrete homes (hollow block/CHB walls severely attenuate WiFi signals); PoE recommendation must be emphasized
- **Power outage vulnerability**: Philippines experiences frequent power interruptions; without a UPS, the security system is offline during outages — precisely when break-ins may occur; UPS must be recommended in every system design
- **Storage technology confusion**: Customers often select standard desktop HDDs instead of surveillance-rated HDDs, leading to premature failure and loss of footage; system must enforce surveillance-HDD-only recommendation
- **Privacy considerations**: RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act) implications for audio recording and neighbor-facing cameras must be communicated

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate (Electrical Section)**: Security system design consultation adds 15–25 minutes per customer; at ~10–18 consultations per store per week, this represents ~3–6 hours/week — absorbed by existing Electrical Department staff
- **No permanent incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Property assessment: 5–8 min
- Camera layout design: 3–5 min
- Storage & network design: 3–4 min
- Alarm & accessory selection: 3–4 min
- BOM & advisory: 3–5 min
- **Total per consultation**: 17–26 min

## W1085. Store-Level Appliance Display Model Lifecycle & Periodic Refresh Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled quarterly appliance display rotation, new model introduction by vendor, display model reaching maximum display age threshold (typically 90–120 days), or vendor-initiated display refresh program |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly rotation + ad-hoc vendor refreshes (~4–6 refresh cycles per year per store) |
| **Volume** | 15–25 appliance display units per store per rotation cycle (appliances are 5% of SKUs, ~1,750 items, with ~20–30 units on active display per store at any time) |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Appliances Section) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Appliance Section), Receiving Clerk, Store Manager, Merchandising Category Manager (HQ) |

### Background

Home appliances (fans, air conditioners, water heaters, kitchen appliances) represent ~5% of BuildRight's active SKUs (~1,750 items) and a significant portion of revenue per unit. Unlike tools or hardware, appliances are high-value items (PHP 3,000–50,000+) that customers want to see, touch, and operate before purchasing. Each BuildRight store maintains approximately 20–30 appliance display units across categories: ceiling fans, stand fans, wall fans, window-type air conditioners, split-type air conditioners, electric and LPG stoves, water heaters (tank and tankless), refrigerators (selected stores), and washing machines (selected stores). Display models have a finite lifecycle: they accumulate cosmetic damage, become outdated when newer models launch, consume floor space that could be allocated to faster-moving new products, and lose visual appeal. However, display models also represent significant locked-up capital — a single split-type AC unit on display may cost PHP 25,000–40,000. The display model lifecycle workflow ensures that: (a) displays are refreshed at optimal intervals to maintain visual appeal and showcase current models; (b) retired display units are monetized through demo-unit sales at discounted prices (per W524); (c) vendors participate in display costs through co-op funding per W513; (d) display inventory is accurately tracked and valued in the ERP. This is distinct from W97 (sample & demo inventory management) which covers the broader policy; this workflow addresses the operational execution of appliance display rotation and the specific retail challenges of large appliance units.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Display Age & Condition Assessment**: (a) system generates quarterly display aging report for each store: listing all appliance display units by SKU, date placed on display, days on display, and original cost; (b) Department Supervisor physically inspects each display unit: cosmetic condition (scratches, dents, discoloration), functional condition (powered on, all features working, remote control present), cleanliness, shelf label accuracy (correct model number, price, specifications), and visual merchandising compliance (per planogram per W314); (c) Supervisor rates each unit: Grade A (like-new, suitable for continued display), Grade B (minor cosmetic wear, can continue display with discount note), Grade C (significant wear, functional but visually impaired, candidate for immediate retirement); (d) assessment results entered into ERP against each display unit's serial number | Department Supervisor | Store Manager | 2–3 hrs per assessment (quarterly) |
| 2 | **Rotation Decision & Vendor Coordination**: (a) for units exceeding 90-day display age: system flags for rotation review; (b) Category Manager (HQ) reviews flags and determines rotation plan: (i) extend display period for strong-selling models with Grade A condition; (ii) initiate vendor-funded display refresh for models where vendor has co-op display program per W513; (iii) retire and sell display unit as demo model per W524; (iv) transfer to another store in the region where the model is not yet on display per W22 (stock transfers); (c) for vendor-funded refresh: Category Manager contacts vendor for new display unit shipment and return/replacement of old unit; (d) new model introduction: when a vendor launches a new appliance model, Category Manager schedules phased rollout across stores (not all 200 stores receive simultaneously; priority by region and store tier); (e) rotation schedule communicated to each affected store via ERP task notification | Category Manager (HQ) | VP Merchandising | 4–6 hrs per quarterly cycle |
| 3 | **Display Unit Retirement & Demo Sale Preparation**: For units designated for retirement: (a) Department Supervisor powers down, disconnects, and cleans unit; (b) Receiving Clerk performs condition report: documents all cosmetic defects with photos, confirms all accessories present (remote, manual, mounting hardware, warranty card), and verifies functional test; (c) system reclassifies inventory from "Display Asset" to "Demo/Open-Box Saleable" per W549 (damaged & open-box discount processing); (d) system calculates demo sale price: original SRP × discount factor based on condition grade (Grade A: 10–15% off SRP, Grade B: 20–30% off SRP, Grade C: 35–50% off SRP); (e) demo price approved by Store Manager for Grade A/B discounts; Grade C discounts require Category Manager approval via ERP workflow; (f) unit placed on demo sale display area with prominent "Display Model Sale" signage, condition disclosure, and modified warranty terms (typically: 6-month store warranty instead of manufacturer's full warranty) | Department Supervisor / Receiving Clerk | Store Manager | 30–45 min per unit |
| 4 | **New Display Unit Receipt & Setup**: (a) Receiving Clerk receives new display unit from vendor or DC transfer; (b) unboxes and performs initial inspection for shipping damage; (c) Department Supervisor sets up unit on display floor: mounts/positions per planogram (W314), connects to power (ensuring proper electrical load per W985 if air conditioner), programs demo mode (continuous operation mode for display), attaches price tag and feature callout cards, updates shelf label in ERP per W181; (d) system updates display tracking record: new SKU, serial number, date placed on display, expected rotation date (90 days), display location (aisle, bay, shelf position); (e) for air conditioners: Store Manager ensures dedicated electrical circuit for display to avoid tripping store breakers; (f) old display unit's serial number is deactivated from display tracking and activated in demo sale inventory | Department Supervisor / Receiving Clerk | Store Manager | 45–60 min per unit |
| 5 | **Display Inventory Reconciliation & Financial Reporting**: (a) monthly: system generates display inventory valuation report per store showing total capital locked in display models, aging profile, and demo sale revenue; (b) quarterly: Category Manager reviews display ROI — sales generated per display unit vs. capital employed; (c) vendor co-op display funding reconciliation: system matches vendor display invoices against co-op advertising budget per W513; (d) demo sale revenue tracked separately in P&L per store; (e) inventory accuracy: display units included in cycle count per W6 (cycle counting) — verified by serial number during monthly count | System / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | Monthly report: 30 min; Quarterly review: 2–3 hrs |

### System Touchpoints
- Display inventory tracking module (serialized unit tracking with display status flag)
- Inventory reclassification module (display asset → demo saleable)
- Planogram & space planning module (W314)
- Vendor co-op advertising & display funding module (W513)

- **Capital lock-up**: 20–30 display units per store × PHP 10,000 average cost = PHP 200,000–300,000 per store in display capital; across 200 stores = PHP 40M–60M total display inventory; must be managed tightly
- **Display damage risk**: Appliance display units are handled by hundreds of customers per week; damage rates are high (~15–20% of units accumulate significant cosmetic wear within 90 days)
- **Electrical load constraints**: Running multiple air conditioners and appliances on display simultaneously draws significant power; store electrical circuits must be designed to handle display load without tripping breakers
- **Vendor display funding disputes**: Vendors may dispute co-op display charges if rotation timing doesn't align with their new model launch schedule; clear contractual terms required per W62 (vendor contract lifecycle)

### Staffing Implication
- **Department Supervisor (Appliances)**: Display rotation adds ~2–3 hours per quarterly cycle for assessment and setup; absorbed by existing role
- **Receiving Clerk**: Additional receipt and setup work adds ~30–45 min per unit; at ~5–8 units per quarterly rotation per store, this is ~3–6 hours/quarter; absorbed by existing role
- **No permanent incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Quarterly assessment: 2–3 hrs per store
- Rotation decision & vendor coordination: 4–6 hrs (HQ, quarterly)
- Retirement & demo sale prep: 30–45 min per unit
- New display setup: 45–60 min per unit
- Monthly reconciliation: 30 min
- **Total per quarterly cycle**: ~8–12 hrs per store + 4–6 hrs HQ

## W1086. Customer Construction Waste Disposal & Skip/Dumpster Rental Coordination

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | B2B contractor or B2C renovation customer requests construction waste disposal solution, or BuildRight proactively offers waste disposal coordination as a value-added service during large project quotation (W162) |
| **Frequency** | ~1,500–2,500 coordination requests/month across all stores (construction waste disposal is a high-need service for BuildRight's B2B contractor base of ~5,000 trade accounts) |
| **Volume** | 1 skip/dumpster rental coordination per project |
| **Owner** | Trade Counter / Pro Desk Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Trade Counter), Partner Waste Hauler, Customer |

### Background

Construction waste disposal is a significant pain point for Filipino contractors and homeowners undertaking renovation projects. Philippine construction waste — concrete rubble, hollow block debris, rebar cut-offs, tiles and ceramic fragments, lumber scraps, paint cans, PVC pipe offcuts, wire scraps, packaging materials (cement bags, tile boxes), and general renovation debris — must be disposed of at LGU-approved disposal sites. Unauthorized dumping is illegal under DENR regulations (RA 9003 — Ecological Solid Waste Management Act) and carries significant penalties. However, the Philippine waste management infrastructure is fragmented: (a) most LGUs do not provide construction waste collection; (b) private waste haulers operate independently with varying reliability and pricing; (c) contractors typically hire dump trucks on an ad-hoc basis, which is expensive for small-to-medium jobs and risks illegal dumping by unscrupulous haulers; (d) homeowners doing DIY renovations have no easy way to arrange construction waste removal. BuildRight's position as the primary materials supplier for these projects creates a natural opportunity to offer construction waste disposal coordination as a value-added service — similar to how BuildRight coordinates home delivery. This service does not require BuildRight to own waste hauling assets; instead, it partners with vetted waste haulers in each store's service area and earns a coordination fee or commission per booking. This is distinct from W193 (waste management & circular economy) which covers BuildRight's own operational waste; this workflow addresses customer construction waste generated from materials purchased at BuildRight. It is also distinct from W502 (store-level non-hazardous waste management) which covers the store's own waste.

## W1090. Customer HVAC Ductwork Design & Complete Material Sizing Recommendation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests a complete air conditioning system design including ductwork layout for a residential or small commercial project, or the consultation from W985 (aircon BTU calculation) identifies a ducted/central system requirement |

| **Volume** | 1 system design per consultation |
| **Owner** | Electrical Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Appliance/Electrical Section), Customer |

### Background

While W985 covers room-by-room BTU/horsepower sizing for window and split-type air conditioners, it does not address ducted/central air conditioning systems which require ductwork design, air distribution planning, and a significantly more complex material list. Ducted systems are increasingly popular in the Philippine market for: (a) upscale residential homes (6+ bedrooms, 200+ sqm floor area) in Metro Manila, Cebu, and Davao subdivisions; (b) small commercial establishments (restaurants, offices, clinics, retail shops); (c)BuildRight's B2B corporate customers undertaking commercial fit-outs. A ducted system involves: (a) a concealed ducted unit (indoor unit installed in the ceiling space) connected via insulated flexible or galvanized sheet metal ductwork to ceiling diffusers in each room; (b) a condensing unit (outdoor unit) on the roof or ground level; (c) refrigerant piping (copper) between indoor and outdoor units; (d) condensate drain piping (PVC) from indoor unit to drainage; (e) electrical supply (dedicated circuit per W1064); (f) controls (thermostat, zone dampers for multi-room control). The ductwork sizing requires: (a) airflow calculation (CFM — cubic feet per minute) per room based on cooling load; (b) duct diameter calculation based on CFM and acceptable velocity (≤ 800 FPM for branch ducts, ≤ 1,200 FPM for main trunk); (c) supply air register sizing; (d) return air grille sizing; (e) insulation specification (thermal insulation for ducts in unconditioned spaces — Philippine ceiling spaces reach 45–55°C). This workflow extends W985 to cover the complete ducted system design and material list, and integrates with W1064 for the electrical panel requirements.

### Background

BuildRight's ~5,000 trade account customers include contractors, builders, and small construction firms who purchase the same materials repeatedly for ongoing projects or for their regular business operations. ## W1095. Store-Level Customer Electrical Residential Wiring Diagram & Material List Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|

| **Volume** | 1 wiring diagram and material list per consultation |
| **Owner** | Electrical Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Electrical Section), Customer |

### Background

While W1064 covers panel board and circuit breaker sizing, and W982 covers electrical load calculation and wire sizing, there is no existing workflow that produces a complete residential wiring diagram showing the routing of all circuits from the panel board to every outlet, switch, light fixture, and appliance connection point. The typical Filipino residential electrical project involves: (a) service entrance from the utility pole (MERALCO, Davao Light, or electric cooperative) to the meter base to the main panel; (b) branch circuits from the panel to every point of use throughout the house; (c) each circuit consisting of: wire (THHN in EMT conduit, or NM-B cable where permitted), junction boxes, switches, outlets, light fixtures, and connections. The Philippine Electrical Code (PEC) 2017 mandates specific requirements: minimum wire sizes per circuit, maximum number of outlets per circuit, GFCI protection for wet areas, proper grounding, and separate circuits for major appliances. A complete wiring diagram with a matching material list helps the customer: (a) understand the full scope of electrical materials needed (and their cost); (b) provide their electrician or electrical engineer with a clear materials specification; (c) purchase the correct quantities of wire, conduit, boxes, switches, and outlets in a single trip; (d) avoid the common problem of under-purchasing wire ( Filipino electricians often request additional rolls mid-project, causing delays). This workflow integrates with W1064 (panel design) and W982 (load calculation) to produce a comprehensive electrical material package.

## W1096. Customer Solar Water Heater System Sizing & Complete Package Recommendation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer inquires about solar water heating options, or Sales Associate identifies solar water heater applicability during a water heater consultation or solar panel consultation per W990 |
| **Frequency** | ~600–1,200 consultations/month across all stores (growing segment driven by Philippine electricity costs and DOE renewable energy promotion) |
| **Volume** | 1 system design per consultation |
| **Owner** | Electrical Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Electrical/Appliance Section), Customer |

### Background

Solar water heating (SWH) is a mature technology well-suited to the Philippines' tropical climate with abundant sunshine (average 5–6 peak sun hours per day year-round). The Philippine Department of Energy (DOE) actively promotes solar water heating through the Renewable Energy Act (RA 9513) and the Philippine Energy Efficiency and Conservation Act (RA 11285). For BuildRight customers, solar water heaters offer: (a) electricity savings of PHP 2,000–5,000/month compared to electric water heaters (Philippine electricity rates are among the highest in Southeast Asia at PHP 9–12/kWh); (b) hot water availability even during power outages (passive thermosiphon systems require no electricity); (c) environmental benefits (reducing carbon footprint per W192). Two main SWH technologies are available: (a) **Evacuated tube collectors (ETC)**: more efficient in cloudy conditions, lighter weight, higher operating temperatures, but more fragile; (b) **Flat plate collectors (FPC)**: more durable, lower cost, good for Philippine sunny conditions. Systems can be: (i) **passive (thermosiphon)**: water circulates naturally by convection — no pump, no electricity needed, tank must be mounted above collector; (ii) **active (pumped)**: uses a circulation pump and controller — more flexible installation (tank can be at ground level) but requires electricity for the pump. This workflow is distinct from W990 (solar panel system) which covers photovoltaic (PV) solar for electricity generation; this workflow addresses solar thermal for water heating.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hot Water Demand Assessment**: Sales Associate gathers hot water usage information: (a) household size (number of persons); (b) bathrooms with hot water: count and type (shower only, shower + bathtub); (c) kitchen hot water: yes/no (sink hot water tap); (d) laundry hot water: yes/no (washing machine hot water connection); (e) hot water usage pattern: morning peak (bathing), evening peak (bathing + kitchen), or both; (f) current water heating method: electric storage, electric tankless, LPG, or none; (g) desired water temperature (comfortable shower: 38–42°C); (h) cold water inlet temperature: Philippine municipal water ~25–28°C, deep well ~26–29°C; (i) roof type and orientation: flat concrete roof or pitched roof (G.I. sheet or tile), orientation (south-facing optimal in Philippines), available roof area for collector; (j) roof structural capacity: solar water heater + water tank = 150–300 kg — Filipino concrete flat roofs can typically support this, but lightweight G.I. sheet roofs may require structural reinforcement | Sales Associate | Electrical Department Supervisor | 5–8 min |

### Background

Emergency home repairs are one of the most common reasons Filipino homeowners visit a hardware store. The Philippine climate and housing stock create recurring repair needs: (a) **roof leaks** during rainy season (June–November) — G.I. sheet roofing develops rust holes, flashing deteriorates, ridge cap gaps open; (b) **plumbing leaks** — pipe corrosion (especially in older GI pipe systems), faucet washer failure, toilet valve failure; (c) **electrical issues** — tripped breakers during brownouts, faulty outlets, damaged wiring after flooding; (d) **window/door damage** — broken glass from typhoon debris, swollen wooden doors from humidity, broken hinges; (e) **clogged drains** — common in Philippine homes due to insufficient drain pipe diameter and tree root intrusion; (f) **wall cracks and leaks** — concrete hollow block walls develop cracks from settling and seismic activity (Philippines is earthquake-prone). ## W1100. Customer Contractor Project Financing & Supplier Credit Line Partnership Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | BuildRight's B2B contractor customer (trade account) requests extended payment terms or project-specific financing beyond their standard credit limit per W24, or Category Manager proactively offers project financing to high-value contractor accounts during quarterly business review per W617 |
| **Frequency** | ~500–800 financing applications/month across all stores (targeting the top 20% of BuildRight's 5,000 trade accounts by project value) |
| **Volume** | 1 financing arrangement per contractor project |
| **Owner** | Finance Manager (Treasury / Credit) |
| **Participants** | Trade Counter Supervisor, Finance Manager, Partner Bank/Financing Institution, Customer (Contractor), Category Manager |

### Background

BuildRight's contractor customers face a chronic working capital challenge: they purchase materials upfront (from BuildRight), perform the construction work over weeks or months, and only get paid by the project owner upon milestone completion or project handover. This cash flow gap limits: (a) the contractor's ability to take on larger projects (they can't afford to buy all materials upfront); (b) BuildRight's ability to sell larger material packages (the contractor's credit limit constrains order size); (c) the contractor's ability to purchase premium products (they default to cheaper alternatives to conserve cash). The Philippine construction industry's payment terms exacerbate this: (a) residential homeowners typically pay contractors in progress payments (20% down, 30% at rough-in, 30% at finishing, 20% upon turnover); (b) commercial and government projects often have 30–60 day payment terms after milestone certification; (c) retention of 10% is held for 12 months after project completion as warranty security. BuildRight's standard trade account credit terms (Net 30 or Net 60 per W24) are insufficient for large projects spanning 3–12 months. This workflow establishes a structured partnership with banks and financing institutions to provide project-specific financing to BuildRight's contractor customers, with BuildRight acting as the referral channel and material supplier. This is distinct from W953 (contractor micro-lending) which targets small working capital loans; this workflow addresses larger project-specific financing (PHP 500,000–10,000,000). It is also distinct from W1088 (mortgage & housing loan referral) which targets B2C home buyers; this workflow targets B2B contractors.

## W1101. Store-Level Customer Door & Window Security Grille Custom Fabrication Coordination

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests custom-made security grilles for doors or windows during a building material purchase, or Sales Associate identifies the need during a fencing/security consultation per W1084 |
| **Frequency** | ~3,000–5,000 coordination requests/month across all stores (security grilles are a standard feature in Philippine homes — nearly every ground-floor window and door has steel security grilles) |
| **Volume** | 1 fabrication project per coordination (typically covering 4–15 windows + 1–2 doors) |
| **Owner** | Trade Counter / Pro Desk Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Trade Counter / Lumber & Building Materials), Fabrication Service Partner (accredited welder/fabricator), Customer |

### Background

Steel security grilles (locally called "berde" or "rehas" in Filipino) are an essential feature of Philippine residential construction. Due to property crime concerns in both urban and rural areas, virtually every Filipino home installs steel grilles on all ground-floor windows, main doors, and back doors. The standard Philippine security grille is fabricated from: (a) flat bar steel (typically ¼" × 1" or 6mm × 25mm) for the main frame; (b) square tube (16mm × 16mm or 20mm × 20mm) or round bar (12mm or 16mm diameter) for the vertical bars; (c) welding (arc or MIG) at all joints; (d) anti-rust primer and enamel paint finish (black or custom color per W1093). The grilles are custom-made to fit each window or door opening exactly, because Philippine window sizes are not standardized — they vary by house design, builder, and era of construction. The fabrication process requires: (a) on-site measurement of each window/door opening; (b) custom cutting, welding, and finishing at the fabricator's workshop; (c) delivery and installation at the customer's home; (d) anchoring to the concrete or hollow block wall using expansion bolts or embedded steel brackets. This workflow coordinates the custom fabrication process from measurement through installation, connecting BuildRight's material supply (steel, paint, hardware) with accredited fabrication partners.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Measurement Requirements & Design Specifications**: (a) Sales Associate gathers grille specifications: (i) number of windows requiring grilles and approximate dimensions (width × height per window); (ii) number of doors requiring security doors and approximate dimensions; (iii) bar style preference: vertical bars only (most common and cost-effective), vertical + horizontal (cross-hatch for higher security), or decorative scrollwork (premium); (iv) bar spacing: standard (100–120mm between bars — prevents head entry) or tight (80mm — maximum security for high-risk areas); (v) frame material: flat bar (¼" × 1") or angle bar (1" × 1" × ¼"); (vi) bar material: square tube (16mm, 20mm) — modern look, or round bar (12mm, 16mm) — traditional; (vii) finish: primer + black enamel (standard), primer + color-matched enamel (per W1093), powder coating (premium), or stainless steel (highest grade, coastal areas); (viii) hinged or fixed: hinged grilles for windows that need opening (for cleaning or emergency egress) vs. fixed grilles; (ix) locking mechanism for hinged grilles: barrel bolt, padlock hasp, or keyed lock | Sales Associate | Trade Counter Supervisor | 8–12 min |
| 2 | **Fabrication Partner Matching & Quotation**: (a) system queries accredited fabrication partners (welding shops) in the customer's area per W600 (service contractor accreditation); (b) system generates material cost estimate: (i) steel material: flat bar × total perimeter length (all windows/doors) + square tube/round bar × total bar count × standard bar length; (ii) welding consumables: welding rods/electrodes (E6013 or E7018), cutting gas (if oxy-acetylene); (iii) paint: anti-rust primer (red oxide, 1 coat) + enamel paint (2 coats) — quantity per surface area of finished grilles; (iv) hardware: expansion bolts (4 per window grille, 6 per door grille), hinges (2 per hinged grille), barrel bolts/locks, and miscellaneous (wire brush, sandpaper for surface prep); (c) system estimates fabrication labor: cutting, welding, grinding, and finishing — based on linear meters of welding and number of pieces; (d) system estimates installation labor: delivery, fitting, anchoring, and cleanup; (e) total project cost estimate = material cost + fabrication labor + installation labor; (f) typical cost ranges: window grille (1.0m × 1.2m, standard vertical bars, primer + black enamel): PHP 2,500–4,000 per window; door security gate (0.9m × 2.1m, hinged with lock): PHP 6,000–12,000 per door | System | Trade Counter Supervisor | 3–5 min (automated) |
| 3 | **On-Site Measurement & Fabrication Order**: (a) for accuracy: Associate recommends that the fabrication partner perform on-site measurement before fabrication (Philippine window openings often deviate from planned dimensions by 10–30mm); (b) system schedules on-site measurement visit: partner visits customer's home within 2–3 days to measure every opening precisely; (c) partner sends measurement sheet to BuildRight system: exact width, height, and any irregularities (arched top, angled sill, pipe/conduit obstruction); (d) system generates final fabrication order with precise dimensions and updates material quantities accordingly; (e) material delivery: BuildRight delivers steel, paint, and hardware to the fabrication partner's workshop (not to the customer's home — the partner fabricates at their workshop) per W668 | Trade Counter Supervisor / Fabrication Partner | Trade Counter Supervisor | Measurement visit: 1–2 hrs; Order update: 15 min |
| 4 | **Fabrication, Quality Check & Installation**: (a) fabrication partner fabricates grilles per the measurement sheet: (i) cuts flat bar and square tube/round bar to size; (ii) assembles frame by welding; (iii) welds vertical bars at specified spacing; (iv) grinds all welds smooth (no sharp edges); (v) installs hinges and lock hardware (for hinged grilles); (vi) applies anti-rust primer (1 coat) and enamel paint (2 coats) with drying time between coats; (b) fabrication timeline: 3–7 days depending on scope (4–6 windows: 3 days; 10–15 windows + 2 doors: 5–7 days); (c) Trade Counter Supervisor performs quality check (or partner self-certifies per W600 accreditation): (i) dimensions match measurement sheet (±5mm tolerance); (ii) weld quality: no visible gaps, porosity, or undercut; (iii) bar spacing uniform and within specification; (iv) paint coverage: complete, no bare steel visible, smooth finish; (v) hinges operate smoothly, locks engage properly; (d) partner delivers and installs grilles at customer's home: anchors to wall with expansion bolts, seals around frame with silicone sealant, and verifies operation | Fabrication Partner / Trade Counter Supervisor | Trade Counter Supervisor | Fabrication: 3–7 days; Installation: 1 day |
| 5 | **Customer Acceptance, Settlement & Warranty**: (a) customer inspects installed grilles: verifies fit, finish, operation, and security; (b) customer signs acceptance; (c) system generates final invoice: material cost (BuildRight) + fabrication labor (partner) + installation labor (partner) = total project cost; (d) payment: (i) for trade account customers: added to monthly billing per W8; (ii) for B2C customers: 50% deposit at order placement, 50% upon installation acceptance; (e) BuildRight pays partner: fabrication + installation labor minus BuildRight coordination fee (8–12%); (f) warranty: fabrication partner provides 1-year workmanship warranty (weld integrity, paint adhesion); BuildRight provides material quality warranty per manufacturer terms; (g) system sends customer satisfaction survey and updates partner performance record per W600 | System / Trade Counter Supervisor | Finance Manager | Settlement: automated; Satisfaction: 5 min |

## W1102. Customer Retaining Wall & Foundation Formwork Material Estimation Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests material estimation for a retaining wall or foundation formwork project, or Sales Associate identifies the need during a construction material consultation for a sloped lot or multi-level project |
| **Frequency** | ~2,000–3,000 consultations/month across all stores (retaining walls are common in Philippine hillside and terraced construction; foundation formwork is needed for most new construction projects) |
| **Volume** | 1 retaining wall or formwork estimation per consultation |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Lumber & Building Materials) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Lumber/Building Materials Section), Customer |

### Background

Retaining walls and foundation formwork are critical structural elements in Philippine construction. The Philippines' mountainous and hilly terrain (especially in areas like Baguio, Cebu, Davao's hillside subdivisions, and Rizal province) creates widespread need for retaining walls: (a) **garden/landscape retaining walls**: low walls (0.3–1.0m height) for terraced gardens and landscaping, typically built with hollow blocks (CHB) or stone; (b) **structural retaining walls**: medium walls (1.0–3.0m height) for hillside lot leveling, built with reinforced concrete (RC) cantilever or gravity walls; (c) **major retaining walls**: high walls (>3.0m) for commercial and subdivision development, requiring structural engineering design. Foundation formwork is needed for all new construction projects that involve reinforced concrete: footings, columns, beams, and slabs. The formwork (also called "shuttering") is the temporary mold into which concrete is poured — typically made from lumber (plywood sheets and timber studs/wales) or steel formwork panels. This workflow addresses the material estimation for: (a) CHB/concrete block retaining walls; (b) reinforced concrete retaining walls; (c) foundation formwork (footings, columns, beams). This integrates with W976 (lumber grade selection) for formwork lumber, W1044 (concrete mix calculator) for concrete quantities, and W986 (rebar cutting and bending) for reinforcement steel.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Project Specification & Structural Input**: Sales Associate gathers project details: (a) **For retaining walls**: (i) wall height (meters) and total wall length (meters); (ii) retained soil height (may differ from wall height if wall is partially embedded); (iii) soil type: clay (high lateral pressure), sandy (moderate), gravel (low); (iv) surcharge load: is there a structure, driveway, or slope above the wall? (affects wall design); (v) wall type preference: CHB (hollow block — for walls ≤ 1.5m), reinforced concrete cantilever (for walls 1.0–3.0m), or gravity wall (stone/mass concrete — for walls ≤ 2.0m); (vi) drainage provision: weep holes (required for all retaining walls — prevents hydrostatic pressure buildup behind the wall); (b) **For foundation formwork**: (i) foundation type: isolated footing, continuous footing, mat foundation, or pile cap; (ii) number and dimensions of footings (length × width × depth); (iii) column dimensions and heights (from footing to beam level); (iv) beam dimensions (width × depth) and total beam length; (v) slab area and thickness (if slab formwork needed); (vi) formwork material preference: lumber (plywood + timber) — most common in the Philippines, or steel panels — for large projects; (c) **Site conditions**: (i) accessibility for material delivery; (ii) water table level (affects concrete mix and waterproofing needs); (iii) seismic zone (Philippines is Zone 4 — highest seismic risk per NSCP, affects reinforcement requirements) | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 8–12 min |
| 2 | **Retaining Wall Material Calculation**: System calculates retaining wall materials based on wall type: **For CHB wall (≤ 1.5m height)**: (a) CHB quantity = wall area (length × height) ÷ CHB face area (0.2m × 0.4m = 0.08 sqm per 4" CHB or 0.2m × 0.2m = 0.04 sqm per 6" CHB) × 1.05 (5% wastage); (b) cement mortar: 1 bag cement per 70 pieces of 4" CHB or 1 bag per 50 pieces of 6" CHB; sand: 0.08 cubic meters per bag of cement; (c) reinforcement: for CHB walls > 0.6m, vertical rebar (10mm or 12mm) at 0.40m centers grouted into CHB cells; horizontal rebar (10mm) at every 2 courses; (d) foundation: concrete strip footing below wall — width = wall height × 0.5, depth = 0.30m minimum; concrete volume per W1044; (e) drainage: weep holes (50mm PVC pipe) at 1.5m centers along wall base; gravel drain (¾" crushed stone) behind the wall with filter fabric; **For RC cantilever wall (1.0–3.0m)**: (f) concrete volume = (base slab + stem wall + toe + heel) based on standard cantilever wall proportions; system calculates per wall height; (g) reinforcement steel: main vertical bars (12mm or 16mm), horizontal distribution bars (10mm or 12mm), base slab reinforcement (12mm or 16mm both ways); rebar weight calculated per kg per W986; (h) formwork: plywood (12mm thick, 4'×8' sheets) for wall faces; timber studs (2"×3" at 400mm centers); timber wales (2"×4" at 600mm vertical spacing); form ties (snap ties or threaded rod with wing nuts); (i) concrete volume per W1044 — mix ratio 1:2:4 (Class A) for structural retaining walls; (j) drainage: perforated PVC drain pipe (110mm diameter) along wall base with gravel surround; weep holes at 1.5m centers | System | Department Supervisor | 3–5 min (automated) |

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Structural safety liability**: Retaining wall failures can cause property damage, injury, and death; the disclaimer must be prominent and the Associate must verbally emphasize the need for professional engineering design for walls > 1.0m
- **Seismic design complexity**: Philippine Seismic Zone 4 requires specific reinforcement and design considerations that cannot be addressed by a simple material calculator; the system must not produce designs that appear to substitute for professional engineering
- **Concrete quality sensitivity**: Retaining wall concrete must achieve specified strength (typically 3,000–4,000 PSI / 20–28 MPa for structural walls); improper concrete mix ratio or inadequate curing leads to structural weakness; concrete mix instructions per W1044 must be precise
- **Formwork failure risk**: Formwork collapse during concrete pouring is a leading cause of construction accidents in the Philippines; the formwork material estimation must include adequate bracing and the instruction card must emphasize formwork inspection before pouring
