## W124. Corporate Secretarial & Entity Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Statutory requirement or Board request |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly Board meetings; Annual Stockholders' Meeting |
| **Volume** | 5 legal entities |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Board of Directors, CEO, Legal Department, Finance |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Meeting Prep**: Issue notices for Board/Stockholder meetings; prepare agenda and board packs | Corp Sec | Chairman | 1 week |
| 2 | **Minutes**: Attend meetings and record minutes; obtain signatures | Corp Sec | Chairman | 3 days |
| 3 | **GIS Filing**: Prepare and file General Information Sheet (GIS) with SEC within 30 days of meeting | Corp Sec | — | 1 day |
| 4 | **Stock Ledger**: Maintain Stock and Transfer Book; record any share issuances or transfers | Corp Sec | — | Ongoing |
| 5 | **Compliance**: Monitor compliance with Revised Corporation Code and SEC regulations | Legal Counsel | Corp Sec | Ongoing |

### System Touchpoints
- Corporate records document management system for Board minutes, resolutions, and stock ledger
- SEC filing calendar with automated reminders for GIS submission deadlines
- Entity master data module tracking 5 legal entities with registration details and compliance status

### Time Estimate
Board meeting cycle: ~1.5 weeks (1 week preparation + 3 days minutes). GIS filing: 1 day per entity. Ongoing compliance monitoring: ~2–3 hours/month.

### Pain Points / Risks
- GIS filing deadlines are strict (30 days post-meeting); missed filings incur SEC penalties and potential personal liability for the Corporate Secretary.
- Stock ledger accuracy is critical for ownership disputes and investor relations — any discrepancy in share counts or transfer records can trigger legal challenges.
- Board packs are often delayed by department heads submitting late reports, compressing the Corporate Secretary's preparation window.
- Compliance monitoring across 5 entities with different registration renewal dates and regulatory requirements creates calendar management complexity.

### Staffing Implication
Board meeting cycle requires ~1.5 weeks of concentrated effort quarterly, plus ~2–3 hours/month for ongoing compliance monitoring. The Corporate Secretary handles all activities with support from Legal Counsel for regulatory monitoring. No dedicated additional headcount required — the workload is absorbed by the standing Corporate Secretary role across 5 entities.

## W125. Legal Case & Litigation Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Receipt of legal notice, summons, or complaint; or internal decision to initiate legal action |
| **Frequency** | Ad-hoc; ~10–20 active cases at any time (labor, consumer, commercial) |
| **Volume** | Covers labor disputes, customer injury claims, and vendor disputes |
| **Owner** | VP Legal / General Counsel |
| **Participants** | Legal Counsel, HR (for labor), Finance (for provisioning), External Counsel |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Intake**: Receive and log new legal matter in Legal Tracker; scan and attach all documents | Legal Assistant | VP Legal | 1 hour |
| 2 | **Assessment**: Assign internal/external counsel; determine case strategy and potential liability | Legal Counsel | VP Legal | 2–3 days |
| 3 | **Provisioning**: For cases with probable loss, notify Finance to set up accounting provision (W81 linkage) | VP Legal | CFO | 1 day |
| 4 | **Fieldwork**: Gather evidence (CCTV, logs, witness statements) from Store/DC/Dept | Legal Counsel | — | 1 week |
| 5 | **Execution**: File response/pleadings; attend hearings; manage mediation/settlement negotiations | Legal Counsel | VP Legal | Ongoing |
| 6 | **Closure**: Record final judgment or settlement; trigger payment (if loss) or recovery (if win) | Legal Counsel | VP Legal | 1 day |
| 7 | **Reporting**: Quarterly Board report on legal exposure and case status | VP Legal | CEO | 4 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- Legal matter management / case tracking system with document attachment, deadline alerts, and status lifecycle (W125.1)
- Integration with Finance for accounting provision setup on probable-loss cases (W81 linkage) (W125.3)
- CCTV and incident report retrieval from store/DC systems for evidence gathering (W125.4)
- Calendar and deadline management for court dates, filing deadlines, and prescription periods

### Time Estimate
New case intake and assessment: 3–4 days. Active case management: ongoing (2–4 hours/week across all active cases). Quarterly Board reporting: 4 hours/quarter. Case closure: 1 day per case.

### Pain Points / Risks
- Labor cases in the Philippines have lengthy timelines (2–5 years for full resolution at NLRC/Court of Appeals), creating sustained legal cost exposure and management distraction.
- Evidence gathering from stores is unreliable — CCTV footage is overwritten on short retention cycles (typically 14–30 days), and store staff are not trained in evidence preservation protocols.
- Accounting provision estimates for pending litigation are inherently uncertain; under-provisioning creates P&L surprises at resolution, while over-provisioning distorts financial statements.
- External counsel costs are difficult to control — hourly billing without caps leads to budget overruns, especially on complex commercial disputes.

### Staffing Implication
New case intake and assessment: 3–4 days per case. Active case management requires ~2–4 hours/week across all 10–20 active cases. With ~10–20 cases running concurrently, 1 Legal Counsel FTE is fully utilized for litigation management, supported by 1 Legal Assistant for intake and document management. External counsel absorbs the bulk of hearing attendance and specialized legal work. No additional internal headcount required unless case volume exceeds 25 active matters.

## W126. Intellectual Property (IP) Portfolio Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New private label brand launch (W1); or discovery of trademark infringement |
| **Frequency** | Ongoing; Annual renewal reviews |
| **Volume** | ~50–100 active trademarks and designs |
| **Owner** | Legal Counsel |
| **Participants** | Marketing, Merchandising (Private Label), IP Office (IPO) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Search**: Conduct trademark availability search for new brands/logos | Legal Counsel | VP Legal | 3 days |
| 2 | **Filing**: Submit application to Intellectual Property Office (IPO) of the Philippines | Legal Counsel | — | 1 day |
| 3 | **Monitoring**: Monitor for similar filings or unauthorized use of BuildRight brands | Marketing | Legal Counsel | Monthly |
| 4 | **Enforcement**: Issue Cease & Desist notices to infringers; escalate to litigation (W125) if needed | Legal Counsel | VP Legal | 1 week |
| 5 | **Maintenance**: Pay maintenance fees and file Declaration of Actual Use (DAU) per IPO schedule | Legal Counsel | — | Per schedule |

### System Touchpoints
- IP database / portfolio management tool with trademark registration details, renewal dates, and DAU filing deadlines (W126.5)
- Automated renewal and maintenance fee calendar with email alerts to Legal Counsel
- Brand monitoring service integration for detecting similar filings or unauthorized use (W126.3)

### Time Estimate
New trademark filing: 4–5 days (3 days search + 1 day filing + follow-up). Monitoring: 2–4 hours/month for brand watch review. Enforcement (Cease & Desist): 1 week per incident. Annual maintenance: ~4–6 hours/year for portfolio of 50–100 marks.

### Pain Points / Risks
- IPO Philippines processing times are unpredictable (12–18 months for trademark registration), leaving new private label brands unprotected during the pendency period and vulnerable to copycat filings.
- Declaration of Actual Use (DAU) filings are mandatory every 5 years; missing a DAU deadline results in automatic trademark removal, potentially losing established brand protection with no grace period.
- Monitoring for trademark infringement across 200 store locations and online marketplaces is labor-intensive; unauthorized use often goes undetected for months, weakening enforcement claims.
- Budget allocation for IP enforcement (external counsel fees for Cease & Desist letters, litigation costs) competes with operational legal needs, leading to under-enforcement of marginal infringements.

### Staffing Implication
New trademark filing: 4–5 days per brand launch. Monthly monitoring: 2–4 hours. Annual portfolio maintenance: 4–6 hours for 50–100 marks. Enforcement actions: 1 week per incident (ad-hoc). All IP management activities are absorbed by the existing Legal Counsel role; the volume does not justify a dedicated IP specialist at this portfolio size. External IP counsel may be engaged for complex enforcement or litigation matters.

## W127. Annual Strategic Planning & OKRs

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual strategic planning cycle (Q3/Q4) |
| **Frequency** | Annual (Planning); Quarterly (Review) |
| **Volume** | Corporate-level + 14 Department-level OKRs |
| **Owner** | CEO / Chief of Staff |
| **Participants** | Executive Team (C-Level), Board of Directors |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Environmental Scan**: Analyze market trends, macroeconomics, and SWOT | Strategy Team | CEO | 2 weeks |
| 2 | **Strategy Offsite**: Define 3-year vision and Top 5 Objectives for the coming year | Executive Team | CEO | 2 days |
| 3 | **OKR Cascading**: Departments define Key Results (KRs) aligned to corporate Objectives | Dept Heads | CEO | 1 week |
| 4 | **Budget Alignment**: Reconcile OKRs with Annual Budget (W26) | CFO | CEO | 1 week |
| 5 | **Communication**: Publish OKRs to the organization via Town Hall | CEO | — | 1 hour |
| 6 | **Quarterly Review**: Track progress vs. KRs; adjust tactics if needed | Executive Team | CEO | Quarterly |

### System Touchpoints
- Strategic Planning / OKR tracking dashboard with objective-level and department-level key result progress visualization (W127.6)
- Budget integration module linking OKR initiatives to annual budget line items (W127.4)
- Town Hall communication module for organization-wide OKR publication

### Time Estimate
Annual planning cycle: ~5 weeks (2 weeks environmental scan + 2 days strategy offsite + 1 week OKR cascading + 1 week budget alignment + 1 hour communication). Quarterly reviews: 2–4 hours per quarter. Ongoing KR tracking: automated via dashboard.

### Pain Points / Risks
- OKR cascading from corporate to department level often produces misaligned or overly ambitious Key Results that are not achievable within budget constraints, creating unrealistic expectations.
- Quarterly reviews tend to become reporting exercises rather than genuine strategy adjustment discussions; underperforming KRs are often redefined rather than addressed with corrective action.
- Department-level OKRs may conflict across functions (e.g., Sales targets aggressive growth while Finance targets cost reduction), requiring executive mediation that is inconsistently applied.
- Budget alignment step (W127.4) frequently reveals that OKR ambitions exceed approved budget, forcing either OKR reduction or budget reallocation mid-cycle.

### Staffing Implication
Annual planning cycle consumes ~5 weeks of concentrated effort, primarily from the Strategy Team (2–3 FTEs) and Executive Team for the 2-day offsite. Quarterly reviews require 2–4 hours each from the full Executive Team. The Chief of Staff absorbs ongoing KR tracking coordination. No dedicated additional headcount required — the workload is episodic and absorbed by existing executive and strategy roles.

## W128. Enterprise Project Management (EPM) Lifecycle

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Approved strategic initiative (e.g., ERP implementation, new DC) |
| **Frequency** | Ongoing |
| **Volume** | 5–10 major capital/IT projects active |
| **Owner** | Project Management Office (PMO) Lead |
| **Participants** | Project Manager, SteerCo (Sponsors), Dept Heads |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Project Charter: Define scope, budget, timeline, and ROI | Project Manager | Sponsor | 2 weeks |
| 2 | Team Mobilization: Assign internal resources and onboard vendors | PMO Lead | Sponsor | 2 weeks |
| 3 | Execution & Monitoring: Weekly status reports vs. milestones | Project Manager | PMO Lead | Ongoing |
| 4 | Change Control: Approve deviations in scope or budget (W21) | SteerCo | Sponsor | Ad-hoc |
| 5 | UAT & Go-Live: Final validation (W73 for IT) | Dept Users | Sponsor | 1–4 weeks |
| 6 | Post-Implementation Review (PIR): Capture lessons learned | PMO Lead | CEO | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints
- Enterprise Project Management tool with project charter templates, Gantt chart scheduling, milestone tracking, and resource allocation (W128.1–3)
- Change control workflow integration with capex approval (W21) for scope and budget deviations (W128.4)
- UAT tracking module linked to IT service management for go-live validation (W128.5)

### Time Estimate
Project charter and mobilization: 2–4 weeks. Active project management: ongoing (~4–8 hours/week per active project for PM). Post-Implementation Review: 1 week. With 5–10 active projects, PMO Lead spends ~40–60 hours/week on project oversight.

### Pain Points / Risks
- Project scope creep is endemic — business stakeholders continuously add requirements during execution without formal change control, leading to timeline delays and budget overruns.
- Internal resources are pulled from operational duties for project work, creating a dual strain on department capacity (operational gaps + project delays when resources are pulled back to operations).
- UAT participation from department users is often treated as low priority, resulting in rushed testing that misses defects discovered only after go-live.
- Post-Implementation Reviews are frequently skipped or superficially executed due to pressure to move resources to the next project, losing valuable lessons-learned data.

### Staffing Implication
Each active project requires ~4–8 hours/week of dedicated project management effort. With 5–10 concurrent projects, the PMO Lead role is a full-time position (40–60 hours/week). Individual Project Managers are assigned per major initiative. Internal resources are seconded from departments on a part-time basis for project work, creating dual-reporting strain. The PMO function requires dedicated headcount — it cannot be absorbed by existing operational roles.

## W230. Legal Contract Review & Approval

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Request for contract review from any department (Procurement, Property, Marketing) |
| **Frequency** | High; ~40–60 contracts/month |
| **Volume** | Lease agreements, vendor master contracts, service agreements |
| **Owner** | Legal Counsel |
| **Participants** | Requesting Dept, Legal, External Party |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Submission**: Dept submits draft contract or request via Legal Portal with business terms | Dept Manager | — | 15 min |
| 2 | **Review**: Legal reviews for risks, compliance, and alignment with company standards | Legal Counsel | VP Legal | 3–5 days |
| 3 | **Negotiation**: Collaborative editing with external party to finalize terms | Legal Counsel | Dept Manager | 1–2 weeks |
| 4 | **Finalization**: Generate final execution version; obtain internal signatures per Matrix | Legal Counsel | CEO/CFO | 1 day |
| 5 | **Archiving**: Scan and file executed contract in Document Management System (DMS) | Legal Assistant | — | 30 min |

### System Touchpoints
- Contract Lifecycle Management (CLM) / Legal Portal with contract request submission, review workflow, and digital approval routing (W230.1–4)
- Document Management System (DMS) for executed contract storage with version control and full-text search (W230.5)
- Signature authority matrix integration for routing contracts to correct approvers based on contract type and value

### Time Estimate
Standard contract review: 3–5 days per contract. Negotiation cycle: 1–2 weeks. Archiving: 30 min per contract. With ~40–60 contracts/month, Legal Counsel spends ~20–30 hours/week on contract review.

### Pain Points / Risks
- High volume (~40–60 contracts/month) creates a legal review bottleneck; departments bypass Legal for "standard" contracts, exposing the company to unreviewed risk in vendor terms and liability clauses.
- Contract negotiation with external parties often extends beyond the 1–2 week target due to unresponsive counterparties, delaying vendor onboarding, lease commencement, or marketing campaign execution.
- Executed contracts are sometimes filed without proper indexing in the DMS, making retrieval difficult during disputes or audits — the reliance on manual scanning and tagging introduces human error.
- Signature authority matrix is not consistently enforced; contracts occasionally reach final execution without the required VP or CEO sign-off, creating governance gaps.

### Staffing Implication
At ~40–60 contracts/month, Legal Counsel spends ~20–30 hours/week on contract review alone — effectively a full-time workload for 1 FTE. The Legal Assistant contributes ~30 minutes per contract for archiving (20–30 hours/month). This volume justifies dedicated Legal Counsel headcount for contract review and cannot be sustainably absorbed alongside litigation and IP management duties without either hiring additional counsel or implementing a CLM system to reduce review time.

## W231. Management Performance Reporting (QBR)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly results availability |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly |
| **Volume** | Comprehensive review of all 200+ stores and 5 entities |
| **Owner** | CFO |
| **Participants** | Executive Team, Regional VPs, Department Heads |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Data Aggregation**: Pull financial and operational KPIs from ERP/BI (W113) | BI Analyst | CFO | 3 days |
| 2 | **Narrative Prep**: Dept Heads provide "Variance Analysis" (Actual vs. Budget/Target) | Dept Heads | — | 2 days |
| 3 | **QBR Meeting**: Present results to Executive Team; focus on underperforming areas | Executive Team | CEO | 1 day |
| 4 | **Action Items**: Document corrective actions and owners | Strategy Team | CEO | 1 day |
| 5 | **Follow-up**: Track completion of QBR action items in next month's review | Chief of Staff | CEO | Ongoing |

### System Touchpoints
- BI Dashboard (W113) for automated KPI data aggregation across financial and operational metrics (W231.1)
- QBR presentation module with variance analysis templates and store-level drill-down capability
- Action item tracking system with owner assignment, due dates, and follow-up status (W231.4–5)

### Time Estimate
Data aggregation: 3 days. Narrative preparation: 2 days. QBR meeting: 1 full day. Action item documentation: 1 day. Total quarterly cycle: ~7 working days across participants. Follow-up: ongoing (~2 hours/month for Chief of Staff).

### Pain Points / Risks
- Data aggregation from multiple ERP modules (sales, inventory, finance, procurement) requires manual reconciliation when data sources disagree, consuming 3 days and delaying the reporting timeline.
- Variance narratives from Department Heads are often submitted late or lack root-cause analysis, reducing the QBR's strategic value to a data presentation exercise.
- Action items from QBR meetings are frequently not tracked to completion — the follow-up mechanism relies on the Chief of Staff's manual tracking rather than system-enforced accountability.
- Covering 200+ stores and 5 entities in a single day means underperforming areas receive superficial attention; meaningful root-cause discussion requires follow-up sessions that are rarely scheduled.

### Staffing Implication
Quarterly QBR cycle requires ~7 working days across multiple participants: BI Analyst (3 days data aggregation), Department Heads (2 days narrative preparation), full Executive Team (1 day meeting), and Chief of Staff (ongoing ~2 hours/month for action item follow-up). The BI Analyst effort is absorbed by existing FP&A/BI roles. No dedicated additional headcount required, though the quarterly cycle represents a significant time commitment from the Executive Team.

- Digital acknowledgement completion rates are tracked but not enforced — staff who fail to acknowledge updated SOPs within the deadline face no automated consequence, undermining compliance culture.
- Biennial review triggers (W186.8) are often missed because no automated reminder exists; many SOPs become outdated without formal review, creating a gap between documented and actual practice.

### Time Estimate
- Incident logging and evidence gathering: 1–2 hours per incident.
- Liability assessment and insurance filing: 2–3 hours.
- Settlement negotiations and waiver execution: 2–10 hours depending on complexity.

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Delayed Incident Reporting**: Store Managers sometimes fail to log incidents immediately, leading to overwritten CCTV footage (typically 14–30 day retention cycles) and making liability verification difficult.

- **Incomplete Documentation**: Missing police/barangay blotter details (W471) or inadequate medical bills/receipts delaying insurance claim approval.
- **Fraudulent Claims**: Risk of fabricated or exaggerated injury claims to seek financial payouts, requiring rigorous evidence gathering and CCTV audits.

### Staffing Implication
Managing ~5–10 incidents per month requires ~30–100 hours of active legal and store management effort monthly. This is absorbed by the standing Store Managers (for intake), Legal Assistant (for insurance filing and waiver archiving), and Legal Counsel (for major settlement negotiation). No additional headcount is required, but the process demands cross-functional coordination.

## W426. Annual Conflict of Interest (COI) & Gift Policy Disclosure

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual corporate governance calendar (typically Q1) |
| **Frequency** | Annual |
| **Volume** | ~6,715 employees (all regular staff) + ~5,000 active vendors |
| **Owner** | Compliance Manager |
| **Participants** | All Employees, All Vendors, VP Legal & Compliance, Internal Audit |

### Background

As a large Philippine corporation with significant procurement spend and a mix of family/professional management, maintaining a transparent "Conflict of Interest" (COI) environment is critical. This workflow ensures that all employees (especially those in Procurement, Store Management, and Finance) and all active vendors disclose any familial, financial, or professional relationships that could influence business decisions.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cycle Launch**: Compliance Manager triggers the annual COI disclosure campaign via the HR portal and Vendor Portal | Compliance Mgr | VP Legal | 1 day |
| 2 | **Employee Disclosure**: All regular employees log into the HR portal to complete the digital COI form — (a) confirm awareness of the COI/Gift Policy; (b) disclose any relatives working within BuildRight; (c) disclose any personal interest in vendors/competitors; (d) record "Nil" if no conflicts exist | All Employees | — | 15 min |
| 3 | **Vendor Disclosure**: Active vendors log into the Vendor Portal to complete the annual Vendor Integrity Statement — (a) disclose any relatives of BuildRight employees working for the vendor; (b) confirm compliance with the No-Gift Policy | All Vendors | — | 20 min |
| 4 | **Initial Screening**: Compliance Manager reviews disclosures; system flags "Positive" disclosures (where a relationship is declared) for manual review | Compliance Mgr | — | 3 days |
| 5 | **Validation & Action**: For flagged cases, Compliance Manager validates with the relevant Dept Head — (a) determine if a conflict exists; (b) implement "Conflict Mitigation Plan" (e.g., recusal from specific tenders, transfer of reporting line); (c) document the decision | Compliance Mgr / Dept Head | VP Legal | 1 week |
| 6 | **Audit Trail**: Disclosures and mitigation plans are archived for Internal Audit review (W120) | Compliance Mgr | — | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints

- HR Portal (COI Module): digital form for employee disclosures with automated tracking of completion rates (W426.2)

### Time Estimate

## W481. SEC Reportorial Requirements Compliance (GIS, AFS, GAN, MC 28)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual / quarterly SEC filing calendar due dates |
| **Frequency** | Annual (GIS, AFS, GAN); quarterly (MC 28 — material changes); event-driven (significant transactions) |
| **Volume** | 5 legal entities × annual filings = 25+ annual submissions; quarterly MC 28 as needed |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | CFO, Controller, Legal Head, External Auditor, SEC |

### Background

The Philippine Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) requires all registered corporations to submit periodic reportorial requirements under the Revised Corporation Code (RA 11232) and SEC Memorandum Circulars. With 5 SEC-registered legal entities, BuildRight must manage a complex calendar of filings including: General Information Sheet (GIS) — annual report of officers, directors, and corporate structure; Audited Financial Statements (AFS) — annual PFRS-compliant financial statements; General Affiliate Notice (GAN) — disclosure of affiliated companies and intercompany relationships; and MC 28 notifications — material changes in corporate structure, officers, or capital. W124 (Corporate Secretarial & Entity Management) covers corporate secretarial management broadly but does not detail the specific SEC filing calendar, document preparation, and submission lifecycle.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Filing Calendar Maintenance**: Corporate Secretary maintains the SEC filing calendar for all 5 entities: (a) GIS — due within 30 days from annual stockholders' meeting (W482); (b) AFS — due within 120 days from fiscal year-end (or SEC-extended deadline); (c) GAN — due within 30 days from anniversary date of incorporation or registration; (d) MC 28 — within 30 days of any material change (officer changes, address changes, capital changes); (e) calendar alerts set 30 days, 14 days, and 3 days before each deadline | Corporate Secretary | Legal Head | 4 hours/annual setup |
| 2 | **Data Gathering**: For each filing, Corporate Secretary coordinates with relevant departments: (a) **GIS**: HR provides updated list of officers, directors, and corporate secretary; (b) **AFS**: Controller provides audited financial statements (after W9B year-end close and external audit completion W95/W351); (c) **GAN**: Legal Head provides updated list of affiliated entities, intercompany relationships, and beneficial ownership; (d) **MC 28**: Corporate Secretary identifies material changes from board resolutions (W124) and corporate events | Corporate Secretary | CFO / Legal Head | 2–5 days/filing |
| 3 | **Document Preparation**: Corporate Secretary prepares filing documents: (a) complete SEC-required forms per entity; (b) attach supporting documents (financial statements, board resolutions, secretary's certificate); (c) obtain required certifications (CPA certificate for AFS, notarization where required); (d) ensure consistency across all 5 entities' filings (e.g., common directors, intercompany disclosures in GAN) | Corporate Secretary | Legal Head | 2–4 days/filing |
| 4 | **Internal Review & Approval**: CFO reviews AFS filing package; Legal Head reviews GIS, GAN, and MC 28 filings; Controller verifies financial figures match audited statements; Corporate Secretary obtains board authorization where required | CFO / Legal Head | CEO | 1–2 days/filing |
| 5 | **SEC Submission**: Corporate Secretary submits filings via SEC Electronic Filing and Submission Tool (eFAST) or SEC Express Nationwide Submission (SENS): (a) upload completed forms and attachments; (b) pay filing fees per entity; (c) receive acknowledgment receipts and confirmation numbers | Corporate Secretary | — | 1–2 days/filing |
| 6 | **Confirmation & Archiving**: Corporate Secretary downloads and archives SEC confirmation receipts; updates entity compliance files in document management system (W255); updates the entity master file with filing confirmation numbers and dates | Corporate Secretary | — | 2 hours/filing |
| 7 | **Deficiency Resolution**: If SEC issues a Notice of Deficiency or Request for Additional Information: (a) Corporate Secretary coordinates with relevant departments to prepare response; (b) submits clarification or additional documents within SEC-prescribed timeframe (typically 10–15 working days) | Corporate Secretary | Legal Head | 1–5 days/response |
| 8 | **Compliance Dashboard Update**: Corporate Secretary updates the Corporate Compliance Dashboard: (a) mark each entity's filing status as Filed, Pending, or Overdue; (b) archive all confirmation receipts; (c) generate annual compliance status report for Board review (W124) | Corporate Secretary | Legal Head | 2 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- SEC eFAST / SENS portal (external)
- ERP Financial Reporting module (AFS generation via W9B)
- Document Management System (W255) for archiving SEC submissions
- Corporate Entity Master (W124) for officer/director data
- Compliance Dashboard for filing status tracking
- Integration with W124 (corporate secretarial), W9B (year-end close), W95/W351 (external audit), W482 (ASHM — GIS filing trigger)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Multi-entity complexity**: 5 entities with different incorporation dates, different officer compositions, and different filing deadlines create a complex calendar; missing a single filing for any entity can result in SEC penalties, revocation of certificate of incorporation, or personal liability for responsible officers
- **AFS dependency on external audit**: The AFS filing deadline is 120 days from fiscal year-end, but external audit completion (W95/W351) for 5 consolidated entities with intercompany elimination can consume 60–90 days; any external audit delay compresses the AFS filing window to as little as 30 days
- **GAN intercompany disclosure accuracy**: With 5 legal entities and complex intercompany relationships (leasing, logistics, ecommerce, property management), the GAN must accurately disclose all affiliates, common directors, and beneficial ownership — any omission risks SEC investigation
- **MC 28 timeliness**: Material changes (e.g., officer resignation, address change, capital restructuring) must be reported within 30 days; in a 200-store chain with frequent organizational changes, tracking all reportable events across 5 entities is challenging

### Time Estimate
- Annual GIS preparation and filing (5 entities): ~20 hours total
- Annual AFS preparation and filing (5 entities): ~15 hours (excluding external audit time)
- Annual GAN preparation and filing (5 entities): ~10 hours
- Quarterly MC 28 monitoring: ~4 hours/quarter
- Deficiency resolution: ~2–5 hours/event (estimated 2–3 events/year)
- **Total annual**: ~65–80 hours/year for Corporate Secretary

### Staffing Implication
- **Corporate Secretary**: Absorbs SEC reportorial compliance as a core function. ~80 hours/year. Feasible within existing role capacity.
- **CFO**: Reviews AFS filings. ~10 hours/year.
- **Legal Head**: Reviews GIS, GAN, MC 28 filings. ~15 hours/year.
- **Controller**: Provides financial data for AFS. ~8 hours/year. Absorbed within existing year-end close duties (W9B).

## W482. Annual Stockholders' Meeting (ASHM) Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual corporate calendar; typically held within 4–6 months after fiscal year-end, per Revised Corporation Code |
| **Frequency** | Annual (1 per entity); may hold joint meeting for parent + subsidiaries where permissible |
| **Volume** | 5 entities; typically consolidated into 1–2 meeting dates |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | CEO, CFO, Board of Directors, Shareholders, External Auditor, Legal Head |

### Background

Under the Revised Corporation Code (RA 11232), all Philippine stock corporations must hold an annual stockholders' meeting. For BuildRight's 5 legal entities, the ASHM covers: (a) approval of the preceding year's audited financial statements; (b) election of directors (if applicable per entity's term schedule); (c) appointment or re-appointment of the external auditor; (d) declaration of dividends (if any, per W327); (e) ratification of acts of the board and management; (f) amendments to the articles of incorporation or by-laws (if any). The ASHM is the governance trigger for the GIS filing (W481) which must be submitted to SEC within 30 days after the meeting.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Meeting Planning**: Corporate Secretary sets ASHM date(s) for all 5 entities, considering: (a) fiscal year-end close completion (W9B); (b) external audit completion (W95/W351); (c) SEC AFS filing deadline (120 days from year-end); (d) GIS filing deadline (30 days from ASHM); (e) director availability; (f) venue booking (or virtual meeting platform setup); coordinate meeting dates to maximize director attendance across entities | Corporate Secretary | CEO | 1 week |
| 2 | **Notice & Agenda Preparation**: Corporate Secretary prepares and sends meeting notices to all stockholders: (a) written notice at least 21 days before meeting (per BuildRight by-laws); (b) include agenda, audited financial statements, director profiles (if election year), and proxy forms; (c) publish notice in newspaper of general circulation if required by by-laws; (d) prepare board books with supporting materials | Corporate Secretary | Legal Head | 3–5 days |
| 3 | **Financial Statement Presentation Package**: CFO and Controller prepare the financial presentation for stockholders: (a) consolidated financial highlights (revenue, EBITDA, net income); (b) segment performance by entity; (c) key financial ratios and KPIs; (d) capital expenditure summary (W21); (e) dividend proposal (if applicable, per W327) | CFO | Controller | 3–5 days |
| 4 | **Pre-Meeting Coordination**: Corporate Secretary coordinates logistics: (a) confirm quorum requirements per entity's by-laws; (b) collect and validate proxy forms; (c) coordinate with External Auditor for auditor representation; (d) prepare voting sheets and election materials (if election year); (e) brief CEO and CFO on anticipated stockholder questions; (f) prepare draft meeting minutes template | Corporate Secretary | Legal Head | 2–3 days |
| 5 | **Meeting Execution**: Corporate Secretary convenes and manages the meeting: (a) call to order; establish quorum; (b) approve minutes of previous ASHM; (c) CEO presents operational highlights; (d) CFO presents financial results; (e) External Auditor presents audit opinion; (f) stockholder Q&A; (g) voting on agenda items (financial statement approval, director election, auditor appointment, dividend declaration); (h) adjournment | Corporate Secretary / CEO | Board Chair | 2–4 hours/entity |
| 6 | **Minutes Preparation & Certification**: Corporate Secretary prepares meeting minutes: (a) draft minutes within 5 working days; (b) circulate to attending directors for review; (c) finalize and certify; (d) archive certified minutes in document management system (W255) | Corporate Secretary | Legal Head | 3–5 days |
| 7 | **Post-Meeting SEC Filings**: Corporate Secretary triggers SEC reportorial filings: (a) GIS filing within 30 days of ASHM (W481); (b) MC 28 filing if directors or officers changed at the meeting (W481); (c) SEC Form ADT (Address/ directors/ TIN) if any director changes | Corporate Secretary | — | Per W481 |
| 8 | **Action Item Follow-Up**: Corporate Secretary tracks post-ASHM action items: (a) dividend payment processing (if declared, per W327); (b) board committee reconstitution (if directors changed); (c) external auditor engagement letter (if appointed or re-appointed); (d) update corporate records and entity master file (W124) | Corporate Secretary | CEO | 1–2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Corporate Entity Master (W124) for stockholder registry, director profiles, and corporate records
- ERP Financial Reporting module for financial statement generation (W9B)
- Document Management System (W255) for meeting minutes, proxy forms, and certified documents
- Communication platform for meeting notices and proxy collection
- Integration with W124 (corporate secretarial), W481 (SEC filings), W327 (dividend payment), W351 (external audit), W9B (year-end close)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Multi-entity scheduling complexity**: Coordinating 5 ASHMs (or consolidated meetings) within the SEC-mandated window while waiting for external audit completion creates a compressed timeline; any delay in the external audit pushes all subsequent steps
- **Quorum risk**: For privately-held family corporations, achieving quorum depends on the availability of key family shareholders; if the controlling shareholder is unavailable, the meeting may need to be adjourned and re-scheduled
- **Stockholder disputes**: For a family-owned corporation, disputes over financial performance, dividend policy, or management decisions can create contentious meetings requiring legal counsel presence and extended deliberation
- **Proxy management**: Collecting, validating, and tabulating proxies for 5 entities is administratively intensive; errors in proxy validation can invalidate meeting actions

### Time Estimate
- Planning and notice preparation: ~1 week across 5 entities
- Financial presentation preparation: ~5 days (CFO/Controller)
- Meeting execution: ~2–4 hours per entity (1–2 days total for 5 entities)
- Minutes and post-meeting filings: ~1 week
- **Total annual cycle**: ~3–4 weeks elapsed for Corporate Secretary; ~1–2 weeks for CFO/Controller

### Staffing Implication
- **Corporate Secretary**: ~80–100 hours/year for full ASHM cycle across 5 entities. Core function of the role.
- **CFO/Controller**: ~40 hours/year for financial presentation preparation. Absorbed within existing reporting duties (W35).
- **Legal Head**: ~10–15 hours/year for legal review. Absorbed within existing legal duties.
