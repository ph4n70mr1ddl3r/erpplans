## W943. Customer Glass Cutting & Custom Flat Glass Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests custom-cut flat glass at the in-store glass cutting station or via ecommerce special order |
| **Frequency** | ~3,000–4,000 transactions/month chain-wide (~15–20/store/month) |
| **Volume** | 1–3 pieces per transaction; average 2 cuts per piece |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Building Materials) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Glass Specialist), Customer, Receiving Clerk (glass inventory handling), Category Manager (pricing) |

### Background

Flat glass (clear, tinted, frosted, textured, mirrored) is a core home improvement category. Customers frequently need glass cut to exact dimensions for window replacements, cabinet doors, tabletops, shower enclosures, and picture frames. Off-the-shelf standard sizes do not cover the vast majority of Philippine residential window dimensions, which vary widely due to non-standardized construction practices. BuildRight's in-store glass cutting service differentiates it from competitors who sell only standard sizes. The service generates incremental revenue through cutting fees and drives additional sales of associated products (glazing compound, silicone sealant, window putty, glass clips, moulding). Safety is paramount — glass handling, cutting, and disposal require strict PPE and handling protocols per W140 and W759.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer Request & Measurement**: Customer approaches glass cutting station with dimensions or physical item (broken glass, window frame) for measurement; Sales Associate (Glass Specialist) verifies glass type (clear 3mm/4mm/5mm/6mm, tinted, frosted, textured, mirror), dimensions (max 1.22m × 2.44m standard sheet), edge finish (plain cut, polished, beveled), and quantity; for walk-in: Sales Associate measures physical item with steel tape; for ecommerce: customer provides dimensions via online order form (W949 visualizer integration for photo-to-dimension estimation) | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 5–10 min |
| 2 | **Feasibility Check & Quotation**: Sales Associate confirms: (a) glass type availability at store — system checks real-time stock per W533; if unavailable, Sales Associate checks nearest store (W22 transfer) or DC replenishment lead time; (b) dimensional feasibility — maximum cut size, minimum cut size (10cm × 10cm), tolerances (±2mm manual, ±1mm CNC); (c) safety compliance — tempered glass required for shower enclosures, large panels > 0.5sqm per National Building Code; tempered glass outsourced to accredited fabricator per W600 with 3–5 day lead time; (d) system generates quotation: glass material cost (per sqm) + cutting fee (per cut) + edge finish surcharge (per linear meter) + optional delivery fee; quotation valid for 7 days | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 5–8 min |
| 3 | **Order Acceptance & Payment**: Customer accepts quotation; Sales Associate creates service work order (SWO) in POS system per W544; customer pays at POS — material cost + cutting fee + edge finish + associated products (sealant, clips, moulding) in single transaction; POS prints SWO receipt with: order number, glass specifications, dimensions, edge finish, promised ready time, and customer copy; system allocates glass sheet from inventory and creates cutting instruction for Glass Specialist | Sales Associate / Cashier | Department Supervisor | 5 min |
| 4 | **Glass Cutting Execution**: Glass Specialist retrieves glass sheet from storage rack (backroom glass storage area, vertical rack with dividers by type/thickness); Glass Specialist dons PPE per W140 (cut-resistant gloves, safety goggles, closed-toe shoes); performs cuts using manual glass cutter (score-and-snap for straight cuts) or CNC glass cutting table (available at top 30 stores); applies edge finish: plain cut (default), polished edge (hand polisher), or beveled edge (beveling machine — top 30 stores); measures finished piece against order dimensions; records cut dimensions and waste factor on SWO | Sales Associate (Glass Specialist) | Department Supervisor | 10–25 min/piece |
| 5 | **Quality Inspection & Customer Handover**: Glass Specialist inspects: (a) dimensional accuracy (±2mm tolerance); (b) edge quality (no chips, cracks, burrs); (c) glass type and thickness match to order; failed inspection → recut from new sheet, waste piece dispositioned per W91; wraps finished piece in protective packing material (bubble wrap or corrugated sheet); customer inspects and signs acceptance on SWO; system closes SWO and posts sale; system updates inventory: glass sheet consumed + waste factor (average 10–15% waste per sheet) posted to scrap per W443 | Sales Associate / Customer | Department Supervisor | 5–10 min |
| 6 | **Daily Station Cleanup & Waste Disposal**: At end of day: Glass Specialist cleans glass cutting station; collects glass waste (offcuts, broken pieces) in designated sharps/glass waste container; waste glass disposed per W82 hazardous/non-hazardous waste protocol (flat glass is non-hazardous but requires special handling for sharp edges); system records daily glass waste volume for inventory reconciliation; glass cutting tools inspected and maintained (cutter wheel replacement every 200 linear meters of cut) | Sales Associate (Glass Specialist) | Department Supervisor | 15 min/day |

### System Touchpoints
- POS service work order module (W544) for order creation and payment
- Real-time inventory availability check per glass type and store (W533)
- Inventory allocation and consumption posting with waste factor
- Quotation generation with dynamic pricing (material + cutting fee + edge finish)
- Ecommerce special order integration (W545) for online glass orders
- AI visualizer integration (W949) for photo-to-dimension estimation
- PPE compliance tracking per W140
- Waste disposal tracking per W82, W443
- Service SKU catalog (W794) for glass cutting service items and fee structure
- Glass waste inventory reconciliation for shrinkage monitoring

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Injury risk**: Glass cutting is inherently dangerous — cuts, lacerations, eye injuries; mandatory PPE enforcement, first aid kit proximity per W501, and incident reporting per W140 mitigate; buildRight liability insurance per W863 provides coverage
- **Waste factor variance**: Manual cutting generates 10–15% waste; CNC cutting reduces to 5–8%; waste factor directly impacts margin; pricing must account for average waste without overcharging customers
- **Dimensional disputes**: Customer-provided dimensions that are wrong lead to unusable pieces; Sales Associate must verify dimensions independently and have customer sign off before cutting; re-cuts due to customer error are chargeable; re-cuts due to BuildRight error are absorbed
- **Tempered glass lead time**: Shower enclosures and large panels require tempered glass which cannot be cut in-store; 3–5 day outsourced lead time may disappoint walk-in customers expecting same-day service; clear communication of lead time at quotation stage
- **Glass storage fragility**: Glass sheets are fragile and bulky; vertical storage racks require dedicated floor space; breakage during handling (receiving, storage, retrieval) is a shrinkage risk; proper rack maintenance and handling training reduce breakage
- **Seasonal demand spikes**: Post-typhoon glass replacement demand can surge 300–400% in affected areas; pre-positioned buffer stock per W960 and emergency procurement per W60 address surge demand

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate (Glass Specialist)**: 1 per store designated as Glass Specialist with certified glass cutting training; cutting activity consumes ~2–3 hours/day at 15–20 transactions; absorbed by existing Department Supervisor or Sales Associate role with specialized training certification per W518
- **Training**: Initial 40-hour glass cutting certification + 8-hour annual refresher per W51; CNC cutting machine operation training for top 30 stores (16 hours)
- **No incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Customer request & measurement: 5–10 min
- Feasibility check & quotation: 5–8 min
- Order acceptance & payment: 5 min
- Glass cutting execution: 10–25 min/piece
- Quality inspection & handover: 5–10 min
- Daily cleanup: 15 min
- **Total per transaction**: 30–60 min of staff time

## W944. Customer Pipe Threading, Cutting & Fabrication Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests custom-cut or threaded pipe at the in-store pipe fabrication station or via ecommerce special order |
| **Frequency** | ~4,000–6,000 transactions/month chain-wide (~20–30/store/month) |
| **Volume** | 1–5 pieces per transaction; average 2 cuts + 1 thread per transaction |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Plumbing/Electrical) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Pipe Specialist), Customer, Category Manager (pricing), vendor (special alloy sourcing) |

### Background

Pipes (galvanized iron/GI, black iron, stainless steel, copper, PVC) are sold in standard lengths (typically 6 meters for GI, 3 meters for copper). Customers — especially plumbers, contractors, and DIY renovators — frequently need pipes cut to specific lengths for installations where standard lengths produce excessive waste. Threaded pipe ends (BSPT/NPT) are required for GI pipe connections and are not always available in all sizes from vendors. BuildRight's in-store pipe cutting and threading service provides immediate, custom-length pipes while driving incremental revenue through cutting fees, threading fees, and associated product sales (fittings, Teflon tape, pipe joint compound, valves). This service is especially valuable for the Philippine market where residential plumbing systems predominantly use GI pipes with threaded connections, and on-site threading is common practice but many contractors prefer pre-threaded pieces for precision.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer Request & Specification**: Customer provides pipe specification: (a) pipe type — GI (galvanized iron), black iron, stainless steel, copper, or PVC; (b) nominal diameter — ½" to 4" for GI/black iron; ¼" to 2" for copper; PVC cut only (no threading); (c) cut lengths — specific lengths in cm; (d) thread requirements — both ends, one end, or no threads; (e) thread type — BSPT (standard in Philippines) or NPT; Sales Associate verifies specifications and selects appropriate pipe from inventory | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 5–10 min |
| 2 | **Feasibility Check & Quotation**: Sales Associate confirms: (a) pipe type and diameter availability at store — system checks real-time stock per W533; (b) cutting feasibility — pipe cutter capacity (manual for ≤ 2", power cutter for 2½"–4"); (c) threading feasibility — pipe threading machine capacity (½"–4" BSPT); (d) minimum piece length — 5cm for cuts, 3cm minimum threaded section; (e) system generates quotation: pipe material cost (charged per standard length consumed, not per cut piece) + cutting fee (per cut) + threading fee (per end) + optional delivery; customer informed that leftover pipe pieces are customer's property per standard practice | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 5–8 min |
| 3 | **Order Acceptance & Payment**: Customer accepts quotation; Sales Associate creates SWO in POS per W544; customer pays at POS; POS prints SWO receipt with: order number, pipe specifications, cut lengths, thread details, promised ready time; system allocates pipe from inventory; for large orders (≥10 pieces or ≥4" diameter): Sales Associate schedules pickup time within 2 hours to avoid station congestion | Sales Associate / Cashier | Department Supervisor | 5 min |
| 4 | **Pipe Cutting & Threading Execution**: Pipe Specialist retrieves pipe from storage rack; dons PPE per W140 (safety goggles, cut-resistant gloves, steel-toe boots, ear protection for power cutting); measures and marks cut points with measuring tape and marker; performs cuts: (a) PVC — manual PVC cutter (quick, clean cut); (b) GI/copper ≤ 2" — manual pipe cutter (rotating blade); (c) GI/black iron 2½"–4" — power pipe cutter (abrasive chop saw); deburrs cut ends with file or deburring tool; for threaded ends: mounts pipe in pipe threading machine (½"–4" capacity), applies cutting oil, threads to BSPT standard (11 threads per inch for ½"–¾", taper per BS 21); cleans threaded ends (removes metal chips, applies anti-rust coating for GI); measures finished pieces against order | Sales Associate (Pipe Specialist) | Department Supervisor | 10–20 min/piece |
| 5 | **Quality Inspection & Customer Handover**: Pipe Specialist inspects: (a) cut length accuracy (±3mm); (b) thread quality — thread gauge fit check (go/no-go gauge for BSPT threads); (c) deburr quality — no sharp edges; (d) anti-rust coating applied (GI only); customer inspects and signs acceptance on SWO; system closes SWO; leftover pipe pieces offered to customer or disposed per W91 if customer declines | Sales Associate / Customer | Department Supervisor | 5–10 min |
| 6 | **Weekly Machine Maintenance**: Pipe threading machine: weekly oil level check, die head cleaning, blade inspection; pipe cutter: blade replacement every 500 cuts; maintenance logged in equipment maintenance module per W650; consumable tracking: cutting oil, deburring tools, anti-rust spray replenished from store inventory | Sales Associate (Pipe Specialist) | Department Supervisor | 30 min/week |

### System Touchpoints
- POS service work order module (W544) for order creation and payment
- Real-time inventory availability check per pipe type, diameter, and store (W533)
- Inventory allocation and consumption posting
- Quotation generation with dynamic pricing
- Service SKU catalog (W794) for pipe cutting and threading service items
- Ecommerce special order integration (W545)
- Equipment maintenance module (W650) for machine maintenance tracking
- PPE compliance tracking per W140

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Metal chip and cutting oil waste**: Threading generates metal chips and uses cutting oil that requires proper disposal; chips collected in metal waste bin for recycling per W167; cutting oil stored for reuse with periodic disposal per W82; environmental compliance per DENR regulations
- **Machine breakdown**: Pipe threading machine is critical equipment; breakdown halts service; spare die heads and blades maintained in stock; backup manual threading die set available; maintenance per W650 prevents unplanned downtime
- **Thread quality inconsistency**: Worn dies produce poor threads that leak; thread gauge quality check at step 5 catches defects; die replacement schedule (every 1,000 threads or quarterly) prevents quality degradation
- **Noise**: Power pipe cutting and threading generates significant noise (>85dB); ear protection mandatory; operations limited to store operating hours; station positioned away from customer consultation areas
- **Customer dimension errors**: Incorrect measurements lead to unusable pieces; Sales Associate must independently verify critical dimensions; re-fabrication due to customer error is chargeable; re-fabrication due to BuildRight error is absorbed

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate (Pipe Specialist)**: 1 per store designated with pipe threading certification; activity consumes ~2–3 hours/day at 20–30 transactions; absorbed by existing role
- **Training**: 24-hour pipe cutting and threading certification + 4-hour annual refresher per W51
- **No incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Customer request & specification: 5–10 min
- Feasibility check & quotation: 5–8 min
- Order acceptance & payment: 5 min
- Cutting & threading execution: 10–20 min/piece
- Quality inspection & handover: 5–10 min
- Weekly maintenance: 30 min
- **Total per transaction**: 25–55 min of staff time

## W945. Customer Key Duplication & Lock Rekeying Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests key duplication or lock rekeying at the in-store hardware service counter |
| **Frequency** | ~6,000–8,000 transactions/month chain-wide (~30–40/store/month) |
| **Volume** | 1–5 keys per duplication transaction; 1–3 locks per rekeying transaction |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Tools/Hardware) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Hardware Specialist), Customer, Category Manager (key blank inventory) |

### Background

Key duplication is a high-frequency, low-complexity service that drives significant foot traffic and incremental sales of associated security products (padlocks, deadbolts, door locks, security chains, peepholes). In the Philippine residential market, homeowners frequently need duplicate keys for family members, tenants, and domestic helpers. Lock rekeying (changing the internal pin configuration so old keys no longer work) is commonly requested during tenant turnover, after domestic helper separation, or when moving into a previously occupied residence. BuildRight's key duplication service takes 2–5 minutes per key, creating strong customer engagement at the hardware counter that leads to additional hardware purchases. The service is a proven traffic driver in the Philippine hardware retail market.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer Request & Key Identification**: Customer presents original key(s) for duplication or lock cylinder for rekeying; Sales Associate identifies: (a) key type — standard flat key (Yale, Master, Weiser, local brands), dimple key, tubular key, automotive key (basic — no transponder), or high-security restricted key (system flags restricted brands that cannot be duplicated per IP agreements per W126); (b) key blank availability — system checks key blank inventory per type; (c) for rekeying: lock brand, number of pins, and cylinder type (rim cylinder, mortise cylinder, tubular latch) | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 3–5 min |
| 2 | **Service Execution — Key Duplication**: Sales Associate selects matching key blank from inventory; mounts original and blank in key duplicating machine; aligns cutting wheel; operates machine (trace-and-cut, 30–60 seconds per key); deburrs cut key on brush wheel; tests key in test lock (store maintains a test lock set for common key types); if key does not operate smoothly, adjusts and re-cuts on new blank (defective blank dispositioned per W91); repeats for additional copies | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 2–5 min/key |
| 3 | **Service Execution — Lock Rekeying**: Sales Associate removes lock cylinder from customer-provided lock or from new lock purchased in-store; disassembles cylinder using pin kit; removes old pins; installs new pin configuration matching new key (randomly selected from key blank stock or customer-provided new key); reassembles cylinder; tests new key operation; old pins returned to pin kit inventory; customer receives new key(s) with rekeyed lock | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 15–30 min/lock |
| 4 | **Payment & Handover**: Customer verifies key(s) operate correctly in original lock (for duplication) or test lock (for rekeying); Sales Associate processes payment at POS: duplication fee per key + rekeying fee per lock + key blank cost + new lock (if purchased); system creates service ticket and posts revenue to service income account; customer receives keys with BuildRight key tag (branded, includes store phone number for reorders) | Sales Associate / Cashier | Department Supervisor | 3–5 min |
| 5 | **Daily Key Blank Replenishment**: At end of day, Sales Associate counts key blank inventory by type; system auto-generates replenishment transfer request from DC or vendor for blanks below reorder point; key blanks are low-cost, high-turn items maintained at 200–300 blanks per store across common types | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 15 min/day |

### System Touchpoints
- POS service item for key duplication and lock rekeying fees
- Key blank inventory tracking and auto-replenishment per SKU
- Restricted key brand database (IP compliance per W126)
- Service SKU catalog (W794) for key-related service items
- Revenue posting to service income GL account
- Customer communication module (W708) for service-ready notification (ecommerce orders)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Restricted key duplication liability**: Some lock brands restrict key duplication to authorized locksmiths only; duplicating restricted keys exposes BuildRight to IP infringement claims per W126; system must flag restricted key types and Sales Associates must decline with customer explanation; signage at service counter displays restricted brands
- **Key accuracy complaints**: Poorly cut keys damage locks over time; quality test at step 2 catches most defects; customer satisfaction guarantee: free re-cut within 30 days with original receipt; estimated re-cut rate: 2–3%
- **Security concern perception**: Some customers are uncomfortable having keys duplicated outside a locksmith shop; counter staff must be professional, maintain key security (customer keeps original key in sight at all times), and never retain customer keys overnight
- **Automotive transponder keys**: Modern car keys with transponder chips cannot be duplicated on standard key machines; Sales Associates must identify and decline transponder keys to avoid customer frustration; signage at counter clarifies service scope
- **Key blank stockout**: Running out of common key blanks causes immediate customer dissatisfaction; safety stock of 200–300 blanks per store with weekly replenishment; emergency procurement from local locksmith suppliers per W921 if DC stockout

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate (Hardware Specialist)**: Key duplication consumes ~1.5–2 hours/day at 30–40 transactions; absorbed by existing Hardware counter Sales Associate role
- **Training**: 8-hour key cutting and lock rekeying certification per W51
- **No incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Key identification: 3–5 min
- Key duplication: 2–5 min/key
- Lock rekeying: 15–30 min/lock
- Payment & handover: 3–5 min
- Daily replenishment: 15 min
- **Total per duplication transaction**: 8–15 min
- **Total per rekeying transaction**: 25–40 min

## W946. Customer Screen Door & Window Screen Custom Fabrication Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests custom-fabricated screen door or window screen at the in-store service counter or via ecommerce |
| **Frequency** | ~1,500–2,500 transactions/month chain-wide (~8–12/store/month) |
| **Volume** | 1–3 screens per transaction |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Building Materials) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Fabrication Specialist), Customer, Category Manager (screen material pricing) |

### Background

Screen doors and window screens are essential in the Philippine tropical climate for insect control (mosquitoes, flies) while allowing natural ventilation. Standard pre-fabricated screen sizes rarely fit Philippine residential openings due to non-standardized window and door dimensions, especially in older homes. Custom fabrication — cutting aluminum or wood frames to size, stretching fiberglass or stainless steel mesh, and assembling with spline and groove — is a high-value service that competitors without fabrication capabilities cannot offer. The service drives significant incremental sales of associated products (hinges, handles, locking mechanisms, weather stripping, mounting hardware). With rising dengue awareness and government health campaigns, demand for window screens has grown steadily.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer Request & Measurement**: Customer provides window or door opening dimensions (width × height), or brings old/damaged screen for replication; Sales Associate records: (a) frame material — aluminum (standard) or wood (premium); (b) frame profile — standard flat, decorative, or retractable; (c) mesh type — fiberglass (standard, 18×16 mesh), stainless steel (security screen, 12×12 mesh), or pet-resistant (heavy-duty fiberglass); (d) mesh color — charcoal (standard), gray, or silver; (e) frame color — white, brown, bronze, or natural aluminum; (f) hardware — hinge type (butt hinge, piano hinge), handle type, latch/lock type; (g) installation type — surface mount or recessed; Sales Associate independently measures customer-provided physical items for verification | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 8–15 min |
| 2 | **Quotation & Order Acceptance**: System generates quotation: (a) frame material cost (per linear meter of aluminum/wood profile); (b) mesh material cost (per sqm); (c) hardware kit (hinges, handle, latch); (d) fabrication labor fee (per screen, based on size); (e) optional installation fee (outsourced per W138, only for BuildRight service area); customer accepts; Sales Associate creates SWO per W544; customer pays material deposit (50%) with balance due at pickup; system allocates materials from inventory | Sales Associate / Cashier | Department Supervisor | 5–10 min |
| 3 | **Frame Cutting & Assembly**: Fabrication Specialist retrieves aluminum profile stock or wood stock; cuts frame pieces to dimensions using miter saw (aluminum) or table saw (wood) at 45° corners; for aluminum: joins corners with aluminum corner keys and crimping tool; for wood: joins with wood glue, dowels, and corner braces; dry-fits frame to verify dimensions | Sales Associate (Fabrication Specialist) | Department Supervisor | 20–30 min/screen |
| 4 | **Mesh Installation**: Fabrication Specialist cuts mesh to frame dimensions plus 2cm overhang on all sides; lays mesh over frame with groove facing up; presses rubber spline into groove over mesh using spline roller tool, starting from one corner and working around; trims excess mesh with utility knife; inspects mesh tension (uniform, no wrinkles, no gaps) and spline seating (fully seated in groove, no protrusion) | Sales Associate (Fabrication Specialist) | Department Supervisor | 10–15 min/screen |
| 5 | **Hardware Installation & Quality Check**: Fabrication Specialist installs hinges (2–3 per screen depending on size), handle, and latch/lock per customer specification; quality check: (a) frame squareness (diagonal measurement within ±3mm); (b) mesh tension and uniformity; (c) hardware alignment and operation; (d) overall dimensions match order (±3mm tolerance); marks BuildRight label on frame (date, store, order number) | Sales Associate (Fabrication Specialist) | Department Supervisor | 10–15 min/screen |
| 6 | **Customer Pickup & Final Payment**: Customer picks up completed screen(s) within 7 days of notification; inspects and verifies fit against physical opening (or Customer Service Counter assists with measurement verification); customer pays remaining balance at POS; system closes SWO; if customer requests delivery, W668 delivery coordination triggered | Sales Associate / Cashier | Department Supervisor | 10–15 min |

### System Touchpoints
- POS service work order module (W544)
- Inventory availability and allocation for aluminum profile, mesh, spline, hardware
- Service SKU catalog (W794) for screen fabrication service items
- Quotation generation with material + labor pricing
- Customer communication module (W708) for fabrication-ready notification
- Delivery coordination (W668) for delivered orders
- Installation service referral (W138) for customers needing professional installation

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Measurement accuracy**: Incorrect dimensions are the #1 source of customer complaints; Sales Associate must independently verify all customer-provided measurements; for high-value orders (≥PHP 5,000), recommend customer bring physical frame/opening dimensions confirmed by contractor
- **Lead time management**: Screen fabrication is labor-intensive (40–60 min per screen); at peak demand (summer, post-dengue outbreaks), backlog can reach 3–5 days; customer expectations must be set at order acceptance; priority queue for loyalty Gold/Platinum members
- **Mesh inventory complexity": Multiple mesh types × colors × roll widths create inventory management challenges; top 3 combinations (fiberglass charcoal 1.2m, fiberglass gray 1.5m, stainless steel 1.2m) cover 80% of demand; specialty mesh ordered per W545 special order
- **Workstation space**: Screen fabrication requires dedicated workbench (2m × 1m) with cutting and assembly tools; competing for backroom space with receiving, BOPIS staging, and stockroom; workstation scheduling per store capacity

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate (Fabrication Specialist)**: 1 per store with screen fabrication skills; fabrication activity consumes ~2–4 hours/day at 8–12 transactions; absorbed by existing role with specialized training
- **Training**: 16-hour screen fabrication certification per W51
- **No incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Customer request & measurement: 8–15 min
- Quotation & order acceptance: 5–10 min
- Frame cutting & assembly: 20–30 min/screen
- Mesh installation: 10–15 min/screen
- Hardware installation & QC: 10–15 min/screen
- Customer pickup: 10–15 min
- **Total per screen**: 65–100 min of staff time (across multiple steps, often batch-processed)

## W947. Ecommerce Live Video Shopping & Virtual Store Walkthrough

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer schedules or initiates a live video shopping session via BuildRight mobile app or website |
| **Frequency** | ~2,000–3,000 sessions/month |
| **Volume** | 1 customer per session; average session duration 15–30 min; average order value PHP 4,500 (higher than standard ecommerce AOV of PHP 3,500) |
| **Owner** | Ecommerce Manager |
| **Participants** | Customer, Video Shopping Associate, Category Specialist (on-call), Ecommerce Operations, Payment Gateway |

### Background

Live video commerce is a rapidly growing channel in Southeast Asian retail, driven by social media platforms and the desire for interactive, trust-building shopping experiences. For BuildRight's home improvement customers — especially those undertaking first-time renovation projects — seeing and discussing products in real-time with a knowledgeable associate builds confidence and reduces purchase hesitation. ## W953. Customer Contractor Micro-Lending Partnership Program Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Trade/B2B customer requests short-term financing for materials purchase, or lending partner submits loan application derived from BuildRight purchase history |
| **Frequency** | ~500–800 loan applications/month; ~70% approval rate |
| **Volume** | PHP 10,000–500,000 per loan; average PHP 80,000 |
| **Owner** | VP Sales & Trade Operations |
| **Participants** | Customer (Contractor/Trade), Lending Partner (accredited microfinance institution/bank), Trade Sales Representative, Finance (AR), Legal |

### Background

Small-to-medium contractors — who represent 30% of BuildRight's revenue — frequently face cash flow gaps between purchasing materials and receiving payment from their clients (homeowners, developers). Traditional bank loans are often inaccessible due to documentation requirements, processing time, and lack of formal credit history (many Filipino contractors operate as sole proprietors without audited financial statements). BuildRight's Contractor Micro-Lending Partnership Program connects qualified trade customers with accredited lending partners (rural banks, microfinance institutions, fintech lenders) who offer short-term working capital loans (30–90 day terms) based on the contractor's purchase history and payment behavior with BuildRight. BuildRight does NOT lend directly — it facilitates the connection and shares (with customer consent) verified purchase data to help lending partners assess creditworthiness. BuildRight benefits from: (a) increased trade customer loyalty and purchase volume; (b) reduced credit risk (lending partner absorbs default risk); (c) referral fee income from lending partners (1–2% of loan amount). This is distinct from W24 (Trade Credit Application, where BuildRight extends direct credit) — W953 is third-party lending facilitation.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 3 | **Loan Disbursement & Material Purchase**: Lending partner approves loan and disburses: (a) directly to customer's bank account or e-wallet; (b) customer purchases materials from BuildRight using: (i) cash/card payment with loan proceeds; (ii) lending partner's virtual card (if partner offers co-branded card); (iii) BuildRight trade account (W24) with loan proceeds used to pay trade balance; system tracks loan-funded purchases for program analytics; customer is NOT required to spend loan proceeds at BuildRight (no tie-in per BSP regulations), but BuildRight expects high share-of-wallet based on convenience and loyalty | Customer / System / Lending Partner | VP Sales | 5–10 min (purchase) |

Cash variance — the difference between POS system-recorded cash and physical cash counted — is an inevitable reality in high-volume retail with 2.8M monthly transactions. Existing workflows cover individual aspects: W541 (cash office operations), W764 (daily opening safe count), W522 (daily transaction review), W272 (cashier over/short dispute), and W842 (cash handling exception monitoring). ### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Automated Variance Detection**: At POS end-of-day closing per W574: (a) system calculates cash variance per terminal: (system expected cash from Z-read) minus (physical cash count by cashier + Store Manager dual count); (b) system applies variance thresholds: (i) Green: ≤ PHP 50 absolute variance — acceptable, no action; (ii) Yellow: PHP 50.01–200 — acceptable, logged for trending; (iii) Orange: PHP 200.01–1,000 — requires cashier explanation and Store Manager investigation; (iv) Red: > PHP 1,000 — mandatory investigation, LP Officer notification, and Regional Manager alert; (c) system automatically categorizes each terminal's variance and sends alerts per threshold level | System | — | Automated |

## W961. BSP Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Covered Transaction Reporting

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Cash transaction at POS exceeds PHP 500,000 single transaction threshold, or aggregate cash transactions from same customer exceed PHP 500,000 in a 5-business-day rolling period, or suspicious transaction indicators detected |
| **Frequency** | ~50–100 covered transactions/month chain-wide |
| **Volume** | 1 covered transaction report (CTR) per triggering event |
| **Owner** | Compliance Officer |
| **Participants** | Cashier, Store Manager, Compliance Officer, BSA/AML Analyst, Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC), Finance |

### Background

The Philippines' Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA, RA 9160 as amended by RA 10365 and RA 11521) requires "covered persons" — which includes businesses accepting cash payments — to report cash transactions exceeding PHP 500,000 (single transaction) or PHP 10M (aggregate in a year) to the Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC). While BuildRight is primarily a retail business, its trade and wholesale operations (30% of revenue from B2B customers) and acceptance of large cash payments for project sales (W162, W164) create exposure to AML obligations. Large cash purchases of building materials are a known method for placing illicit funds into the formal economy (construction spending is difficult to trace). BuildRight must file Covered Transaction Reports (CTRs) with AMLC within 5 business days of the triggering transaction and maintain records for 5 years. Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) must be filed when red flags are detected regardless of amount. This workflow is distinct from W730 (AML Compliance Program Operations, which covers the program framework) — W961 covers the operational execution of CTR/STR filing.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Transaction Monitoring & Threshold Detection**: (a) system monitors all cash transactions in real-time per W533: (i) single cash transaction ≥ PHP 500,000 → immediate CTR trigger; (ii) aggregate cash transactions per customer (matched by loyalty ID, customer account, or cashier-entered name/mobile) ≥ PHP 500,000 within 5-business-day rolling window → CTR trigger; (iii) system flags: customer identity (name, ID type/number, address, nationality, date of birth, contact number from loyalty profile or cashier entry), transaction details (amount, date, time, store, items purchased, POS terminal ID); (b) Cashier procedure for large cash transactions: (i) for transactions ≥ PHP 500,000 cash: Cashier must request and record valid government ID per W530 high-value transaction documentation; (ii) ID details entered into POS before transaction completion; (iii) Cashier does NOT inform customer that transaction is being reported to AMLC (tipping off is a criminal offense per AMLA) | System / Cashier | Compliance Officer | 5–10 min (Cashier ID verification) |
| 2 | **Suspicious Transaction Indicator Detection**: System and Cashier monitor for red flags regardless of amount: (a) system-detected: (i) multiple cash transactions just below PHP 500,000 threshold by same customer in short period (structuring); (ii) customer frequently purchases high-value items with cash and returns them for store credit (placement-layering); (iii) cash purchases by customers flagged on sanction/PEP lists per W730; (iv) unusual purchasing patterns (large volume of easily resellable items — power tools, appliances); (b) Cashier-observed: (i) customer appears nervous or evasive about providing ID; (ii) customer accompanied by multiple individuals each making separate cash payments; (iii) cash appears unusual (brand new bills, sequential serial numbers); (iv) customer insists on paying cash despite having credit card; (c) flagged transactions routed to Compliance Officer for STR assessment | System / Cashier | Compliance Officer | 5–10 min (Cashier observation) |
| 3 | **CTR/STR Preparation & Filing**: Compliance Officer prepares and files: (a) Covered Transaction Report (CTR): (i) filed via AMLC online portal within 5 business days of transaction; (ii) includes: customer identification, transaction details, store location, purpose of transaction (as stated by customer); (iii) supporting documents: POS transaction record, customer ID copy, CCTV footage reference per W838; (b) Suspicious Transaction Report (STR): (i) filed via AMLC portal within 5 business days of determination that transaction is suspicious; (ii) includes: all CTR data plus suspicious indicators, Cashier observations, and Compliance Officer assessment; (iii) STR filed EVEN IF transaction is below PHP 500,000 threshold; (c) AMLC acknowledgment receipt stored in compliance document repository per W255 with 5-year retention | Compliance Officer / BSA-AML Analyst | VP Legal & Compliance | 30–60 min/report |
| 4 | **Record Retention & Audit Trail**: (a) all CTR/STR-related documents retained for 5 years per AMLA: (i) POS transaction details; (ii) customer ID copies (secure storage per RA 10173); (iii) AMLC acknowledgment receipts; (iv) internal assessment notes; (v) CCTV footage for transaction period; (b) system maintains audit trail: who prepared, who reviewed, when filed, AMLC receipt number; (c) Internal Audit includes AML compliance in annual audit plan per W354 AML screening audit | Compliance Officer / IT | VP Legal & Compliance | Ongoing |
| 5 | **Quarterly AML Program Review**: (a) Compliance Officer reviews: (i) CTR filing volume and timeliness; (ii) STR filing volume and outcomes; (iii) false positive rate (transactions flagged but determined non-suspicious); (iv) Cashier AML awareness metrics per W730 training program; (v) any AMLC inquiries or investigations; (b) annual: Compliance Officer updates AML risk assessment for BuildRight's business model; (c) annual: Cashier AML training refresher per W730 program | Compliance Officer | VP Legal & Compliance / CEO | 4–6 hours/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- POS system with real-time cash transaction monitoring and threshold detection
- Customer identification module (loyalty ID, customer account, cashier-entered data)
- Government ID capture and storage (RA 10173 compliant per W405)
- AMLC online portal integration for CTR/STR filing
- Transaction pattern analysis (structuring detection)
- Sanction/PEP list screening per W730
- CCTV integration per W838 for evidence preservation
- Document management system per W255 for 5-year retention
- Compliance case management system
- Internal audit module per W354

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Customer experience impact**: Requesting government ID for large cash transactions may frustrate customers; Cashier trained to present as standard procedure ("For transactions above PHP 500,000, we're required to record your ID for our records, sir/ma'am") without mentioning AMLC
- **Threshold structuring detection**: Sophisticated money launderers may structure transactions to stay below PHP 500,000; aggregate monitoring per customer across 5-day windows catches basic structuring; however, structuring across multiple stores or different customer names is harder to detect; ongoing pattern analysis and Cashier awareness per step 2 mitigate
- **Tipping off prohibition**: Accidentally informing a customer that their transaction is being reported to AMLC is a criminal offense; strict training that Cashier describes the process as "standard documentation" only; Compliance Officer communication to customer (if any) carefully scripted
- **AMLC investigation response**: If AMLC requests additional information or initiates an investigation, BuildRight must respond within prescribed timelines; Legal Counsel coordinates response per W125 legal case management; BuildRight may not disclose AMLC inquiry to the customer
- **Data privacy vs. AML compliance**: Collecting and retaining customer ID information for AML purposes must comply with RA 10173; AMLA provides legal basis for collection; Privacy Notice and Consent per W405 updated to include AML reporting purpose

### Staffing Implication
- **BSA/AML Analyst**: 1 dedicated role (HQ-based, reporting to Compliance Officer); manages CTR/STR preparation, filing, and monitoring; estimated PHP 30,000–40,000/month
- **Compliance Officer**: ~4–6 hours/week on AML program oversight; absorbed by existing role (VP Legal & Compliance)
- **Cashiers**: ~5–10 min additional processing time for large cash transactions; absorbed by existing role with training per W730
- **Net new headcount**: 1 BSA/AML Analyst

### Time Estimate
- Transaction monitoring & detection: automated + 5–10 min (Cashier)
- STR assessment: 15–30 min
- CTR/STR preparation & filing: 30–60 min/report
- Record retention: ongoing (automated storage)
- Quarterly review: 4–6 hours/quarter
- **Total per covered transaction**: 45–90 min of Compliance staff time

## W962. Vendor-Sponsored In-Store Display Compliance Audit & Chargeback

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly display compliance audit cycle; or vendor reports display not meeting contractual requirements |
| **Frequency** | ~400–600 display audits/month (covering ~200 vendor-sponsored displays across 200 stores) |
| **Volume** | 1–5 displays audited per store per month |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Department Supervisor (store), Category Manager, Visual Merchandising Coordinator, Vendor Account Manager, Finance (chargeback processing) |

### Background

Many BuildRight vendors invest in sponsored in-store displays, endcaps, promotional islands, and branded sections under co-op advertising agreements per W513. These agreements typically include contractual requirements for: display location (specified aisle/zone), display dimensions, product assortment (minimum X facings of specified SKUs), signage and branding compliance, stock replenishment (vendor-sponsored displays must be kept stocked), and display condition (clean, organized, undamaged). In exchange, vendors pay BuildRight monthly display fees (PHP 5,000–50,000/display/month) or promotional funding. Existing W262 covers store promotional setup, and W86 covers planogram compliance. W962 addresses the gap of verifying that vendor-sponsored displays meet contractual requirements and processing chargebacks for non-compliance — either BuildRight charging the vendor for failing to maintain their display (e.g., expired promotional materials, damaged signage) or the vendor disputing display fees because BuildRight failed to provide agreed display location/conditions. Proper display compliance management ensures BuildRight receives contracted display revenue and maintains vendor relationships.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Monthly Display Audit Schedule**: Category Manager generates monthly audit schedule: (a) all active vendor-sponsored displays across 200 stores listed by vendor, store, and display type; (b) audit priority: (i) new displays (installed within last 60 days — highest priority); (ii) displays with previous non-compliance findings; (iii) high-value display agreements (≥ PHP 20,000/month); (iv) random sample of remaining displays (30%); (c) audit assignments distributed to Department Supervisors via mobile task management; (d) target: all high-priority displays audited within first 2 weeks of month; random sample completed by month-end | Category Manager / System | VP Merchandising | 2–3 hours/month |
