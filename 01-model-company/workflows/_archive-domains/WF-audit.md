## W120. Internal Audit Planning & Risk Assessment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual planning cycle |
| **Frequency** | Annual (with quarterly updates) |
| **Volume** | ~30–40 high-priority audits planned per year |
| **Owner** | Head of Internal Audit |
| **Participants** | Audit Team, Board Audit Committee, CEO, CFO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gather data on risk indicators: shrinkage (W92), margin exceptions (W85), high turnover | Audit Team | Head of IA | 2 weeks |
| 2 | Survey department heads on process risks and control weaknesses | Audit Team | Head of IA | 1 week |
| 3 | Rank entities/processes by risk score (Impact x Likelihood) | Head of IA | Head of IA | 3 days |
| 4 | Develop Annual Audit Plan (list of stores, DCs, and processes to be audited) | Head of IA | CEO | 3 days |
| 5 | Present plan to Board Audit Committee for final approval | Head of IA | Audit Committee | 2 hours |
| 6 | Allocate audit staff and timeline in ERP/Audit Management System | Audit Team | Head of IA | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints

- Audit Management portal for plan creation, scheduling, and resource allocation
- Risk Register module for risk scoring and ranking
- ERP reporting: shrinkage reports (W92), margin exception reports (W85), employee turnover data
- Board portal for Audit Committee presentation and approval
- Access control reviews (SOD — Segregation of Duties matrix) as planning input

### Pain Points / Risks

- **SEC revised corporate governance framework** — Audit plan must satisfy enhanced independence and scope requirements.
- **Board Audit Committee expectations and independence** — Committee may request scope changes that shift resources mid-year.
- **Revised Corporation Code requirements** — Statutory audit coverage obligations may exceed team capacity.
- **Whistleblower protection (W79 integration)** — Plan must reserve capacity for ad-hoc investigations triggered by whistleblower reports.
- Limited audit team size (~4–6) may not cover all 200 stores and 4 DCs within a single fiscal year.

### Staffing Implication

Annual cycle. ~4 weeks of concentrated planning work. Requires 4–6 audit team members contributing data-gathering and survey effort. Head of IA oversees the entire process and presents to the Board Audit Committee. No dedicated additional headcount required beyond the standing audit team.

### Time Estimate

~4 weeks of dedicated planning effort (data gathering through Board approval). Quarterly updates require ~2–3 days each to refresh risk scores and adjust the audit calendar.

## W121. Operational Audit Execution (Store/DC/HQ)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Approved Audit Plan (W120) or special request from CEO |
| **Frequency** | Monthly (rotational) |
| **Volume** | ~3–4 audits executed per month |
| **Owner** | Senior Internal Auditor |
| **Participants** | Audit Team, Store/DC Manager (Auditee) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Announcement of audit (or unannounced for cash/inventory checks) | Audit Team | Head of IA | — |
| 2 | Fieldwork: Test controls for POS (W5), Inventory (W4), Petty Cash (W25), LP (W37) | Audit Team | Senior Auditor | 3–5 days |
| 3 | Identify discrepancies: Missing documents, unauthorized overrides, stock variance | Audit Team | Senior Auditor | Ongoing |
| 4 | Exit Meeting: Discuss preliminary findings with Auditee Manager | Senior Auditor | Senior Auditor | 2 hours |
| 5 | Draft Audit Report: Categorize findings (Critical, Major, Minor) | Audit Team | Senior Auditor | 3 days |
| 6 | Management Response: Auditee provides Corrective Action Plan (CAP) | Auditee | Auditee | 1 week |
| 7 | Final Report Distribution & Filing in ERP | Head of IA | Audit Committee | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints

- POS system logs and override records for control testing
- Inventory management module for stock variance analysis (W4)
- Petty Cash tracking system for reimbursement testing (W25)
- Loss Prevention exception reports (W37)
- Audit Management portal for report storage and CAP tracking
- ERP activity logs and POS override logs for evidence capture

### Pain Points / Risks

- **SEC revised corporate governance framework** — Findings must be reported with sufficient rigor to satisfy external auditors and regulators.
- **Philippine Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act (RA 3019)** — Discovery of bribes or kickbacks during store audits may trigger legal obligations.
- **Board Audit Committee expectations and independence** — Committee expects timely reporting; delays erode credibility.
- **Whistleblower protection (W79 integration)** — Auditors must handle informant identities with strict confidentiality.
- **Forensic audit capability in-house vs. external** — Complex fraud cases discovered during operational audits may exceed internal team expertise.
- Store managers may be uncooperative or attempt to conceal discrepancies before unannounced audits.

### Staffing Implication

3–4 audits per month, each requiring 3–5 fieldwork days. Approximately 12–20 audit-days per month. 4–6 auditors rotating across engagements, with the Senior Auditor leading each field assignment and the Head of IA reviewing final reports. No additional headcount needed beyond the standing audit team, but peak periods (Q4 physical inventory season) may require temporary support.

### Time Estimate

Each audit engagement runs 3–5 fieldwork days plus 3 days report drafting, 1 week for management response, and 1 day for finalization — approximately 2–3 weeks elapsed per audit. With 3–4 audits per month, the team operates at near-full capacity year-round.

## W122. Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly risk committee meeting |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly |
| **Volume** | Top 20 corporate risks reviewed |
| **Owner** | Head of Internal Audit (acting as Risk Officer) |
| **Participants** | Executive Team (C-Level), Board of Directors |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Update Enterprise Risk Register: Financial, Operational, Strategic, Compliance | Head of IA | CEO | 1 week |
| 2 | Assess external risks: Inflation, port congestion, regulatory changes, competition | Executive Team | CEO | Ongoing |
| 3 | Review mitigation plans for top risks (e.g., FX Hedging W80, DR Plan W55) | Head of IA | CFO/CIO | 2 days |
| 4 | Validate Risk Appetite Statement with Board of Directors — confirm thresholds for revenue volatility, FX exposure, inventory shrinkage, and operational downtime remain aligned with strategic direction | Head of IA | Audit Committee | 1 day |
| 5 | Assign mitigation owners for each top-20 risk with measurable KPIs and firm deadlines for next quarter | Executive Team | CEO | 1 day |
| 6 | Generate Risk Heat Map (Impact vs. Likelihood matrix) for visual presentation — categorize risks as Critical (red), High (orange), Medium (yellow), Low (green) | Head of IA | Head of IA | 1 day |
| 7 | Present Risk Dashboard and Heat Map to Board Risk Committee; obtain formal recommendations and sign-off; set up action item tracker for next quarter follow-through | Head of IA | CEO | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints

- Risk Register module for ERM tracking and heat map generation
- ERP reporting dashboards for financial and operational risk indicators
- FX hedging and treasury module (W80) for financial risk data
- Business Continuity / DR system (W55) for operational resilience metrics
- Board portal for Risk Committee presentation, sign-off, and action item documentation

### Pain Points / Risks

- **SEC revised corporate governance framework** — ERM disclosures must meet enhanced reporting standards for publicly relevant entities.
- **Revised Corporation Code requirements** — Director responsibilities for risk oversight carry personal liability implications.
- **Board Audit Committee expectations and independence** — Board may challenge risk appetite thresholds, requiring iterative revisions before sign-off.
- **Philippine Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act (RA 3019)** — Corruption risks in government-facing operations (permits, licenses) must be explicitly scored and mitigated.
- Risk appetite thresholds are difficult to quantify for a retail business exposed to typhoon-season disruptions and supply-chain volatility across 200 store locations.
- Mitigation owner accountability can weaken if KPIs are not cascaded into individual performance scorecards.

### Staffing Implication

Quarterly review cycle. Approximately 1 week of preparation work (risk register updates, heat map generation, data gathering) plus a 2-hour Board Risk Committee presentation. The effort is absorbed by the Head of IA with support from the audit team for data compilation. No dedicated additional headcount required.

### Time Estimate

~1 week of preparation per quarter (risk register update, heat map, mitigation owner assignment, presentation materials). The Board Risk Committee session itself is ~2 hours. Total quarterly effort: ~6–7 working days, largely absorbed by the Head of IA.

## W123. Fraud Investigation Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Whistleblower tip (W79), LP Exception (W37), or Audit discovery |
| **Frequency** | Ad-hoc |
| **Volume** | ~5–10 investigations/year |
| **Owner** | Head of Internal Audit |
| **Participants** | HR (for employee discipline), Legal (for prosecution), Security |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Intake of report/discovery; initial evidence review (CCTV, ERP logs) | Audit Team | Head of IA | 1–2 days |
| 2 | Form Investigation Committee (IA, HR, Legal) | Head of IA | CEO | 1 day |
| 3 | Detailed Forensic Audit: Transaction tracing, witness interviews | Audit Team | Head of IA | 1–3 weeks |
| 4 | Investigative Report: Document findings, suspects, and loss value | Head of IA | CEO | 3 days |
| 5 | Administrative Hearing / Disciplinary Action | HR Manager | CHRO | 1–2 weeks |
| 6 | Legal Action (if applicable): Filing of criminal/civil cases | Legal Counsel | VP Legal | 1–3 months |
| 7 | Control Gap Remediation: Update process to prevent recurrence | Head of IA | CEO | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints

- ERP activity logs and POS override logs for evidence capture and transaction tracing
- CCTV / security system integration for incident verification
- Whistleblower portal (W79) for case intake and anonymous reporting
- Exception reporting dashboard (shrinkage, voids, high-value returns) for anomaly detection
- Audit Management portal for investigation documentation and case tracking
- HR module for disciplinary action workflows and employee records

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Philippine Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act (RA 3019)** — Procurement fraud involving government officials carries criminal liability for the company and individual officers.
- **Whistleblower protection (W79 integration)** — Inadequate protection can lead to retaliation claims and discourage future reporting.
- **Forensic audit capability in-house vs. external** — The team lacks certified forensic accountants; complex cases require external specialists at significant cost.
- **DOLE involvement in employee disciplinary proceedings** — Procedural errors in administrative hearings can result in illegal dismissal cases before the National Labor Relations Commission (NLRC).
- **NBI coordination for criminal cases** — Criminal referrals to the National Bureau of Investigation require careful evidence handling and may take months to resolve.
- **Board Audit Committee expectations and independence** — High-profile fraud cases attract Board scrutiny and may require direct reporting outside normal channels.
- Evidence spoliation risk if suspects become aware of the investigation before evidence is secured.

### Staffing Implication

Ad-hoc, with 5–10 investigations per year. Duration varies significantly: 1–3 weeks per investigation for fieldwork, plus potential legal proceedings lasting 1–3 months. An Investigation Committee is formed ad-hoc from Internal Audit, HR, and Legal. The Head of IA leads each investigation. External forensic specialists may be engaged for complex cases, adding cost but not permanent headcount.

### Time Estimate

Each investigation runs 1–3 weeks for forensic fieldwork plus 3 days for the investigative report. Administrative hearings add 1–2 weeks. If legal action is pursued, the external process runs 1–3 months. Total elapsed time per case: 3 weeks (internal only) to 4–5 months (including legal proceedings). With 5–10 cases per year, the team must manage multiple concurrent investigations.

## W159. Anti-Bribery & Corruption (ABC) Monitoring & Audit

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Periodic audit cycle; or "Red Flag" system alert |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly monitoring; Annual audit |
| **Volume** | Covers high-risk areas (Procurement, Permits, Real Estate) |
| **Owner** | Compliance Officer |
| **Participants** | Internal Audit, Procurement, Legal |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Red Flag Monitoring**: Monitor high-risk transactions: non-PO payments to "consultants," gifts/entertainment expenses, and unusual vendor credit notes | Compliance Officer | Head of IA | Weekly |
| 2 | **Vendor Due Diligence**: Perform background checks on vendors in high-risk categories (Construction, Government Sales) | Procurement | Compliance Officer | 1 day/vendor |
| 3 | **Conflict of Interest (COI)**: Annual collection of COI disclosures from all employees in Procurement and Management | HR | Legal | 2 weeks |
| 4 | **Audit**: Detailed audit of LGU permit payments (W54) and land acquisition fees to ensure compliance with the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) and local anti-graft laws | Internal Audit | VP Legal | 1 week |
| 5 | **Training**: Mandatory ABC training for all employees with spending authority | HR / Compliance | CHRO | Annual |

### System Touchpoints

- Exception reporting dashboard for red flag transaction monitoring (non-PO payments, gifts, entertainment, unusual vendor credits)
- Vendor management module for due diligence records and background check documentation
- HR module for COI disclosure collection and employee spending-authority records
- AP module for LGU permit payment audit trail (W54)
- Audit Management portal for ABC audit documentation and finding tracking
- Learning Management System (LMS) for mandatory ABC training completion records

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Philippine Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act (RA 3019)** — Direct criminal liability for company officers who authorize bribe payments, even through intermediaries.
- **SEC revised corporate governance framework** — Enhanced disclosure requirements for related-party transactions and politically exposed persons (PEPs).
- **Revised Corporation Code requirements** — Director duties now include explicit anti-corruption oversight obligations.
- **Board Audit Committee expectations and independence** — Committee expects real-time escalation of red flags; delayed reporting creates governance risk.
- **Whistleblower protection (W79 integration)** — ABC red flags often originate from whistleblower tips; weak protection undermines the detection pipeline.
- **Forensic audit capability in-house vs. external** — Complex ABC cases involving foreign jurisdictions (FCPA) may require external forensic investigators.
- **DOLE involvement in employee disciplinary proceedings** — Employees terminated for corruption-related offenses may file labor cases; documentation must be legally defensible.
- **NBI coordination for criminal cases** — Corruption cases referred to the NBI require parallel internal and external investigations.
- Vendor due diligence is resource-intensive (1 day per vendor) and may bottleneck procurement for high-risk categories.
- COI disclosure compliance rate is often below 100%, especially among senior management.

### Staffing Implication

Weekly red flag review takes ~2 hours per week. Quarterly ABC audit requires ~1 week of focused work. Annual training coordination (Step 5) requires ~2 weeks of preparation and delivery. The workload is handled by 1 Compliance Officer with support from Internal Audit during the quarterly audit cycle. No additional dedicated headcount required, though the Compliance Officer role itself is essential and cannot be absorbed by other positions.

### Time Estimate

Weekly red flag review: ~2 hours/week (ongoing). Vendor due diligence: 1 day per high-risk vendor (ad-hoc, ~20–30 vendors/year). Annual COI collection: 2 weeks. Quarterly ABC audit: 1 week each (4 weeks/year). Annual training coordination: 2 weeks. Total annual effort: approximately 8–10 weeks of dedicated Compliance Officer time, distributed across the year.

## W331. IT General Controls (ITGC) & Cybersecurity Audit

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Approved Audit Plan (W120) or significant IT change (e.g., ERP migration) |
| **Frequency** | Annual |
| **Volume** | 1 comprehensive IT audit per year |
| **Owner** | IT Auditor / Head of IA |
| **Participants** | IT Operations, Information Security, CIO, Business Process Owners |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Define IT audit scope (e.g., Access Management, Change Management, IT Operations, Data Backup, Cybersecurity) | IT Auditor | Head of IA | 1 week |
| 2 | Send PBC (Prepared by Client) list to IT Management | IT Auditor | CIO | 1 day |
| 3 | Execute Access Control testing: Verify User Access Reviews (UAR), Segregation of Duties (SOD), and termination de-provisioning | IT Auditor | Head of IA | 1 week |
| 4 | Execute Change Management testing: Sample ERP changes to verify approval, testing, and migration protocols | IT Auditor | Head of IA | 1 week |
| 5 | Assess Cybersecurity posture: Review penetration testing results, vulnerability scans, and incident response readiness | IT Auditor | Head of IA | 3 days |
| 6 | Conduct closing meeting to present preliminary findings | IT Auditor | CIO | 2 hours |
| 7 | Issue IT Audit Report with agreed Corrective Action Plans (CAP) | Head of IA | Audit Committee | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints

- Active Directory / IAM for access provisioning logs
- ERP access rights and role-based permissions matrix
- IT Helpdesk & Change Management system (e.g., Jira/ServiceNow) for change approvals
- Penetration testing and vulnerability scanning reports
- Backup & Disaster Recovery management consoles

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Highly technical scope** — Generalist auditors may lack the expertise to audit complex ERP databases or cloud infrastructure, necessitating external co-sourcing.
- **Cybersecurity risk** — A missed vulnerability during audit could lead to a significant data breach.
- **Systematic vs. manual changes** — Identifying unauthorized direct-to-database changes is difficult without specialized monitoring tools.

### Staffing Implication

Annual execution. ~3-4 weeks of fieldwork and reporting. Often requires an auditor with specific IT certifications (CISA) or co-sourcing with an external audit firm.

### Time Estimate

Total elapsed time of 4–5 weeks from planning to final report.

## W332. Continuous Control Monitoring (CCM) & Exception Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled script execution (daily/weekly) |
| **Frequency** | Daily / Weekly |
| **Volume** | Hundreds of transactions evaluated; generates handful of exceptions |
| **Owner** | Internal Audit / Data Analyst |
| **Participants** | Business Process Owners, Compliance Officer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Run automated scripts to extract ERP data based on defined rules (e.g., duplicate payments, POs split to avoid limits, Segregation of Duties conflicts) | System | Internal Audit | Automated |
| 2 | Analyze exceptions generated by the CCM tool | Audit Analyst | Head of IA | 1-2 days |
| 3 | Filter out false positives and route valid exceptions to the respective Business Process Owner for justification | Audit Analyst | Head of IA | 1 day |
| 4 | Review BPO justifications; accept or escalate to a formal audit finding or investigation (W123) | Head of IA | Head of IA | 2 days |
| 5 | Update and refine CCM rules to reduce false positives | Audit Analyst | Head of IA | Quarterly |

### System Touchpoints

- ERP databases (direct query or BI tool extraction)
- Audit Data Analytics software (e.g., ACL, IDEA, or custom BI dashboards)
- Workflow engine to route exceptions to business owners

### Pain Points / Risks

- **High false-positive rate** — Poorly tuned rules can overwhelm the business with exceptions, leading to "alert fatigue."
- **Data access and latency** — Extracting large volumes of data directly from the ERP without impacting production performance.
- **Integration limitations** — Disparate systems (POS, ERP, HRIS) make cross-system monitoring difficult.

### Staffing Implication

Requires dedicated effort to build, tune, and monitor the scripts. Usually handled by an IT Auditor or a specialized Data Analyst within the Internal Audit team. ~10 hours per week for ongoing monitoring.

### Time Estimate

Daily/Weekly automated execution. Exception review requires 1-2 days of analyst time per cycle.

## W333. Audit Issue Remediation & CAP Tracking

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Finalized Audit Report with findings and CAPs |
| **Frequency** | Monthly review; Quarterly reporting |
| **Volume** | ~50–100 open audit findings across the enterprise |
| **Owner** | Internal Audit |
| **Participants** | Auditees (Action Owners), Executive Team, Audit Committee |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Log all findings, Corrective Action Plans (CAPs), owners, and due dates into the Audit Management System | Audit Team | Head of IA | 1 day |
| 2 | Send automated reminders to Action Owners 14 days and 7 days prior to CAP deadline | System | Internal Audit | Automated |
| 3 | Action Owner submits evidence of remediation to Internal Audit | Action Owner | Action Owner | Variable |
| 4 | Perform follow-up testing (re-audit) to validate that the control is operating effectively | Audit Team | Head of IA | 2–5 days |
| 5 | Close finding if validated; escalate to Executive Team if CAP is delayed or evidence is insufficient | Head of IA | CEO | 1 day |
| 6 | Generate quarterly Open Issues Report for the Board Audit Committee, highlighting overdue high-risk findings | Head of IA | Audit Committee | 2 days |

### System Touchpoints

- Audit Management System / GRC Tool for issue tracking and evidence repository
- BI Dashboards for real-time visibility into open/overdue issues

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Remediation delays** — Business units often deprioritize audit fixes in favor of operational tasks, leading to aging issues.
- **"Band-aid" fixes** — Business owners may implement superficial fixes that pass the immediate re-test but fail to address the root cause.
- **Manual tracking** — Using spreadsheets instead of a dedicated GRC tool makes tracking 100+ findings across 200 stores highly error-prone.

### Staffing Implication

Continuous activity. Requires ~10-15% of the audit team's total capacity for follow-up testing and reporting.

### Time Estimate

Monthly status checks (~1 day). Quarterly Audit Committee reporting preparation (~2-3 days). Follow-up testing takes 2-5 days per batch of closed issues.

## W335. Major Capex & Post-Implementation Project Audit

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Completion of a major capital project (e.g., new DC, ERP implementation) |
| **Frequency** | Post-project (Ad-hoc) |
| **Volume** | 2-4 major project audits per year |
| **Owner** | Internal Audit |
| **Participants** | Project Management Office (PMO), Finance, Business Owners |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Select major projects completed in the last 6-12 months for post-implementation review (PIR) | Head of IA | Audit Committee | 1 day |
| 2 | Compare actual project spend against approved Capex budget (W21) | Audit Team | Senior Auditor | 2 days |
| 3 | Evaluate project deliverables against original business case and ROI projections | Audit Team | Senior Auditor | 3 days |

## W490. Organized Retail Crime (ORC) Investigation & Task Force

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Pattern detection from EBR analytics (W466); law enforcement tip; multi-store incident correlation; industry ORC alert |
| **Frequency** | Event-driven (investigation activation); monthly (task force review); quarterly (industry coordination) |
| **Volume** | ~5–10 active ORC investigations/year; monthly task force meetings |
| **Owner** | Loss Prevention Director |
| **Participants** | LP Investigators, Store Managers, Regional Managers, Legal Head, Security Lead, PNP/CIDG, Industry ORC Alliance |

### Background

Organized Retail Crime (ORC) refers to professional theft rings that systematically steal from retail stores for resale, unlike opportunistic shoplifting handled by standard LP procedures (W466). For BuildRight with 200 stores and high-value merchandise (power tools, copper wire, appliances, branded hardware), ORC manifests as: (a) organized shoplifting rings hitting multiple stores in a region; (b) return fraud (stolen merchandise returned for cash/store credit using fake receipts); (c) employee-assisted theft (collusion between staff and external rings); (d) cargo theft during inter-island transit (W66); (e) vendor delivery short-shipment fraud. ORC is distinct from standard LP (W37, W466) in its scale, sophistication, and cross-store impact. W466 handles individual store-level LP incidents; this workflow handles the coordinated multi-store investigation and law enforcement engagement required for organized rings.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pattern Detection & Intelligence Gathering**: LP Analyst identifies ORC patterns from multiple data sources: (a) EBR exception reports (W466) — correlated voids, returns, and inventory shortages across multiple stores; (b) inventory shrinkage reports (W248) — stores with abnormal shrinkage spikes; (c) return transaction analysis — high-frequency returns across stores with similar merchandise, same customer IDs; (d) POS exception data — transactions involving same SKUs repeatedly at different stores; (e) external intelligence — industry ORC alerts from Philippine Retailers Association (PRA), local law enforcement bulletins; (f) social media monitoring for resale of stolen BuildRight merchandise (Facebook Marketplace, Lazada, Shopee) | LP Analyst | LP Director | Ongoing |
| 2 | **Case File Creation**: LP Director creates ORC case file: (a) case number, classification (organized shoplifting, return fraud, employee collusion, cargo theft, vendor fraud); (b) suspected ring profile (number of individuals, vehicles, stores targeted); (c) financial impact estimate (total known losses from correlated incidents); (d) scope: affected stores, regions, time period; (e) assign lead LP Investigator; (f) classify confidentiality level (ORC investigations are strictly confidential to avoid tipping off suspects) | LP Director | VP Store Ops | 2–4 hours/case |

### System Touchpoints
- Exception-Based Reporting (EBR) module (W466): pattern detection analytics
- POS Transaction Database: cross-store transaction correlation
- CCTV System (W71): multi-store footage retrieval
- Inventory Shrinkage Reports (W248/W92): loss quantification
- Employee Scheduling System (W34): schedule-incident correlation
- Fleet Telematics (W199): cargo theft investigation
- ORC Case Management System: case files, evidence tracking, law enforcement coordination
- Social Media Monitoring Tools: resale activity detection
- Integration with W37 (loss prevention), W466 (LPAP), W248 (inventory variance), W71 (CCTV), W92 (inventory adjustment), W140 (OHS — if employee injury during incident), W43 (separation — ORC-related termination)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Cross-store evidence correlation difficulty**: With 200 stores using different CCTV systems (potentially different retention periods, different quality), correlating footage of the same individuals across stores in different cities is manually intensive and time-sensitive (30-day online retention per W71)
- **Employee collusion detection**: Internal LP systems (W466) are designed to detect individual employee theft; detecting coordinated collusion between employees at multiple stores requires advanced analytics and confidential investigation techniques that can be perceived as intrusive, creating HR and labor relations friction (W493)
- **Law enforcement capacity**: PNP CIDG has limited capacity for retail theft investigations; low-value individual cases (even if part of an organized ring) may not receive priority attention, reducing prosecution success
- **Social media resale detection**: Stolen BuildRight merchandise resold on Facebook Marketplace, Lazada, or Shopee is difficult to detect systematically; manual monitoring is labor-intensive and reactive
- **Jurisdictional complexity**: ORC rings operating across multiple cities and provinces involve multiple PNP jurisdictions; coordinating a multi-jurisdictional investigation requires significant law enforcement liaison effort
