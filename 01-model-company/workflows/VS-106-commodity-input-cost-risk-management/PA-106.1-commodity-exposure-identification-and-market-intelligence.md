## W3401. Commodity Exposure Mapping by SKU / Category / Vendor

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual commodity-risk review; category strategy refresh |
| **Frequency** | Annual + quarterly refresh |
| **Volume** | 35,000 active SKUs across 13 departments; ~800–1,000 vendors |
| **Owner** | Procurement Director |
| **Participants** | Procurement Director, Category Managers (links to VS-01), Supply Planning (links to VS-02), Finance (links to VS-17.4), Treasury (links to VS-18) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Procurement/Category Managers map each category's commodity exposure: identify the dominant input commodities (steel/rebar/cement/lumber for building materials; copper for electrical/plumbing; titanium dioxide/resins/solvents for paint; oil-derived plastics for tools/appliances), the exposure weight (% of COGS tied to each commodity), and the import-vs-local sourcing split (links to VS-02.2/VS-87) | Category Managers / Procurement | Procurement Director | Annual |
| 2 | Procurement tag SKUs and vendors with commodity exposure attributes in the item/vendor master (links to VS-29/VS-287), compute commodity-weighted spend by vendor/category/quarter using actuals, and rank the top commodity exposures by PHP impact (e.g., steel, copper, cement, lumber, resins, plastics) | Procurement / Finance | Procurement Director | Quarterly |
| 3 | Procurement/Treasury validate the exposure map against the merchandise plan (links to VS-101) and import pipeline (links to VS-02.2), publish the commodity-exposure heatmap, and feed it to sensitivity analysis (W3404) and mitigation planning (PA-106.2) | Procurement / Treasury | Procurement Director | Quarterly |

### System Touchpoints
- Item/vendor master (links to VS-29/VS-287); spend analytics (links to VS-67/VS-28.1); merchandise plan (links to VS-101); import pipeline (links to VS-02.2)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Mapping-staleness risk**: Outdated exposure map masks new risks (e.g., a vendor switches input source); mitigated by quarterly refresh and vendor transparency (W3405)
- **Hidden-exposure risk**: Indirect commodity content (e.g., oil-derived packaging/transport) understates true exposure; mitigated by landed-cost analysis (W3408)


### Automation Opportunity
- auto-import (and the import-vs-local sourcing split (links)
- auto-weigh (vs-29/vs-287), compute commodity-weighted spend by vendor/category/quarter)
- auto-validate (procurement/treasury validate the exposure map)

### Controls
operational: quarterly refresh and vendor transparency (W3405); operational: landed-cost analysis (W3408)
### Time Estimate
- 3–4 weeks annual + quarterly refresh

## W3402. Bill-of-Material (BOM) & Input-Cost Decomposition Modeling

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Exposure mapping (W3401); vendor cost-transparency initiative; private-label (links to VS-41) |
| **Frequency** | Periodic + on key-vendor review |
| **Volume** | Top ~200–500 commodity-intensive SKUs (A-items per profile §6.6 ABC) |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Procurement, Vendor (links to VS-03.3/VS-41), Finance (links to VS-17.4/VS-85) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Category Managers/Procurement build a BOM/input-cost decomposition for commodity-intensive SKUs (esp. private-label per VS-41): break the unit cost into raw-material commodity components (e.g., a power drill = motor copper + steel + plastic housing + battery + labor/overhead), with commodity weight and unit-of-measure per component | Category Managers / Procurement | Procurement Director | Per SKU |
| 2 | Category Managers/Vendor obtain vendor cost-transparency (open-book costing for strategic/private-label vendors per VS-03.3/VS-41), validate the BOM against vendor quotes and landed cost (links to VS-02.2/VS-87), and identify cost-driver concentration and negotiable vs fixed components | Category Managers / Vendor | Procurement Director | Per SKU |
| 3 | Finance/Category Managers load the decomposition into cost/margin analytics (links to VS-85/W85, VS-101.3), compute commodity-cost elasticity (how unit cost moves with commodity index), and feed to sensitivity (W3404) and indexed-pricing design (W3412) | Finance / Category Managers | Procurement Director | Periodic |

### System Touchpoints
- Product costing (links to VS-85/W85); private-label (links to VS-41); vendor contract (links to VS-03.3); merchandise analytics (links to VS-101.3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Vendor-resistance risk**: Vendors withhold cost transparency; mitigated by strategic/private-label leverage and open-book clauses (links to VS-41/VS-03.3)
- **Decomposition-error risk**: Wrong commodity weights distort sensitivity; mitigated by vendor validation and periodic re-baselining


### Automation Opportunity
- auto-measure (weight and unit-of-measure per component)
- auto-validate (per vs-03.3/vs-41), validate the bom against)

### Controls
operational: strategic/private-label leverage and open-book clauses (links to VS-41/VS-03; operational: vendor validation and periodic re-baselining
### Time Estimate
- Per SKU; 1–2 days typical

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Procurement/Treasury define the commodity benchmark set: LME steel/copper/nickel/aluminum, regional cement/clinker index, lumber/timber index, oil/brent (for plastics/solvents/transport), titanium dioxide/resin indices, and the PHP/USD and PHP/CNY rates (links to VS-18.3/W307); source data feeds (subscriptions, vendor indices, exchange data) | Procurement / Treasury | Procurement Director | Setup + ongoing |
| 2 | BI/Procurement integrate commodity-benchmark data into the analytics platform (links to VS-28.1), maintain history and seasonality, normalize units/currency, and compute price-change metrics (MoM, YoY, vs moving average); quality-check feeds | BI / Procurement | Procurement Director | Weekly |
| 3 | Procurement/Category Managers benchmark vendor-quoted prices and landed costs (links to VS-02.2/VS-87/W239) against market indices to detect over-pricing or lagging pass-through; feed deviations to vendor negotiation (links to VS-03.3) and pass-through governance (W3417) | Procurement / Category Managers | Procurement Director | Monthly |

- **Benchmark-mismatch risk**: Wrong grade/region index distorts comparison; mitigated by careful index selection and normalization
- **Data-reliability risk**: Poor-quality feeds drive wrong decisions; mitigated by multi-source cross-check and quality monitoring


### Automation Opportunity
- auto-validate (moving average); quality-check feeds)

### Controls
operational: careful index selection and normalization; operational: multi-source cross-check and quality monitoring
### Time Estimate
- Setup + weekly/monthly

## W3404. Commodity Sensitivity, Value-at-Risk (VaR) & Exposure Measurement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly risk review; exposure map update (W3401) |
| **Frequency** | Monthly + scenario-driven |
| **Volume** | Top commodities × categories × forecast horizon |
| **Owner** | Treasury Manager |
| **Participants** | Treasury Manager, Procurement, FP&A (links to VS-17.4), Risk (links to VS-21.2) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Treasury/Procurement compute commodity sensitivity: for each top commodity, the PHP impact of a ±5/10/20% price move on the forward purchase plan (links to VS-02.1/VS-101 receipt plan), using the BOM decomposition (W3402) and exposure map (W3401); produce a sensitivity matrix | Treasury Manager / Procurement | Treasury Manager | Monthly |
| 2 | Treasury/Risk measure commodity Value-at-Risk (VaR) and stress scenarios: probability-weighted exposure over the planning horizon, stress tests (e.g., +30% steel, oil shock, supply disruption links to VS-69/VS-02.3), and the combined commodity+FX impact (W3408); reconcile to the ERM risk register (links to VS-21.2) | Treasury Manager / Risk | Treasury Manager | Monthly |
| 3 | Treasury/Procurement publish the commodity-exposure and VaR report, set the hedge/mitigation priority (links to PA-106.2), and report to the commodity-risk governance (W3406) and FP&A margin plan (links to VS-101.3/VS-17.4) | Treasury Manager / Procurement | CFO | Monthly |

### System Touchpoints
- Risk/analytics platform (links to VS-28.1/VS-21.2); FP&A (links to VS-17.4); merchandise plan (links to VS-101); ERM (links to VS-21.2)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Model-risk**: Wrong sensitivity/VaR assumptions mis-state exposure; mitigated by validation and back-testing
- **Concentration-blindspot risk**: Aggregate VaR masks single-vendor/single-category concentration; mitigated by granular decomposition


### Automation Opportunity
- auto-reconcile (impact (w3408); reconcile to the erm)
- auto-report (and var report, set the hedge/mitigation)

### Controls
operational: validation and back-testing; operational: granular decomposition
### Time Estimate
- Monthly + scenario-driven

## W3408. Import-FX-Commodity Interaction & Landed-Cost Exposure Analysis

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Import buy planning (links to VS-02.2); monthly exposure review |
| **Frequency** | Monthly + import-cycle |
| **Volume** | ~40% of COGS import; ~400–600 TEUs/month (per profile §7.1) |
| **Owner** | Treasury Manager |
| **Participants** | Treasury Manager, Import/Procurement (links to VS-02.2/VS-87), FP&A, Logistics Finance (links to VS-17) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Treasury/Import decompose the import landed cost into commodity + FX + freight/duty + handling components (links to VS-02.2 W2B.12 landed cost, VS-87 duty, VS-18.3 FX), and quantify each component's share and volatility for top import commodities (steel, copper, tiles, paint raws, tools) | Treasury Manager / Import | Treasury Manager | Monthly |
| 2 | Treasury measure the interaction/correlation between commodity price, FX (PHP/USD, PHP/CNY), and freight (per VS-06/VS-87), and the combined landed-cost sensitivity (e.g., steel up 10% + PHP weakens 5% + freight up 20%); isolate hedgeable vs unhedgeable components | Treasury Manager / FP&A | Treasury Manager | Monthly |
| 3 | Treasury/Import publish the integrated landed-cost exposure view, coordinate the FX hedge (links to VS-18.3/W80/W321) with the commodity hedge (PA-106.2), and feed the combined exposure to margin/price decisions (links to VS-101.3/VS-01.2) and the merchandise plan (links to VS-101) | Treasury Manager / Import / Merchandising | CFO | Monthly |

### System Touchpoints
- Landed-cost (links to VS-02.2 W2B.12); FX (links to VS-18.3/W307); freight (links to VS-06/VS-87); margin analytics (links to VS-101.3)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Double-count/miss risk**: Hedging commodity and FX separately without interaction view can double-hedge or leave a gap; mitigated by integrated landed-cost analysis
- **Freight-blindspot risk**: Ignoring freight volatility understates import exposure; mitigated by landed-cost decomposition


### Automation Opportunity
- auto-import (treasury/import decompose the import)
- auto-measure (treasury measure the interaction/correlation between)
- workflow orchestration (exposure view, coordinate the fx hedge)

### Controls
operational: integrated landed-cost analysis; operational: landed-cost decomposition
### Time Estimate
- Monthly + import-cycle
