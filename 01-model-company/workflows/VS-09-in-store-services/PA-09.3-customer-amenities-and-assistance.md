## W746. Store-Level Building Material Sample Management & Customer Selection Assistance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests to view or compare material samples (tiles, countertops, wood, paint, fabric, flooring) |
| **Frequency** | Daily; ~10–20 sample requests per store per day |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × ~15 sample requests/day = ~3,000 sample interactions/day chain-wide |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Building Materials) |
| **Participants** | Floor Associate, Department Supervisor, Customer, Merchandising (W1) |

### Background

Unlike most retail categories, building materials require tactile evaluation — customers need to see, touch, and compare tile finishes, wood grains, countertop materials, paint colors, and fabric swatches before purchasing. BuildRight Depot stores maintain sample libraries that must be curated, organized, and replenished. This workflow manages the sample library lifecycle from display to customer selection assistance to replenishment.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sample library maintenance — Daily: Floor Associate verifies sample board displays are complete, organized, and labeled; reports missing or damaged samples for replacement | Floor Associate | Department Supervisor | 15 min |
| 2 | Customer sample request — Customer requests to view samples; Floor Associate guides customer to sample display area, provides sample boards, and offers to check inventory availability for preferred options | Floor Associate | — | 10 min |
| 3 | Sample checkout — For take-home samples (tile pieces, wood samples, paint swatches), Floor Associate checks out sample against customer loyalty account; system tracks sample borrower and due date (7-day loan period) | Floor Associate | Department Supervisor | 5 min |
| 4 | Selection consultation — Floor Associate assists with quantity calculation (square meters of tiles, linear meters of lumber, liters of paint), provides product specifications from W50 PIM, and generates quotation (W542) | Floor Associate | — | 15 min |
| 5 | Sample return tracking — System tracks overdue sample loans; sends automated reminders at 7 and 14 days; after 21 days, system flags for W605 return fraud review if patterns suggest abuse | System | — | Automated |
| 6 | Sample replenishment — When sample inventory drops below threshold, system generates replenishment request to merchandising (W1) or vendor; merchandising ships new samples via W4 | System / Department Supervisor | — | 5 min/request |
| 7 | Seasonal sample rotation — At seasonal transitions (W583), Department Supervisor coordinates with merchandising to rotate sample displays for new seasonal products | Department Supervisor | Merchandising | 1 hour |
| 8 | Sample performance analytics — System tracks which samples drive the highest conversion to sale; merchandising uses data for assortment decisions (W1) and planogram placement (W314) | System | — | Automated |

### System Touchpoints

- Sample inventory tracking module with per-SKU sample quantity and display location
- Sample checkout/loan module linked to customer loyalty account (W17)
- Overdue sample loan tracking with automated reminders
- W50 PIM integration — product specifications and imagery for consultation
- W542 quotation generation — quantity calculation and pricing
- W1 assortment review — sample performance data feed
- W314 planogram master — sample display position mapping

### Pain Points / Risks

- Sample theft and loss — small tile pieces, wood samples, and fabric swatches are easily pocketed; without checkout tracking, sample libraries deplete rapidly
- Outdated samples — vendors discontinue products but samples remain on display, leading to customer orders for unavailable items
- Quantity calculation errors — incorrect square meter or board foot calculations lead to under/over-ordering, costly for large projects
- Sample display space — building material departments require significant floor space for sample displays, competing with merchandise for selling space

### Time Estimate

Library maintenance: 15 min. Customer sample interaction: 10–20 min. Sample checkout: 5 min. Selection consultation: 15 min. Total daily: ~3–4 hours per building materials department.

### Staffing Implication

Building materials department typically staffed with 2–3 specialized associates who have product knowledge in construction materials. 200 stores × 2–3 associates = ~400–600 specialized building material associates. Sample management is absorbed within existing department duties but requires dedicated floor space.

## W772. Store-Level Rain Check Issuance for Out-of-Stock Promotional Items

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests promotional item that is out of stock during promotional period |
| **Frequency** | During promotional periods; ~5-10 rain check requests per store per promotional event |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × ~7 rain checks per promo event × 6 major promos/year = ~8,400 rain checks/year |
| **Owner** | Customer Service Representative |
| **Participants** | CSR, Department Supervisor, Customer |

### Background
Per the Philippine Consumer Act (RA 7394) and DTI regulations, retailers are expected to honor advertised promotional prices even when the item is temporarily out of stock. While not a strict legal obligation to issue rain checks in all circumstances, BuildRight Depot issues rain checks as a customer service policy and competitive practice — ensuring customers receive the promotional price when the item is restocked. This manages customer goodwill, DTI compliance risk per W731 Consumer Act monitoring, and competitive reputation.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer request received**: Customer approaches customer service counter requesting out-of-stock promotional item; CSR verifies: (a) item is currently in advertised promotion per W13 promotional calendar, (b) item is out of stock at this store per POS inventory check | CSR | — | 3 min |
| 2 | **Inventory check and alternative offer**: CSR checks: (a) current store inventory (confirmed OOS), (b) nearby store availability for customer pickup, (c) expected replenishment date from W596 replenishment exception management; offers alternatives: (i) rain check at this store, (ii) transfer from nearby store per W214, (iii) substitute product per W279 | CSR | — | 5 min |
| 3 | **Rain check issuance**: If customer chooses rain check: (a) CSR creates rain check in ERP: customer name, contact number, item SKU, promotional price, quantity, promotional period end date, rain check expiry (30 days from promo end), (b) system validates promotional price against W300 promotional rule master, (c) prints rain check document with unique tracking number | CSR | Department Supervisor | 5 min |
| 4 | **System inventory reservation**: System creates soft reservation: (a) flags item for priority replenishment per W596, (b) allocates next incoming stock to rain check queue before general availability, (c) maximum 10 rain checks per SKU per store to prevent excessive reservation | System | — | Automated |
| 5 | **Customer notification on restock**: When item is received and allocated to rain check queue: system triggers customer notification per W708 (SMS, email, app push): "Your rain check item [SKU] is now available at [Store Name]. Please pick up within 7 days. Your promotional price: PHP [X]." | System | — | Automated |
| 6 | **Rain check redemption**: Customer presents rain check at pickup: (a) CSR verifies rain check validity (not expired, correct item), (b) processes sale at rain check promotional price per POS override per W529, (c) system closes rain check and releases inventory reservation | CSR | — | 5 min |
| 7 | **Rain check expiry and close**: For unredeemed rain checks: (a) 7 days after restock notification — final reminder, (b) 14 days after notification — rain check expired, (c) system closes rain check, releases inventory reservation, (d) customer notified of expiration | System / CSR | — | Automated + 2 min/expired |
| 8 | **Monthly rain check analytics**: Store Manager reviews: (a) rain check issuance rate by promotion, (b) redemption rate (target > 70%), (c) average time from issuance to redemption, (d) OOS rate on promoted items feeding W596 replenishment exception analysis | Store Manager | — | 30 min/month |

### System Touchpoints

- Rain check module — issuance, tracking, reservation, and redemption lifecycle
- W13 promotions & pricing execution — promotional price validation
- W300 promotional rule master — promotional price verification
- W596 replenishment exception management — priority replenishment trigger
- W214 store-to-store expedited transfers — alternative fulfillment option
- W279 product substitution rules — substitute product suggestion engine
- W529 POS void & refund tiered authorization — promotional price override
- W708 customer communication management — restock notification
- W731 Consumer Act compliance monitoring — rain check policy compliance tracking

### Pain Points / Risks

- DTI regulatory risk — failure to offer rain checks for advertised promotional items may trigger DTI complaints per W469; Consumer Act compliance per W731 requires documented rain check process
- Inventory gaming — excessive rain check reservations reduce available-to-promise for regular customers; 10-per-SKU cap mitigates
- Promotional pricing conflict — if promotional period ends and regular price resumes, rain check customer expects promotional price; rain check must clearly state expiry
- Customer no-show — customer never returns to redeem rain check, tying up inventory reservation; 14-day post-restock expiry limits exposure
- Store-level compliance — inconsistent rain check issuance across 200 stores creates compliance risk; W731 Consumer Act monitoring must include rain check compliance

### Time Estimate

Per rain check: issuance (5 min) + inventory check (5 min) = 10 min. Redemption: 5 min. Monthly analytics: 30 min. Per promotional event: ~1-2 hours additional CSR time per store.

### Staffing Implication

Absorbed within existing CSR duties. Per promotional event: ~1-2 hours additional CSR time. No incremental headcount.

## W773. Store-Level Customer Hold & Will-Call Order Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests item be held for later pickup; or customer special order (W545) arrives and needs staging |
| **Frequency** | Daily; ~3-5 will-call orders per store per day |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × ~4 will-call orders/day = ~800 will-call orders/day chain-wide |
| **Owner** | Customer Service Representative |
| **Participants** | CSR, Stock Associate, Customer |

### Background
Customers frequently request that items be held at the store for pickup within a short period — a contractor rushing to a job site who wants materials guaranteed for afternoon pickup, or a homeowner who found an item online but wants to see it in person before purchasing. Unlike BOPIS (online order, pre-paid), will-call holds are typically in-store requests without pre-payment, requiring different inventory management, expiration, and customer communication processes.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hold request received**: Customer requests hold at customer service counter: (a) CSR verifies item availability per POS inventory, (b) confirms item is in sellable condition (not display unit per W524 unless customer agrees), (c) identifies customer via loyalty account or phone number | CSR | — | 3 min |
| 2 | **Hold creation in system**: CSR creates will-call hold order: (a) customer details (name, phone, email), (b) item SKU, quantity, and location, (c) hold expiry (standard: 24 hours; extended: 48 hours with Department Supervisor approval), (d) special instructions (e.g., "customer will measure lumber to length"), (e) no payment collected — hold is a reservation, not a sale | CSR | — | 3 min |
| 3 | **Inventory reservation**: System creates soft hold on inventory: (a) removes held quantity from available-to-promise across all channels (POS, ecommerce, BOPIS), (b) physical item pulled from shelf by Stock Associate and placed in will-call staging area with hold tag showing customer name, hold number, and expiry date | Stock Associate | — | 10 min |
| 4 | **Customer notification**: System sends confirmation to customer: "Your item [SKU] has been held at [Store Name] until [Date/Time]. Please bring a valid ID for pickup. Hold reference: #[X]." | System | — | Automated |
| 5 | **Expiry monitoring**: System monitors hold expiry: (a) 4 hours before expiry — reminder notification to customer, (b) at expiry — hold auto-cancelled if no pickup, (c) item returned to shelf and ATP restored | System / CSR | — | Automated |
| 6 | **Customer pickup and sale completion**: Customer arrives for pickup: (a) CSR retrieves item from will-call staging, (b) verifies customer identity, (c) processes sale transaction at current price (not price at time of hold — holds do not guarantee pricing unless rain check per W772), (d) system closes hold order and confirms sale | CSR | — | 5 min |
| 7 | **Expired hold restock**: For expired holds: (a) Stock Associate returns item to shelf per planogram (W86), (b) system releases inventory reservation, (c) CSR attempts to contact customer (one-time courtesy call), (d) no penalty or charge to customer for expired holds | Stock Associate / CSR | — | 10 min |
| 8 | **Weekly will-call analytics**: Store Manager reviews: (a) hold-to-sale conversion rate (target > 75%), (b) average hold duration, (c) expired hold rate, (d) items most frequently held (indicates possible planogram or inventory positioning improvement) | Store Manager | — | 15 min/week |

### System Touchpoints

- Will-call/hold management module — creation, tracking, expiry, and release
- POS inventory ATP — inventory reservation and availability update across channels
- W536 unified order management — will-call integration with order lifecycle
- W545 special order processing — special order arrival staging integration
- W708 customer communication — hold confirmation and expiry reminders
- W86 planogram compliance — restock location guidance
- W772 rain check management — pricing guarantee distinction (rain check vs. hold)

### Pain Points / Risks

- Inventory distortion — excessive holds reduce visible ATP, potentially deterring ecommerce and BOPIS customers who see "out of stock" online
- Staging area space — will-call items in customer service staging area create clutter, especially for bulky items (tiles, lumber, cement bags)
- Pricing expectation mismatch — customer may expect price at time of hold, not current price; clear communication at hold creation essential
- Abandoned holds — frequent expired holds indicate process abuse or customer misunderstanding; analytics must identify patterns
- Cross-channel conflict — item held for will-call but also available online creates fulfillment conflict; system must enforce inventory reservation across all channels

### Time Estimate

Per hold: creation (3 min) + inventory check (3 min) + pull and stage (10 min) = 16 min. Pickup: 5 min. Expired restock: 10 min. Daily per store: ~1 hour for 4 holds.

### Staffing Implication

Absorbed within existing CSR and Stock Associate duties. Daily per store: ~1 hour. No incremental headcount.

## W775. Store-Level Customer Loyalty Card Replacement & Account Recovery

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer reports lost, damaged, or defective loyalty card; or customer forgets loyalty number |
| **Frequency** | Daily; ~3-5 replacement requests per store per day |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × ~4 replacements/day = ~800 replacements/day chain-wide (~292,000/year) |
| **Owner** | Customer Service Representative |
| **Participants** | CSR, Customer |

### Background
BuildRight Depot's loyalty program has ~600,000 registered members. Physical loyalty cards are lost, damaged, or worn out through regular use. Digital cards (mobile app) may also have access issues. When customers cannot access their loyalty account, they lose the ability to earn points, receive personalized pricing, and redeem rewards — directly impacting customer satisfaction and retention. This workflow ensures rapid account recovery and card replacement at the customer service counter.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer identification and account lookup**: CSR looks up customer account: (a) by mobile number (primary), (b) by name + birthday, (c) by email address, (d) by scanning old card barcode if partially readable; verifies customer identity by confirming 2 of: registered phone, address, or last transaction details | CSR | — | 2 min |
| 2 | **Account status verification**: System displays account: (a) active status, (b) current points balance, (c) current tier (Bronze/Silver/Gold/Platinum per W17), (d) any account flags (frozen per W496 fraud, merged per W619); confirms account is valid and not flagged for fraud | System | CSR | Instant |
| 3 | **Old card deactivation**: If old card is being replaced: system deactivates old card barcode and links; if card was lost, system flags old card as "Lost — Do Not Honor" to prevent unauthorized use of points | System | — | Instant |
| 4 | **New card issuance**: CSR issues replacement: (a) physical card — scans new blank card barcode and links to account, (b) digital card — sends new digital card link via SMS/email for mobile wallet; system logs replacement with date, store, and CSR ID | CSR | — | 2 min |
| 5 | **Account verification**: CSR verifies: (a) new card scans correctly at POS terminal, (b) points balance displays correctly, (c) customer tier and benefits are intact, (d) customer confirms information is current (phone, email, address per RA 10173 consent) | CSR | — | 2 min |
| 6 | **Customer communication**: System sends confirmation: "Your BuildRight loyalty card has been replaced at [Store Name]. Your points balance: [X]. Your tier: [Y]. If you did not request this replacement, please contact us immediately at [hotline]." | System | — | Automated |
| 7 | **Replacement analytics**: Monthly: system reports: (a) replacement rate by store, (b) replacement reason (lost, damaged, defective), (c) time-to-resolution, (d) flagged accounts (repeated replacements may indicate fraud per W496) | System | CRM Manager | Automated |

### System Touchpoints

- Loyalty member module — account lookup, card issuance, and deactivation per W17
- W17 customer loyalty program operations — loyalty account management
- W253 customer master data governance — deduplication per W619
- W496 customer loyalty fraud detection — fraud flagging for suspicious replacement patterns
- W551 POS loyalty enrollment & account lookup — POS account verification
- W708 customer communication — replacement confirmation notification
- RA 10173 consent management — customer data verification during replacement

### Pain Points / Risks

- Account fraud — unauthorized person may attempt to claim another customer's account by providing partial information; identity verification (2 of 4 data points) mitigates but does not eliminate risk
- Points theft — if old card is not promptly deactivated, finder could redeem points before customer reports loss; real-time deactivation is critical
- Replacement frequency — customers who request multiple replacements may be attempting to exploit the system; fraud detection per W496 must flag patterns
- Customer frustration — inability to quickly access account during checkout (no card, no phone, can't remember number) leads to missed points earning; W551 on-the-spot lookup mitigates

### Time Estimate

Per replacement: identification (2 min) + verification (instant) + issuance (2 min) + verification (2 min) = ~6 minutes. Daily per store: ~24 minutes for 4 replacements.

### Staffing Implication

Absorbed within existing CSR duties. Daily per store: ~25 minutes. No incremental headcount.

## W903. Store-Level Customer Material Sample Loan & Return Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests to take a material sample (tile, wood, countertop, laminate, paint color chip, fabric swatch, carpet piece) home for color/texture matching against their existing décor or under their home lighting conditions |
| **Frequency** | ~2,000–3,000 sample loans/month chain-wide; ~10–15 per store/month |
| **Volume** | 1–3 samples per loan request |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Tiles/Flooring or Lumber/Building Materials) |
| **Participants** | Customer, Department Supervisor, Stock Associate, System (sample tracking) |

### Background

BuildRight's model company profile identifies tiles & flooring (12% of SKUs, ~4,200 items), lumber & building materials (14%, ~4,900 items), and home décor (5%, ~1,750 items) as major categories. A common customer need in home improvement retail is taking material samples home before committing to a large purchase — a homeowner needs to see how a specific tile looks in their bathroom lighting, how a wood stain matches their existing cabinets, or how a countertop material complements their wall color. W746 covers in-store material sample management (customer selection assistance), but the physical act of loaning a sample for off-site use — with accountability, return tracking, and loss prevention — is a distinct workflow. Competitors like Home Depot and Lowe's in the US have formalized sample loan programs because they directly drive conversion: customers who take samples home are 3–5x more likely to complete the purchase at that retailer. Without a formal process, customers either: (a) cannot take samples (driving them to competitors who allow it), (b) take samples informally (theft/shrinkage per W37), or (c) purchase small quantities as "samples" and return them per W12 (abusing the return policy). This workflow creates a structured sample loan program that balances customer service with inventory accountability.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sample Loan Request**: Customer at the Tiles/Flooring Gallery, Lumber section, or Home Décor area requests to take a sample home: (a) Department Supervisor identifies the item: (i) **pre-cut sample SKUs** (cataloged sample items, ~200 SKUs across categories — tile sample pieces, wood sample blocks, countertop sample chips, paint color cards, carpet swatches): these are purpose-made samples, not regular merchandise; (ii) **regular merchandise used as sample** (customer wants to take an actual tile, piece of lumber, or laminate sheet): system treats this as a loan from sellable inventory; (b) Supervisor explains loan terms: (i) loan period: 7 days for standard samples, 14 days for high-value items; (ii) return condition: undamaged; (iii) deposit: none for pre-cut sample SKUs; for regular merchandise used as sample: credit card hold or cash deposit equal to item value (fully refundable on return); (iv) limit: 3 samples per customer per visit | Customer / Department Supervisor | — | 5–10 min |
| 2 | **Loan Transaction Processing**: (a) Supervisor scans sample item barcode and customer's loyalty card (W551) or government ID; (b) system creates Sample Loan record: (i) sample item SKU and description; (ii) customer name and loyalty ID; (iii) loan date and due date; (iv) deposit amount (if applicable — recorded as customer deposit per W94); (v) loan status: Active; (c) system tags the inventory: (i) for pre-cut sample SKUs: deducted from sample inventory pool (not regular merchandise inventory); (ii) for regular merchandise: item moved from "Available" to "On Loan" in ATP — excluded from ecommerce and store-level ATP; (d) customer receives loan receipt with: item description, due date, return instructions, deposit amount, and store contact number; (e) system sends due date reminder via SMS/app notification 2 days before due date | Department Supervisor / System | — | 5 min |
| 3 | **Sample Return**: (a) customer returns sample to originating store within loan period: (i) Department Supervisor inspects returned sample for damage; (ii) if undamaged: system closes loan record; (iii) for pre-cut samples: returned to sample inventory pool; (iv) for regular merchandise: item moved from "On Loan" back to "Available" in ATP; (v) deposit refunded per W94 customer deposit process (cash refund or credit card hold release); (b) if damaged: (i) customer does not receive deposit refund; (ii) damaged regular merchandise dispositioned per W91 damaged goods; (iii) damaged pre-cut sample: discarded; (iv) system logs damage incident for analytics; (c) return confirmation sent to customer via SMS/app | Department Supervisor / System | — | 5 min |
| 4 | **Overdue Sample Management**: (a) day after due date: system flags overdue loans; sends follow-up SMS/app notification to customer; (b) 7 days overdue: Department Supervisor calls customer to arrange return; (c) 14 days overdue: loan converted to sale — system processes: (i) customer deposit applied as payment; (ii) for regular merchandise: inventory permanently deducted from store stock; (iii) for pre-cut samples: sample inventory adjusted; (iv) system records as "Sample Loan — Converted to Sale" for analytics; (v) loyalty points earned on converted sale credited to customer's account per W17; (d) customer returning sample after conversion: treated as merchandise return per W12 (with receipt — loan receipt serves as proof of purchase) | System / Department Supervisor | Store Manager | ~30 min/month total per store |
| 5 | **Sample Loan Analytics**: (a) monthly: system generates sample loan analytics per store and chain-wide: (i) loans issued by category; (ii) return rate and conversion-to-sale rate; (iii) average loan duration; (iv) damage rate; (v) top loaned samples; (b) conversion correlation: system correlates sample loans with subsequent large purchases (did the customer who borrowed a tile sample buy 50 sqm of that tile within 30 days?); (c) Category Manager uses analytics for: (i) pre-cut sample SKU assortment optimization — which sample SKUs drive the most conversions?; (ii) sample inventory planning — pre-cut sample replenishment per W4; (iii) vendor negotiation — vendors whose samples drive high conversions may co-fund sample production; (d) Store Manager reviews sample loan-to-sale conversion rate as a KPI per W665 store dashboard | System / Department Supervisor / Category Manager | Category Manager | 2–3 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints

- Sample loan tracking module (new module or extension of W22 transfer order system)
- Loyalty account system (W17, W551) for customer identification
- Customer deposit management (W94) for deposit collection and refund
- ATP/inventory system for "On Loan" inventory status
- SMS/app notification system (W708) for due date reminders
- Damaged goods disposition (W91) for damaged returned samples
- Returns processing (W12) for post-conversion returns
- POS system for converted-to-sale transactions
- Store KPI dashboard (W665) for loan-to-sale conversion rate
- BI analytics for sample program performance

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Low return rate for regular merchandise samples**: customers may "borrow" expensive tile or wood pieces with no intention of returning — effectively a free acquisition; credit card hold requirement for regular merchandise samples mitigates, but may deter legitimate customers
- **Sample inventory pool depletion**: pre-cut samples are a finite resource; high-demand samples (popular tile colors, trending wood finishes) may be perpetually on loan, unavailable for other customers; maintain 2–3 units per popular sample SKU
- **Store-to-store inconsistency**: some stores may be strict about deposits and due dates while others are lenient, creating inconsistent customer experience; system-enforced loan periods (hard due dates) mitigate
- **Damage disputes**: customer claims sample was already chipped/scratched when received; pre-loan condition inspection with photo documentation mitigates
- **Regular merchandise ATP distortion**: items on loan reduce visible ATP for online shoppers; if a tile sample is on loan for 7 days, ecommerce shows one fewer unit available — may trigger unnecessary replenishment
- **Seasonal demand spikes for samples**: pre-summer (tiling projects) and pre-holiday (home renovation) periods see 2–3x sample loan volume; sample inventory must be pre-stocked

### Staffing Implication

- **Department Supervisor**: ~5 min per loan/return transaction × 10–15/month = ~1–1.5 hours/month; absorbed by existing role
- **Stock Associate**: ~30 min/month on sample inventory maintenance and re-stocking; absorbed by existing role
- **No incremental headcount**.

### Time Estimate

- Loan transaction processing: 5–10 min per loan
- System processing: automated
- Return processing: 5 min per return
- Overdue management: ~30 min/month total per store
- Monthly analytics: 2–3 hours chain-wide
- **Total per loan cycle**: ~15–20 min of staff time (issuance + return)

## W924. Store-Level Self-Service Kiosk & Interactive Product Information Station Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily kiosk startup as part of store opening (W5A); customer-initiated kiosk interaction; system health monitoring alert |
| **Frequency** | Daily operations; 1–2 kiosks per store × 200 stores = ~300 kiosks chain-wide |
| **Volume** | ~50–100 customer interactions per kiosk per day; ~15,000–30,000 daily interactions chain-wide |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (kiosk assistance), IT Helpdesk (W48, technical support), Department Supervisor (content accuracy) |

### Background

Modern big-box retail increasingly deploys self-service kiosks to enhance the customer experience while reducing staff workload. For BuildRight, self-service kiosks serve multiple purposes: (a) product information lookup — customers can search for products by category, barcode scan, or keyword and see specifications, pricing, stock availability, and aisle location; (b) project calculators — customers can input room dimensions to calculate tile quantity, paint coverage, lumber requirements, or electrical wiring needs; (c) tutorial access — short video tutorials on common DIY projects (how to install a faucet, how to mix paint, how to lay tiles); (d) ecommerce integration — customers can browse the full 35,000 SKU catalog online (store only stocks ~35,000 but not all at each location), check online-only products, and place endless aisle orders per W273; (e) loyalty account management — check points balance, view purchase history, redeem rewards; (f) store navigation — interactive store map showing product locations and current promotions. Each store has 1–2 kiosks positioned in high-traffic areas (entrance, tools department, tile gallery). This workflow covers daily kiosk operations, content management, health monitoring, and customer usage analytics.

### Steps

## W1162. Store-Level Customer Power Tool & Equipment Pre-Purchase Try-Before-You-Buy Program

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer expresses interest in a power tool or equipment purchase but wants to test before committing, or customer inquires about tool demo, or sales associate identifies high-value equipment opportunity |
| **Frequency** | ~1,500–2,500 try-before-you-buy sessions/month across all stores |
| **Volume** | 1–2 tools per session |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Tools & Hardware) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Tools Department), Customer, Tool Demo Station Attendant |

### Background

Power tools and equipment represent a high-value, high-margin category at BuildRight Depot (5% of active SKUs, ~1,750 SKUs, but significantly higher margin than building materials). Customers hesitate to purchase expensive power tools (PHP 3,000–50,000+) without hands-on testing because: (a) ergonomics vary significantly between brands — weight, grip size, vibration level, and balance affect user comfort; (b) performance claims (power, speed, battery life) are difficult to evaluate from specifications alone; (c) brand loyalty is strong among professional contractors — they want to verify that a new model meets their expectations before investing; (d) DIY customers switching from hand tools to power tools want reassurance they can handle the equipment safely. A try-before-you-buy program increases power tool conversion rates (estimated +15–25% for tested tools), reduces returns (customers are confident in their purchase), and drives brand switching (when customers discover a superior tool through testing). This workflow manages the in-store try-before-you-buy experience. It complements W1007 (tool demo reservation), W607 (product demo station daily operations), W1087 (brand ambassador demo events), and W749 (POS safety acknowledgment) by providing a structured pre-purchase testing experience.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer Need Assessment & Tool Selection**: (a) customer approaches tools department or is identified by sales associate browsing power tools; (b) associate assesses customer needs: professional contractor (daily use, durability priority), serious DIYer (weekly use, value priority), first-time buyer (ease of use priority); (c) associate recommends 2–3 tool options based on needs and budget: cordless drill (DeWalt vs. Makita vs. Bosch), circular saw, angle grinder, rotary hammer, etc.; (d) associate explains try-before-you-buy program: "You can test any of these tools at our demo station before purchasing. I'll set up the tools and safety gear for you." | Sales Associate | Sales Associate | 3–5 min |
| 2 | **Demo Station Setup & Safety Briefing**: (a) associate guides customer to dedicated tool demo station (equipped with workbench, scrap wood, test materials, power supply, and safety barriers); (b) associate retrieves selected tools from demo inventory (separate from sale stock — clearly marked "DEMO UNIT"); (c) safety briefing: safety glasses and ear protection donned, hair and loose clothing secured, safe handling instructions provided ("Keep both hands on the tool. Never carry a plugged-in tool with finger on trigger. Unplug before changing bits/blades."); (d) customer signs safety acknowledgment per W749 (power tool safety acknowledgment); (e) demo materials prepared: scrap wood boards (for drills, saws, sanders), metal pipe sections (for grinders), concrete block (for rotary hammers) | Sales Associate / Demo Station Attendant | Department Supervisor | 5–8 min |
| 3 | **Hands-On Testing & Performance Comparison**: (a) customer tests each tool under associate guidance: cordless drill — drive screws into wood, drill holes, feel torque and speed settings, check weight and balance, evaluate trigger feel; circular saw — make cuts in plywood and 2×4 lumber, evaluate cutting speed, blade visibility, dust extraction; angle grinder — cut metal pipe, evaluate cutting speed and vibration; (b) associate facilitates comparison: "Notice how the DeWalt has a tighter grip but the Makita is lighter. The Bosch has more speed settings. Which feels most comfortable for the kind of work you do?"; (c) if battery-powered: associate demonstrates battery swap, checks battery life indicator, shows charger; (d) associate answers questions about warranty (W33), service center (W440), and accessory compatibility (W971) | Customer / Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 10–20 min |
| 4 | **Purchase Conversion & Demo-to-Sale Transition**: (a) after testing, associate asks: "Which tool feels right for you?"; (b) if customer selects a tool: associate retrieves new-in-box unit from shelf (NOT the demo unit); (c) associate verifies all accessories included (case, battery, charger, manual, warranty card); (d) processes sale at POS with safety acknowledgment per W749; (e) if customer needs time to decide: offers to hold a unit for 24 hours; (f) demo unit returned to demo station; (g) if customer declines all tested tools: feedback captured — "What didn't you like?" → feeds into product assortment review (W1) | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 3–5 min |
| 5 | **Demo Unit Lifecycle Management**: (a) demo units tracked separately in inventory: "Demo — Not for Sale" status; (b) demo units replaced every 3–6 months depending on wear; (c) retired demo units sold at discount per W524 (demo/display unit selling) with appropriate condition disclosure; (d) new demo unit ordered from vendor as replacement — often provided at deep discount or free by vendor for promotional purposes; (e) demo station maintained per W607 (demo station daily operations): tools cleaned, blades/bits replaced, safety gear inspected | Demo Station Attendant / Department Supervisor | Store Manager | 15–20 min/day maintenance |

### System Touchpoints
- ERP inventory module — demo unit tracking (separate from sale stock)
- POS module — new unit sale processing, safety acknowledgment per W749
- Demo reservation module (W1007) — appointment scheduling for popular demo times
- Customer account module — test history, purchase conversion tracking
- Warranty module (W33) — warranty registration at sale
- Product assortment module (W1) — customer feedback on tested/declined tools
- Demo unit lifecycle module — rotation schedule, wear tracking, replacement ordering
- Analytics module — try-before-you-buy conversion rate, most-tested tools, average time-to-purchase

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Safety risk**: Power tools are inherently dangerous; demo station must have proper safety barriers, PPE, and trained supervision; liability insurance coverage verified per W59
- **Demo unit cost": Maintaining demo units for 1,750 power tool SKUs is impractical; limit demo program to top 20–30 tools per store (by sales volume)
- **Space requirement": Demo station requires 10–15 sqm of selling area; stores with limited space may use mobile demo cart
- **Noise": Power tool testing generates significant noise; demo station positioned away from customer service areas and noise-sensitive departments
- **Theft risk": Demo units are attractive targets; secured when not in use

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate**: Try-before-you-buy adds ~20–35 min per session; at ~8–12 sessions/store/week across tools department associates, ~2–4 hours/week total — absorbed by existing role
- **Demo Station Attendant**: Can be a dedicated role or rotated among stock associates; ~15–20 min/day maintenance — absorbed by existing stock associates
- **No permanent incremental headcount** — estimated 15–25% increase in power tool conversion rate for tested tools justifies time investment

### Time Estimate
- Need assessment: 3–5 min
- Setup & safety: 5–8 min
- Hands-on testing: 10–20 min
- Purchase processing: 3–5 min
- **Total per session**: 21–38 min

## W1179. Store-Level Gift Card Sales, Redemption & Balance Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer purchases or redeems a BuildRight gift card at POS or online |
| **Frequency** | ~8,000–12,000 gift card transactions/month chain-wide; peaks during Christmas, Mother's Day, Father's Day |
| **Volume** | ~40–60 gift card sales/store/month; ~20–30 redemptions/store/month |
| **Owner** | CSR / Cashier |
| **Participants** | CSR, Cashier, Customer, Finance (gift card liability management), Ecommerce (online sales) |

### Background

Gift cards are a standard retail product that drives incremental revenue, captures pre-payment cash, and introduces new customers to BuildRight through gifting. For a home improvement retailer, gift cards are particularly relevant during housewarming, renovation, and holiday seasons. Filipino gift-giving culture (Christmas, fiestas, birthdays) creates strong demand. Gift cards also serve corporate customers who provide them as employee benefits or project incentives. This workflow manages the full lifecycle: issuance, sale, activation, redemption, balance inquiry, and financial liability tracking. Philippine regulations under the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) require that gift card balances and terms are clearly disclosed, and unused balances must be managed according to escheatment rules.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Gift card issuance & activation**: (a) physical gift cards pre-printed and distributed to stores in inactive state; (b) customer selects denomination (PHP 500, 1,000, 2,000, 5,000) at POS; (c) cashier scans gift card barcode and enters load amount; (d) system activates card with unique serial number and loads balance; (e) customer pays via standard tender (cash, card, e-wallet); (f) receipt prints gift card number, loaded amount, and terms; (g) for ecommerce: customer purchases digital gift card on buildright.com.ph; system generates digital gift card code delivered via email; digital cards are also redeemable in-store via barcode on mobile | Cashier / System | Store Manager | 3 min |
| 2 | **Redemption at POS**: (a) customer presents gift card (physical barcode or digital code) at checkout; (b) cashier scans gift card barcode; (c) system displays current balance; (d) cashier applies gift card as tender for purchase amount; (e) if purchase > balance: system applies full gift card balance and prompts for additional tender; (f) if purchase < balance: system deducts purchase amount, updates remaining balance, and prints remaining balance on receipt; (g) system records transaction against gift card serial number for audit trail | Cashier / System | Store Manager | 2 min |
| 3 | **Balance inquiry**: Customer requests balance check: (a) at POS: cashier scans card and reads balance; (b) online: customer enters gift card number on buildright.com.ph; (c) via mobile app: customer saves gift card to digital wallet per W615 and views balance; (d) system provides transaction history on request | Cashier / System / Customer | — | 1 min |
| 4 | **Lost/stolen card handling**: (a) customer reports lost physical gift card with receipt or gift card number; (b) CSR verifies customer identity and original purchase transaction; (c) system cancels lost card serial number and transfers remaining balance to new card; (d) customer pays PHP 50 replacement fee; (e) for digital gift cards: no replacement needed — customer retrieves from email or app | CSR | Store Manager | 10 min |
| 5 | **Financial liability management**: (a) system tracks total outstanding gift card liability (unredeemed balances) as a current liability on balance sheet; (b) Finance reviews monthly: total liability, redemption rate, aging of unredeemed cards; (c) for cards unused > 5 years: comply with Philippine escheatment rules — remit unredeemed balances to BSP per regulatory guidance; (d) breakage revenue (cards never fully redeemed): recognize proportionally based on historical redemption patterns per PFRS 15 revenue recognition | System / Finance | Finance Controller | 4 hours/month |
| 6 | **Corporate bulk sales**: For corporate customers ordering gift cards in bulk (> 50 cards): (a) Sales Rep per W103 processes bulk order with volume discount (5% for > PHP 100,000 order); (b) Finance invoices corporate customer on credit terms per W16; (c) cards activated and delivered with corporate branding option; (d) track corporate gift card program separately for ROI analysis | Sales Rep / Finance | VP Sales | Per order |

### System Touchpoints
- POS gift card module with activation, redemption, and balance management
- Ecommerce platform for digital gift card sales
- Mobile app per W615 for digital gift card storage and balance inquiry
- Gift card liability ledger in Finance module
- Transaction audit trail per gift card serial number
- Corporate bulk order processing per W103

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Gift card fraud**: counterfeit or cloned gift cards; system must validate against activation database in real-time; staff training on handling suspicious cards
- **Unredeemed liability growth**: as program scales, unredeemed balances grow on balance sheet; Finance must actively manage breakage recognition and escheatment compliance
- **Customer confusion on partial redemption**: customers may not understand remaining balance after partial use; clear receipt printing and app-based balance inquiry essential
- **Seasonal demand spikes**: Christmas gift card sales can be 3–5x monthly average; stores must stock sufficient physical cards and POS must handle activation volume
- **Digital vs. physical fragmentation**: customers may try to redeem digital cards by showing phone screen; POS scanner must read mobile barcodes reliably

### Staffing Implication
- **Cashier/CSR**: 3–5 min per gift card transaction; absorbed by existing role
- **Finance**: 4 hours/month on liability management; absorbed by existing role
- **No incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Per sale: 3 min; per redemption: 2 min; per balance inquiry: 1 min
- Monthly volume: ~10,000 transactions × 3 min average = ~500 hours/month chain-wide
- Financial management: 4 hours/month
