## W1918. Category Shopper Behavior Analysis & Purchase Path Research

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual category strategy review; new category launch planning |
| **Frequency** | Annual per category; per new category launch |
| **Volume** | 13 product categories per model company profile |
| **Owner** | Market Research Analyst |
| **Participants** | Market Research Analyst, Category Manager, VP Merchandising |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Market Research Analyst analyzes shopper behavior by category using loyalty/POS data: (a) purchase frequency by category: how often do customers buy from each category? (b) average basket composition: what categories are bought together? (c) category penetration: what % of BuildRight customers buy from each category? (d) purchase trigger: planned purchase vs. impulse purchase by category (survey data), (e) channel preference: in-store vs. online by category, (f) time-of-purchase patterns: weekend vs. weekday, payday vs. non-payday, (g) trade vs. DIY purchase patterns by category | Market Research Analyst | Category Manager | 3–5 days/category |
| 2 | Category Manager uses shopper behavior data for category strategy: (a) identify cross-selling opportunities: categories frequently bought together (e.g., lumber + nails + adhesive) → create bundle promotions, (b) identify underperforming categories: low penetration or declining frequency → investigate cause, (c) optimize shelf allocation: high-frequency categories in accessible locations; low-frequency categories in destination locations, (d) inform assortment decisions per W679 | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 days/category |

### System Touchpoints
- Loyalty data analytics with basket composition and purchase frequency (W1918.1)
- Category penetration and cross-category analysis (W1918.1c)
- Integration with W679 (assortment optimization), W67 (store performance), W85 (product costing)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Data granularity**: Loyalty program data covers ~600,000 members but may not represent non-loyalty walk-in customers (~45% of transactions)


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-analyze of "analyzes shopper behavior by category using loyalty/POS data" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-create of "create bundle promotions," (replaces manual Step 2).

### Controls
- CTL-375 (ensure controlled execution — Product Category & Shopper Research (PA-44.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by Market Research Analyst and Category Manager

### Time Estimate
- Per category: ~5–7 days
- Annual: ~2–3 categories/year prioritized = ~15–20 days/year

## W1919. New Category Viability Study & Consumer Demand Assessment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Category Manager proposes new product category addition |
| **Frequency** | ~3–5 category viability studies/year |
| **Volume** | Per proposed category |
| **Owner** | Market Research Analyst |
| **Participants** | Market Research Analyst, Category Manager, VP Merchandising, FP&A |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Market Research Analyst assesses consumer demand for proposed category: (a) desk research: category market size in Philippines, growth rate, key players, (b) customer survey: include questions about proposed category in quarterly CSAT survey, (c) trade account feedback: Account Managers ask trade professionals about demand for proposed products, (d) competitor analysis: do competitors carry this category? How much shelf space? What pricing?, (e) import feasibility: can the products be sourced at competitive landed cost? | Market Research Analyst | Category Manager | 1–2 weeks |
| 2 | FP&A builds financial model: (a) projected annual revenue from category (conservative, base, optimistic), (b) projected gross margin, (c) required investment: initial inventory, fixtures, training, marketing, (d) payback period and NPV, (e) impact on adjacent categories (cannibalization vs. complementarity) | FP&A | Category Manager | 1 week |
| 3 | Category Manager presents viability study to VP Merchandising for go/no-go decision | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1 day |

- Financial viability model template (W1919.2)
- Integration with W1830 (private label opportunity), W679 (assortment optimization), W1652 (site selection)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Demand overestimation**: New category demand surveys overstate actual purchase intent; use conservative conversion rates


### Automation Opportunity

### Controls
- CTL-375 (ensure controlled execution — Product Category & Shopper Research (PA-44.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by Market Research Analyst, Category Manager, and FP&A

## W1920. Price Sensitivity & Elasticity Research by Category

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual pricing strategy review; significant cost change; competitive pricing pressure |
| **Frequency** | Annual per key category; ad-hoc for pricing decisions |
| **Volume** | Top 8 categories by revenue (representing ~80% of revenue) |
| **Owner** | FP&A |
| **Participants** | FP&A, Category Manager, VP Merchandising |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FP&A analyzes price elasticity using historical data: (a) Van Westendorp price sensitivity analysis: survey customers on what they consider too cheap, cheap, expensive, and too expensive for key products in each category, (b) historical price-volume analysis: when BuildRight changed prices in the past, what was the volume impact? (c) competitive price positioning: BuildRight's price relative to competitors and corresponding market share, (d) segment analysis: are trade professionals more price-sensitive than DIY customers? (e) category elasticity ranking: which categories are most/least price-sensitive? | FP&A | Category Manager | 3–5 days/category |
| 2 | Category Manager applies elasticity insights to pricing strategy: (a) for elastic categories (price-sensitive): maintain competitive pricing; use promotions to drive volume; avoid price increases unless cost-pass-through is necessary, (b) for inelastic categories (price-insensitive): optimize margin; premium positioning where quality perception supports it, (c) for trade vs. DIY: differentiated pricing — trade accounts get quantity discounts; DIY customers pay SRP | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 days/category |
| 3 | VP Merchandising approves pricing strategy adjustments per W40 (price change execution) | VP Merchandising | CFO | Per W40 |

### System Touchpoints
- Price elasticity analytics with historical price-volume data (W1920.1b)
- Van Westendorp survey analysis module (W1920.1a)
- Competitive price positioning tracker (W1920.1c)
- Integration with W40 (price management), W85 (product costing), W1833 (private label pricing)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Historical data limitations**: BuildRight may not have sufficient price variation history for robust elasticity estimation; supplement with survey-based methods


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-analyze of "analyzes price elasticity using historical data" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "counts get quantity discounts" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approves pricing strategy adjustments per W40" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-375 (ensure controlled execution — Product Category & Shopper Research (PA-44.3).)
- operational: FP&A review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by FP&A and Category Manager

### Time Estimate
- Per category: ~5–7 days
- Annual: ~2–3 priority categories × 6 days = ~12–18 days/year

## W1921. Seasonal Demand Pattern Analysis & Forecast Calibration

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual seasonal planning cycle (pre-summer, pre-rainy, pre-Ber months) |
| **Frequency** | Annual analysis; quarterly calibration |
| **Volume** | All 13 product categories with seasonal patterns |
| **Owner** | Merchandise Planner |
| **Participants** | Merchandise Planner, Market Research Analyst, Category Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Merchandise Planner analyzes seasonal demand patterns: (a) 3-year monthly sales by category: identify seasonal peaks and troughs, (b) peak categories: paint and tiles (Jan–Feb dry season), garden and outdoor (Mar–Apr summer), waterproofing (Jun–Aug rainy), appliances and décor (Sep–Dec Ber months), (c) calculate seasonal indices: monthly sales as multiple of average monthly sales, (d) identify external drivers: typhoon events (spike in repair materials), government infrastructure spending timing, OFW remittance peaks (Dec), (e) regional variation: Mindanao dry season differs from Luzon; adjust seasonal indices by region | Merchandise Planner | Category Manager | 1–2 weeks |
| 2 | Market Research Analyst provides forward-looking indicators: (a) PAGASA weather forecast: El Niño/La Niña cycle impacts construction and renovation timing, (b) PSA construction permit trends: leading indicator for contractor demand, (c) consumer sentiment survey data: planned home improvement projects, (d) competitor promotional calendar intelligence: when are competitors running major sales? | Market Research Analyst | VP Merchandising | 1 week |
| 3 | Merchandise Planner adjusts demand forecast with seasonal factors: (a) apply seasonal indices to annual forecast, (b) adjust for known events (e.g., stronger La Niña → higher waterproofing demand), (c) coordinate with Buyer for seasonal inventory buildup per W2, (d) coordinate with VP Merchandising for seasonal promotional calendar per W14 | Merchandise Planner | Category Manager | 1 week |
| 4 | Quarterly: Merchandise Planner compares actual demand to seasonal forecast: (a) forecast accuracy by category, (b) identify categories where actual deviated significantly from forecast — investigate cause, (c) recalibrate seasonal indices based on actuals | Merchandise Planner | VP Merchandising | 1 day/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Seasonal demand analytics with historical pattern recognition (W1921.1)
- Seasonal index model with regional adjustment (W1921.1c)
- Forecast vs. actual accuracy tracking (W1921.4)
- Integration with W2A (auto-replenishment), W2B (import PO), W14 (marketing calendar), W57 (promotional stock)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Typhoon unpredictability**: Major typhoons create demand spikes for repair materials (tarpaulins, plywood, nails, roofing) that are difficult to forecast; maintain safety stock buffer for known typhoon-season categories


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-calculate of "calculate seasonal indices" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-forecast of "forecast" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System workflow orchestration of "coordinate with Buyer for seasonal inventory buildup per W2," (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-375 (ensure controlled execution — Product Category & Shopper Research (PA-44.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by Merchandise Planner and Market Research Analyst

### Time Estimate
- Annual analysis: 1–2 weeks
- Forward-looking indicators: 1 week
- Forecast adjustment: 1 week
- Quarterly calibration: 1 day/quarter
- Total: ~25–30 days/year

## W1922. DIY vs. Trade Professional Shopper Segment Research

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual segment analysis; category strategy requiring segment understanding |
| **Frequency** | Annual comprehensive analysis |
| **Volume** | 2 primary segments (DIY homeowner, trade professional) + corporate buyer sub-segment |
| **Owner** | Market Research Analyst |
| **Participants** | Market Research Analyst, Category Manager, VP Store Operations |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Market Research Analyst profiles DIY vs. trade segments: (a) average transaction value: DIY ~PHP 800–1,500; trade ~PHP 5,000–15,000; corporate ~PHP 50,000–500,000, (b) purchase frequency: DIY ~2–4 visits/month; trade ~4–8 visits/month; corporate ~1–2 visits/month but larger orders, (c) category preferences: DIY → paint, hand tools, garden, décor; trade → lumber, cement, plumbing, electrical, hardware, (d) purchase drivers: DIY → home improvement project, renovation, repair; trade → construction project requirements, (e) decision-making: DIY → influenced by price, brand, Sales Associate recommendation; trade → influenced by specification, availability, credit terms, (f) channel preference: DIY → open to ecommerce; trade → prefer in-store with account card; corporate → prefer account manager + delivery | Market Research Analyst | CMO | 1–2 weeks |
| 2 | Category Manager applies segment insights: (a) store-level assortment tuning: stores near residential areas stock more DIY-friendly products; stores near construction sites stock more trade-oriented products, (b) marketing message differentiation: DIY messaging focuses on project inspiration and ease; trade messaging focuses on specification, availability, and pricing, (c) service differentiation: DIY customers need more associate guidance; trade customers need fast checkout and account access, (d) cross-selling: DIY customer buying paint → suggest brushes, tarps, tape; trade customer buying cement → suggest rebar, sand, gravel | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1 week |
| 3 | VP Store Operations applies segment insights to store staffing and layout: (a) stores with high trade traffic: allocate more experienced associates to trade-heavy departments, (b) stores with high DIY traffic: allocate associates with strong customer service skills, (c) adjust store hours if trade segment prefers early morning visits | VP Store Operations | COO | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints
- Customer segmentation analytics from loyalty/CRM data (W1922.1)
- Segment-level purchase behavior analytics (W1922.1)
- Integration with W679 (assortment optimization), W14 (marketing), W34 (workforce scheduling)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Segment overlap**: Some customers blur segments (small contractor who also does DIY projects at home); segment profiles are directional, not absolute


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "count card" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "checkout and account access," (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-allocate of "allocate more experienced associates to trade-heavy departments," (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-375 (ensure controlled execution — Product Category & Shopper Research (PA-44.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by Market Research Analyst, Category Manager, and VP Store Operations

### Time Estimate
- Annual analysis: 1–2 weeks
- Application to strategy: 2 weeks
- Total: ~20–25 days/year

|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Category review; customer feedback on packaging; competitive packaging analysis |
| **Frequency** | Annual per category |
| **Volume** | High-volume SKU packaging review |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Market Research Analyst, Merchandise Planner |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Category Manager identifies SKUs for package size review: (a) SKUs where customers buy multiple units frequently (opportunity for bulk packaging), (b) SKUs where customers complain about packaging size (too large for DIY, too small for trade), (c) SKUs where competitor packaging is more convenient or cost-effective, (d) private label SKUs where packaging optimization can improve margin | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1 day/category |
| 2 | Market Research Analyst conducts packaging preference research: (a) survey: "What package size would you prefer for [product]?", (b) trade account feedback: "Do you need bulk packaging for this product?", (c) price per unit analysis: compare unit economics across different package sizes, (d) shelf space productivity: which package sizes generate highest sales per linear meter?, (e) competitive packaging audit: what package sizes do competitors offer? | Market Research Analyst | Category Manager | 1 week |
| 3 | Category Manager recommends packaging changes: (a) add bulk pack for trade-frequented categories (e.g., 1kg nail pack → 5kg contractor pack), (b) add smaller pack for DIY categories (e.g., 4L paint → 1L sample size), (c) optimize packaging for ecommerce: smaller, lighter, damage-resistant, (d) coordinate with Buyer and factory for packaging change implementation | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1 week |

- Integration with W1832 (private label specification), W252 (item master), W1848 (planogram)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **SKU proliferation**: Adding package size variants increases SKU count and inventory complexity; only add sizes with clear demand evidence


### Automation Opportunity

- System auto-count of "count feedback" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System workflow orchestration of "coordinate with Buyer and factory for packaging change implementation" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls

## W1924. Store Layout & Category Adjacency Effectiveness Research

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual planogram refresh; new store layout design; renovation |
| **Frequency** | Annual analysis; per new store design |
| **Volume** | Per store format |
| **Owner** | VP Store Operations |
| **Participants** | VP Store Operations, Market Research Analyst, Category Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Market Research Analyst analyzes category adjacency effectiveness: (a) basket analysis: which categories are most frequently bought together? (these should be adjacent or connected), (b) sales per sqm by department: which departments generate the highest/lowest revenue per sqm? (consider expanding high-productivity departments and reducing low-productivity), (c) traffic flow analysis: what % of customers visit each department? Are there dead zones where traffic is low?, (d) trade vs. DIY adjacency: trade-focused departments (lumber, plumbing, electrical) should be clustered; DIY departments (paint, décor, garden) should be clustered | Market Research Analyst | VP Store Operations | 1–2 weeks |
| 2 | VP Store Operations and Category Manager evaluate layout changes: (a) cross-merchandising opportunities: place adhesives near lumber; place paint accessories near paint station; place safety equipment near power tools, (b) destination vs. impulse: destination categories (lumber, cement, tiles) at back of store to drive traffic past impulse categories (hardware, tools, accessories), (c) flow optimization: ensure logical customer path from entrance through departments to checkout, (d) test layout changes at 3–5 pilot stores before chain-wide rollout | VP Store Operations / Category Manager | COO | 1–2 weeks |
| 3 | Measure pilot results: (a) compare sales per sqm before and after layout change at pilot stores, (b) compare customer satisfaction scores at pilot vs. control stores, (c) if positive: implement chain-wide in next seasonal reset (W1407) | Market Research Analyst / VP Store Operations | COO | 4–8 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Basket analysis module for category adjacency (W1924.1a)
- Sales per sqm analytics by department (W1924.1b)
- Traffic flow analytics from CCTV/heatmap (W1924.1c)
- Integration with W1407 (seasonal reset), W1848 (planogram), W67 (store performance)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Layout change cost**: Rearranging 200 stores is expensive (fixtures, signage, labor); pilot testing at 3–5 stores is essential before chain-wide implementation


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-generate of "generate the highest/lowest revenue per sqm?" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "logical customer path from entrance through departments to checkout," (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-measure of "Measure pilot results" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-375 (ensure controlled execution — Product Category & Shopper Research (PA-44.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount

### Time Estimate
- Analysis: 1–2 weeks
- Layout design and pilot: 1–2 weeks
- Pilot measurement: 4–8 weeks
- Chain-wide rollout: per W1407
- Total: ~8–12 weeks/year
