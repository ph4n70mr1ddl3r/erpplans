## W1143. Customer Construction Material Pre-Order Reservation & Price Lock Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests reservation of construction materials for a future project with guaranteed pricing, or customer presents an approved project quotation requiring material price lock |
| **Frequency** | ~1,500–2,500 pre-order reservations/month across all stores (primarily B2B/project accounts) |
| **Volume** | 1 reservation per request; average PHP 80,000–500,000 per reservation |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Lumber & Building Materials) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate, Department Supervisor, Customer, Supply Chain Planning Team (HQ) |

### Background

Construction projects in the Philippines often span several months from planning to execution. During this period, material prices — particularly cement, steel rebar, lumber, and plywood — can fluctuate significantly due to commodity price movements, foreign exchange changes (for imported items), and seasonal demand spikes. For BuildRight's B2B/project customers (30% of revenue from trade accounts and 10% from corporate accounts), price certainty is critical for accurate project budgeting and bid submissions. This workflow provides a formal pre-order reservation service where customers can: (a) reserve specific quantities of construction materials for future delivery; (b) lock in pricing at current rates for a defined period (typically 30–90 days); (c) pay a deposit (10–25% of estimated order value) to secure the reservation; (d) call off materials in staged deliveries aligned with project milestones per W164. This service differentiates BuildRight from competitors who sell only at prevailing prices, increases customer commitment, and provides demand visibility for procurement planning. It complements W979 (B2B blanket purchase agreements) by offering a lighter-weight, deposit-based price lock for smaller or first-time project accounts, and W1159 (price escalation protection) which focuses on long-term contract-based forward pricing.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Reservation Request & Price Lock Assessment**: (a) customer presents project material list and requests price lock; (b) sales associate verifies customer account status (active, good credit standing, no overdue AR per W888); (c) associate enters material list into ERP reservation module with requested quantities and preferred delivery dates; (d) system checks current inventory and incoming supply for each item across relevant DCs; (e) system calculates total estimated order value at current prices; (f) associate reviews price lock eligibility: cement, rebar, lumber, plywood, roofing, pipes — eligible for 30–90 day lock; electrical, plumbing fixtures, tiles — eligible for 30–60 day lock; promotional and seasonal items — not eligible for price lock | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 10–15 min |
| 2 | **Deposit Collection & Reservation Confirmation**: (a) customer pays reservation deposit: 10% for trade accounts with credit history, 25% for new or cash accounts; (b) deposit collected via cash, card, or GCash at POS; (c) system creates formal reservation order with unique reservation number; (d) reserved quantities deducted from available-to-promise (ATP) inventory but not from physical stock; (e) price lock expiry date set (30/60/90 days based on material category and customer tier); (f) customer receives reservation confirmation via email/SMS: reserved items, quantities, locked prices, expiry date, deposit amount, and delivery scheduling instructions | Sales Associate / System | Department Supervisor | 5–10 min |
| 3 | **Reservation Monitoring & Expiry Management**: (a) system generates daily report of active reservations approaching expiry (7-day warning, 3-day warning, 1-day warning); (b) sales associate contacts customer for reservations within 7 days of expiry: confirm delivery schedule, request extension (one-time 15-day extension permitted with supervisor approval), or convert to order; (c) if customer confirms delivery: reservation converted to sales order with delivery scheduling per W1126; (d) if reservation expires without action: deposit forfeited per terms (deposit refundable minus 5% restocking fee if cancelled within first 7 days, non-refundable after); (e) expired reservation quantities released back to available inventory | System / Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 2–3 min/reservation |
| 4 | **Partial Delivery Call-Off & Balance Tracking**: (a) customer calls off partial delivery as project progresses; (b) each call-off creates a delivery order from reservation balance; (c) delivered quantities billed at locked price regardless of current market price; (d) deposit applied proportionally against delivered value (e.g., 10% deposit credited against each delivery invoice); (e) system tracks reservation balance: original quantity, delivered quantity, remaining quantity, remaining deposit; (f) if customer requests additional quantity beyond reservation: additional quantity priced at prevailing rate | Sales Associate / System | Department Supervisor | 5–10 min |
| 5 | **Reservation Closure & Deposit Reconciliation**: (a) when all reserved quantities delivered, or customer confirms remaining quantities no longer needed, reservation closed; (b) final invoice generated reflecting all deliveries at locked prices; (c) remaining deposit balance applied to final invoice or refunded within 7 business days; (d) system generates reservation performance summary: price saved vs. market (positive if prices rose during lock period, zero/negative if prices fell), fill rate (quantity delivered vs. reserved), delivery timeliness; (e) summary stored in customer account record for future quotation reference | System / Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 5 min |

### System Touchpoints
- ERP reservation / pre-order module — reservation creation, price lock, ATP deduction
- POS module — deposit collection and receipt
- Customer account module — credit standing verification, reservation history
- Inventory ATP module — available quantity check, reservation allocation
- Pricing module — current price capture and lock, expiry date management
- Delivery scheduling module (W1126) — call-off delivery creation
- CRM module — customer communication for expiring reservations
- Financial module — deposit tracking, refund processing, invoice generation
- Analytics module — reservation performance, price lock value tracking

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Price risk**: If commodity prices rise significantly during lock period, BuildRight absorbs the margin differential; deposit (10–25%) may not cover full price increase for large orders
- **Inventory commitment**: Reserved quantities reduce ATP for other customers; over-reservation can lead to stockouts for walk-in customers
- **Expired reservation stock**: If large reservations expire, previously committed inventory may now be excess
- **Administrative overhead**: Tracking 1,500–2,500 active reservations with varying expiry dates requires automated system management
- **Dispute risk**: Customers may contest deposit forfeiture on expired reservations; clear terms and acknowledgment required

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate**: Reservation processing adds ~15–25 min per reservation; at ~8–12 reservations/store/week across 12 associates, ~1–2 hours/week per associate — absorbed by existing role
- **Department Supervisor**: Extension approvals and dispute resolution add ~2–3 hours/week — absorbed by existing role
- **No permanent incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Reservation request & assessment: 10–15 min
- Deposit & confirmation: 5–10 min
- Monitoring (per reservation over lifetime): 2–3 min
- Call-off & balance tracking: 5–10 min per delivery
- Closure & reconciliation: 5 min
- **Total per reservation lifecycle**: 30–50 min (spread over 30–90 days)

## W1144. Store-Level Customer Hazardous Material Transport Regulation Advisory

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer purchases hazardous materials (paint, solvents, adhesives, pesticides, LPG, chemicals) requiring transport, or customer inquires about transport regulations |
| **Frequency** | ~15,000–20,000 transactions/month involving hazmat items across all stores |
| **Volume** | 1–10 hazmat items per transaction |
| **Owner** | Sales Associate / Cashier |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate, Customer, Delivery Partner (if delivery selected) |

### Background

BuildRight Depot sells numerous products classified as hazardous materials under Philippine regulations: paints and solvents (flammable), adhesives and thinners (flammable, toxic), pesticides and herbicides (toxic, environmental hazard), LPG tanks (compressed gas), chlorine and pool chemicals (oxidizer, corrosive), muriatic acid and cement (corrosive, irritant), and construction adhesives (toxic, flammable). Philippine transportation regulations (DOTC Department Order, DENR DAO, and PPA/Marina rules for maritime transport) restrict or require special handling for transporting these materials, particularly: (a) private vehicles carrying certain quantities may violate transport regulations; (b) maritime shipping between islands has strict hazmat cargo restrictions — passengers cannot carry hazmat on roll-on-roll-off (RORO) ferries; (c) some chemicals require dangerous goods (DG) placarding for commercial transport; (d) certain materials cannot be co-loaded (e.g., oxidizers with flammables). Many BuildRight customers are unaware of these regulations and may inadvertently violate them by transporting hazmat in private cars, multicabs, or on ferries. This workflow provides point-of-sale advisory and transport guidance to ensure customer safety and regulatory compliance, reducing BuildRight's liability exposure. It complements W236 (hazmat storage at DC), W237 (hazmat safety training), W238 (spill response), and W5B.9 (age-restricted prompts) by addressing the transport leg of the hazmat lifecycle.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hazmat Item Detection at POS**: (a) POS system scans item and identifies hazmat classification from item master flag (linked to SDS data per W698); (b) system displays hazmat transport advisory prompt with hazard class: flammable (Class 3), corrosive (Class 8), toxic (Class 6.1), compressed gas (Class 2), oxidizer (Class 5.1); (c) system checks total quantity of hazmat items in transaction against threshold limits for private vehicle transport: paint/thinners ≤ 60L per vehicle (per DOTC guidelines for personal use exemption), LPG ≤ 1 tank per private vehicle, pesticides ≤ 5L per private vehicle; (d) if any item exceeds private transport limit, system flags for delivery recommendation | System / Cashier | Department Supervisor | 15–30 sec |
| 2 | **Customer Transport Advisory & Safety Briefing**: (a) cashier informs customer of transport advisory: material classification, quantity restrictions, and safe transport recommendations; (b) key safety instructions provided verbally and printed on receipt: "Keep containers upright. Do not expose to direct sunlight. Ensure vehicle ventilation. Do not transport in enclosed passenger compartment."; (c) for island customers (especially Visayas/Mindanao): "Maritime regulation prohibits carrying flammable liquids, LPG, and toxic substances as passenger baggage on RORO ferries. These items must be declared as cargo and transported in the vehicle hold or via separate cargo booking."; (d) for items exceeding private transport limits: customer offered BuildRight delivery service (W5D) or 3PL hazmat-certified delivery (W19) with DG-compliant vehicle | Cashier | Cashier | 1–2 min |
| 3 | **Delivery Option Processing (If Required)**: (a) if customer selects delivery for hazmat items: delivery order created in ERP with DG classification flag; (b) system routes order to DG-certified delivery partner or BuildRight's own fleet (if hazmat-equipped); (c) delivery fee includes hazmat handling surcharge (10–15% premium); (d) delivery partner ensures proper DG placarding, segregation (incompatible materials separated), and documentation (dangerous goods declaration if applicable); (e) customer signs DG transport acknowledgment at delivery | Cashier / Delivery Partner | Department Supervisor | 3–5 min |
| 4 | **Compliance Logging & Reporting**: (a) POS logs hazmat advisory delivery with transaction record: items sold, quantities, hazard classes, advisory type given, customer response (self-transport or delivery); (b) monthly compliance report generated: total hazmat transactions, delivery conversion rate, advisory completion rate; (c) data feeds into hazmat regulatory compliance reporting per DENR requirements (W433 SMR/CMR) and internal safety audit (W140); (d) recurring customers who repeatedly self-transport large hazmat quantities flagged for safety follow-up | System | Department Supervisor | Automated |

### System Touchpoints
- POS module — hazmat item detection, advisory prompt, transport advisory printing
- Item master (W252) — hazmat classification flag, hazard class, transport threshold quantities
- SDS management module (W698) — safety data sheet data linked to item master
- Delivery module (W5D, W19) — hazmat delivery order creation with DG flag
- Fleet management (W199) — DG-certified vehicle assignment
- Compliance reporting module — hazmat transaction logging, advisory completion tracking
- Receipt printer — transport advisory text on customer receipt

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Customer resistance**: Customers may view advisory as an inconvenience or upsell tactic; clear safety-first framing is essential
- **Cashier knowledge**: Cashiers need basic hazmat transport knowledge to answer customer questions; training required (W518)
- **Liability**: Even with advisory, BuildRight may face liability if a customer has an accident while transporting hazmat purchased from the store; signed acknowledgment recommended
- **RORO enforcement gaps**: Philippine maritime enforcement of hazmat transport on RORO ferries is inconsistent; customers may disregard advisory
- **Delivery cost**: Hazmat-certified delivery is expensive; customers in remote areas may have no alternative to self-transport

### Staffing Implication
- **Cashier**: Advisory adds ~1–2 min per hazmat transaction; at ~75–100 hazmat transactions/store/week across 3 cashiers, ~25–33 min/week per cashier — absorbed by existing role
- **No permanent incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Hazmat detection: 15–30 sec (automated)
- Advisory & safety briefing: 1–2 min
- Delivery processing (if needed): 3–5 min
- **Total per transaction**: 1.5–8 min (depending on delivery need)

## W1145. Vendor Seasonal Import Container Consolidation & Optimization Planning

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Seasonal buy planning cycle (W32) generates import PO projections, or monthly import volume review identifies consolidation opportunity |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly planning cycles; monthly execution adjustments |
| **Volume** | ~400–600 TEUs/month across ~400 import vendors; consolidation targets 10–15% reduction in container count |
| **Owner** | Supply Chain Planning Manager (HQ) |
| **Participants** | Supply Chain Planner, Buyer / Category Manager, Import Logistics Coordinator, Vendor Representatives, Freight Forwarder |

### Background

BuildRight Depot imports approximately 400–600 TEUs (twenty-foot equivalent units) of merchandise per month from ~400 international vendors across China, Taiwan, Indonesia, Malaysia, Japan, and Europe. Import freight represents a significant cost component (estimated PHP 150M–250M/year in ocean freight). Current procurement practices often result in: (a) multiple small shipments from the same country/region shipped in partially filled containers; (b) inconsistent container utilization rates (average 70–75% vs. industry benchmark of 85–90%); (c) missed opportunities to combine orders from multiple vendors shipping from the same port into consolidated containers. Container consolidation — grouping multiple purchase orders from vendors in the same geographic region into shared containers — can reduce freight cost by 10–15% (estimated PHP 15M–38M/year savings), improve container utilization to 85%+, reduce port congestion impact, and lower per-unit landed cost. This workflow manages the quarterly planning and monthly execution of import container consolidation. It complements W2B (import PO lifecycle), W144 (international logistics), and W32 (seasonal buy planning) by focusing on the freight optimization dimension.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Quarterly Consolidation Opportunity Analysis**: (a) at the start of each quarter, Supply Chain Planning Manager extracts projected import PO data from seasonal buy plan (W32) and demand forecast (W31); (b) group projected import volume by country of origin, port of loading, and shipping window; (c) identify consolidation clusters: vendors shipping from same port (e.g., Shenzhen, Shanghai, Keelung) within same 2-week window; (d) calculate current vs. projected container utilization per cluster; (e) quantify consolidation savings: fewer containers × freight per TEU = estimated quarterly savings; (f) prioritize consolidation opportunities by savings potential (top 10 clusters typically represent 60–70% of savings) | Supply Chain Planner | Supply Chain Planning Manager | 1–2 days |
| 2 | **Vendor Coordination & Shipment Scheduling Alignment**: (a) for each prioritized consolidation cluster, Buyer contacts vendors to align production and shipment schedules; (b) negotiate delivery-to-port windows that allow consolidation (e.g., "Deliver to Shanghai warehouse by week 15 for consolidated sailing week 16"); (c) coordinate with freight forwarder to arrange consolidated booking: single bill of lading per container with multiple vendor shipments; (d) vendors ship to designated freight forwarder warehouse at port of loading for consolidation; (e) system updates import POs with consolidated shipment reference and estimated sailing date | Buyer / Import Logistics Coordinator | Supply Chain Planning Manager | 3–5 days per cluster |
| 3 | **Container Loading Optimization & Documentation**: (a) freight forwarder receives goods from multiple vendors at consolidation warehouse; (b) forwarder optimizes container loading plan: heavy items (tiles, cement, steel) at bottom, lighter items (tools, hardware, accessories) on top; (c) incompatible materials segregated (hazmat per W699, chemicals separated from food-contact items); (d) container loaded to target 85–90% utilization by volume and weight (max payload per TEU: ~18–20 metric tons); (e) consolidation documentation prepared: combined packing list, house bills of lading per vendor, container manifest; (f) system receives container loading confirmation with actual quantities per vendor PO | Freight Forwarder / Import Logistics Coordinator | Supply Chain Planning Manager | 1–2 days per container |
| 4 | **Inbound Tracking & Cost Allocation**: (a) consolidated container tracked via supply chain control tower (W250) from port of loading to Philippine destination port; (b) upon arrival and customs clearance (W464), container devanned at DC; (c) goods received per individual vendor PO using standard goods receipt process (W3); (d) freight cost allocated proportionally to each vendor's PO based on volume/weight share: vendor A occupied 40% of container → vendor A's items absorb 40% of freight cost in landed cost calculation (W2B.12); (e) actual consolidation savings tracked: freight cost per unit vs. baseline (non-consolidated) rate | Import Logistics Coordinator / System | Supply Chain Planning Manager | 1 day |
| 5 | **Consolidation Performance Review & Savings Reporting**: (a) monthly consolidation report generated: containers consolidated, utilization rate improvement (target: 70% → 85%), freight savings vs. baseline, vendor compliance rate (vendors meeting consolidation schedule); (b) quarterly review with Category Managers: consolidation savings by category, impact on lead times, vendor feedback; (c) consolidation savings feed into vendor scorecard (W44) as logistics collaboration metric; (d) annual freight cost benchmarking (W762) updated with consolidation data | Supply Chain Planner | VP Supply Chain | 2–3 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- ERP import PO module — PO data, shipment scheduling, vendor-country mapping
- Supply chain planning module (W31, W32) — demand projection and seasonal buy plan
- Container consolidation optimizer — grouping algorithm, utilization calculator
- Freight forwarder portal — consolidation warehouse receipt, loading plan, documentation
- Supply chain control tower (W250) — container tracking
- Landed cost module (W2B.12) — freight cost allocation per PO
- DC receiving module (W3) — goods receipt for devanned items
- Vendor scorecard module (W44) — consolidation compliance tracking
- Analytics module — consolidation savings, utilization rates, performance dashboards

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Lead time impact**: Consolidation requires vendors to deliver to forwarder warehouse earlier than direct shipment; vendors with inflexible production schedules may resist
- **Delay cascading**: One late vendor delays the entire consolidated container for all vendors; penalty clauses or backup shipment planning needed
- **Damage liability**: Goods from multiple vendors in one container complicate damage claims; clear photography and documentation at loading/unloading essential
- **Vendor compliance**: Not all vendors will agree to consolidation schedule changes; may need incentive (faster payment terms, preferential order allocation)
- **Freight forwarder dependency**: Consolidation quality depends on forwarder capability; regular forwarder performance review (W242) essential

### Staffing Implication
- **Supply Chain Planner**: Consolidation planning adds ~10–15 hours/quarter — absorbed by existing planning team
- **Import Logistics Coordinator**: Vendor coordination and loading supervision adds ~5–8 hours/month — existing role
- **Buyer**: Vendor schedule alignment adds ~2–3 hours/quarter per category — absorbed by existing role
- **No permanent incremental headcount** — savings of PHP 15M–38M/year far exceed incremental staff time cost

### Time Estimate
- Quarterly analysis: 1–2 days (central team)
- Vendor coordination: 3–5 days per cluster (spread over weeks)
- Loading & documentation: 1–2 days per container (freight forwarder)
- Inbound & cost allocation: 1 day per container
- Performance review: 2–3 hours/month
- **Total HQ effort**: ~40–60 hours/quarter

## W1146. Store-Level Customer Cement & Bagged Material Shelf Life Monitoring & Freshness Guarantee

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily stock check identifies cement or bagged material approaching shelf life limit, or customer concern about material freshness, or receiving inspection flags age concern |
| **Frequency** | ~500–800 shelf life checks/month across all stores (daily for cement, weekly for other bagged materials) |
| **Volume** | 35,000 active SKUs; ~200–300 cement/bagged material SKUs per store requiring shelf life monitoring |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Lumber & Building Materials) |
| **Participants** | Stock Associate, Department Supervisor, Receiving Clerk, Vendor (for returns) |

### Background

Cement and other bagged construction materials have finite shelf lives that directly affect product performance: (a) Portland cement has a shelf life of approximately 3 months when stored in dry conditions, but degrades faster in humid Philippine tropical weather (especially during rainy season); (b) bagged mortar mix, thin-set adhesive, and grout have shelf lives of 6–12 months from manufacture date; (c) expired or moisture-exposed cement loses compressive strength, leading to structural failures in customer construction projects; (d) chemical-based products (waterproofing, sealants, adhesives) degrade after expiry, causing application failures. BuildRight's reputation as a trusted building materials supplier depends on ensuring customers receive fresh, performance-guaranteed materials. Selling expired or degraded cement can result in: customer project failures, liability claims, product returns, and brand damage. Philippine construction industry standards (DPWH specifications) require fresh cement for structural applications. This workflow monitors shelf life of bagged materials at store level, ensures FEFO (first-expired-first-out) rotation, and provides customers with freshness verification. It complements W1005 (shelf stock rotation for paint/chemicals), W4.5 (FEFO picking at DC), and W3.4 (shelf-life capture at receiving).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Receiving Date Capture & Shelf Life Assignment**: (a) when cement or bagged materials are received at store (from DC per W4 or DSD per W18), receiving clerk scans batch/lot number and manufacture date from packaging; (b) system calculates expiry date: cement — 90 days from manufacture date (reduced to 75 days during June–November rainy season); mortar/grout mix — 12 months from manufacture; waterproofing compounds — 18 months from manufacture; (c) expiry date assigned in inventory record; (d) system alerts if received batch already exceeds 50% of shelf life (e.g., cement received at 50+ days old) — flags for priority shelving and early sale | Receiving Clerk / System | Department Supervisor | 30–60 sec/pallet |
| 2 | **Daily Shelf Life Monitoring & FEFO Rotation**: (a) each morning, stock associate performs shelf life spot-check on cement and bagged materials display; (b) system-generated daily alert lists items within 14 days of expiry; (c) stock associate physically rotates stock using FEFO: oldest stock pulled to front, newest stock placed behind; (d) for cement: check for hardened lumps, torn bags, water stains — remove and quarantine any compromised bags; (e) physical rotation confirmed in system with scan of batch barcode on oldest stock | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 15–20 min/day |
| 3 | **Customer Freshness Guarantee at POS**: (a) when customer purchases cement or age-sensitive materials, cashier scans batch barcode at checkout; (b) system displays manufacture date and remaining shelf life; (c) if batch is within 30 days of expiry for cement, or 60 days for other bagged materials: POS prompts cashier to offer freshest batch; (d) if fresher batch available in backroom, stock associate retrieves it; (e) receipt prints manufacture date and "Freshness Guaranteed" statement with batch number; (f) for large cement orders (50+ bags): customer may request freshness verification certificate for project documentation | Cashier / Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 30–60 sec/transaction |
| 4 | **Near-Expiry Markdown & Disposal**: (a) items reaching 14 days before expiry automatically flagged for markdown: 20% discount at 14 days, 40% at 7 days; (b) markdown applied in POS system with "Near-Expiry — Use Immediately" shelf label; (c) customer informed of reduced shelf life and advised to use within stated period; (d) items past expiry removed from shelf, quarantined, and dispositioned: return to vendor if return agreement exists per W88 (RTV); dispose via authorized construction waste hauler if non-returnable; (e) disposal recorded in system with waste code and documentation for DENR compliance (W82) | Stock Associate / System | Department Supervisor | 5–10 min/batch |

### System Touchpoints
- ERP inventory module — batch/lot tracking, shelf life assignment, expiry alerting
- POS module — batch scan at checkout, freshness date display, near-expiry markdown
- Receiving module (W109) — manufacture date capture at goods receipt
- FEFO rotation module — stock rotation prioritization
- Markdown module (W93) — near-expiry pricing automation
- RTV module (W88) — vendor return for expired stock
- Waste disposal module (W82, W502) — authorized disposal documentation
- Analytics module — shelf life performance, waste rates, freshness compliance

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Climate impact**: Philippine humidity and rainy season accelerate cement degradation; shelf life assumptions must account for actual storage conditions
- **Customer awareness**: Most customers don't check cement manufacture dates; relying on BuildRight's freshness guarantee builds trust but creates liability
- **Waste cost**: Expired cement represents full cost write-off; estimated 2–3% waste rate on cement = ~PHP 25M–40M/year across chain
- **Vendor cooperation**: Not all cement vendors accept returns of aged stock; negotiation during vendor onboarding (W36) critical
- **Physical handling**: Rotating 50kg cement bags is physically demanding; ergonomic training required for stock associates

### Staffing Implication
- **Stock Associate**: Daily shelf life rotation adds ~15–20 min/day for 4 stock associates = ~1–1.3 hours/day total — absorbed by existing role
- **Receiving Clerk**: Date capture adds ~30–60 sec per pallet — absorbed by existing role
- **No permanent incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Receiving date capture: 30–60 sec/pallet
- Daily monitoring & rotation: 15–20 min/store/day
- POS freshness check: 30–60 sec/transaction
- Near-expiry markdown: 5–10 min/batch
- **Total per store per month**: ~8–10 hours

## W1147. Customer B2B Project Material Return Window Management & Excess Inventory Reconciliation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | B2B/project customer requests return of unused construction materials after project completion, or project account reconciliation identifies surplus materials |
| **Frequency** | ~300–500 project return requests/month across all stores |
| **Volume** | 5–50 line items per return; average return value PHP 15,000–80,000 |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Department Supervisor, Sales Associate, Customer Service Rep, Customer (Project Manager) |

### Background

## W1150. Store-Level Customer Lumber & Plywood Moisture Content Testing & Quality Verification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests moisture content verification for lumber or plywood purchase, or receiving inspection identifies potential moisture damage, or routine quality sampling |
| **Frequency** | ~1,500–2,500 moisture tests/month across all stores (higher during rainy season) |
| **Volume** | 1–10 boards/sheets per test |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (Lumber & Building Materials) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Lumber Yard), Customer |

### Background

Lumber and plywood are among BuildRight's highest-value product categories (14% of active SKUs, ~4,900 SKUs), and their quality is directly affected by moisture content. In the Philippines' tropical climate with 70–90% ambient humidity and distinct rainy seasons: (a) lumber stored outdoors or in poorly ventilated areas absorbs moisture, leading to warping, swelling, mold growth, and reduced structural strength; (b) plywood exposed to rain delaminates as adhesive bonds weaken; (c) kiln-dried lumber (target moisture content: 12–18%) reabsorbs moisture if stored improperly; (d) construction projects using wet lumber experience nail pops, joint separation, and surface defects as wood dries and shrinks in place. Professional contractors and quality-conscious customers increasingly demand moisture content verification before purchasing lumber, especially for: structural framing (floor joists, roof trusses), cabinetry and furniture-grade wood, and marine/coastal construction. This workflow provides in-store moisture content testing using pin-type moisture meters, building customer confidence and differentiating BuildRight from competitors who sell lumber without quality verification. It complements W976 (lumber grade selection), W1139 (lumber yard seasonal rotation), and W1103 (lumber yard rain protection).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer Moisture Test Request**: (a) customer selects lumber or plywood from yard display; (b) customer requests moisture content test, or sales associate proactively offers test for structural-grade lumber; (c) sales associate retrieves pin-type moisture meter from tool station; (d) selects appropriate test setting on meter: softwood, hardwood, or plywood mode | Sales Associate | Sales Associate | 1–2 min |
| 2 | **Moisture Content Measurement**: (a) sales associate inserts meter pins into wood surface at 2–3 points along the board length (middle and both ends); (b) meter displays moisture content percentage; (c) associate records readings in mobile ERP app linked to the specific board/batch; (d) moisture content interpreted against standards: kiln-dried lumber: target 12–18% (acceptable), 18–22% (borderline — advise customer to acclimate before use), >22% (too wet — do not sell as kiln-dried, offer as air-dried at reduced price); plywood: target ≤14% (acceptable), 14–18% (borderline), >18% (potential delamination risk — do not sell for structural use); (e) associate communicates results to customer with explanation of implications | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 3–5 min |
| 3 | **Quality Grading & Pricing Adjustment**: (a) if moisture content is within acceptable range: board sold at standard kiln-dried price with moisture reading noted on sales receipt; (b) if moisture content is borderline: customer informed and given option to purchase at standard price with advisory to acclimate wood before installation, or select a different board; (c) if moisture content is excessive: board reclassified from "kiln-dried" to "air-dried/green" in system with corresponding markdown (10–20% discount per W93); (d) if board shows visible defects (warping, mold, delamination): quarantined and dispositioned per W91 (damaged goods) | Sales Associate / System | Department Supervisor | 2–3 min |
| 4 | **Batch-Level Quality Tracking**: (a) moisture readings aggregated by supplier batch/lot for quality tracking; (b) batches with consistently high moisture content (>25% of readings above threshold) flagged for vendor feedback per W110 (supplier quality CAPA); (c) seasonal moisture trend analysis: during rainy season (June–November), expect higher moisture readings → adjust storage practices (cover lumber stacks, improve ventilation); (d) monthly lumber quality report generated: average moisture by species and grade, moisture-related markdown cost, vendor quality scores | System / Department Supervisor | Store Manager | Automated + 30 min/month review |

### System Touchpoints
- Mobile ERP app — moisture reading capture linked to board/batch
- Item master module — lumber species, grade, and target moisture range
- Pricing/markdown module — moisture-based reclassification pricing
- Inventory module — batch/lot quality tracking
- Vendor quality module (W110) — batch-level quality feedback
- Analytics module — moisture trend analysis, seasonal patterns, vendor performance

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Meter accuracy**: Pin-type moisture meters can vary ±2–3%; calibration checks required weekly
- **Surface vs. core moisture**: Pins measure surface moisture which may differ from core moisture in thick lumber; deep-pin meters needed for beams and posts
- **Customer education**: Many customers don't understand moisture content implications; sales associate must explain in practical terms ("This wood will shrink and your nails will pop")
- **Testing throughput**: Testing 10 boards for a large contractor adds 10–15 minutes; scheduling during low-traffic periods preferred
- **Moisture meter maintenance**: Pins bend and break; regular replacement needed (~PHP 500/pin set)

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate**: Testing adds 3–5 min per request; at ~8–12 tests/store/day across lumber yard associates, ~30–60 min/day total — absorbed by existing 4 stock associates
- **No permanent incremental headcount** — moisture testing service estimated to increase lumber sales by 5–8% through improved customer confidence

### Time Estimate
- Request & setup: 1–2 min
- Measurement: 3–5 min
- Grading & pricing: 2–3 min
- **Total per test**: 6–10 min

### Background

## W1154. Store-Level Customer Paint VOC & Indoor Air Quality Compliance Advisory

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer purchases paint or coating products, or customer inquires about indoor air quality, or customer specifies enclosed-space application |
| **Frequency** | ~8,000–12,000 VOC advisory interactions/month across all stores (paint is 8% of active SKUs, ~2,800 SKUs) |
| **Volume** | 1–5 paint/coating products per interaction |
| **Owner** | Sales Associate (Paint & Finishes Department) |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate, Customer |

### Background

Paint and coating products emit volatile organic compounds (VOCs) that affect indoor air quality and human health. Philippine regulations and global standards increasingly restrict VOC content in paints: (a) DENR Administrative Order (DAO) sets VOC limits for architectural paints; (b) Green building standards (BERDE, the Philippine green building rating system per W800) require low-VOC paints for certified buildings; (c) enclosed spaces (bathrooms, kitchens, small rooms) amplify VOC exposure risks; (d) vulnerable populations (children, elderly, pregnant women, those with respiratory conditions) face heightened health risks from VOC exposure; (e) occupational safety standards require proper ventilation during and after painting. BuildRight's paint category (2,800 SKUs, 8% of active assortment) includes products with varying VOC levels: zero-VOC (water-based acrylics), low-VOC (most latex paints, <50g/L VOC), medium-VOC (some enamels, 50–150g/L), and high-VOC (solvent-based paints, lacquers, industrial coatings, >150g/L). This workflow provides point-of-sale advisory to help customers select appropriate paint products based on application environment, ventilation conditions, and health considerations. It complements W168 (paint mixing), W1130 (tinting machine calibration), and W1093 (paint color scheme consultation) by adding the health and regulatory compliance dimension.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Application Environment Assessment**: (a) customer approaches paint department or system identifies paint purchase at POS; (b) sales associate asks application questions: "Where will this paint be used?" (bedroom, bathroom, kitchen, living room, exterior, commercial space), "How large is the space?", "Is the space well-ventilated?", "Are there children, elderly, pregnant women, or anyone with respiratory conditions in the household?", "Is this for a BERDE/LEED-certified building project?"; (c) associate enters responses into ERP paint advisory module | Sales Associate | Sales Associate | 1–2 min |
| 2 | **VOC Level Matching & Product Recommendation**: (a) system displays paint options filtered by application: enclosed rooms with vulnerable occupants → recommend zero-VOC or low-VOC only; well-ventilated spaces without vulnerable occupants → low or medium VOC acceptable; exterior applications → no VOC restriction (outdoor ventilation sufficient); commercial/industrial with PPE → all options available; BERDE/LEED projects → zero or low-VOC with compliance documentation; (b) system shows VOC content (g/L) for each paint option alongside standard performance specs (coverage, durability, finish); (c) associate explains trade-offs: zero/low-VOC paints may have slightly fewer color options or longer drying time but are safer for indoor use; (d) for high-VOC products selected for enclosed spaces: system displays advisory warning and requires customer acknowledgment per W520-style prompt | Sales Associate / System | Department Supervisor | 2–3 min |
| 3 | **Ventilation & Safety Guidance**: (a) for any indoor paint purchase, associate provides ventilation guidance: "Open windows and use fans during and after painting", "Wait at least 24 hours before reoccupying the room for low-VOC; 48–72 hours for medium/high-VOC", "Wear a paint mask (N95 or VOC-rated) when applying solvent-based paints"; (b) system suggests complementary safety products: paint mask (N95), gloves, drop cloths, painter's tape, ventilation fan; (c) for large indoor projects: recommend purchasing or renting a portable ventilation fan per W139 (tool rental); (d) guidance printed on receipt alongside paint mixing instructions | Sales Associate | Sales Associate | 1–2 min |

- ERP paint advisory module — application questionnaire, VOC filtering, product recommendation
- Product master (W252) — VOC content per SKU, certification data

- POS module — advisory prompts, receipt printing of safety guidance
- Item compatibility module — complementary product suggestions
- Compliance certificate generator — BERDE/LEED documentation
- Analytics module — VOC advisory completion rate, low-VOC product sales mix, customer compliance tracking

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Product availability**: Low/zero-VOC paint options may be limited in the Philippine market, especially for specialty finishes; customer may need to accept higher-VOC alternative
- **Price premium**: Zero/low-VOC paints typically cost 15–30% more than standard formulations; customer may resist the premium without clear health benefit explanation
- **Advisory fatigue**: Adding VOC advisory to every paint transaction may slow checkout; automate where possible and limit detailed advisory to high-risk applications (enclosed spaces, vulnerable occupants)

## W1159. Customer Construction Material Price Escalation Protection & Forward Pricing Agreement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Corporate/project customer requests fixed pricing for a multi-month construction project, or bid/tender submission requires price commitment for project duration |
| **Frequency** | ~100–200 forward pricing agreements/year (large corporate/project accounts) |
| **Volume** | 20–200 SKUs per agreement; agreement value PHP 500,000–50,000,000 |
| **Owner** | VP for Merchandising |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, VP Merchandising, Finance Manager, Customer (Corporate Purchasing), VP Sales |

### Background

Large construction projects in the Philippines typically span 6–24 months from groundbreaking to completion. During this period, material prices can escalate significantly: (a) cement prices increased 15–25% during 2022–2023 supply chain disruptions; (b) steel rebar prices follow global commodity indices with 10–30% annual volatility; (c) imported tiles and fixtures are subject to foreign exchange fluctuations (PHP/USD moved from 48 to 56 during 2022); (d) lumber prices are affected by both global supply chains and domestic typhoon-related demand spikes. For corporate/project customers (10% of revenue) bidding on construction projects, price uncertainty is a critical risk: if they quote a project price based on today's material costs and prices rise 15% during construction, their profit margin evaporates. Competitors who offer forward pricing agreements win these accounts. This workflow provides BuildRight's most strategic customers with formal forward pricing agreements (FPAs): contractual commitments to supply specified materials at fixed (or capped-escalation) prices for the project duration. FPAs are distinct from W1143 (pre-order reservation — deposit-based, shorter duration) by being formal contracts with legal commitments, covering larger value, and spanning longer durations. They complement W163 (contract pricing), W165 (project retention billing), and W979 (blanket purchase agreements) by adding the price escalation protection dimension.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Forward Pricing Request & Material Scope Definition**: (a) customer (developer, government agency, or large contractor) submits forward pricing request with project details: project name and location, estimated project duration (months), material list with estimated quantities per month, current market pricing reference, required delivery schedule and locations; (b) Category Manager reviews material scope: which items are eligible for forward pricing (cement, rebar, lumber, plywood, tiles, pipes — commodities with volatile pricing), which are excluded (specialty items, seasonal items, items with less than 10% annual price volatility); (c) preliminary pricing assessment: current BuildRight cost, current market price, historical 12-month price trend, projected price risk (using commodity indices and FX forecasts) | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1–2 days |
| 2 | **Pricing Risk Assessment & Margin Modeling**: (a) finance manager performs pricing risk analysis: for each eligible item, calculate maximum expected price increase over project duration using Monte Carlo simulation with inputs from commodity price indices (cement: Philippine Cement Manufacturers Association index, steel: London Metal Exchange, FX: BSP historical volatility); (b) margin modeling: at proposed fixed price, what is BuildRight's expected margin if (a) prices remain flat, (b) prices increase by expected amount, (c) prices increase by 2× expected amount (stress scenario); (c) risk mitigation options: BuildRight hedges commodity exposure through forward purchase contracts with own vendors; price escalation cap (e.g., fixed for first 6 months, then capped at 5% increase per quarter); material substitution clause (if specific brand unavailable, equivalent quality substitute at same price); (d) minimum volume commitment required from customer to justify price lock (typically 80% of estimated quantities) | Finance Manager / Category Manager | CFO | 2–3 days |
| 3 | **Forward Pricing Agreement Negotiation & Execution**: (a) Category Manager and VP Merchandising present pricing proposal to customer: fixed price per item for agreement duration, minimum volume commitment, delivery terms and locations, escalation cap (if applicable), material substitution clause, early termination terms, force majeure clause (typhoon, supply chain disruption); (b) negotiation on key terms: customer wants lowest possible fixed price, BuildRight needs margin protection; (c) agreement executed with legal review (W230) and signed by both parties; (d) FPA loaded into ERP: customer account linked to agreement, eligible items with agreed pricing, minimum volume tracking, expiry date, escalation schedule (if any); (e) customer's trade account updated: FPA pricing automatically applied to qualifying purchases | VP Merchandising / Category Manager | CEO (for agreements > PHP 10M) | 1–2 weeks |
| 4 | **Ongoing Monitoring & Margin Tracking**: (a) monthly FPA margin report: actual cost vs. FPA price per item, cumulative margin impact, volume consumed vs. minimum commitment; (b) if actual costs exceed FPA price (negative margin): margin shortfall tracked and reported to CFO; (c) if customer purchasing below minimum volume commitment: account manager contacts customer for volume catch-up plan; (d) if commodity prices spike >20% above FPA price: force majeure review and potential renegotiation per agreement terms; (e) at agreement expiry: actual margin performance compared to risk model; lessons learned feed into next FPA risk assessment | Finance Manager / Category Manager | CFO | 2–3 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- ERP pricing module — FPA pricing loaded per customer, automatic application at POS/order
- Commodity price tracking module (W760) — cement, steel, FX indices
- Financial risk modeling module — Monte Carlo simulation, margin stress testing
- Customer account module — FPA linkage, volume tracking
- Contract management module (W402) — FPA document storage, expiry tracking
- AP module — vendor forward purchase cost tracking
- Analytics module — FPA margin performance, risk model accuracy, cumulative P&L impact

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Margin erosion**: If commodity prices spike beyond risk model projections, BuildRight absorbs significant margin loss; FPAs must include adequate margin buffer or escalation caps
- **Volume commitment enforcement**: Customers may purchase less than committed (project delays, scope changes); minimum volume enforcement is difficult in practice
- **Administrative complexity**: Each FPA is a custom contract with unique terms; system must handle multiple active FPAs with different pricing, durations, and terms simultaneously
- **Competitive pressure**: If competitors offer lower forward pricing, customer may split purchases; relationship management critical
- **Force majeure disputes**: Defining what constitutes a force majeure event (typhoon clearly qualifies; a 20% price increase is debatable) requires precise contract language

### Staffing Implication
- **Category Manager**: FPA management adds ~40–60 hours/year for ~100–200 agreements — absorbed by existing 5 category managers
- **Finance Manager**: Risk assessment and monitoring adds ~30–40 hours/year — absorbed by existing FP&A team
- **No permanent incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Request & scope definition: 1–2 days
- Risk assessment & modeling: 2–3 days
- Negotiation & execution: 1–2 weeks
- Ongoing monitoring: 2–3 hours/month per active FPA
- **Total per FPA**: 20–40 hours (spread over agreement duration)

### Background

Power tools and equipment represent a high-value, high-margin category at BuildRight Depot (5% of active SKUs, ~1,750 SKUs, but significantly higher margin than building materials). Customers hesitate to purchase expensive power tools (PHP 3,000–50,000+) without hands-on testing because: (a) ergonomics vary significantly between brands — weight, grip size, vibration level, and balance affect user comfort; (b) performance claims (power, speed, battery life) are difficult to evaluate from specifications alone; (c) brand loyalty is strong among professional contractors — they want to verify that a new model meets their expectations before investing; (d) DIY customers switching from hand tools to power tools want reassurance they can handle the equipment safely. A try-before-you-buy program increases power tool conversion rates (estimated +15–25% for tested tools), reduces returns (customers are confident in their purchase), and drives brand switching (when customers discover a superior tool through testing). This workflow manages the in-store try-before-you-buy experience. It complements W1007 (tool demo reservation), W607 (product demo station daily operations), W1087 (brand ambassador demo events), and W749 (POS safety acknowledgment) by providing a structured pre-purchase testing experience.

### System Touchpoints
- ERP inventory module — demo unit tracking (separate from sale stock)
- POS module — new unit sale processing, safety acknowledgment per W749
- Demo reservation module (W1007) — appointment scheduling for popular demo times
- Customer account module — test history, purchase conversion tracking
- Warranty module (W33) — warranty registration at sale
- Product assortment module (W1) — customer feedback on tested/declined tools
- Demo unit lifecycle module — rotation schedule, wear tracking, replacement ordering
- Analytics module — try-before-you-buy conversion rate, most-tested tools, average time-to-purchase

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Safety risk**: Power tools are inherently dangerous; demo station must have proper safety barriers, PPE, and trained supervision; liability insurance coverage verified per W59
- **Demo unit cost": Maintaining demo units for 1,750 power tool SKUs is impractical; limit demo program to top 20–30 tools per store (by sales volume)
- **Space requirement": Demo station requires 10–15 sqm of selling area; stores with limited space may use mobile demo cart
- **Noise": Power tool testing generates significant noise; demo station positioned away from customer service areas and noise-sensitive departments
- **Theft risk": Demo units are attractive targets; secured when not in use

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate**: Try-before-you-buy adds ~20–35 min per session; at ~8–12 sessions/store/week across tools department associates, ~2–4 hours/week total — absorbed by existing role
- **Demo Station Attendant**: Can be a dedicated role or rotated among stock associates; ~15–20 min/day maintenance — absorbed by existing stock associates
- **No permanent incremental headcount** — estimated 15–25% increase in power tool conversion rate for tested tools justifies time investment

### Time Estimate
- Need assessment: 3–5 min
- Setup & safety: 5–8 min
- Hands-on testing: 10–20 min
- Purchase processing: 3–5 min
- **Total per session**: 21–38 min
