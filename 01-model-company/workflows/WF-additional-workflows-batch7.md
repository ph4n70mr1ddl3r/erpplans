## W1043. Customer Paint Coverage Area Calculator & Primer/Finish Quantity Estimator Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests paint quantity recommendation at paint mixing station, pro desk, or via ecommerce |
| **Frequency** | ~8,000–12,000 customer interactions/month across all stores (paint is 8% of SKU portfolio, ~2,800 SKUs) |
| **Volume** | 1 estimation per customer visit; average 3–5 surface calculations per project |
| **Owner** | Paint Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Paint Section), Paint Mixing Station Operator, Customer |

### Background

Paint & Finishes is a high-margin category (8% of active SKUs, ~2,800 items) that benefits significantly from consultative selling. Filipino homeowners and contractors frequently over- or under-purchase paint due to uncertainty about surface area coverage, primer requirements, and number of coats needed. Over-purchasing leads to customer dissatisfaction (perceived waste) and potential returns of tinted paint (which cannot be restocked). Under-purchasing leads to repeat store visits and color inconsistency between batches. The Philippine paint market has specific considerations: (a) tropical humidity affects drying time and coverage rates; (b) concrete block ("hollow block") and CHB (concrete hollow block) walls — the dominant wall material in Philippine construction — are highly porous and absorb 30–40% more paint than flat drywall; (c) new concrete requires alkali-resistant primer; (d) exterior surfaces need weather-resistant formulations; (e) the typical Filipino home has 40–80 sqm of interior wall area per room and 60–120 sqm of exterior wall per facade. This workflow provides an in-store (and ecommerce) paint quantity estimation tool that converts customer room dimensions into precise paint, primer, and supply quantities, reducing over-buying by an estimated 15–20% and increasing customer confidence in project scope. It is distinct from W168 (custom paint mixing operations) which governs the actual tinting process; this workflow addresses the upstream estimation and consultation phase.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer Project Intake & Surface Area Calculation**: Sales Associate gathers project details: (a) surface type — interior wall, exterior wall, ceiling, trim/door, metal gate/railing, roof; (b) wall material — new CHB/concrete, old concrete (previously painted), drywall, plywood, wood, metal, stucco/roughcast; (c) surface condition — bare/unpainted, previously painted (good condition), previously painted (peeling/flaking), with stains/water marks; (d) room/area dimensions — length, width, height of each wall or surface; (e) deductions — window openings, door openings, built-in cabinets; (f) desired finish — flat, satin, semi-gloss, gloss; (g) system calculates net paintable surface area (gross area minus deductions) and applies surface-type absorption factor (CHB: 1.3–1.4×; smooth concrete: 1.0×; wood: 1.1×; metal: 0.9×) | Sales Associate | Paint Department Supervisor | 5–10 min |
| 2 | **Primer & Undercoat Recommendation**: System determines primer requirements: (a) new CHB/concrete surfaces: alkaline-resistant primer (mandatory, 1 coat); (b) previously painted surfaces in good condition: no primer needed unless changing from oil-based to water-based; (c) peeling/flaking surfaces: scraping + patching compound + primer (1 coat); (d) stained/water-marked surfaces: stain-blocking primer (1 coat); (e) metal surfaces: anti-rust primer (1 coat); (f) exterior surfaces: weather-resistant primer (1 coat); (g) system calculates primer volume using primer coverage rate (typically 30–40 sqm per gallon for Philippine brands) and surfaces requiring primer | Sales Associate / System | Paint Department Supervisor | 2–3 min (automated) |
| 3 | **Finish Coat Quantity Calculation**: System computes finish coat quantity: (a) number of coats recommended — interior walls: 2 coats; exterior walls: 2–3 coats (tropical rain exposure); ceiling: 2 coats; metal: 2 coats; (b) coverage rate per product — varies by paint line (economy: 25–30 sqm/gallon; premium: 35–45 sqm/gallon); system pulls coverage data from product master (W252) based on selected paint SKU; (c) total volume = (net surface area × absorption factor × number of coats) ÷ coverage rate per gallon; (d) system rounds up to nearest gallon/4-liter container and adds 10% contingency for touch-ups; (e) if customer has selected paint color, system checks availability in required volume per real-time inventory (W11/W533) | System / Sales Associate | Paint Department Supervisor | 2–3 min (automated) |
| 4 | **Ancillary Supplies Recommendation**: System generates companion product list: (a) paint rollers — 7" or 9" width based on wall area; roller covers (nap length: 3/8" smooth, 1/2"–3/4" rough surfaces); (b) paint brushes — 2" trim brush, 4" wall brush; (c) paint tray and liners; (d) masking tape (painter's tape) — 1" and 2" widths, calculated linear meters based on edge/trim length; (e) sandpaper — grit 120 (surface prep), grit 220 (between coats); (f) putty/spackling knife and patching compound for surface repair; (g) drop cloth/plastic sheeting — sized to room dimensions; (h) solvent/cleaner — water for latex, mineral spirits for oil-based; (i) system bundles these as a "project supply kit" with total estimated cost | System | Sales Associate | 1–2 min (automated) |
| 5 | **Estimate Generation & Customer Handoff**: (a) system generates printable/pdf estimate with: project summary (room name, dimensions, surface types), calculated paintable area, primer recommendation (brand, product, volume, coats), finish coat recommendation (brand, product, color code, volume, coats), ancillary supply list with quantities and prices, total estimated project cost, estimated project time (drying time between coats: 2–4 hours water-based, 6–8 hours oil-based in Philippine humidity); (b) estimate saved to customer's loyalty account or as guest session; (c) customer can directly convert estimate to in-store transaction by scanning estimate QR code at POS (W542); (d) for trade account customers, estimate can be saved as project quote per W542 with 30-day validity; (e) paint mixing station operator receives color formula and volume — proceeds to W168 (custom paint mixing) if customer confirms | Sales Associate | Paint Department Supervisor | 3–5 min |

### System Touchpoints
- Paint coverage calculator engine (surface area, absorption factor, coverage rate per product)
- Product master (W252) — coverage rates, primer compatibility, paint line specifications
- Real-time inventory availability per store (W533)
- Paint mixing station integration (W168) — formula and volume handoff
- POS quotation module (W542) — estimate-to-sale conversion
- Loyalty account integration (W17) — saved estimates and purchase history
- Ecommerce product page integration — online paint calculator widget

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Surface condition misjudgment**: Customer self-reported surface condition may be inaccurate; over-the-phone or online estimates carry higher error risk; recommend in-store consultation for projects >20 sqm
- **Coverage rate variance**: Philippine paint brands (Boysen, Davies, Island Paints, Nippon Paint) have different coverage rates even within the same product line; product master must be updated per SKU per W252 governance
- **Color batch consistency**: If customer under-purchases and returns for additional paint of the same color, the second batch may have slight color variation; system should recommend rounding up and note this risk on the estimate
- **Returns of tinted paint**: Tinted paint cannot be restocked; returns policy (W12A) should clearly communicate non-returnability of custom-mixed paint; this workflow aims to reduce over-buying which in turn reduces return disputes

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate (Paint Section)**: Estimation adds 5–10 minutes per consultation; at 40–60 paint consultations per store per week, this represents ~5–10 hours/week; absorbed by existing 12 Sales Associates with paint section rotation
- **No permanent incremental headcount**; paint calculator tool reduces manual estimation errors and speeds up consultation by 40%

### Time Estimate
- Customer intake & surface area calculation: 5–10 min
- Primer recommendation: 2–3 min (automated)
- Finish coat calculation: 2–3 min (automated)
- Ancillary supplies: 1–2 min (automated)
- Estimate generation & handoff: 3–5 min
- **Total per consultation**: 13–23 min (reduced from 25–40 min without calculator tool)

## W1044. Customer Concrete Mix Ratio & Volume Calculator for Slab/Foundation/Column Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests concrete quantity estimate for a construction project (slab, footing, column, beam, driveway, post filler) |
| **Frequency** | ~4,000–6,000 customer interactions/month across all stores (cement and concrete materials are in the lumber & building materials category — 14% of SKUs) |
| **Volume** | 1 calculation per customer project |
| **Owner** | Lumber & Building Materials Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Building Materials Section), Customer |

### Background

Concrete is the most fundamental building material in Philippine construction. Virtually every residential and commercial building project in the Philippines involves concrete work — from simple post footings for a fence (0.05 cubic meters) to full house slabs (5–20 cubic meters) to multi-story foundation works (50+ cubic meters). The Philippine standard concrete mix uses bags of Portland cement (40 kg per bag), sand (fine aggregate), gravel (coarse aggregate), and water. Class A mix (1:1½:3 cement:sand:gravel) is standard for structural elements; Class B (1:2:4) for non-structural; Class C (1:3:6) for fill and non-critical work. Filipino contractors and DIY homeowners frequently miscalculate material quantities because: (a) concrete volume depends on three-dimensional formwork dimensions, which many customers struggle to compute; (b) waste factors for concrete are 5–10% due to spillage, over-excavation, and form irregularities; (c) the number of cement bags must be derived from the mix ratio and volume, not estimated visually. BuildRight Depot sells ~PHP 3–4 billion/year in cement, sand, gravel, and related concrete materials — this consultative service drives correct purchasing, reduces under-buying (which causes project delays and repeat visits), and differentiates BuildRight from competitors who do not offer estimation services. This workflow is distinct from W964 (bulk cement, sand & aggregates delivery) which covers the logistics of delivery; this workflow covers the upstream quantity estimation.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Project Type & Structural Element Identification**: Sales Associate identifies the concrete application: (a) slab-on-grade (floor slab, driveway, carport); (b) footing/foundation (isolated footing, continuous footing, mat foundation); (c) column (rectangular, circular); (d) beam (tie beam, grade beam, lintel); (e) wall (CHB wall footings, retaining wall); (f) post filler (hollow block post filler, 4"/6" round column filler); (g) stairs; (h) other custom form. System displays the applicable structural standards and minimum recommended concrete class (Class A for structural, Class B for general, Class C for fill) | Sales Associate | Lumber & Building Materials Dept Supervisor | 2–3 min |
| 2 | **Dimension Input & Volume Calculation**: Customer provides dimensions and system computes volume: (a) rectangular slab: length × width × thickness (in meters; default thickness: 0.10m for residential floor, 0.15m for garage/driveway); (b) L-shaped or irregular slab: decomposed into rectangles; (c) footing: length × width × depth (typical depth 0.60–1.20m for single-story); (d) column: width × depth × height; (e) circular column: π × radius² × height; (f) beam: width × depth × length; (g) retaining wall: length × height × thickness (tapers if applicable); (h) system adds waste factor: 5% for simple shapes, 10% for irregular shapes or complex formwork; (i) system outputs total concrete volume in cubic meters (m³) | Sales Associate / Customer / System | Lumber & Building Materials Dept Supervisor | 3–5 min |
| 3 | **Mix Design & Material Quantity Breakdown**: System computes material quantities based on selected mix class: (a) **Class A (1:1½:3)**: 9.5 bags cement per m³, 0.50 m³ sand per m³, 1.0 m³ gravel per m³; (b) **Class B (1:2:4)**: 7.0 bags cement per m³, 0.67 m³ sand per m³, 1.33 m³ gravel per m³; (c) **Class C (1:3:6)**: 5.0 bags cement per m³, 0.83 m³ sand per m³, 1.67 m³ gravel per m³; (d) system multiplies per-m³ quantities by total volume (including waste factor); (e) outputs: number of cement bags (40 kg), volume of sand (m³ or cubic meters equivalent in truck loads), volume of gravel (m³ or cubic meters equivalent in truck loads), estimated water volume (liters); (f) for projects >3 m³, system recommends ready-mixed concrete as an alternative per W964 (bulk delivery) and shows cost comparison: bagged DIY vs. ready-mix delivered | System | Sales Associate | 1–2 min (automated) |
| 4 | **Companion Materials Recommendation**: System generates companion product list: (a) reinforcing steel (rebar) — size and quantity per standard spacing (10mm @ 200mm O.C. for slab; 12mm or 16mm for columns and beams) per W986/W1060; (b) tie wire — 1.5 kg per 100 kg of rebar; (c) formwork materials — plywood (1/4" or 1/2" for concrete forming), 2×2 or 2×3 wood studs for form support; (d) plastic sheeting — for curing cover (kept wet for 7 days per Philippine engineering practice); (e) vibro-tamping rod or concrete vibrator rental per W139; (f) wheelbarrow and mixing tools; (g) waterproofing compound for below-grade concrete; (h) system bundles companion materials with total estimated cost | System | Sales Associate | 2–3 min (automated) |
| 5 | **Estimate Generation, Delivery Scheduling & Customer Handoff**: (a) system generates printable/PDF estimate with: project description, structural element dimensions, computed concrete volume, selected mix class with ratio, material quantity breakdown (cement bags, sand m³, gravel m³, water liters), companion materials list with prices, total estimated project cost; (b) for orders >2 m³ of sand/gravel: system triggers bulk delivery scheduling per W964 with delivery fee estimate based on distance; (c) for orders ≤2 m³: customer can load materials at store yard per W950 (vehicle loading assistance); (d) estimate saved to customer's loyalty account; (e) customer can convert estimate to transaction at POS per W542; (f) for trade account B2B customers, estimate converts to project quote per W162 with delivery window scheduling | Sales Associate | Lumber & Building Materials Dept Supervisor | 3–5 min |

### System Touchpoints
- Concrete volume calculator engine with dimensional input for multiple structural shapes
- Mix design database (Class A/B/C ratios, per-m³ material factors)
- Product master (W252) — cement bag size, sand/gravel pricing per m³
- Real-time inventory availability (W533) for cement, sand, gravel
- Bulk delivery scheduling module (W964) integration
- POS quotation module (W542) — estimate-to-sale conversion
- Loyalty account integration (W17) — saved estimates
- B2B quotation module (W162) — trade account project quotes

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Structural engineering disclaimer**: BuildRight staff are not structural engineers; the calculator provides material quantity estimates only, not structural design; system must display disclaimer on every estimate: "This estimate is for material quantity guidance only and does not constitute structural engineering advice. Consult a licensed civil engineer for structural design requirements."
- **Soil condition variability**: Actual excavation depth and footing size may differ from customer's stated dimensions due to soil conditions; recommend 10% contingency on volume for foundation works
- **Sand/gravel volume measurement**: Philippine sand and gravel are sold by the cubic meter (truck load) with inherent ±10% measurement variance; system should note this on estimates
- **Cement shelf life**: Cement bags have a 3-month shelf life in Philippine humidity; customers ordering large quantities (>50 bags) should be advised to schedule delivery closer to pour date

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate (Building Materials)**: Estimation adds 10–15 minutes per consultation; at 20–30 concrete consultations per store per week, this represents ~5–8 hours/week; absorbed by existing 12 Sales Associates with building materials section rotation
- **No permanent incremental headcount**; calculator tool reduces manual estimation time by ~60%

### Time Estimate
- Project type identification: 2–3 min
- Dimension input & volume calculation: 3–5 min
- Mix design & material breakdown: 1–2 min (automated)
- Companion materials: 2–3 min (automated)
- Estimate & delivery scheduling: 3–5 min
- **Total per consultation**: 11–18 min (reduced from 25–40 min without calculator tool)

## W1045. Store-Level Customer PVC Pipe Cutting, Jointing & Fabrication Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer purchases PVC pipe (or brings project requirements) and requests custom-length cutting or jointing/fabrication |
| **Frequency** | ~2,000–3,000 requests/month across all stores (plumbing is 12% of SKUs, ~4,200 items; PVC pipe is the highest-volume plumbing SKU) |
| **Volume** | 1–10 cuts per customer visit; average 3–5 cuts |
| **Owner** | Plumbing Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Plumbing Section), Customer |

### Background

PVC (polyvinyl chloride) pipe is the dominant plumbing material in Philippine construction, used for water supply lines (PVC Schedule 40/80), drainage (PVC DWV), and electrical conduit. BuildRight Depot sells PVC pipe in standard lengths (3m and 6m) across diameters from ½" to 6". Filipino DIY homeowners and small contractors frequently need custom-length PVC pipe for tight installations (under-sink drainage, toilet rough-in, shower lines) but lack the cutting tools (PVC cutter, hacksaw with fine-tooth blade, miter box) for clean, square cuts. Improperly cut PVC pipe has burrs that prevent solvent-weld joints from sealing, leading to leaks. This in-store cutting service provides: (a) clean, square cuts to customer-specified lengths; (b) solvent-weld joining of fittings to cut lengths (optional); (c) pipe threading for galvanized iron (GI) pipe (distinct from PVC). This is distinct from W944 (pipe threading, cutting & fabrication for metal pipe) which covers steel/GI pipe; PVC pipe requires different cutting tools and solvent cement joining. This service reduces customer frustration, increases average transaction value (customers buy full-length pipes and fittings instead of settling for pre-cut sections at higher per-meter cost), and differentiates BuildRight from competing hardware stores.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer Requirement Intake**: Sales Associate gathers project requirements: (a) pipe material — PVC (Schedule 40, Schedule 80, DWV, conduit), CPVC (hot water), or PPR; (b) pipe diameter — ½", ¾", 1", 1¼", 1½", 2", 3", 4", 6"; (c) number of pieces and lengths needed (in centimeters or inches); (d) jointing requirements — solvent-weld fittings (elbow, tee, coupling, reducer, adapter) to be pre-joined; (e) intended application — water supply, drainage, electrical conduit, irrigation, other; (f) system validates that requested lengths are achievable from standard 3m/6m stock, minimizes waste (nesting algorithm for multiple cuts) | Sales Associate | Plumbing Department Supervisor | 3–5 min |
| 2 | **Cutting Execution**: Sales Associate performs PVC cutting at dedicated cutting station in plumbing section: (a) select correct pipe from stock per diameter and schedule; (b) measure and mark cut points using tape measure and marker; (c) position pipe in cutting jig (or miter box for smaller diameters); (d) execute cut using dedicated PVC cutter (for diameters ≤2") or fine-tooth hacksaw with miter box (for diameters >2"); (e) deburr cut ends using chamfering tool or utility knife — remove internal and external burrs to ensure proper solvent-weld seating; (f) verify cut length within ±2mm tolerance using tape measure; (g) wipe cut ends clean of dust and shavings | Sales Associate | Plumbing Department Supervisor | 3–8 min (depending on number of cuts) |
| 3 | **Optional Jointing / Fabrication**: If customer requests pre-joined assemblies: (a) dry-fit all components to verify alignment before solvent cement application; (b) apply PVC primer (purple primer, mandatory for Schedule 40/80) to outside of pipe end and inside of fitting socket; (c) apply PVC solvent cement to primed surfaces; (d) insert pipe into fitting with quarter-turn twist; (e) hold joint for 15–30 seconds for initial set; (f) wipe excess cement; (g) mark joint set time: 15 min for handling, 2 hours for light pressure, 24 hours for full pressure; (h) customer takes pre-joined assembly with remaining uncut pipe lengths | Sales Associate | Plumbing Department Supervisor | 5–15 min (depending on number of joints) |
| 4 | **Transaction Processing & Waste Handling**: (a) system charges for: full-length pipe(s) consumed (prorated by meter), fittings used, primer/cement consumed (if jointing), cutting service fee (or waived for purchases >PHP 500); (b) cutting waste (offcuts <30 cm) collected in store PVC recycling bin per W502 (non-hazardous waste management); (c) offcuts ≥30 cm offered to customer for potential use; (d) system updates inventory: full-length pipe decremented, cutting waste accounted per disposition code; (e) customer receives cut pieces, any pre-joined assemblies, and remaining pipe | Sales Associate / POS Cashier | Plumbing Department Supervisor | 3–5 min |

### System Touchpoints
- PVC cutting service module — cut list entry, nesting optimization, waste calculation
- Product master (W252) — pipe dimensions, cutting service SKU, fitting compatibility
- Inventory deduction — full-length pipe consumed, waste disposition (W502)
- POS service item processing (W544) — service fee SKU
- Real-time inventory availability (W533)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Cut accuracy**: PVC cutter blade wear leads to angled cuts; weekly blade replacement schedule required for high-volume stores
- **Solvent cement safety**: PVC primer and cement contain tetrahydrofuran (THF) and cyclohexanone — volatile, flammable chemicals; cutting station must be in well-ventilated area; PPE (gloves, safety glasses) required per W172
- **Customer mismeasurement**: Customers frequently provide incorrect measurements; recommend dry-fit verification before solvent cement application
- **Waste management**: PVC offcuts cannot be recycled through standard channels; partner with specialized PVC recyclers per W167

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate (Plumbing Section)**: Cutting adds 10–20 min per customer; at 10–15 PVC cutting requests per store per day, this represents ~2–5 hours/day; absorbed by existing department staff with rotating cutting duty
- **No permanent incremental headcount**

### Time Estimate
- Requirement intake: 3–5 min
- Cutting execution: 3–8 min
- Jointing (if applicable): 5–15 min
- Transaction & waste handling: 3–5 min
- **Total per customer**: 14–33 min (average ~20 min)

## W1046. Customer Door & Window Measurement, Sizing & Custom Order Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests door or window sizing assistance, custom-order door/window, or replacement for existing unit |
| **Frequency** | ~2,500–3,500 customer interactions/month across all stores |
| **Volume** | 1–4 door/window units per customer project |
| **Owner** | Lumber & Building Materials Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Building Materials Section), Special Order Coordinator, Customer |

### Background

Doors and windows are high-value, project-critical items in Philippine home construction and renovation. A single-panel flush door costs PHP 1,500–4,000; a panel door PHP 3,000–8,000; a sliding glass window PHP 2,500–15,000; and a main entrance door with frame PHP 8,000–25,000. Unlike commodity items (cement, nails), doors and windows are dimension-critical — a 10mm error in rough opening measurement results in an unusable unit. Philippine residential construction uses non-standardized rough openings (unlike US/Europe where modular sizes are common), making custom sizing the norm rather than the exception. Key door/window types in the Philippine market: (a) flush doors (standard sizes: 0.70m × 2.03m, 0.80m × 2.03m); (b) panel doors (decorative, same standard widths); (c) sliding glass windows (jalousie/awful windows being replaced by modern aluminum sliding); (d) swing casement windows; (e) main entrance doors (solid hardwood, customized); (f) fire-rated doors (for commercial buildings, per BFP requirement). BuildRight Depot carries standard-size doors and windows as stock items, but ~40% of door/window sales involve custom orders from partner manufacturers with 7–14 day lead times. This workflow covers the measurement consultation, sizing recommendation, and custom-order placement process.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## W1050. Customer Staircase Tread, Riser & Railing Material Calculator Service

| Field | Detail |

| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Building Materials Section), Customer |

### Background

Staircases are a universal element in Philippine multi-story residential construction (2–3 story homes are common in urban areas due to limited lot sizes). A typical Philippine residential staircase has 12–16 risers (floor-to-floor height ~3.0 meters) with 2–3 flights and 1–2 intermediate landings. Customers (both DIY homeowners and small contractors) frequently miscalculate staircase material quantities because: (a) tread and riser counts depend on precise floor-to-floor height and desired step dimensions (rise and run); (b) the Philippine National Building Code (PD 1096) mandates maximum riser height of 200mm and minimum tread depth of 250mm for residential stairs; (c) stringer (carriage) length must be calculated from total rise and run using the Pythagorean theorem; (d) railing balusters must comply with maximum 100mm spacing per PD 1096 (child safety). Common errors lead to under-buying (multiple store visits) or over-buying (waste and returns). This calculator service provides precise material quantities for: treads, risers, stringers, landing framing, railing posts, handrails, and balusters — converting a complex geometric calculation into a simple bill of materials that drives a complete sale.

## W1052. Customer Gutter, Downspout & Flashing Sizing Calculator Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests gutter system sizing for new construction, renovation, or replacement |
| **Frequency** | ~1,200–1,800 consultations/month across all stores (gutters are part of the lumber & building materials and plumbing categories) |
| **Volume** | 1 calculation per customer project |
| **Owner** | Lumber & Building Materials Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Building Materials/Plumbing Section), Customer |

### Background

Gutter and downspout systems are essential in Philippine residential and commercial construction due to high annual rainfall (1,500–4,000mm/year depending on region). Proper gutter sizing prevents: (a) roof runoff damaging building foundations and walls; (b) water ingress through windows and doors; (c) soil erosion around building perimeter; (d) flooding of ground-floor areas during intense downpours. Philippine construction commonly uses: (a) GI (galvanized iron) half-round or box gutters — traditional, durable; (b) PVC gutters — lightweight, corrosion-resistant, easier installation; (c) aluminum gutters — premium, lightweight, no rust; (d) pre-painted GI gutters — aesthetic appeal. Downspouts are typically PVC (75mm or 100mm diameter) or GI (round or rectangular). Flashing materials include GI sheet, aluminum sheet, and rubberized asphalt membrane for valley flashing, chimney flashing, and roof-wall intersections. Customers frequently miscalculate gutter lengths (forgetting to account for roof pitch and overhang) and downspout quantities (underestimating for large roof areas). This service converts a complex hydraulic calculation into a complete bill of materials.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Roof & Building Parameter Input**: Sales Associate gathers project parameters: (a) roof type — gable, hip, shed, flat (with parapet), combination; (b) roof dimensions — length and width of each roof plane (or total roof footprint area); (c) roof pitch/slope angle (in degrees or rise-to-run ratio); (d) number of stories (affects downspout length); (e) gutter type preference — half-round (125mm or 150mm), box/K-style (125mm or 150mm), PVC, GI, aluminum; (f) downspout type — round PVC (75mm/100mm), rectangular GI, round GI; (g) building location (for rainfall intensity lookup per PAGASA data per W310); (h) gutter run direction — along eaves (both sides for gable), along all sides (hip roof) | Sales Associate | Lumber & Building Materials Dept Supervisor | 5–8 min |

### Background

Exhaust ventilation is critical in Philippine homes due to tropical humidity (60–90% relative humidity year-round). BuildRight Depot sells exhaust fans from PHP 500 (basic axial fan, 4" duct) to PHP 8,000 (premium centrifugal inline fan with humidity sensor, 6" duct). Electrical wire and cable are sold in standard rolls (100m for THHN/THWN building wire, 50m for larger gauges, 305m/1000ft boxes for NM-B romex equivalent) at BuildRight Depot. ## W1060. Customer Rebar Stirrup, Tying Wire & Binding Material Quantity Estimator Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer requests rebar accessory quantities (stirrups, tying wire, spacers) for a reinforced concrete project |

| **Volume** | 1 estimation per project |
| **Owner** | Lumber & Building Materials Department Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Sales Associate (Building Materials Section), Customer |

### Background

Reinforced concrete construction is the dominant building method in the Philippines. Rebar (deformed steel bar) is the skeletal reinforcement inside concrete elements — columns, beams, slabs, and footings. But rebar alone is not sufficient; it must be properly configured with: (a) stirrups (rectangular ties that wrap around the longitudinal rebar in columns and beams, providing shear reinforcement and confinement — typically 10mm bar bent into rectangles at 100–200mm spacing per structural engineer's specification); (b) tying wire (annealed black wire, typically 16-gauge, used to tie intersections of longitudinal bars and stirrups — consumes 1.0–1.5 kg per 100 kg of rebar); (c) concrete spacers/cover blocks (maintain specified concrete cover between rebar and formwork surface — typically 40mm for interior exposure, 50mm for exterior exposure per Philippine structural code); (d) mechanical couplers (for splicing rebar where lap splicing is impractical — higher-value item at PHP 50–200 per coupler). Filipino contractors and foremen routinely underestimate tying wire and stirrup quantities, leading to work stoppages and mid-project store runs. This estimator service provides precise quantities for all rebar accessories based on the structural element dimensions and rebar configuration, bundled with the rebar cutting/bending service per W986.

### Steps

|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | **Stirrup Quantity & Cutting Schedule Calculation**: System computes stirrup requirements: (a) stirrup perimeter per piece: for rectangular column/beam stirrup = 2 × (width − 2 × cover) + 2 × (depth − 2 × cover) + hook length (typically 75mm × 2 hooks); (b) stirrup length per piece = perimeter (including hooks); (c) number of stirrups = element length ÷ stirrup spacing + 1 (first stirrup at 50mm from end); (d) total stirrup bar length = number of stirrups × length per piece; (e) system converts to number of 6m standard bars: total stirrup bar length ÷ 6m, rounded up; (f) system generates cutting schedule for in-store rebar cutting/bending per W986: piece number, stirrup dimensions (inside width × inside depth), bend angle (90° × 4 corners), hook length, quantity; (g) for circular columns: stirrup perimeter = π × (diameter − 2 × cover) + overlap (typically 300mm for lap splice) | System | Sales Associate | 1–2 min (automated) |
