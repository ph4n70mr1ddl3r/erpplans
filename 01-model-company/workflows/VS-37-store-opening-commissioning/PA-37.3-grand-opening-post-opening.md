## W1755. Grand Opening Event Planning & Execution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Go/no-go decision confirmed (W1744); 2 weeks before opening |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 events/year |
| **Volume** | 1 event per new store |
| **Owner** | CMO |
| **Participants** | CMO, Marketing Team, Store Manager, VP Store Operations, LGU Officials, Media |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Marketing develops grand opening plan: (a) event date selection (preferably Saturday for maximum foot traffic; avoid rainy season dates if possible), (b) promotional offers: doorbuster deals (10–20 items at deep discount), gift-with-purchase, raffle prizes, (c) local marketing: barangay flyers, local radio, Facebook ads targeted within 10 km radius, (d) VIP preview: invite local government officials (barangay captain, mayor), contractors, trade account customers, (e) media coverage: invite local press/social media influencers, (f) store decoration: balloons, banners, promotional signage, (g) entertainment: local band, kids' activities, product demos | Marketing Team | CMO | 1–2 weeks |
| 2 | Budget: (a) grand opening budget ~PHP 200–500K per store (from marketing budget, partially funded by vendor co-op per VS-39), (b) promotional discount costs (markdown budget from merchandising), (c) venue setup (tent, sound system, decorations) | FP&A / CMO | CFO | 1 day |
| 3 | Coordination with Store Manager: (a) staffing plan for opening day (all 29 staff on duty, split into morning and evening shifts), (b) crowd management plan for high-traffic periods, (c) security coordination (additional security for opening day crowd), (d) emergency plan (medical standby for large crowd events) | Store Manager / Marketing Team | VP Store Operations | 1 week |
| 4 | Grand opening day execution: (a) store opens at announced time (typically 8 AM), (b) VIP ribbon-cutting ceremony, (c) promotional offers activated in POS system, (d) marketing team manages event logistics, (e) Store Manager focuses on store operations and customer service | Marketing Team / Store Manager | CMO / VP Store Operations | 1 day |


### System Touchpoints
- Grand opening plan — date (Saturday preference, rainy-season avoidance), program, promotions (step 1)
- Budget management — ~PHP 200–500K per store with vendor co-op funding per VS-39 (step 2)
- Opening-day staffing plan — all 29 staff split shifts (step 3)
- Execution runbook — ribbon cutting, promotions, VIP program (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Weather risk**: a typhoon washes out the opening weekend; mitigated by season-aware date selection and a contingency date (step 1)
- **Budget-overrun risk**: event costs balloon beyond the PHP 200–500K envelope; mitigated by co-op funding offsets and budget tracking (step 2)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-capture of "entertainment" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-count of "count costs" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "logistics," (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-354 (ensure controlled execution — Grand Opening Execution & Post-Opening Stabilization (PA-37.3).)
- operational: CMO review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
2.3–3.3 weeks elapsed per occurrence (1–2 weeks + 1 day + 1 week + 1 day) — at ~10–15 events/year ≈ 10–15 occurrences/year.

## W1756. Grand Opening Day Operations Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Grand opening day |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 opening days/year |
| **Volume** | 1 opening day per store |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, all 29 staff, Regional Manager, VP Store Operations, IT Support |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pre-opening (6 AM): (a) Store Manager opens store, (b) all staff arrive and prepare: stock replenishment, display checks, cash float in registers, (c) final systems check: POS, network, CCTV all operational, (d) staff briefing: expected traffic, promotional items, role assignments, safety reminders, (e) last-minute merchandising touches, (f) security team positioned | Store Manager | — | 2 hours |
| 2 | Opening day operations: (a) Store Manager monitors floor for customer service issues, (b) Assistant Manager monitors checkout queues (add backup cashier if lines > 5 customers), (c) Department Supervisors support sales associates and handle customer escalations, (d) Receiving Clerks handle any deliveries (DSD vendors may deliver opening day stock), (e) Stock Associates continuously replenish fast-selling promotional items, (f) IT on standby (on-site or remote) for system issues | Store Manager / all staff | Regional Manager | Full day |
| 3 | Real-time monitoring by Regional Manager and VP Store Operations: (a) POS transaction count and revenue (viewed in real-time dashboard), (b) stockout alerts for promotional items, (c) system health monitoring, (d) customer complaints or safety incidents | Regional Manager | VP Store Operations | Full day |
| 4 | End-of-day: (a) extended hours if traffic warrants (standard hours: 8 AM–8 PM; may extend to 10 PM), (b) end-of-day close: Z-report, cash count, reconciliation, (c) Store Manager sends opening day summary to Regional Manager: revenue, transactions, stockouts, issues, incidents, (d) cash deposit prepared and secured per W1754 | Store Manager / Cashiers | Regional Manager | 2 hours |


### System Touchpoints
- Pre-opening preparation checklist — 6 AM opening, stock replenishment, display checks, floats (step 1)
- Real-time monitoring dashboards — POS transaction count and revenue for RM/VP visibility (step 3)
- Queue management — Assistant Manager on checkout; contingency lanes (step 2)
- End-of-day close discipline — Z-report, cash reconciliation (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Checkout-meltdown risk**: queues exceed tolerance and walkouts spike; mitigated by dedicated queue monitoring and contingency lanes (step 2)
- **Cash-variance risk**: opening-day chaos produces unexplained cash differences; mitigated by strict end-of-day close and reconciliation (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "checks, cash float in registers," (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-monitor of "monitors floor for customer service issues," (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-count of "count and revenue" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-354 (ensure controlled execution — Grand Opening Execution & Post-Opening Stabilization (PA-37.3).)
- operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
Per opening day ~16–20 hours across the store team (pre-opening preparation 2 hours + full-day opening operations + full-day Regional/VP real-time monitoring + close-out 2 hours) × ~10–15 openings/year ≈ 160–300 hours/year.

## W1757. Opening Week Performance Monitoring

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Day 2 of new store operations |
| **Frequency** | Daily for first 7 days; weekly for first month |
| **Volume** | 1 dashboard per new store |
| **Owner** | VP Store Operations |
| **Participants** | VP Store Operations, Regional Manager, Store Manager, FP&A |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Daily metrics tracked for opening week: (a) revenue vs. target, (b) transaction count and ATV, (c) basket size (lines per transaction), (d) conversion rate (foot traffic estimate vs. transactions), (e) stockout rate (items ordered but not available), (f) customer complaints, (g) system uptime, (h) staff overtime hours | Store Manager | Regional Manager | Daily review |
| 2 | Daily standup call (15 min) between Store Manager and Regional Manager: (a) yesterday's performance, (b) today's issues and support needed, (c) inventory replenishment status, (d) staffing adjustments if needed | Store Manager / Regional Manager | VP Store Operations | 15 min/day |
| 3 | VP Store Operations reviews all new store dashboards weekly: (a) compare opening week performance across stores opened in same period, (b) identify stores underperforming target (provide additional support — marketing boost, staff coaching, inventory rebalancing), (c) share best practices between stores | VP Store Operations | COO | 1 hour/week |
| 4 | FP&A prepares first week flash report for CFO: (a) revenue vs. plan, (b) gross margin (promotional markdown impact), (c) operating costs, (d) initial customer feedback summary | FP&A | CFO | 1 day |


### System Touchpoints
- Daily opening-week metrics — revenue vs. target, transaction count, ATV, basket size (step 1)
- Daily 15-minute standup between Store Manager and Regional Manager (step 2)
- VP weekly dashboard comparison across same-period openings (step 3)
- FP&A first-week flash report to CFO — revenue, margin, OpEx (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Rationalization risk**: a soft opening week is explained away rather than acted on; mitigated by daily RM standups forcing issue surfacing (step 2)
- **Margin-blindness risk**: promotional opening pricing erodes margin unnoticed; mitigated by margin-impact analysis in the first-week flash (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-count of "count and ATV," (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-issue of "issues and support needed," (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews all new store dashboards weekly" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-354 (ensure controlled execution — Grand Opening Execution & Post-Opening Stabilization (PA-37.3).)
- operational: VP Store Operations review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Per new store's opening month: daily standups 15 min × 7 days plus daily metric reviews ≈ 3.5 hours, weekly VP dashboard reviews 1 hour × 4 ≈ 4 hours, and the FP&A first-week flash report 1 day ≈ 8 hours — ≈ 15 hours/store × ~10–15 openings/year ≈ 150–225 hours/year.

## W1758. Post-Opening Inventory Rebalancing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | End of opening week; actual sales data available |
| **Frequency** | Weekly for first month; then transitions to standard replenishment |
| **Volume** | Rebalancing across ~35,000 SKUs per new store |
| **Owner** | Merchandise Planner |
| **Participants** | Merchandise Planner, Category Managers, VP Supply Chain, DC Operations |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Merchandise Planner analyzes first-week sell-through data: (a) fast movers (selling faster than initial plan): need immediate replenishment to avoid stockouts, (b) slow movers (selling slower than plan): may have excess stock — reduce replenishment frequency, (c) dead stock (zero sales): assess if SKU is appropriate for this store's trade area, (d) promotional items: calculate promotional lift and replenishment need for rest of promo period | Merchandise Planner | VP Supply Chain | 1 day |
| 2 | Adjust replenishment parameters: (a) update ROP and safety stock for fast movers (increase), (b) reduce replenishment for slow movers, (c) expedite replenishment orders from DC for critical stockouts, (d) adjust auto-replenishment rules in system based on actual demand pattern | Merchandise Planner | VP Supply Chain | 1–2 days |
| 3 | Inter-store transfers (if needed): (a) if new store has excess stock of items that nearby stores need, initiate transfer per W22 (Stock Transfers), (b) if new store needs items available at nearby store, pull from closest store for immediate relief | VP Supply Chain | DC Operations | As needed |
| 4 | After 4 weeks: Merchandise Planner formalizes new store demand profile; system auto-replenishment takes over with store-specific parameters based on actual sales history | Merchandise Planner | VP Supply Chain | 1 day |


### System Touchpoints
- First-week sell-through analysis — fast movers vs. initial plan (step 1)
- Replenishment parameter adjustment — ROP/safety stock up for fast movers, down for slow (step 2)
- Inter-store transfer initiation per W22 for excess stock (step 3)
- Store-specific demand profile locked at 4 weeks; auto-replenishment takes over (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Fast-mover stockout risk**: opening hits exhaust initial stock of winners; mitigated by immediate replenishment and parameter increases (steps 1–2)
- **Excess-capital drag**: slow movers tie up working capital in the new store; mitigated by inter-store transfers and parameter reduction (steps 2–3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-calculate of "calculate promotional lift and replenishment need for rest of promo period" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-update of "update ROP and safety stock for fast movers" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-file of "file" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-354 (ensure controlled execution — Grand Opening Execution & Post-Opening Stabilization (PA-37.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
Per new store ~3–4 days of planner effort (first-week sell-through analysis 1 day + ROP/safety-stock adjustment 1–2 days + demand-profile formalization after 4 weeks 1 day; inter-store transfers as needed) × ~10–15 stores/year ≈ 30–60 days/year before handoff to standard replenishment.

## W1759. First Month Performance Review & Stabilization

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | 30 days post-opening |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 reviews/year |
| **Volume** | 1 review per new store |
| **Owner** | VP Store Operations |
| **Participants** | VP Store Operations, Regional Manager, Store Manager, CFO, CMO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FP&A prepares 30-day store performance report: (a) revenue vs. plan (daily trend), (b) gross margin (actual vs. plan — adjusted for promotional markdowns), (c) controllable expenses vs. budget, (d) customer traffic and conversion, (e) top-selling categories and SKUs, (f) stockout rate and replenishment fill rate, (g) customer complaints and returns, (h) staff overtime and turnover, (i) cash management accuracy | FP&A | CFO | 1 day |
| 2 | VP Store Operations conducts store visit: (a) operational assessment: store cleanliness, stock organization, planogram compliance, receiving efficiency, (b) staff performance review with Store Manager, (c) customer service observation, (d) safety inspection, (e) discuss issues and develop action plan | VP Store Operations / Regional Manager | COO | 1 day on-site |
| 3 | Stabilization actions: (a) staffing adjustments (add or reduce based on actual traffic patterns), (b) inventory parameter finalization, (c) schedule optimization (match staffing to peak hours), (d) any system configuration adjustments, (e) Store Manager coaching on specific improvement areas | Store Manager / Regional Manager | VP Store Operations | 1–2 weeks |
| 4 | 30-day report presented to steering committee; project formally moves from "opening" to "operations" per W1760 | VP Store Operations | COO | 30 min |


### System Touchpoints
- 30-day performance report — revenue, margin, OpEx vs. plan (step 1)
- Store visit assessment — cleanliness, planogram compliance, staff readiness (step 2)
- Stabilization actions — staffing and inventory parameter adjustments (step 3)
- Steering committee presentation; formal transition to operations (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Premature-normalization risk**: the store is declared stable while issues persist; mitigated by verified stabilization actions before the transition (steps 3–4)
- **Staffing-mismatch risk**: actual traffic patterns differ from plan; mitigated by traffic-based staffing adjustment at 30 days (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-generate of "prepares 30-day store performance report" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "review with Store Manager," (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-schedule of "schedule optimization" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-354 (ensure controlled execution — Grand Opening Execution & Post-Opening Stabilization (PA-37.3).)
- operational: VP Store Operations review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Per new store ~2.5 days (FP&A 30-day performance report 1 day + on-site VP operational assessment 1 day + steering-committee presentation 30 min; stabilization actions over 1–2 weeks) × ~10–15 reviews/year ≈ 25–38 days/year.

## W1760. Store Handover from Project to Operations

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | 30-day review completed (W1759); store deemed stable |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 handovers/year |
| **Volume** | 1 handover per new store |
| **Owner** | Store Opening Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Store Opening Coordinator, Store Manager, VP Store Operations, all functional leads |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Store Opening Coordinator prepares handover checklist: (a) construction: all work complete, punch list cleared, warranties transferred, (b) permits: all required permits received and filed, (c) equipment: all fixtures and equipment installed and operational, registered as fixed assets per W1690, (d) IT: all systems live and stable, user accounts active, support process transferred to standard IT helpdesk, (e) HR: all staff hired, onboarded, and in payroll system, (f) inventory: fully stocked and on auto-replenishment, (g) financial: bank account active, cash management operational, GL posting verified, (h) marketing: grand opening complete, store included in standard marketing calendar | Store Opening Coordinator | VP Store Operations | 1 day |
| 2 | Handover meeting: Store Opening Coordinator walks through checklist with Store Manager and Regional Manager; all items confirmed or outstanding items assigned to functional owners with deadlines | Store Opening Coordinator / Store Manager | VP Store Operations | 2 hours |
| 3 | Project closure: (a) Store Opening Coordinator closes project in project management system, (b) FP&A finalizes project cost per W1745, (c) project documents archived (construction drawings, permits, vendor contracts, equipment manuals), (d) Store Manager assumes full operational responsibility | Store Opening Coordinator / FP&A | VP Store Operations | 1 day |
| 4 | VP Store Operations formally confirms store transition to standard operations; Store Opening Coordinator released to next project | VP Store Operations | COO | 1 day |


### System Touchpoints
- Handover checklist — construction complete, punch list cleared, warranties transferred (step 1)
- Handover meeting with Store Manager and Regional Manager (step 2)
- Project closure — cost finalization per W1745, CIP-to-asset per W40.3 (step 3)
- Formal transition confirmation; coordinator released (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Orphaned-issue risk**: items fall between the project team and operations with no owner; mitigated by the checklist walkthrough with named confirmation (steps 1–2)
- **Warranty-loss risk**: contractor warranties lapse undocumented after handover; mitigated by warranty transfer as an explicit checklist item (step 1)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "checklist" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-assign of "assigned to functional owners with deadlines" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-document of "documents archived" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-188 (prevent premature store go-live.)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
2 hours per occurrence (per-step effort 2 hours); elapsed timeline 1 day + 1 day + 1 day — at ~10–15 handovers/year ≈ 10–15 occurrences/year ≈ 20–30 hours/year.

## W1761. Post-Opening Issue Resolution & Punch List Closure

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Outstanding items from handover (W1760) or issues discovered post-opening |
| **Frequency** | ~50–100 punch list items across all new stores/year |
| **Volume** | ~5–10 items per store on average |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, VP Real Estate, IT, Maintenance, contractors |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Store Manager maintains punch list of outstanding construction, equipment, and system issues: (a) construction defects (leaks, uneven flooring, door alignment), (b) equipment not functioning properly (POS glitch, HVAC noise, generator test failure), (c) missing items (signage not delivered, fixture missing), (d) system issues (incorrect pricing, missing SKUs, reporting errors) | Store Manager | VP Store Operations | Continuous |
| 2 | Each item assigned to responsible party: (a) construction: VP Real Estate coordinates with contractor (typically under warranty for 6–12 months), (b) equipment: vendor support or maintenance team, (c) IT: IT helpdesk ticket and resolution, (d) system: IT Applications team for configuration fixes | Store Opening Coordinator / Store Manager | Functional owners | As assigned |
| 3 | Store Manager tracks resolution; escalates to Regional Manager if items not resolved within agreed timeframes; all items must be closed before contractor retention payment released (typically 10% of construction cost held for 6 months) | Store Manager / Regional Manager | VP Store Operations | 1–6 months |
| 4 | Final punch list closure confirmed by VP Real Estate and VP Store Operations; retention payment released to contractor | VP Real Estate / VP Store Operations | CFO | 6 months post-opening |


### System Touchpoints
- Punch list register of outstanding construction/equipment/system issues (step 1)
- Item assignment — contractor (warranty), vendor, IT — with owners (step 2)
- Escalation tracking through Regional Manager (step 3)
- Retention-release gate on confirmed closure (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Contractor-drift risk**: defects linger past warranty windows; mitigated by holding retention payment until confirmed closure (step 4)
- **Recurrence risk**: the same defects repeat at every new store; mitigated by feeding patterns into the W1762 lessons-learned loop (step 1)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-report of "reporting errors" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-assign of "assigned to responsible party" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-escalate of "escalates to Regional Manager if items not resolved within agreed timeframes" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-354 (ensure controlled execution — Grand Opening Execution & Post-Opening Stabilization (PA-37.3).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
Per store ~5–10 punch-list items tracked continuously to closure within 1–6 months (~2–4 hours of Store Manager tracking per store plus party-specific remediation at contractor/vendor effort); final closure confirmation and retention release at 6 months post-opening × ~10–15 stores/year.

## W1762. Lessons Learned & Store Opening Process Improvement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | 3 months after store opening |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 sessions/year |
| **Volume** | 1 lessons-learned session per new store |
| **Owner** | VP Store Operations |
| **Participants** | VP Store Operations, Store Opening Coordinator, functional leads, Store Manager |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | VP Store Operations convenes lessons-learned session (virtual or in-person, 2 hours): (a) what went well (best practices to standardize), (b) what didn't go well (process failures, delays, budget overruns), (c) what surprised us (unexpected challenges), (d) what would we do differently next time | VP Store Operations | COO | 2 hours |
| 2 | Store Opening Coordinator documents findings and recommendations: (a) timeline accuracy (actual vs. planned milestones), (b) budget accuracy (actual vs. planned costs), (c) quality of handoff from construction to operations, (d) system readiness and IT support effectiveness, (e) staffing and training effectiveness, (f) vendor and supplier readiness | Store Opening Coordinator | VP Store Operations | 1 day |
| 3 | Process improvements implemented: (a) update store opening playbook (standardized procedures document), (b) update project charter template, (c) update critical path template, (d) revise vendor notification timeline if needed, (e) adjust training program based on staff readiness gaps observed | VP Store Operations | COO | 1 week |
| 4 | Annual review: VP Store Operations compiles lessons learned across all openings in the year; presents to COO for strategic improvements to store opening methodology | VP Store Operations | COO | 1 day/year |


### System Touchpoints
- Lessons-learned session at 3 months — what went well, what failed (step 1)
- Documented findings — timeline accuracy, budget accuracy, issue patterns (step 2)
- Store opening playbook updates institutionalizing fixes (step 3)
- Annual cross-opening compilation for the COO (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Repeat-mistakes risk**: each opening re-learns the same lessons; mitigated by playbook updates as a required output (step 3)
- **Single-store learning risk**: insights from one opening never propagate; mitigated by the annual compilation across all openings (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "documents findings and recommendations" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-update of "update store opening playbook" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-log of "logy" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-354 (ensure controlled execution — Grand Opening Execution & Post-Opening Stabilization (PA-37.3).)
- operational: VP Store Operations review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
2 hours per occurrence (per-step effort 2 hours); elapsed timeline 1 day + 1 week; cadence-carried steps as authored (1 day/year) — at ~10–15 sessions/year ≈ 10–15 occurrences/year ≈ 20–30 hours/year.
