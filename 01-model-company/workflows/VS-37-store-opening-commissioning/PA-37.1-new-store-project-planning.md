## W1737. New Store Project Charter & Kickoff

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Site approved per W20.1 (Site Selection & Lease Management); lease signed |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 new stores/year |
| **Volume** | 1 project charter per new store |
| **Owner** | VP Store Operations |
| **Participants** | VP Store Operations, COO, CFO, CIO, CHRO, CMO, VP Supply Chain |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | VP Store Operations creates project charter for new store: (a) store number and name (format: BRD-XXX sequential), (b) location: address, region, store size (sqm), (c) target opening date (typically 4–6 months from lease signing), (d) project budget per financial model from W1652, (e) project team: Store Opening Coordinator (dedicated), Store Manager (appointed 3 months before opening), department representatives from IT, HR, Supply Chain, Marketing, (f) key milestones: construction complete, staff hired, inventory received, systems live, soft opening, grand opening | VP Store Operations | COO | 2–3 days |
| 2 | Kickoff meeting with all functional leads: (a) review project charter and timeline, (b) assign functional workstreams and owners, (c) agree on communication cadence (weekly status meetings, steering committee bi-weekly), (d) identify cross-functional dependencies, (e) risk identification and mitigation planning | VP Store Operations | COO | 2 hours |
| 3 | Project setup in project management tool: (a) work breakdown structure per functional area, (b) task assignments with due dates, (c) critical path highlighted, (d) budget allocated per workstream, (e) status reporting template | Store Opening Coordinator | VP Store Operations | 1 day |
| 4 | Steering committee formed: COO (chair), VP Store Operations, CFO, CIO, COO; meets bi-weekly to review progress and resolve escalations | COO | — | Ongoing |

### System Touchpoints
- Project management module with Gantt chart and critical path tracking (W1737.3)
- Budget allocation per workstream linked to capex project per W40.1 (W1737.3)
- Status reporting dashboard accessible to steering committee (W1737.4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Timeline pressure**: 4–6 months from lease to opening is aggressive for a big-box store; construction delays, permit delays, or IT setup issues can push opening date
- **Resource contention**: Opening 10–15 stores/year means multiple concurrent projects competing for the same IT, HR, and Supply Chain resources
- **Remote locations**: New stores in provincial areas (North/Central Luzon, Mindanao) may have limited local contractor availability and longer permit processing times

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-create of "creates project charter for new store" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "review project charter and timeline" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-assign of "assignments with due dates" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-352 (ensure controlled execution — New Store Project Planning & Coordination (PA-37.1).)
- operational: VP Store Operations review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
2 hours per occurrence (per-step effort 2 hours); elapsed timeline 2–3 days + 1 day; cadence-carried steps as authored (Ongoing) — at ~10–15 new stores/year ≈ 10–15 occurrences/year ≈ 20–30 hours/year.

## W1738. Store Layout Design & Planogram Planning

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Project kickoff (W1737); floor plans received from Real Estate/Construction team |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 designs/year |
| **Volume** | 1 layout per new store (8,000–15,000 sqm) |
| **Owner** | VP Merchandising |
| **Participants** | VP Merchandising, Category Managers, VP Store Operations, Store Opening Coordinator |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | VP Merchandising and Category Managers design store layout based on standardized big-box template with adjustments for store size and local market: (a) zone allocation: main sales floor, lumber yard, tile gallery, appliance section, tool wall, paint station, checkout area, receiving/backroom, customer service counter, (b) category adjacencies: plumbing near tools, electrical near lighting, paint near finishes, (c) traffic flow: entrance → impulse/high-margin → destination categories → checkout, (d) fixture type and quantity per category per store size | VP Merchandising / Category Managers | VP Store Operations | 1–2 weeks/store |
| 2 | Planogram creation per category: (a) shelf layout with SKU placement, (b) product grouping (brand blocks, price laddering), (c) signage and price tag placement, (d) seasonal/promotional display locations, (e) use standardized planogram templates (adjusted for store size tier: small 8,000 sqm, medium 10,000 sqm, large 15,000 sqm) | Category Managers | VP Merchandising | 3–5 days/store |
| 3 | VP Store Operations reviews layout for operational feasibility: (a) receiving dock accessibility for trucks, (b) backroom size adequate for receiving and BOPIS staging, (c) aisle width for cart and forklift access, (d) POS terminal placement for customer flow, (e) safety: fire exits, emergency routes, hazmat storage for paint/chemicals | VP Store Operations | COO | 2–3 days |
| 4 | Final layout approved; fixture and equipment list generated for procurement per W1740 | VP Merchandising / VP Store Operations | COO | 1 day |


### System Touchpoints
- Store layout design workspace — standardized big-box template adjusted for store size and format (step 1)
- Planogram creation per category — shelf layout, SKU placement, brand blocks, price laddering (step 2)
- Operational feasibility review checklist — dock accessibility, backroom adequacy (step 3)
- Fixture & equipment list generation feeding W1740 procurement (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Template-straitjacket risk**: the standardized layout ignores site-specific realities (column spacing, dock orientation); mitigated by documented size/format adjustments at design (step 1)
- **Ops-infeasibility risk**: a layout that merchandising loves but trucks and receiving cannot serve; mitigated by the VP Store Operations feasibility review before approval (step 3)

- System auto-review of "reviews layout for operational feasibility" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-generate of "generated for procurement per W1740" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-352 (ensure controlled execution — New Store Project Planning & Coordination (PA-37.1).)
- operational: VP Merchandising review and approval gate

### Time Estimate

## W1739. Initial Inventory Planning & Allocation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Store layout approved (W1738); target opening date confirmed |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 inventory plans/year |
| **Volume** | ~35,000 active SKUs per store; initial stock value ~PHP 15–25M per store |
| **Owner** | VP Supply Chain |
| **Participants** | VP Supply Chain, Category Managers, Merchandise Planners, DC Operations, Store Opening Coordinator |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Merchandise Planners develop initial stock plan per category: (a) planogram-driven SKU assortment (35,000 active SKUs adjusted for store size), (b) initial quantity per SKU: (i) A-items: 3–4 weeks of projected sales, (ii) B-items: 2–3 weeks, (iii) C-items: 1–2 weeks, (c) projected sales by category based on comparable store performance in similar trade area, (d) new store ramp curve: 70% of mature sales in Month 1, 85% in Month 3, 95% in Month 6 | Merchandise Planners | VP Supply Chain | 1 week/store |
| 2 | VP Supply Chain assigns sourcing DC and schedules replenishment: (a) identify which DC serves the new store location, (b) initial stock pull from assigned DC — order placed 2 weeks before opening, (c) DSD vendors (cement, lumber, sand) scheduled for delivery 3–5 days before opening, (d) import-specific items: ensure stock allocated from existing DC inventory or special import order placed with sufficient lead time | VP Supply Chain | Category Managers | 3–5 days |
| 3 | DC picks and stages initial stock order: (a) separate pick wave for new store initial stock, (b) labeled per store zone/section for efficient shelving, (c) loaded on trucks for delivery 3–5 days before opening, (d) DC coordinates delivery schedule with Store Opening Coordinator | DC Operations | VP Supply Chain | 3–5 days |
| 4 | Initial stock received at new store: (a) Receiving Clerk and stock associates shelve merchandise per planogram, (b) system verifies quantities received vs. initial stock plan, (c) variances flagged for immediate replenishment, (d) all inventory visible in ERP system at store opening | Receiving Clerk / Stock Associates | Store Manager | 3–5 days before opening |

### System Touchpoints
- Initial stock plan per category — planogram-driven assortment from the 35,000-SKU active set adjusted for store format (step 1)
- Sourcing DC assignment and initial replenishment scheduling (step 2)
- New-store dedicated pick wave staging at the DC, labeled per store zone (step 3)
- Planogram-verified shelving and receipt confirmation at the store (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Overstock risk**: Initial stock based on projections; if actual sales are lower than expected, store carries excess inventory in first months; markdowns may be needed for slow-moving items; mitigated by CTL-352 (controlled execution — New Store Project Planning & Coordination (PA-37.1))
- **DC capacity impact**: Pulling initial stock for a new store (PHP 15–25M) reduces DC available inventory for existing stores; must be planned into DC replenishment cycles
- **Perishable/hazardous items**: Paint and chemicals require special storage; cement bags must be kept dry during store setup (Philippine rainy season risk)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-schedule of "VP Supply Chain assigns sourcing DC and schedules replenishment" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System workflow orchestration of "coordinates delivery schedule with Store Opening Coordinator" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-flag of "flagged for immediate replenishment" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-352 (ensure controlled execution — New Store Project Planning & Coordination (PA-37.1).)
- operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Time Estimate
Per new store: initial stock planning 1 week + DC assignment and scheduling 3–5 days + DC pick/stage wave 3–5 days + store receiving and shelving 3–5 days — ~2 weeks of planner/DC effort over a 3–4-week window; × ~10–15 stores/year ≈ 20–30 weeks/year on ~PHP 15–25M initial stock per store.

- **ISP-lead-time risk**: connectivity arrives days before opening with no test window; mitigated by 4-week advance scheduling and the backup link (step 3)
- **Configuration-error risk**: wrong pricing zone or tax setup misprices every item on day one; mitigated by configuration verification in the W1752 go-live check (step 2)

## W1742. Permit & License Acquisition for New Store

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Lease signed; construction nearing completion |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 permit sets/year |
| **Volume** | ~8–12 permits/licenses per new store |
| **Owner** | Legal Counsel |
| **Participants** | Legal Counsel, COO, Store Opening Coordinator, Compliance Officer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Required permits and licenses for each new store (Philippine requirements): (a) Barangay Clearance, (b) Mayor's Permit / Business Permit (LGU), (c) BIR Certificate of Registration (BIR Form 1903), (d) BIR Authority to Print (ATP) for receipts, (e) BIR Point-of-Sale Registration (for POS system), (f) Fire Safety Inspection Certificate (BFP), (g) DENR Environmental Compliance Certificate (ECC) or CNC (if required for store size), (h) Locational Clearance (zoning), (i) Sanitary/Health Permit (if applicable), (j) Building Permit and Certificate of Occupancy (for new construction or major renovation) | Legal Counsel / Compliance Officer | VP Legal | 4–8 weeks |
| 2 | Legal Counsel coordinates with LGU and government agencies: (a) applications submitted with required documents (SEC registration, lease contract, fire safety plan, environmental plan), (b) follow-up on processing timelines (LGU processing can take 2–6 weeks), (c) arrange inspections (fire, building, health), (d) pay permit fees | Legal Counsel | VP Legal | 4–8 weeks |
| 3 | BIR registration critical path: (a) register store as BIR taxpaying location, (b) obtain authority to use POS system (CAS registration if not covered by group registration), (c) register books of accounts, (d) obtain ATP for sales invoices, (e) BIR POS inspection and sealing | Tax Accountant / Legal Counsel | CFO | 2–4 weeks |
| 4 | Permits received and filed; Store Manager trained on permit renewal requirements (annual Business Permit renewal in January per LGU schedule); copies maintained at store for government inspection | Legal Counsel / Store Manager | VP Legal | Ongoing |

### System Touchpoints
- Philippine permit checklist — Barangay Clearance, Mayor's/Business Permit, BIR, and LGU requirements (step 1)
- LGU coordination and application tracking (step 2)
- BIR critical path — store registration and CAS/POS authority to invoice (step 3)
- Permit register with renewal training (annual Business Permit in January) (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **LGU processing variability**: Each LGU has different processing times and requirements; stores in smaller municipalities may process faster or slower depending on local capacity
- **BIR POS registration**: If CAS (Computerized Accounting System) registration is per-location (not per-entity), each new store requires BIR approval to use the POS system — can take 2–4 weeks and requires physical BIR inspection
- **Fire safety inspection**: Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP) inspection must pass before opening; paint and chemical storage areas receive particular scrutiny

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-generate of "Required permits and licenses for each new store" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "Legal Counsel coordinates with LGU and government agencies" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-inspect of "inspection and sealing" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-188 (prevent premature store go-live.)
- operational: — can take 2–4 weeks and requires physical BIR inspection

### Time Estimate
10–20 weeks elapsed per occurrence (4–8 weeks + 4–8 weeks + 2–4 weeks); cadence-carried steps as authored (Ongoing) — at ~10–15 permit sets/year ≈ 10–15 occurrences/year.

| **Frequency** | Continuous through project completion; final reconciliation post-opening |
| **Volume** | 1 budget per new store (~PHP 40–60M) |
| **Owner** | FP&A |
| **Participants** | FP&A, Store Opening Coordinator, CFO, VP Store Operations |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | FP&A establishes project budget per financial model from W1652: (a) construction/renovation (~60% of budget), (b) fixtures and equipment (~15%), (c) initial inventory (~35–40%, aligned to W1739's ~PHP 15–25M initial stock), (d) IT infrastructure (~5%), (e) marketing/grand opening (~2%), (f) staff recruitment and training (~2%), (g) permits and fees (~1%) | FP&A | CFO | 1 day |
| 2 | Monthly: FP&A tracks actual costs against budget: (a) committed costs (POs issued but not yet invoiced), (b) incurred costs (invoices received and paid), (c) forecast to complete (remaining budget needed), (d) total forecast vs. original budget | FP&A | CFO | 2 hours/month/project |
| 3 | Variance management: (a) < 5% variance: Store Opening Coordinator adjusts within project, (b) 5–10% variance: VP Store Operations approval required, (c) > 10% variance: CFO and COO approval; steering committee informed; corrective action plan required | Store Opening Coordinator / VP Store Operations / CFO | COO | As needed |
| 4 | Post-opening reconciliation: FP&A finalizes project cost; reconciles to capex project per W40.3; CIP converted to fixed assets; pre-opening expenses (training, marketing, trial runs) expensed per PFRS | FP&A / Fixed Asset Accountant | CFO | 2–4 weeks post-opening |


### System Touchpoints
- Project budget structure from the W1652 model — construction ~60%, fixtures/equipment, initial inventory, pre-opening OpEx (step 1)
- Monthly committed + incurred cost tracking (step 2)
- Variance escalation tiers — < 5% coordinator; 5–10% VP; > 10% steering committee (step 3)
- Post-opening CIP reconciliation to the capex project per W40.3 (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Committed-cost blindness**: POs issued but not yet invoiced hide the true overrun; mitigated by tracking committed and incurred costs together (step 2)
- **Scope-creep risk**: opening scope grows (extra fixtures, signage) without approval; mitigated by variance-tier escalation authority (step 3)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-track of "tracks actual costs against budget" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-reconcile of "reconciles to capex project per W40.3" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-352 (ensure controlled execution — New Store Project Planning & Coordination (PA-37.1).)
- operational: FP&A review and approval gate; periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
Per store project: budget establishment 1 day + monthly cost tracking 2 hours/month across the ~4–6-month build (~8–12 hours) + post-opening reconciliation 2–4 weeks ≈ 25–35 hours per project × ~10–15 projects/year ≈ 250–525 hours/year on ~PHP 40–60M budgets.
