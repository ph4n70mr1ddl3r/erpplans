## W1838. Private Label Ongoing Production Quality Monitoring & Factory Audit Program

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Ongoing program; periodic factory audits scheduled semi-annually or annually per factory risk tier |
| **Frequency** | Semi-annual audit for Tier 1 factories (top volume); annual for Tier 2; triggered for cause (quality incident, delivery failure) |
| **Volume** | ~15–25 active private label factories; ~8–12 scheduled audits/year + ~2–4 for-cause audits |
| **Owner** | Quality Assurance Manager |
| **Participants** | QA Manager, Buyer, Factory Quality Team, Third-Party Auditor (SGS/BV) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | QA Manager schedules factory audit calendar at start of year: (a) tier factories by risk: Tier 1 (top 5 by volume — semi-annual), Tier 2 (remaining — annual), (b) coordinate audit dates with factory and Buyer (production schedule awareness), (c) select audit type: on-site for Tier 1 and new factories; desk audit (document review + video walkthrough) for Tier 2 with consistently high scores (≥ 90%), (d) engage third-party auditor for on-site audits in China/Taiwan (QA Manager does not travel for routine audits) | QA Manager | Category Manager | 1 day/year |
| 2 | Pre-audit preparation: (a) QA Manager compiles factory performance data from previous period: defect rate, delivery performance, customer complaint rate, corrective action closure rate, (b) identify specific focus areas for this audit based on performance trends and known issues, (c) prepare audit checklist customized to product category and factory, (d) send audit notification to factory 2 weeks in advance with scope and documentation requirements | QA Manager | — | 2–4 hours/audit |
| 3 | Conduct factory audit (on-site by third-party auditor or desk review by QA Manager): (a) production process review: raw material control, manufacturing parameters, in-process QC, final inspection, (b) quality management system verification: documentation, calibration records, training records, corrective action logs, (c) product testing review: witness or review test results per BuildRight specification requirements, (d) social compliance walkthrough: working conditions, safety, environmental practices, (e) review factory's own incoming material quality records (traceability to sub-suppliers), (f) scoring: rate factory on 50-point scale across quality, process, social compliance, environmental categories | Third-Party Auditor / QA Manager | QA Manager | 1–2 days |
| 4 | QA Manager reviews audit report and communicates results: (a) if score ≥ 85%: satisfactory — continue standard monitoring, (b) if score 70–84%: issue corrective action request (CAR) for identified non-conformances; factory must respond within 30 days with corrective action plan; follow-up verification within 90 days, (c) if score < 70%: critical — Buyer notified; Category Manager reviews; if critical findings relate to product safety or regulatory compliance: halt orders until corrective actions verified, (d) share audit results with Buyer for vendor scorecard integration (W44) | QA Manager | Category Manager | 2–4 hours/audit |
| 5 | Quarterly: QA Manager presents factory quality summary to Category Manager and VP Merchandising: (a) audit score trends by factory, (b) open corrective action items and closure rate, (c) quality incidents by factory and resolution status, (d) recommendation for factory tier reclassification (upgrade/downgrade), (e) recommendation for factory transition (W1837) if chronic underperformance | QA Manager | VP Merchandising | 1 hour/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Factory audit calendar and scheduling module (W1838.1)
- Audit checklist template library by product category (W1838.2)
- Audit result scoring with historical trend tracking (W1838.3–4)
- Corrective action request (CAR) management with SLA tracking (W1838.4b)
- Factory quality scorecard integration with W44 (vendor performance)
- Quality incident tracking linked to factory and SKU (W1838.5)
- Integration with W44 (vendor scorecard), W160 (factory audit), W1837 (supplier transition)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Audit fatigue**: Factories audited by multiple customers (BuildRight + other retailers) may deprioritize BuildRight-specific audit requirements; sharing audit results with factories and offering improvement support maintains engagement
- **Corrective action implementation gap**: Factories may submit corrective action plans but fail to implement consistently; verification audits (step 4b) are essential, not optional
- **Third-party auditor quality variance**: Different third-party auditors may apply scoring criteria inconsistently; QA Manager should standardize audit protocol and calibrate with auditor before engagement


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "document review + video walkthrough" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-validate of "checklist customized to product category and factory," (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-log of "logs," (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-365 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Quality Assurance (PA-41.2).)
operational: Quality Assurance Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **QA Manager**: Audit program management adds ~8–12 days/year for scheduling, preparation, report review, and follow-up — absorbed by dedicated QA role
- **Third-party audit cost**: ~USD 500–1,500 per on-site audit in China/Taiwan × 8–12 audits/year = ~USD 4,000–18,000/year

### Time Estimate
- Annual scheduling: 1 day/year
- Pre-audit preparation: 2–4 hours/audit
- Audit execution: 1–2 days/audit (elapsed; active time ~2–4 hours for desk reviews)
- Report review and communication: 2–4 hours/audit
- Quarterly review: 1 hour/quarter
- Total: ~30–50 days/year elapsed; ~80–120 hours active time

## W1839. Private Label Customer Quality Complaint Investigation & Corrective Action

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer complaint received for private label product quality issue |
| **Frequency** | ~50–100 quality complaints/month across private label portfolio (~0.5% of transactions involving private label items) |
| **Volume** | Range: packaging defects, product performance failure, safety concerns, labeling errors |
| **Owner** | Quality Assurance Manager |
| **Participants** | QA Manager, Store Manager (complaint receipt), Category Manager, Buyer, Factory |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer reports quality issue at store or through customer service: (a) Store Manager / CSR logs complaint in system with: product (SKU), lot/batch number (if visible), complaint type (safety, performance, cosmetic, packaging), description, customer contact, (b) if safety concern (e.g., power tool malfunction causing injury): Store Manager immediately removes remaining units from shelf and quarantines; QA Manager notified within 2 hours | Store Manager / CSR | QA Manager | 10 min |
| 2 | QA Manager triages complaint: (a) **Safety complaint**: highest priority — immediate investigation within 24 hours; if confirmed safety risk: escalate to product recall evaluation per W1841, (b) **Performance complaint**: standard priority — investigate within 5 business days, (c) **Cosmetic/packaging complaint**: low priority — log for trend analysis; batch for quarterly factory communication | QA Manager | Category Manager | 15 min |
| 3 | QA Manager investigates root cause: (a) review complaint details: batch number, manufacturing date, store, customer usage description, (b) compare to reference sample retained from W1832: is the defective unit within specification or a clear outlier? (c) review factory incoming inspection records for the batch: was the batch flagged at receipt? (d) if systemic: review all incoming quality data from same factory for trending defect patterns, (e) if isolated: document as one-off and monitor for recurrence, (f) request factory investigation for performance and safety complaints: factory must respond within 10 business days with root cause analysis and corrective action plan | QA Manager | Category Manager | 2–4 hours |
| 4 | Resolution and corrective action: (a) customer resolution: return/exchange per W12 (returns), additional compensation if safety-related, (b) factory corrective action: QA Manager evaluates factory response; if acceptable: track implementation per W1838 corrective action process; if inadequate: escalate to Buyer for supplier intervention, (c) if systemic quality issue confirmed: Buyer and Category Manager decide — (i) continue with factory corrective action, (ii) halt future orders pending quality verification, (iii) initiate supplier transition per W1837, (d) store-level actions: if specific batch affected, issue batch hold notice to all stores carrying the SKU | QA Manager / Buyer | Category Manager | 2–4 hours |
| 5 | Monthly: QA Manager compiles private label quality dashboard: (a) complaint rate by SKU, category, and factory, (b) complaint resolution time vs. SLA, (c) top 10 SKUs by complaint volume, (d) factory corrective action closure rate, (e) trending: complaint rate improvement or deterioration by factory | QA Manager | VP Merchandising | 2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Customer complaint logging at store/CSR level with SKU and batch capture (W1839.1)
- Quality complaint triage workflow with priority classification (W1839.2)
- Root cause investigation module linked to batch, factory, and incoming inspection records (W1839.3)
- Batch hold and store notification system (W1839.4d)
- Quality dashboard with factory-level complaint analytics (W1839.5)
- Integration with W12 (returns), W29 (product recall), W13.1 (customer complaints), W1838 (factory audit), W1837 (supplier transition)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Under-reporting**: Many customers simply return defective items without filing a quality complaint; actual defect rate is likely 3–5x the complaint rate; analysis should include return reason data from W12
- **Factory responsiveness**: Chinese factories may resist acknowledging quality issues or providing meaningful root cause analysis; persistent follow-up by Buyer and QA Manager is essential
- **Legal liability**: Safety-related quality failures on BuildRight-branded products create direct liability (no national brand to share risk); product liability insurance review per W59 is critical


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-log of "logs complaint in system with" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-escalate of "escalate to product recall evaluation per W1841," (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "records for the batch" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-365 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Quality Assurance (PA-41.2).)
operational: Quality Assurance Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **QA Manager**: Quality complaint investigation adds ~2–4 hours/day across 50–100 complaints/month = ~50–100 hours/month — this is a core function of the QA Manager role
- **No incremental headcount beyond QA Manager**

### Time Estimate
- Complaint logging: 10 min/complaint
- Triage: 15 min/complaint
- Investigation: 2–4 hours for performance/safety complaints
- Resolution: 2–4 hours for significant issues
- Monthly dashboard: 2 hours/month
- Total: ~150–250 hours/month (mix of triage and deep investigation)

## W1840. Private Label Product Regulatory Compliance Testing & Certification Renewal

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New product launch requiring certification; annual certification renewal; regulatory change |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 new product certifications/year; ~20–30 renewal applications/year |
| **Volume** | Private label products requiring DTI-BPS ICC mark (steel products, electrical items, cement), FDA notification (household hazardous substances), DENR clearance (wood products from certain origins) |
| **Owner** | Quality Assurance Manager |
| **Participants** | QA Manager, Buyer, Import Coordinator, Regulatory Affairs (if dedicated role) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | QA Manager identifies regulatory requirements for each private label product category: (a) DTI-BPS Philippine Standard (PS) Quality Mark or Import Commodity Clearance (ICC): required for steel products (rebar, nails, bolts), electrical products (wires, switches, breakers), cement, galvanized iron sheets, (b) FDA notification: required for household hazardous substances (paint thinners, solvents, adhesives, cleaning chemicals), (c) DENR clearance: required for wood products to verify legal sourcing and ISPM-15 compliance, (d) product-specific labeling requirements per Consumer Act RA 7394 | QA Manager | Category Manager | 4 hours/category (initial mapping) |
| 2 | For new product certifications: (a) QA Manager submits product samples to BPS-accredited testing laboratory, (b) laboratory conducts testing per Philippine National Standard (PNS) or relevant international standard, (c) QA Manager submits test results and documentation to DTI-BPS for PS/ICC certification, (d) BPS issues certification (typically 3–6 months for new products), (e) products cannot be imported or sold until certification obtained | QA Manager | Category Manager | 3–6 months |
| 3 | For certification renewals: (a) system alerts QA Manager 90 days before certification expiry, (b) QA Manager submits renewal application with updated product testing (if required), (c) BPS renews certification (typically 1–3 months), (d) if renewal delayed: products with expired certification must be removed from shelves until renewed | QA Manager | Category Manager | 1–3 months |
| 4 | For regulatory changes: (a) QA Manager monitors regulatory updates from DTI, FDA, DENR, BIR, (b) assesses impact on private label products, (c) if product reformulation or relabeling required: coordinates with Buyer and factory per W1832 (specification change process), (d) updated products retested and recertified before distribution | QA Manager | Category Manager | Variable |

### System Touchpoints
- Product certification registry: certification type, number, issue date, expiry date, linked SKUs (W1840.1)
- Certification expiry alerting with 90-day advance notice (W1840.3)
- Regulatory change tracking module (W1840.4)
- Integration with W447 (DTI-BPS certification), W479 (FDA compliance), W22.3 (regulatory change management), W1832 (product specification)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Certification delays**: DTI-BPS processing times can extend beyond 6 months for new products, delaying launch; start certification process early — ideally concurrent with factory qualification (W1831)
- **Product reformulation cost**: Regulatory changes may require product reformulation that increases manufacturing cost; Category Manager must decide whether to absorb cost or pass through pricing adjustment
- **Expired certification on shelf**: If certification renewal is delayed, products must be pulled from shelves across 200 stores; enforcement is difficult and compliance risk is high


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-validate of "verify legal sourcing and ISPM-15 compliance," (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "documentation to DTI-BPS for PS/ICC certification," (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-submit of "submits renewal application with updated product testing" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-173 (prevent non-compliant private-label products from reaching shelf.)
operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- **QA Manager**: Regulatory compliance management adds ~10–15 hours/month — absorbed
- **Third-party testing laboratory costs**: ~PHP 10,000–50,000 per product test × 30–45 tests/year = ~PHP 300K–2.3M/year

### Time Estimate
- Regulatory mapping: 4 hours/category (one-time)
- New product certification: 3–6 months elapsed; ~20–40 hours active time
- Renewal processing: 1–3 months elapsed; ~5–10 hours active time
- Monthly monitoring: ~10–15 hours/month
- Total: ~200–300 hours/year active time

## W1841. Private Label Product Recall & Safety Notification

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Confirmed safety defect in private label product; regulatory recall order; factory notification of production defect affecting shipped units |
| **Frequency** | Rare — estimated 1–2 recall events/year for private label portfolio |
| **Volume** | Affects specific SKU(s), batch(es), and potentially all units across 200 stores |
| **Owner** | VP Merchandising |
| **Participants** | VP Merchandising, QA Manager, Category Manager, Store Operations, Legal Counsel, Customer Service, CMO |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recall trigger: (a) QA Manager confirms safety defect through investigation per W1839 (customer complaint), incoming inspection finding, or factory notification, (b) Legal Counsel assesses regulatory obligation: is a mandatory recall required by DTI, FDA, or DENR? (c) VP Merchandising convenes recall decision meeting within 24 hours of confirmed safety defect | QA Manager / VP Merchandising | CEO | 2–4 hours |
| 2 | Recall scope definition: (a) QA Manager identifies affected batch(es) using lot tracking data: which factories, which production dates, which DCs received, which stores received, (b) Category Manager quantifies affected units: on shelves, in backrooms, in transit, at DCs, (c) VP Merchandising approves recall scope: full recall (all units) or targeted recall (specific batches only) | QA Manager / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 4–8 hours |
| 3 | Recall execution: (a) Store Operations issues immediate store notification: remove affected SKU(s) from shelves and quarantine in backroom, (b) DC operations quarantine any affected units at DCs, (c) customer service contact center: prepare customer communication script, (d) CMO prepares public recall notice (if required): press release, social media, website notice, in-store signage, (e) ecommerce: remove affected SKU from online catalog immediately, (f) legal counsel files mandatory recall notice with DTI/FDA if required | Store Operations / CMO / Legal | VP Merchandising | 1–2 days |
| 4 | Customer notification and remedy: (a) for loyalty members who purchased affected product: system generates customer list; customer service contacts customers proactively, (b) remedy: full refund, replacement with national brand equivalent, or store credit — per Category Manager decision, (c) process returns per W12 (returns workflow), (d) for trade accounts: Account Manager contacts trade customers directly | Customer Service / Store Manager | VP Merchandising | Ongoing for recall duration |
| 5 | Factory accountability: (a) Buyer notifies factory of recall; provides defect details and batch information, (b) factory conducts root cause analysis (mandatory), (c) QA Manager evaluates factory corrective action, (d) Buyer negotiates cost recovery: reimbursement for recalled units, refund processing costs, customer compensation costs, (e) if factory cooperation inadequate: Category Manager initiates supplier transition per W1837 | Buyer / QA Manager | VP Merchandising | 2–4 weeks |
| 6 | Post-recall review: (a) QA Manager documents lessons learned: root cause, detection timing, recall effectiveness, (b) update factory risk assessment and audit frequency, (c) update product specification if design change warranted, (d) VP Merchandising reports to CEO on recall cost, customer impact, and preventive measures | QA Manager / VP Merchandising | CEO | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints
- Lot/batch tracking for recall scope identification (W1841.2)
- Store notification system for immediate product removal (W1841.3a)
- Customer purchase history lookup by lot/batch for proactive notification (W1841.4)
- Ecommerce catalog removal for affected SKUs (W1841.3e)
- Recall documentation and cost tracking (W1841.5–6)
- Integration with W29 (product recall), W12 (returns), W1839 (quality complaint), W1837 (supplier transition), W44 (vendor scorecard)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Brand damage**: Private label product recalls directly damage the BuildRight brand (unlike national brand recalls where BuildRight is the retailer, not the manufacturer); transparent communication and swift remedy are essential to preserve trust
- **Recall cost**: For a private label recall, BuildRight bears the full cost (inventory loss, refund processing, customer compensation, potential legal liability) with no national brand manufacturer to share the burden; cost recovery from the factory is uncertain and typically partial
- **Lot tracking gaps**: If lot tracking was not enforced at receiving (W3), BuildRight cannot identify which specific stores received affected batches, forcing a full chain-wide recall with higher cost and disruption


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-inspect of "inspection finding, or factory notification," (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-track of "tracking data" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-capture of "enter" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-365 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Quality Assurance (PA-41.2).)
operational: VP Merchandising review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — recall is managed by existing cross-functional team
- **Estimated recall cost per event**: PHP 500K–5M depending on scope (product value, number of units, customer compensation)

### Time Estimate
- Recall decision and scope: 4–8 hours
- Execution: 1–2 days for initial response; ongoing for recall duration
- Factory accountability: 2–4 weeks
- Post-recall review: 1 day
- Total per recall event: ~40–80 hours active time over 4–8 weeks

## W1842. Private Label Shelf-Life Monitoring & Near-Expiry Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly shelf-life review; near-expiry alert from inventory system |
| **Frequency** | Monthly review for shelf-life managed private label products |
| **Volume** | ~200–400 SKUs with shelf life (adhesives, sealants, paint accessories, chemicals, cleaning products) |
| **Owner** | Merchandise Planner |
| **Participants** | Merchandise Planner, Category Manager, Store Operations Coordinator |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monthly: Merchandise Planner generates shelf-life aging report for private label products: (a) SKUs with remaining shelf life < 6 months flagged amber, (b) SKUs with remaining shelf life < 3 months flagged red, (c) SKUs past expiry date flagged critical | Merchandise Planner | Category Manager | 1 hour/month |
| 2 | For amber-flagged SKUs (6 months remaining): (a) Category Manager evaluates: is current sell-through rate sufficient to sell through before expiry? (b) if yes: no action, monitor next month, (c) if sell-through too slow: implement promotional pricing per W93 (markdown) to accelerate movement, (d) prioritize movement at stores with oldest inventory (FEFO compliance) | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 30 min |
| 3 | For red-flagged SKUs (3 months remaining): (a) Category Manager authorizes aggressive markdown (25–40%), (b) offer to trade accounts at bulk discount, (c) move remaining inventory to higher-traffic stores if distribution is uneven | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 30 min |
| 4 | For critical-flagged SKUs (past expiry): (a) Merchandise Planner generates write-off request per W220 (SLOB provisioning), (b) Store Operations coordinates removal and disposal (environmentally compliant disposal for chemical products per DENR requirements), (c) system adjusts inventory and posts write-off to GL | Merchandise Planner / Store Operations | Category Manager | Per W220 |
| 5 | Quarterly: Category Manager reviews shelf-life write-off trends: (a) by product category and factory, (b) identify SKUs with recurring shelf-life issues for potential discontinuation, (c) adjust order quantities and safety stock for shelf-life managed SKUs to reduce future waste | Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 1 hour/quarter |

### System Touchpoints
- Shelf-life date tracking in inventory module (W1842.1)
- Automated aging alerts with configurable thresholds (amber/red/critical) (W1842.1)
- FEFO (First Expired, First Out) picking and replenishment enforcement
- Integration with W93 (markdown & clearance), W220 (SLOB), W6 (cycle counting)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **FEFO compliance at store level**: Store associates may not follow FEFO when replenishing shelves; newer stock placed in front of older stock; monthly shelf-life audit should include physical shelf check
- **Chemical disposal cost**: Expired adhesives, solvents, and cleaning chemicals require DENR-compliant hazardous waste disposal — more expensive than standard disposal


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-generate of "generates shelf-life aging report for private label products" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-monitor of "monitor next month," (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-count of "counts at bulk discount," (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-365 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Quality Assurance (PA-41.2).)
operational: Merchandise Planner review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by existing Merchandise Planner and Category Manager roles

### Time Estimate
- Monthly review: 1–2 hours/month
- Action on flagged items: 1–2 hours/month
- Quarterly review: 1 hour/quarter
- Total: ~20–30 hours/year

## W1843. Private Label Packaging & Labeling Compliance Audit

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual compliance audit; new product launch; regulatory change affecting labeling |
| **Frequency** | Annual full audit; spot-checks on new receipts |
| **Volume** | ~2,800–4,200 active private label SKUs |
| **Owner** | Quality Assurance Manager |
| **Participants** | QA Manager, Category Manager, Legal Counsel |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | QA Manager conducts annual packaging and labeling audit: (a) select random sample of 50–100 private label SKUs from store shelves across 5–10 stores, (b) verify each label against Philippine labeling requirements per Consumer Act RA 7394 and DTI regulations: (i) product name and description, (ii) net quantity, (iii) name and address of manufacturer and distributor (BuildRight Depot Corp.), (iv) country of origin, (v) ingredients/material composition (where applicable), (vi) production date or lot number, (vii) expiry date (for shelf-life products), (viii) safety warnings in Filipino and English, (ix) SRP or price, (x) BPS/ICC mark (where required), (c) verify barcode scans correctly to correct SKU in POS system, (d) verify packaging structural integrity: does packaging protect product adequately during distribution? | QA Manager | Category Manager | 2–3 days/year |
| 2 | Document findings and issue corrective actions: (a) minor labeling deficiencies (missing Filipino translation, incorrect font size): issue CAR to Buyer for factory correction in next production run, (b) major labeling deficiencies (missing safety warnings, incorrect product information): immediate relabeling or removal from shelf until corrected, (c) barcode failures: work with Merchandise Planner and IT to resolve; remove from shelf if barcode cannot be scanned at POS | QA Manager | Category Manager | 1 day/year |
| 3 | Spot-check on new receipts: (a) for first 3 receipts from a new factory or after label redesign: QA Manager or DC Quality Inspector verifies labeling compliance on received goods, (b) if non-conforming: relabel at DC before distribution (cost charged to factory) or return to factory | QA Manager / DC Quality Inspector | Category Manager | 15–30 min/receipt |

### System Touchpoints
- Label compliance checklist template per product category (W1843.1)
- Barcode verification linked to item master (W1843.1c)
- Corrective action request management for labeling issues (W1843.2)
- New receipt labeling verification workflow (W1843.3)
- Integration with W252 (item master governance), W316 (digital asset master for packaging artwork)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Regulatory penalty**: Non-compliant labeling can result in DTI fines (PHP 5,000–50,000 per violation) and product seizure; proactive annual audit mitigates this risk
- **Relabeling cost**: If factory ships products with non-compliant labels, relabeling at DC is labor-intensive and delays distribution; penalty clause in factory contract for labeling non-compliance incentivizes accuracy


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-scan of "scans correctly to correct SKU in POS system," (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-document of "Document findings and issue corrective actions" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-validate of "check on new receipts" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-365 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Quality Assurance (PA-41.2).)
operational: standard operating procedures; system-enforced validation rules

### Staffing Implication
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by QA Manager and DC Quality Inspector roles

### Time Estimate
- Annual audit: 2–3 days
- Corrective action documentation: 1 day
- Spot-checks: ~15–30 min/receipt × 10–15 first receipts/year = ~3–8 hours/year
- Total: ~30–40 hours/year

## W1844. Private Label Incoming Quality Trend Analysis & Factory Scorecard Integration

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly reporting cycle |
| **Frequency** | Monthly trend analysis; quarterly factory scorecard update |
| **Volume** | ~15–25 active private label factories |
| **Owner** | Quality Assurance Manager |
| **Participants** | QA Manager, Category Manager, Buyer |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Monthly: QA Manager compiles incoming quality data for private label products: (a) defect rate by factory (defective units ÷ total received units × 100), (b) defect type distribution: cosmetic, functional, packaging, labeling, safety, (c) rejection rate: % of receipts rejected or requiring corrective action, (d) trend analysis: is quality improving, stable, or deteriorating by factory? (e) correlation analysis: defect rate vs. factory audit score — do factories with lower audit scores have higher defect rates? | QA Manager | Category Manager | 2–4 hours/month |
| 2 | Quarterly: QA Manager updates factory quality scorecard integrated with W44 (vendor performance): (a) quality dimension score (40% weight): defect rate, rejection rate, corrective action closure rate, (b) delivery dimension score (20% weight): on-time delivery, lead time consistency, (c) cost dimension score (20% weight): price competitiveness, landed cost variance, (d) responsiveness dimension score (20% weight): communication timeliness, complaint resolution speed, (e) overall weighted score → factory tier classification (Tier 1: ≥ 85%, Tier 2: 70–84%, Tier 3: < 70%) | QA Manager | Category Manager | 4–6 hours/quarter |

## W1845. Private Label Supplier Social Compliance & Environmental Audit Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual social compliance audit cycle; triggered by media report, NGO investigation, or customer concern about factory conditions |
| **Frequency** | Annual social compliance audit for Tier 1 factories; biennial for Tier 2; triggered for cause |
| **Volume** | ~8–12 social compliance audits/year |
| **Owner** | Quality Assurance Manager |
| **Participants** | QA Manager, Category Manager, Third-Party Auditor (BSCI, SMETA), Legal Counsel |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | QA Manager schedules social compliance audits: (a) coordinate with factory quality audit schedule (W1838) to combine visits where possible, (b) select audit standard: BSCI (Business Social Compliance Initiative), SMETA (Sedex Members Ethical Trade Audit), or SA8000, (c) engage accredited third-party auditor | QA Manager | Category Manager | 1 day/year |
| 2 | Third-party auditor conducts social compliance audit at factory: (a) working conditions: hours, wages, overtime compliance with local labor laws, (b) health and safety: fire safety, machine guarding, chemical handling, personal protective equipment, (c) child labor and forced labor verification, (d) freedom of association, (e) environmental practices: waste disposal, emissions, water treatment, (f) dormitory conditions (if factory provides worker housing), (g) scoring per BSCI/SMETA methodology: critical, major, minor, observations | Third-Party Auditor | QA Manager | 1–2 days/audit |
| 3 | QA Manager reviews audit findings: (a) **Critical findings** (e.g., child labor, forced labor, imminent safety hazard): immediate order suspension; factory must remediate within 30 days; follow-up verification audit required before orders resume; Category Manager and VP Merchandising notified, (b) **Major findings** (e.g., excessive overtime, inadequate safety guards): corrective action plan required within 60 days; progress verification within 90 days, (c) **Minor findings / observations**: factory acknowledges and incorporates into continuous improvement plan; reviewed at next scheduled audit | QA Manager | Category Manager | 2–4 hours/audit |
| 4 | Annual: QA Manager presents social compliance summary to VP Merchandising and Legal Counsel: (a) audit results by factory with trend comparison, (b) corrective action status and closure rate, (c) risk assessment: factories in regions with known labor or environmental issues, (d) recommendation for factory continuation or transition, (e) summary for ESG reporting per W25 (ESG & Sustainability) | QA Manager | VP Merchandising | 1 day/year |
| 5 | If triggered by media/NGO report: (a) Legal Counsel assesses legal and reputational risk, (b) QA Manager arranges emergency for-cause audit within 2 weeks, (c) if allegations confirmed: immediate remediation plan or factory suspension, (d) CMO prepares media response if public communication required | Legal Counsel / QA Manager | CEO | 1–2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Social compliance audit calendar and scheduling (W1845.1)
- Audit result repository with BSCI/SMETA scoring templates (W1845.2)
- Corrective action tracking with SLA management (W1845.3)
- Factory social compliance scorecard for ESG reporting (W1845.4)
- Media/NGO incident tracking and response workflow (W1845.5)
- Integration with W1838 (factory quality audit), W25 (ESG & Sustainability), W59 (insurance — product liability)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Audit accuracy**: Social compliance audits are snapshot assessments; factories may prepare for known audit dates and revert to non-compliant practices between audits; unannounced audits (at least 1 per year for Tier 1 factories) improve accuracy
- **Reputational risk**: BuildRight-branded products manufactured in factories with poor labor practices directly damage the BuildRight brand; ESG-conscious consumers and trade professionals increasingly evaluate supply chain ethics
- **Remediation cost**: If a factory requires significant capital investment to meet social compliance standards, the factory may pass costs to BuildRight through price increases


### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-schedule of "schedules social compliance audits" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-log of "logy" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-review of "reviews audit findings" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-365 (ensure controlled execution — Private Label Quality Assurance (PA-41.2).)
operational: Quality Assurance Manager review and approval gate

### Staffing Implication
- **QA Manager**: Social compliance management adds ~5–8 days/year — absorbed
- **Third-party audit cost**: ~USD 800–2,000 per social compliance audit × 8–12 audits/year = ~USD 6,400–24,000/year

### Time Estimate
- Annual scheduling: 1 day
- Per audit: 2–4 hours (review and follow-up; execution by third party)
- Annual summary: 1 day
- Emergency response: 1–2 weeks (rare)
- Total: ~20–30 days/year elapsed; ~60–100 hours active time
