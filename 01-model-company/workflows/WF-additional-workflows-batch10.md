## W1103. Store-Level Outdoor Lumber Yard Rain Protection & Inventory Preservation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Philippine Weather Bureau (PAGASA) weather advisory, approaching typhoon signal, daily rainy season protocol activation (June–November), or routine daily tarp deployment schedule |
| **Frequency** | Daily during rainy season (June–November, ~180 days); as-needed during dry season (~30 events) |
| **Volume** | 1 lumber yard per store × 200 stores = 200 daily activations during rainy season |
| **Owner** | Receiving Clerk / Stock Associate (Lumber Yard) |
| **Participants** | Stock Associate (Lumber Yard), Department Supervisor (Lumber & Building Materials), Store Manager |

### Background

BuildRight Depot stores maintain an outdoor/covered lumber yard for bulky building materials (plywood, lumber, cement, hollow blocks, rebar, roofing sheets). The Philippines experiences distinct wet and dry seasons, with an average of 20 typhoons per year and heavy monsoon rains from June through November. Lumber and wood products are particularly vulnerable to moisture damage: (a) warping and cupping of dimensional lumber within 24–48 hours of rain exposure; (b) delamination of plywood and OSB (oriented strand board); (c) mold and fungal growth on untreated lumber; (d) cement bag deterioration and hardening when wet; (e) corrosion of galvanized roofing sheets at cut edges. The outdoor lumber yard represents ~14% of active SKUs (~4,900 items) and a significant portion of inventory value. Systematic rain protection is essential to minimize shrinkage, which for lumber can reach 3–5% without proper protection vs. the company target of < 1.5% overall. This workflow addresses the daily operational protocol for lumber yard weather protection, complementing W576 (typhoon preparedness) which covers the broader store emergency response.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Daily Weather Check & Protection Decision**: At 6:00 AM (before yard opening), Stock Associate checks: (a) PAGASA 24-hour weather forecast via mobile app integration in store manager dashboard; (b) current conditions at store location; (c) rainy season daily protocol requires full tarp deployment regardless of morning weather (June–November); (d) dry season: deploy only if >40% rain probability; (e) typhoon signal raised: activate W576 full emergency protocol in addition to yard protection | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 5 min |
| 2 | **Tarp & Cover Deployment**: Stock Associate deploys protection: (a) heavy-duty woven polyethylene tarps (6m × 10m, UV-treated, replace annually) placed over exposed lumber stacks; (b) tarps secured with bungee cords and concrete blocks at corners; (c) cement bags covered with plastic sheeting or moved under covered area; (d) plywood stacks covered on top and sides facing prevailing wind direction; (e) steel rebar bundles do not require covering but should be elevated on dunnage (wooden pallets/blocks) to prevent ground moisture wicking; (f) roofing GI sheets checked for proper stacking (flat, not leaning) to prevent wind damage and water pooling | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 20–30 min |
| 3 | **Post-Rain Inspection & Damage Assessment**: After rain event: (a) remove tarps and inspect lumber for water absorption (surface wet vs. penetrated); (b) separate any warped, swollen, or water-stained pieces; (c) wet lumber stacked with spacers (stickers) between layers for air drying; (d) water-damaged cement bags inspected — hardened lumps mean bag is unsellable and must be dispositioned per W91 (damaged goods); (e) damaged pieces tagged with markdown label per W549 (damaged/open-box item processing); (f) system inventory adjustment initiated per W92 if product is unsalvageable | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 15–20 min |
| 4 | **Inventory Damage Reporting & Markdown**: (a) Stock Associate logs damaged items in ERP: item, quantity, damage type (water warp, cement hardened, mold, rust); (b) system auto-applies markdown based on damage severity: 10–20% for surface water marks on lumber, 30–50% for warped/cupped boards, 100% write-off for hardened cement; (c) markdown items moved to designated clearance area in lumber yard; (d) total damage value tracked per store per weather event for insurance claim documentation (W858) and shrinkage reporting (W845) | Stock Associate / System | Store Manager | 10 min |
| 5 | **Tarp Maintenance & Replacement Tracking**: (a) tarps inspected weekly for tears, UV degradation, and grommet damage; (b) damaged tarps replaced from store maintenance supply; (c) tarp replacement tracked in facility maintenance module (W47) as consumable expense; (d) annual tarp budget: ~PHP 15,000–25,000 per store (10–15 tarps × PHP 1,500–2,000 each) | Stock Associate | Store Manager | 5 min/week |

### System Touchpoints
- PAGASA weather API integration (external data feed)
- Store manager dashboard — weather alert notifications
- ERP inventory module — damage reporting and markdown processing (W93, W549)
- Inventory adjustment workflow (W92) — write-off authorization
- Facility maintenance work order module (W47) — tarp replacement
- Shrinkage analytics dashboard (W845) — weather-related damage tracking
- Insurance claim documentation module (W858)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Sudden afternoon thunderstorms**: Philippine weather can change rapidly; even during dry season, sudden downpours can occur with < 30 minutes warning
- **Tarp theft**: Tarps and bungee cords are themselves retail merchandise; loss prevention must monitor
- **Ventilation vs. protection trade-off**: Completely sealed tarps cause condensation and mold growth in humid Philippine climate; tarps must allow some airflow
- **Manual labor intensity**: Deploying and removing tarps for 4,900 lumber SKUs across a 500–1,000 sqm yard takes 20–30 minutes per cycle; requires dedicated staff time during peak rain periods
- **Seasonal labor**: During rainy season peak, stores may need additional temporary stock associate support for yard protection duties

### Staffing Implication
- **Stock Associate (Lumber Yard)**: Daily tarp deployment/removal adds 40–60 minutes/day during rainy season (~5 hours/week) — absorbed by existing 4 Stock Associates per store with seasonal prioritization
- **No permanent incremental headcount** — damage reduction savings (estimated PHP 30,000–50,000/store/month during rainy season) far exceed labor cost

### Time Estimate
- Weather check: 5 min
- Tarp deployment: 20–30 min
- Post-rain inspection: 15–20 min
- Damage reporting: 10 min
- **Total per event**: 50–65 min (rainy season daily); 30–40 min (dry season as-needed)

## W1104. Customer Tool Rental Return Damage Assessment & Billing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer returns a rented tool or equipment at the store customer service counter |
| **Frequency** | ~3,000–4,000 rental returns/month across all stores (complement to W139 tool rental operations) |
| **Volume** | 1–3 tools per return transaction |
| **Owner** | Customer Service Representative |
| **Participants** | Customer Service Representative, Department Supervisor (Tools), Customer |

### Background

BuildRight Depot operates a tool and equipment rental service (W139) allowing customers to rent power tools (drills, grinders, circular saws, jackhammers), specialty equipment (tile cutters, concrete mixers, scaffolding, pressure washers), and commercial-grade items not typically owned by DIY customers. The rental program is a differentiator for the trade/professional customer segment (30% of revenue) and drives foot traffic from contractors who also purchase consumables and materials during rental visits. Tool rental returns require a structured damage assessment process to: (a) determine if the tool is returned in the same condition as issued (normal wear vs. damage); (b) assess additional charges for damage beyond normal wear; (c) process the security deposit refund or partial charge; (d) determine if the tool needs repair, cleaning, or recalibration before re-rental; (e) update the tool's maintenance history in the rental asset tracking system. This workflow specifically covers the return and damage assessment process; the initial rental issuance and overall rental operations are covered under W139.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rental Record Retrieval & Pre-Return Verification**: (a) Customer Service Representative (CSR) scans the customer's rental agreement barcode or looks up by loyalty card/phone number; (b) system displays: tool ID, tool description, rental start date/time, agreed return date/time, rental rate, security deposit amount, pre-rental condition notes, and photos of tool at issuance; (c) CSR verifies customer identity (government ID matching rental agreement); (d) system auto-calculates elapsed rental time and any overdue charges: overdue rate = 1.5× standard daily rate per day late | Customer Service Representative | Customer Service Representative | 3–5 min |
| 2 | **Physical Tool Inspection & Condition Comparison**: CSR inspects returned tool against pre-rental condition: (a) visual inspection for new scratches, dents, cracks, or missing parts; (b) functional test: power on, run for 30 seconds, check for unusual noise or vibration; (c) accessory check: verify all accessories listed at issuance are returned (blades, bits, chargers, batteries, cables, manuals, carrying case); (d) cleanliness check: tool should be returned free of excessive concrete dust, paint, or mud (reasonable use debris is acceptable); (e) for electrical tools: inspect cord for cuts, check plug integrity; (f) for gas-powered equipment: check fuel level (should be returned with minimum fuel), oil condition; (g) for scaffolding: verify all pins, braces, and platforms are present and undamaged; (h) CSR photographs any damage or missing items using store tablet | Customer Service Representative | Department Supervisor (Tools) | 5–10 min |
| 3 | **Damage Classification & Assessment**: CSR classifies tool condition: (a) **Normal Wear** — minor cosmetic scratches, expected consumable wear (blade dulling, brush wear), dust/debris from normal use → no additional charge; (b) **Minor Damage** — small dent, scuffed housing, missing low-value accessory (screw, washer) → flat PHP 150–500 cleaning/minor repair surcharge; (c) **Moderate Damage** — cracked housing, damaged cord, bent blade, broken accessory → repair cost estimate generated by system based on tool type and damage code, typically PHP 500–2,000; (d) **Major Damage** — motor burnout, cracked frame, missing major component (battery, blade guard) → full repair or replacement cost, capped at the tool's current replacement value; (e) **Total Loss** — tool returned non-functional, submerged in water, dropped from height, or stolen → security deposit forfeited + additional charge up to replacement value per rental agreement terms. For categories (c)–(e), Department Supervisor is called for visual confirmation and approval | Customer Service Representative | Department Supervisor (Tools) | 5–8 min |
| 4 | **Security Deposit Settlement & Additional Billing**: (a) system calculates final bill: base rental charge + overdue charges (if any) + damage surcharge (if any) - security deposit held; (b) if net amount is positive (customer owes more): customer pays balance via POS (W5B); (c) if net amount is negative (deposit exceeds charges): refund processed to original payment method per W101 (customer refund processing), or issued as store credit per W781; (d) system generates itemized rental return receipt with condition assessment details; (e) customer signs digital acknowledgment of return condition; (f) if customer disputes damage assessment, escalate to Store Manager per dispute resolution hierarchy | Customer Service Representative / POS | Department Supervisor (Tools) | 5–7 min |
| 5 | **Post-Return Tool Reconditioning & Availability Update**: (a) tool placed in designated "inspection/reconditioning" area in backroom; (b) if **Normal Wear**: CSR cleans tool (compressed air, wipe down), functional test confirmation, return to rental shelf; system updates tool status to "Available"; (c) if **Minor/Moderate Damage**: tool sent to in-store repair station (W440 — power tool service & repair center) with damage assessment form; system updates status to "In Repair" with estimated availability date; (d) if **Major Damage / Total Loss**: tool dispositioned per W91 (damaged goods disposition) if uneconomical to repair, or sent to vendor for factory refurbishment; insurance claim filed per W859 (vehicle & fleet insurance) if rental tool is covered under rental equipment policy; (e) system tracks repair turnaround time and updates rental availability calendar | Stock Associate / CSR | Department Supervisor (Tools) | 10–20 min (for normal wear); varies for repair |

### System Touchpoints
- Tool rental management module — agreement lookup, inventory status, availability calendar
- Pre-rental condition photo repository (linked to tool asset ID)
- POS module (W5B) — rental return billing and deposit settlement
- Customer refund processing (W101) — deposit refund
- Store credit issuance (W781) — credit in lieu of cash refund
- Power tool repair center module (W440) — repair work order creation
- Damaged goods disposition (W91) — write-off for total loss
- Fixed asset tracking — rental tool depreciation and lifecycle (W39)
- Customer loyalty integration — rental history tracked for tier benefits

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Subjective damage assessment**: "Normal wear" vs. "minor damage" can be subjective; photography at issuance and return is critical evidence but adds processing time
- **Customer disputes**: Contractors who use tools heavily may contest damage charges; clear rental agreement terms and photographic evidence are essential
- **Rental tool downtime**: Damaged tools in repair reduce available rental fleet, potentially losing rental revenue; a 5–10% fleet buffer is recommended
- **Hygiene concerns**: Returned tools that have been used in construction sites (concrete dust, paint, adhesives) require thorough cleaning before re-rental
- **Liability for tool malfunction**: If a tool is returned damaged but not flagged, and subsequently rented to another customer who is injured, BuildRight faces liability exposure

### Staffing Implication
- **Customer Service Representative**: Tool return processing adds 15–30 minutes per return; at ~15–20 returns/store/week, this represents ~4–6 hours/week — absorbed by existing CSR (1 per store)
- **Department Supervisor (Tools)**: Damage escalation review adds ~1–2 hours/week during peak rental periods
- **No permanent incremental headcount** — damage surcharge revenue (estimated PHP 100,000–200,000/month chain-wide) more than covers processing labor

### Time Estimate
- Record retrieval: 3–5 min
- Physical inspection: 5–10 min
- Damage classification: 5–8 min
- Billing settlement: 5–7 min
- Tool reconditioning: 10–20 min (normal wear)
- **Total per return**: 28–50 min

## W1105. Vendor Early Payment Discount Capture & Dynamic Discounting Optimization

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | AP payment run execution (W556), or vendor invoice with early payment discount terms received, or weekly treasury cash surplus review (W589) |
| **Frequency** | Weekly (during AP payment run planning); continuous (real-time discount opportunity alerts) |
| **Volume** | ~1,200–1,600 vendor invoices reviewed per payment run for discount capture opportunity |
| **Owner** | AP Manager (Finance) |
| **Participants** | AP Manager, Treasury Analyst, VP Finance / CFO (approval threshold) |

### Background

Philippine retail suppliers frequently offer early payment discounts (commonly 2/10 Net 30, 1/15 Net 30, or 2/10 Net 60 for imports) as an incentive for faster payment. With BuildRight's monthly AP volume of ~8,500–9,500 invoices and ~PHP 3.5–3.75 billion in monthly COGS-related payables, even a 1% discount captured on a fraction of eligible invoices represents significant savings. For example, capturing 2% early payment discount on just 30% of eligible invoices (assuming PHP 2B eligible) yields ~PHP 12M/month or ~PHP 144M/year in cost savings. Additionally, BuildRight's treasury function can offer dynamic discounting — proposing early payment to vendors in exchange for a sliding-scale discount — leveraging the company's strong cash position (PHP 62.3B annual revenue, 12–14% EBITDA margin). This workflow manages the identification, approval, execution, and tracking of early payment discount capture across the vendor base, complementing W556 (AP payment run) which handles the mechanical payment processing, and W324 (supply chain finance & dynamic discounting) which manages the broader program design.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Invoice Discount Terms Extraction & Opportunity Identification**: (a) during weekly AP payment run preparation, system scans all approved and unmatched invoices for early payment discount terms; (b) system extracts: vendor ID, invoice number, invoice amount, invoice date, discount terms (e.g., "2/10 Net 30" = 2% discount if paid within 10 days, full amount due in 30 days), discount eligibility deadline date, net due date; (c) system calculates: discount amount available (invoice amount × discount %), days until discount deadline, effective annualized return of taking the discount (e.g., 2/10 Net 30 = 36.7% annualized return); (d) system prioritizes invoices by: (i) highest effective annualized return, (ii) largest absolute discount amount, (iii) discount deadline approaching (< 3 days); (e) system flags invoices where taking the discount exceeds the company's minimum threshold for early payment (currently set at 12% annualized return, exceeding BuildRight's weighted average cost of capital) | System | AP Manager | automated |
| 2 | **Cash Position Validation & Payment Capacity Check**: (a) Treasury Analyst reviews current cash position from W589 (weekly cash flow forecast); (b) system displays: available cash for early payments = total cash balance − upcoming committed payments (payroll, tax remittances, rent, Capex scheduled payments) − minimum cash reserve threshold (set by treasury policy); (c) system ranks discount opportunities that pass the minimum threshold and calculates total early payment amount required; (d) if total early payment required exceeds available cash, system recommends prioritization: highest annualized return first, then largest absolute discount, until cash is fully allocated; (e) Treasury Analyst confirms available cash and approves the early payment budget for the week | Treasury Analyst | VP Finance | 15–30 min |
| 3 | **Dynamic Discounting Offer Generation (Vendor Opt-In Program)**: (a) for vendors enrolled in the dynamic discounting program (W324), system generates early payment offers: (i) for invoices with standard Net 30 terms (no built-in discount), system offers to pay in 10 days in exchange for a negotiated discount (typically 1.0–1.5%); (ii) for invoices with existing discount terms (e.g., 2/10 Net 30), system may offer to pay even earlier (e.g., Day 5) for an enhanced discount (e.g., 2.5%); (b) offers transmitted to vendors via vendor portal (W866) or email; (c) vendors accept/reject within 24 hours; (d) accepted dynamic discounts are added to the payment run | System / AP Manager | AP Manager | 10–15 min |
| 4 | **Early Payment Execution & Discount Capture**: (a) AP Manager adds discount-eligible invoices and dynamic discounting offers to the current week's AP payment run (W556); (b) system calculates payment amount = invoice amount − discount amount; (c) payment method selected per vendor preference: bank transfer (BDO, BPI, Metrobank, Chinabank), PDC (W424), or check; (d) system records discount taken in AP subledger: debit AP (full invoice amount), credit Cash (payment amount), credit Purchase Discount (discount amount); (e) discount captured tracked per vendor per month for vendor scorecard (W44, W706) | AP Manager / System | VP Finance (if > PHP 5M single vendor early payment) | 20–30 min |
| 5 | **Monthly Discount Capture Reporting & Program Optimization**: (a) system generates monthly discount capture report: (i) total invoices eligible for discount, (ii) total discounts captured (amount and count), (iii) total discounts missed (amount and count) with reason code (insufficient cash, processing delay, vendor dispute), (iv) effective annualized return on early payments, (v) dynamic discounting program participation rate, (vi) top 20 vendors by discount value; (b) AP Manager and Treasury Analyst review missed discount opportunities and root causes; (c) process improvements identified: (i) invoice processing speed improvement to capture more invoices within discount windows, (ii) vendor negotiation for better discount terms during contract renewal, (iii) cash reserve threshold adjustment; (d) report submitted to VP Finance for quarterly program review | System / AP Manager | VP Finance / CFO | 1–2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- AP invoice processing module (W7) — discount terms extraction from invoice OCR/manual entry
- AP payment run module (W556) — early payment inclusion
- Cash flow forecast module (W589, W233) — available cash validation
- Treasury management system — cash position, bank balances
- Vendor portal (W866) — dynamic discounting offer delivery and acceptance
- GL posting — purchase discount accounting entries
- Vendor scorecard module (W44, W706) — discount capture rate as vendor KPI
- Supply chain finance program module (W324) — dynamic discounting enrollment
- BI analytics dashboard (W879, W880) — discount capture reporting

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Invoice processing latency**: If invoices take 5–7 days to process through 3-way match (W7), the 10-day discount window is already half gone; AP processing speed is critical
- **Cash flow timing mismatch**: Early payments reduce cash available for other obligations; accurate cash forecasting (W233) is essential to avoid liquidity crunches
- **Vendor discount term non-compliance**: Some vendors may not honor stated discount terms if payment is made on Day 9 or Day 10; system must track actual discount realized vs. offered
- **Dynamic discounting vendor resistance**: Smaller Philippine vendors may prefer full payment at Net 30 over discounted early payment due to their own cash flow constraints
- **Manual discount identification**: If vendor discount terms are not consistently captured in the invoice master during AP processing, opportunities are missed entirely

### Staffing Implication
- **AP Manager**: Weekly discount review adds ~2–3 hours/week to payment run planning
- **Treasury Analyst**: Cash position validation adds ~30 min/week
- **No permanent incremental headcount** — savings from discount capture far exceed labor cost

### Time Estimate
- Discount opportunity identification: automated
- Cash position validation: 15–30 min/week
- Dynamic discounting offers: 10–15 min/week
- Payment execution: 20–30 min/week
- Monthly reporting: 1–2 hours/month
- **Total per week**: ~3–5 hours (AP Manager + Treasury Analyst combined)

## W1106. Store-Level Community Building Materials Donation & LGU Partnership

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | LGU (barangay/municipality/city) formal donation request, community disaster relief event (W428), annual CSR budget allocation, or corporate social responsibility initiative activation |
| **Frequency** | ~2–5 donation requests per store per month; ~400–1,000 total/month across chain |
| **Volume** | 1 donation request per event |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Department Supervisor (Lumber/Building Materials), CMO (Marketing/CSR), VP Legal & Compliance, Receiving Clerk |

### Background

BuildRight Depot, as the largest hardware and home improvement retailer in its Philippine market, regularly receives requests from LGUs (barangays, municipalities, cities), public schools, churches, and community organizations for building material donations. These requests range from: (a) small barangay requests — 10 bags of cement and 20 hollow blocks for a community basketball court repair; (b) medium school/hospital requests — paint, lumber, and electrical supplies for classroom renovation or barangay health center improvement; (c) large disaster relief donations — roofing sheets, nails, lumber, and cement for typhoon-affected families (complementing W428); (d) annual CSR commitments — corporate pledged donations to partner communities. These donations serve dual purposes: community goodwill (essential for store operations in Philippine communities where barangay captain and local government support is critical for business permits, traffic management, and security) and brand visibility. However, donations must be properly controlled to prevent abuse, ensure equitable distribution, maintain inventory accuracy, and comply with tax requirements (BIR donation documentation, tax deductibility). This workflow manages the end-to-end donation process from request to delivery, complementing W444 (community solicitation & donation processing) which handles corporate-level donation policy, and W135 (CSR program execution) which manages the marketing and reporting aspects.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Donation Request Receipt & Validation**: (a) Store Manager receives donation request from LGU/community organization — format may be formal letter (with LGU letterhead and barangay captain signature), email, or in-person request; (b) requestor must provide: requesting organization name, authorized signatory, purpose of donation, specific materials and quantities requested, target completion date, and benefit to community; (c) Store Manager validates: (i) request is from a legitimate organization (LGU, registered NGO, public school, religious institution with SEC registration); (ii) request is within BuildRight's CSR focus areas (community infrastructure, education, disaster relief, environmental sustainability); (iii) request does not involve political campaign materials or partisan activities; (iv) requesting entity has not received a donation from the same store in the past 6 months (to prevent repeated requests from the same barangay) | Store Manager | Store Manager | 15–30 min |
| 2 | **Donation Value Assessment & Approval Routing**: (a) Store Manager assesses donation value at SRP (standard retail price): (i) **< PHP 5,000**: Store Manager approval authority; items from SLOB/clearance inventory preferred (W220); (ii) **PHP 5,001–25,000**: Regional Manager approval required; (iii) **PHP 25,001–100,000**: VP Legal & CMO approval required; (iv) **> PHP 100,000**: CEO/CFO approval required via corporate CSR committee; (b) system checks inventory availability at store and nearby stores; (c) preference given to: (i) SLOB/clearance items that would otherwise be written off, (ii) slow-moving seasonal items, (iii) display/demo units; (d) Store Manager prepares donation authorization form in ERP with: requesting entity details, items and quantities, estimated value, justification, and proposed delivery method | Store Manager | Approver per threshold | 20–30 min |
| 3 | **Inventory Deduction & Donation Documentation**: (a) upon approval, system generates a zero-value sales order (donation order type) with special pricing = PHP 0.00; (b) inventory deducted from store stock at WAC (weighted average cost); (c) system generates: (i) donation delivery receipt (DDR) — itemized list of donated materials with quantities and SRP values; (ii) BIR-compliant donation certificate — for tax deductibility, includes BuildRight's TIN, donee's TIN, description and fair market value of goods; (iii) acknowledgment receipt — for donee's signature upon receipt; (d) cost of donated goods recorded as CSR expense in GL: debit CSR Expense, credit Inventory; (e) for tax purposes: donation expense is deductible up to a certain percentage of taxable income per NIRC (National Internal Revenue Code); Finance tracks cumulative annual donation expense vs. cap | System / Store Manager | Store Manager | 15–20 min |
| 4 | **Donation Staging & Handover**: (a) Stock Associate picks donated items from inventory and stages at receiving dock; (b) Receiving Clerk loads donated materials onto requestor's vehicle or BuildRight delivery truck (if delivery is approved); (c) requestor's authorized representative signs acknowledgment receipt and presents valid ID; (d) photos taken of donated materials and handover for CSR documentation and social media (coordinated with Marketing per W135); (e) Store Manager sends handover confirmation to regional office | Stock Associate / Receiving Clerk | Store Manager | 20–45 min |
| 5 | **Post-Donation Reporting & Community Impact Tracking**: (a) Store Manager logs donation completion in CSR module with photos and acknowledgment receipt scan; (b) monthly CSR donation report generated per store and consolidated at corporate: total donations by value, by category, by beneficiary type; (c) Marketing team (W135) follows up with beneficiary for community impact story and social media content; (d) annual CSR report (W694) includes donation statistics; (e) LGU relationship management: Store Manager follows up with barangay captain to ensure materials were used as intended and maintains positive relationship for business permit renewal (W54) and community support | Store Manager / Marketing | CMO | 15 min per donation |

### System Touchpoints
- Zero-value donation order type in ERP (special sales order with zero pricing)

### Background

BuildRight Depot stores receive 2–3 trucks per week of heavy building materials (cement bags, hollow blocks, rebar bundles, plywood stacks, tile pallets) and load customer vehicles in the lumber yard. Each store operates 1 forklift (primarily counterbalance, LPG or electric) for yard operations. ## W1109. Customer Project Material Surplus Buy-Back & Recycling Program

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer brings unused/surplus building materials to store for buy-back, or customer requests a project completion surplus pickup |

### Background

Filipino contractors and DIY homeowners frequently end up with surplus building materials after project completion: unused cement bags (leftover from a pour), extra tiles (bought 10% extra for wastage but didn't use all), unopened paint cans, extra lumber pieces, surplus electrical wire, and leftover plumbing fittings. In the Philippines, the common practice is to either: (a) store the surplus indefinitely (cluttering homes and job sites), (b) sell to neighbors at a discount, or (c) discard as construction waste. BuildRight Depot's surplus buy-back program creates a structured channel for customers to return unused materials for a partial refund or store credit, which BuildRight can then resell as open-box/discounted items (per W549) or return to vendors (per W88). This program: (i) drives customer loyalty by reducing the perceived risk of over-buying; (ii) generates foot traffic as customers return to the store for the buy-back and often purchase additional items; (iii) provides a source of discounted inventory for budget-conscious customers; (iv) supports sustainability goals by reducing construction waste. This workflow is distinct from W12 (returns & exchanges) which handles defective or unwanted items within the standard return window; this workflow specifically handles post-project surplus materials that may be beyond the standard return period but are in resalable condition.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Surplus Material Intake & Eligibility Check**: (a) Customer brings surplus materials to the customer service counter; (b) CSR verifies eligibility: (i) items must be BuildRight-branded or identifiable as BuildRight inventory (receipt, loyalty account purchase history, or SKU barcode scan); (ii) items must be in resalable condition: unopened packaging (cement bags intact, tile boxes sealed, paint cans unopened) or minimally used and clean (lumber without damage, full-length); (iii) items must not be hazardous materials requiring special handling (paint/chemicals must be unopened); (iv) items must be in the current active catalog (not discontinued); (c) system looks up original purchase price from customer's transaction history; (d) items not meeting eligibility criteria are declined with explanation and disposal guidance offered per W502 (non-hazardous waste management) | Customer Service Representative | Customer Service Representative | 5–8 min |

## W1110. Vendor-Managed Inventory (VMI) Seasonal Ramp-Up & Wind-Down

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Seasonal calendar event trigger (W306), S&OP seasonal planning cycle (W133), or vendor-initiated seasonal forecast discussion |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly (aligned with Philippine seasonal patterns); ad-hoc for weather-driven events |
| **Volume** | 12 VMI vendors × 300 SKUs × 4 seasonal transitions = ~14,400 SKU-level parameter adjustments per year |
| **Owner** | Merchandise Planner (Supply Chain) |
| **Participants** | Merchandise Planner, Category Manager, VMI Vendor Account Manager, DC Operations Manager |

### Background

BuildRight Depot operates a Vendor-Managed Inventory (VMI) program with 12 key vendors covering ~300 SKUs (per model company profile, Section 6.5). VMI vendors are responsible for monitoring BuildRight's inventory levels and generating replenishment orders within agreed parameters (min/max, reorder point, order quantity). However, the Philippine retail environment has distinct seasonal demand patterns that require periodic adjustment of VMI parameters: (a) **Dry season (Jan–May)**: High demand for garden/outdoor, aircon, water tanks, paint, construction materials (peak building season); (b) **Rainy season (Jun–Oct)**: High demand for waterproofing, flood control items, tarps, umbrellas, plumbing repairs; low demand for garden/outdoor; (c) **Ber months / holiday (Sep–Dec)**: High demand for Christmas lights, gift items, appliances, home décor; (d) **Summer build-out (Mar–May)**: Schools and government buildings undergo renovation, driving institutional B2B demand. Without seasonal parameter adjustments, VMI systems either: (i) overstock during low-demand periods (tying up working capital and warehouse space) or (ii) understock during high-demand periods (causing lost sales and customer dissatisfaction). This workflow manages the quarterly transition of VMI parameters, complementing W20 (VMI base operations), W621 (VMI daily monitoring), and W672 (VMI quarterly business review). While W672 reviews overall program health, this workflow specifically addresses the seasonal demand parameter reconfiguration.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Seasonal Demand Forecast Preparation**: (a) Merchandise Planner prepares seasonal demand forecast per VMI vendor and SKU: (i) pull 3-year historical sales data by month for each VMI SKU; (ii) apply seasonal index factors from demand planning module (W31): e.g., waterproofing items have a 3.0× seasonal index in Jun–Aug vs. annual average; aircon has a 2.5× index in Mar–May; (iii) adjust for known events: upcoming typhoon forecast (PAGASA), government infrastructure project pipeline, new store openings; (iv) calculate target inventory levels for the upcoming season: target weeks of supply = base weeks × seasonal multiplier; (b) Planner prepares seasonal parameter change proposal: current parameters → proposed parameters for each SKU (min/max/ROP/EOQ), expected impact on inventory value and service level | Merchandise Planner | Category Manager | 4–6 hours/quarter |
| 2 | **VMI Vendor Collaborative Planning Session**: (a) Merchandise Planner and Category Manager meet with each VMI vendor's account team (video call or in-person at HQ Davao): (i) present seasonal demand forecast and proposed parameter changes; (ii) vendor shares their production capacity constraints, lead time changes, and any supply-side risks; (iii) jointly agree on: seasonal safety stock levels, peak demand replenishment frequency (e.g., from weekly to twice-weekly deliveries), allocation priorities if supply is constrained; (iv) document agreed changes in VMI seasonal addendum to the vendor agreement; (b) for seasonal ramp-up: vendor commits to building buffer stock in their warehouse 4–6 weeks before season starts; (c) for seasonal wind-down: vendor agrees to reduce replenishment quantities 4 weeks before season ends to prevent excess inventory carry-over | Merchandise Planner / Category Manager | Category Manager | 1–2 hours per vendor × 12 vendors = 12–24 hours/quarter |

## W1112. Store-Level Inventory Age-Based Auto-Markdown & Clearance Trigger

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Nightly batch job (inventory aging report), or manual review triggered by category manager |
| **Frequency** | Nightly (automated batch); weekly review by Department Supervisor |
| **Volume** | 35,000 active SKUs × 200 stores = 7,000,000 SKU-location combinations checked nightly |
| **Owner** | Department Supervisor (store-level execution) |
| **Participants** | Department Supervisor, Stock Associate, Store Manager, Category Manager (pricing approval) |

### Background

BuildRight Depot maintains 35,000 active SKUs across 200 stores, with an inventory turns target of 6–8x per year and a shrinkage target of < 1.5%. Despite replenishment optimization (W4, W2A) and SLOB management (W220), a significant portion of inventory ages beyond healthy thresholds due to: (a) seasonal items that didn't sell through; (b) discontinued items pending full phase-out; (c) localized slow movers specific to a store's demographics (e.g., snow-related items in Mindanao stores, or high-end power tools in provincial locations); (d) overstocks from cancelled projects; (e) display/demo units beyond their useful life. Currently, markdown decisions are reactive — Department Supervisors manually identify slow movers during weekly reviews. An age-based auto-markdown system applies progressive markdowns based on inventory age, ensuring timely price reductions that maximize recovery value before items become unsalable. This complements W93 (markdown & clearance pricing) which handles strategic markdowns, and W220 (SLOB provisioning) which handles accounting provisions. This workflow focuses on the automated trigger and execution mechanism for age-based markdowns at the store level.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Nightly Inventory Aging Analysis**: (a) nightly batch job runs against all inventory records: for each SKU at each location, calculates days since last receipt (age of oldest unit in stock), days since last sale (sell-through velocity), and total on-hand quantity; (b) system classifies each SKU-location into aging buckets: **Fresh** (0–60 days) — no action; **Maturing** (61–90 days) — monitor list; **Aging** (91–120 days) — 1st markdown trigger; **Old** (121–180 days) — 2nd markdown trigger; **Stale** (181–270 days) — 3rd markdown trigger; **Dead** (271+ days) — clearance/write-off trigger; (c) system adjusts thresholds by ABC class: A-items (fast movers) trigger markdowns earlier (60/90/120/150/180 days), C-items (slow movers) get longer grace periods (120/180/270/360/400 days); (d) seasonal items have separate aging profiles based on seasonal calendar (W306) — e.g., Christmas items that age past January 15 trigger accelerated markdown regardless of standard thresholds | System | automated | nightly batch |
| 2 | **Auto-Markdown Calculation & Notification**: (a) for items triggering a markdown threshold, system calculates new price: (i) 1st markdown (Aging): 15% off current price; (ii) 2nd markdown (Old): 30% off current price; (iii) 3rd markdown (Stale): 50% off current price; (iv) Dead: 70% off or write-off (if below cost); (b) system generates daily markdown notification list per store, distributed to Department Supervisor's tablet and Store Manager's dashboard; (c) list includes: item description, current price, proposed markdown price, markdown %, current on-hand, days since last sale, and estimated markdown value impact; (d) Department Supervisor reviews the list: (i) approve — system auto-updates price; (ii) defer — item placed on 7-day hold with reason code (e.g., "seasonal item expected to sell in upcoming promotion"); (iii) escalate to Category Manager — for items where markdown may conflict with upcoming vendor-funded promotions (W513) or contractual minimum price requirements | System / Department Supervisor | Department Supervisor (≤ 30% markdown); Store Manager (> 30% markdown) | 15–30 min/day |
| 3 | **Price Update Execution & Shelf Label Reprint**: (a) upon approval, system updates the item's price in the pricing master for that store location; (b) system generates shelf label reprint batch for all approved markdown items; (c) Stock Associate prints new shelf labels from store label printer and replaces old labels on shelf; (d) POS system automatically picks up new prices (real-time event streaming per W533); (e) markdown items tagged with clearance flag in POS — visible on receipt as "CLEARANCE — No Returns" per W532 (clearance & final sale processing); (f) system tracks markdown items on e-commerce catalog with updated pricing and "Clearance" badge | System / Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 20–30 min/day for label updates |
| 4 | **Clearance Event Coordination**: (a) when items reach the "Stale" or "Dead" bucket across multiple stores, system aggregates and recommends a coordinated clearance event: (i) if ≥ 5 stores have the same SKU in Stale/Dead status, system proposes a "Chain-Wide Clearance" promotion; (ii) Category Manager reviews and approves chain-wide clearance pricing; (iii) Marketing notified for promotional signage and social media announcement (W83); (b) for items in "Dead" status with quantity < 5 units across the chain, system recommends: (i) consolidation to a single store via inter-store transfer (W22) for efficient clearance, or (ii) write-off and disposition per W91 (damaged goods) if below recovery threshold | System / Category Manager | Category Manager | 1–2 hours/week |
| 5 | **Monthly Markdown Effectiveness Report**: (a) system generates monthly markdown effectiveness report: (i) total markdown value by aging bucket, category, and store; (ii) sell-through rate post-markdown (target: ≥ 60% of markdown units sold within 30 days); (iii) recovery rate: markdown revenue / original inventory cost; (iv) items requiring multiple markdown rounds (indicating initial markdown was insufficient); (v) dead stock write-off value; (b) report informs Category Manager's assortment rationalization decisions (W679) and buyer replenishment parameter tuning; (c) financial provision for markdown liability adjusted in GL per W220 (SLOB provisioning) | System / Finance | Category Manager / VP Finance | automated monthly |

### System Touchpoints
- Inventory aging module — nightly batch job, aging bucket classification
- Pricing module — auto-markdown price calculation, store-level price updates
- POS pricing sync (W533) — real-time price change propagation
- Shelf label printing module — batch label reprint
- E-commerce catalog (W180) — clearance pricing and badge
- Inter-store transfer module (W22) — clearance consolidation
- SLOB provisioning module (W220) — financial provision
- Damaged goods disposition (W91) — dead stock write-off
- Category performance reporting (W101) — markdown effectiveness
- BI analytics dashboard (W879) — daily markdown list distribution

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Markdown racing**: If auto-markdowns are too aggressive, customers may learn to wait for markdowns rather than purchase at full price, eroding margins on slow-but-selling items
- **Shelf label accuracy**: If Stock Associates do not promptly replace shelf labels after auto-markdown, customers see a different price at POS than on shelf — a Consumer Act (RA 7394) violation
- **Vendor price protection conflict**: If a vendor has agreed to fund markdowns (W161) but the auto-markdown system triggers before vendor approval is obtained, BuildRight may absorb the markdown cost instead of the vendor
- **Category manager override fatigue**: If the system generates too many notifications, Department Supervisors may auto-approve without proper review, leading to unnecessary markdowns
- **E-commerce price parity**: Markdown items must be reflected online simultaneously to avoid customer complaints about price discrepancies between online and in-store

### Staffing Implication
- **Department Supervisor**: Daily markdown review adds 15–30 minutes/day — absorbed within existing role
- **Stock Associate**: Shelf label updates add 20–30 minutes/day — absorbed by existing 4 Stock Associates
- **Category Manager**: Weekly clearance coordination adds 1–2 hours/week
- **No permanent incremental headcount** — the system automates what was previously a manual, reactive process

### Time Estimate
- Nightly batch: automated
- Markdown review: 15–30 min/day
- Label updates: 20–30 min/day
- Clearance coordination: 1–2 hours/week
- **Total per store per week**: ~4–6 hours (Department Supervisor + Stock Associate combined)

### Background

Gift cards are an important revenue and customer acquisition channel for retail. ## W1115. Vendor Performance-Based Shelf Space Allocation Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Semi-annual planogram review cycle, vendor scorecard quarterly publication (W871), or category performance review (W101) |
| **Frequency** | Semi-annual (major review); quarterly (minor adjustments) |
| **Volume** | ~800–1,000 active vendors × 12 product categories = ~2,000–3,000 vendor-category combinations reviewed per cycle |
| **Owner** | Category Manager |
| **Participants** | Category Manager, Planogram Analyst, Merchandise Planner, VP Merchandising |

### Background

BuildRight Depot operates big-box stores with 8,000–15,000 sqm of selling area and 35,000 active SKUs. Shelf space is the store's most valuable asset — every linear meter of shelving, every endcap, every promotional display area must generate maximum revenue per sqm. Currently, shelf space allocation decisions are made during annual planogram reviews (W86 — planogram compliance) and promotional planning (W13), but there is no systematic mechanism to reallocate shelf space from underperforming vendors to high-performing ones. In a vendor performance-based shelf space allocation model: (a) vendors are scored on a composite index including: sales velocity (units/week per facing), gross margin contribution, fill rate / in-stock rate, promotional support (co-op funding, demo participation), and consumer demand (market share trend); (b) high-scoring vendors earn premium shelf positions (eye-level, endcaps, near entrance) and additional facings; (c) low-scoring vendors face shelf space reduction, repositioning to less desirable locations, or potential delisting; (d) vendor scorecards are published on the vendor portal (W871), creating transparency and motivating vendor performance improvement. This approach is standard practice in global retail but requires systematic execution to avoid subjectivity and vendor relationship damage.

### Steps

Live plants are unique inventory because they are: (a) **perishable** — plants have a finite shelf life (7–30 days depending on species) and deteriorate visibly if not sold; (b) **maintenance-intensive** — plants require daily watering, sunlight exposure, and occasional pruning while on the sales floor; (c) **weather-dependent** — Philippine tropical climate means plants thrive in some seasons and struggle in others (excessive rain causes root rot; extreme heat causes wilting); (d) **locally sourced** — unlike most BuildRight inventory (sourced through DCs), live plants are typically sourced from local nurseries within the store's region, delivered direct-to-store by the nursery; (e) **high markdown risk** — unsold plants at end of shelf life must be heavily discounted or disposed of (composted). This workflow manages the unique lifecycle of live plant inventory at the store level, from delivery through daily maintenance to markdown/disposition. This complements W1010 (outdoor garden center weather protection) which covers physical infrastructure protection, and W587 (inventory obsolescence) which covers non-perishable obsolescence.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Seasonal Plant Assortment Planning & Ordering**: (a) Category Manager defines seasonal plant assortment: (i) **Dry/Summer (Mar–May)**: flowering annuals (zinnias, marigolds, cosmos), vegetables (tomato, chili pepper, eggplant seedlings), herbs (basil, mint, rosemary), lawn grass plugs; (ii) **Rainy Season (Jun–Oct)**: hardy ornamentals (croton, bougainvillea, palm varieties), indoor plants (pothos, sansevieria, aglaonema), ground covers; (iii) **Holiday (Nov–Dec)**: poinsettias, Christmas cactus, wreath materials; (iv) **Year-round**: succulents, cacti, orchids, bonsai; (b) Department Supervisor places order with local nursery supplier 1–2 weeks before planned display date; (c) order quantities based on: (i) historical sell-through rate by SKU by season; (ii) store's garden center display capacity (shelf/ground space); (iii) local demographic preferences (urban stores sell more indoor plants; rural stores sell more vegetables and fruit trees) | Department Supervisor / Category Manager | Category Manager | 1–2 hours/week during peak |
| 2 | **Live Plant Receiving & Quality Inspection**: (a) Stock Associate receives plant delivery from nursery supplier: (i) inspects each tray/pot for: healthy foliage (no yellowing, wilting, or pest damage), moist soil, intact root system (gently tug plant to verify root establishment), correct species/variety label; (ii) rejects substandard plants at receiving — driver returns rejected plants to nursery (no credit note needed as plants were not accepted); (iii) records receipt in ERP: item, quantity, supplier, date; (b) plants staged in garden center in designated display areas: (i) sun-loving plants in full sun area; (ii) shade-loving plants under canopy or shade cloth; (iii) indoor plants positioned near store entrance for visibility; (c) plant labels and price tags applied per W63 (shelf label & price tag distribution) | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 30–60 min per delivery |
| 3 | **Daily Plant Maintenance & Inventory Monitoring**: (a) Sales Associate performs daily garden center maintenance: (i) watering: early morning (6:00–7:00 AM) before store opens, adjusted for weather (extra watering during hot days, reduced during rainy periods); (ii) pruning: remove dead leaves/flowers, trim leggy growth; (iii) pest inspection: check for aphids, mealybugs, whiteflies; isolate and treat affected plants; (iv) rotation: move plants to ensure even sun exposure; (v) cleanup: remove fallen leaves, spilled soil, empty pots; (b) daily inventory scan: Associate scans plant barcode/QR during maintenance to update system on: (i) current on-hand quantity; (ii) plant condition (healthy, declining, distressed); (iii) days since receipt; (c) system alerts when plants approach end of shelf life: (i) 7 days before: "Monitor" alert; (ii) 3 days before: "Markdown recommended" alert; (iii) past shelf life: "Remove & compost" alert | Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 30–45 min/day |
| 4 | **Progressive Markdown & Clearance**: (a) system auto-triggers markdown for aging plants: (i) **7+ days since receipt**: 20% markdown — price tag updated, clearance label applied; (ii) **10+ days**: 40% markdown; (iii) **14+ days**: 60% markdown; (iv) **past shelf life (species-dependent, typically 14–21 days)**: remove from sales floor; (b) markdown plants moved to designated "Garden Clearance" rack for visibility; (c) all markdowns tracked in system for category-level margin analysis; (d) end-of-season clearance event (W93): remaining seasonal plants marked down 50–70% in a coordinated promotion | System / Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 15–20 min/day for markdowns |
| 5 | **Unsold Plant Disposition & Composting**: (a) plants past shelf life or unsellable condition (diseased, severely wilted) are removed from sales floor: (i) inventory write-off in system per W92 (inventory adjustment & shrinkage authorization); (ii) healthy plant waste composted on-site if store has space, or collected by organic waste hauler per W502; (iii) plastic pots and trays cleaned and returned to nursery supplier for reuse (sustainability initiative per W193); (b) write-off value tracked by Category Manager for: (i) supplier quality assessment (high write-off rate = poor nursery quality); (ii) assortment decision (high write-off variety = discontinue); (iii) seasonal demand calibration (adjust next season's order quantities); (c) monthly garden center shrinkage report generated per store and consolidated | Stock Associate / System | Department Supervisor | 15–20 min/disposition cycle |

### System Touchpoints
- Seasonal calendar master (W306) — seasonal assortment triggers
- Purchase order module — nursery supplier ordering
- Inventory receiving module — plant receipt and quality logging
- Plant-specific inventory aging — shelf life tracking by species
- Mobile scanning — daily inventory condition updates during maintenance
- Markdown module (W93) — progressive markdown automation
- Inventory adjustment module (W92) — write-off authorization
- Supplier scorecard (W44) — nursery quality assessment
- Category performance module (W101) — garden center margin analysis
- Sustainability module (W193) — pot return tracking, composting metrics

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Shrinkage rate**: Live plant shrinkage is significantly higher than general merchandise (target: < 1.5% overall); live plants typically see 15–25% shrinkage, requiring dedicated markdown and disposition management
- **Staff expertise gap**: Garden center maintenance requires basic horticultural knowledge (watering frequency by species, pest identification, sunlight requirements) that not all Sales Associates possess; training investment is needed
- **Weather impact**: A single severe typhoon can destroy the entire garden center inventory overnight; insurance coverage (W857) and rapid replenishment planning are essential
- **Nursery supplier reliability**: Local nurseries may have inconsistent supply or quality; building a panel of 2–3 nurseries per region provides backup
- **Customer handling damage**: Customers browsing plants may damage foliage or knock over pots; gentle display design and clear "Please handle with care" signage mitigate this

### Staffing Implication
- **Sales Associate (Garden Center)**: Daily maintenance adds 30–45 minutes/day; absorbed by existing Sales Associates with garden center assignment rotation
- **Department Supervisor**: Weekly ordering and markdown oversight adds 1–2 hours/week
- **No permanent incremental headcount** — garden center maintenance is part of existing Sales Associate duties

### Time Estimate
- Seasonal planning: 1–2 hours/week (Category Manager)
- Receiving: 30–60 min per delivery (weekly/bi-weekly)
- Daily maintenance: 30–45 min/day
- Markdown processing: 15–20 min/day
- Disposition: 15–20 min/cycle
- **Total per store per week**: ~5–8 hours (Sales Associate + Department Supervisor)

## W1120. Customer Loyalty Points Donation to Charity / Community Cause

| Field | Detail |

| **Trigger** | Loyalty member initiates points donation via mobile app, website, or in-store; or BuildRight activates a corporate cause campaign |
| **Frequency** | ~8,000–12,000 points donation transactions/month (estimated 1–2% of loyalty members participate) |
| **Volume** | 1 cause selection per donation; points transferred in real-time |
| **Owner** | Marketing Manager (Loyalty Program) |
| **Participants** | Customer (Loyalty Member), Marketing Manager, Finance (GL), Partner Charity/NGO |

### Background

BuildRight Depot's loyalty program has ~600,000 registered members earning 1 point per PHP 100 spent (redemption value: PHP 1.00 per point). Many members accumulate small point balances that are unlikely to be redeemed meaningfully (e.g., 23 points = PHP 23). Rather than letting these points expire unused (per W570 — loyalty points expiry management), a points donation program allows members to: (a) donate their loyalty points to a selected charity or community cause; (b) BuildRight converts donated points to cash and disburses to partner organizations quarterly; (c) BuildRight optionally matches a percentage of member donations as a corporate contribution (enhancing the CSR impact per W135). This program: (i) reduces loyalty liability on the balance sheet (unredeemed points are a liability per W104 — loyalty program financial governance); (ii) enhances brand perception and customer engagement; (iii) supports BuildRight's CSR strategy; (iv) provides an emotional connection that increases member retention. Philippine consumers are particularly responsive to cause-related marketing, especially during disaster relief periods (typhoons) and the holiday season. This workflow manages the points donation lifecycle from member action to charity disbursement.

This funded [Z] homes / [Z] relief packs / [Z] school rooms."; (ii) top donating stores recognized; (iii) individual donors who contributed ≥ 10,000 points receive a thank-you email from the charity; (c) impact stories developed for social media (W142) and newsletter | Finance / Marketing Manager | CMO | 2–3 hours/quarter |

## W1121. Store-Level Intercom & Public Address System Daily Operations

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily store opening procedure (W5A); ongoing throughout operating hours for announcements |
| **Frequency** | Daily (opening test); 5–15 announcements per store per day |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × 5–15 announcements/day = 1,000–3,000 announcements/day chain-wide |
| **Owner** | Store Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Assistant Store Manager, Cashiers, Sales Associates, Customer Service Representative |

### Background

BuildRight Depot stores operate an intercom and public address (PA) system for both operational and customer-facing communications. The PA system is used for: (a) **Operational announcements**: spill cleanup alerts, receiving dock calls, manager calls to specific departments, security alerts, shift change notifications; (b) **Customer service announcements**: paging customers for BOPIS pickup (W11), paging customers for special order arrivals, "your vehicle is loaded" notifications in the lumber yard; (c) **Promotional announcements**: scheduled promotional messages ("Visit Aisle 5 for today's Hot Deal on Portland Cement — PHP 220 per bag!"), seasonal sale event announcements; (d) **Emergency announcements**: evacuation instructions, severe weather alerts (W576), lockdown procedures (W330); (e) **Store opening/closing**: opening welcome message, 15-minute and 5-minute closing warnings, closing thank-you message. In Philippine retail culture, PA announcements are more accepted and expected than in Western markets — customers are accustomed to hearing promotions, birthday greetings, and community announcements. Proper PA system management ensures consistent messaging, appropriate volume and frequency, compliance with noise ordinances, and readiness for emergency use.

### Steps

- PA system controller — zone management, volume control, scheduled playback

- Maintenance work order system (W47) — equipment repair requests
- Store daily operations checklist (W5A, W574) — PA test confirmation

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Announcement overuse**: If staff make too many live announcements, the PA becomes noise pollution that annoys customers; a guideline of ≤ 15 live announcements per day (excluding emergencies) should be enforced

- **Noise ordinance compliance**: Municipal noise ordinances vary by LGU; stores near residential areas must be particularly careful with PA volume during early morning and evening hours
- **Emergency system reliability**: If the PA system fails during an emergency, lives are at risk; battery backup and a manual megaphone are mandatory backup measures

### Staffing Implication
- **No incremental headcount** — PA system operation is part of existing Store Manager, Assistant Store Manager, and staff duties
- **Training**: 15-minute PA usage module included in new hire onboarding (W518)

### Time Estimate
- Opening test: 5–10 min/day
- Live announcements: 30 sec–1 min each × 5–15/day = 3–15 min/day
- Closing sequence: 5 min/day
- **Total per store per day**: 13–30 min

## W1122. Customer Project Material Delivery Photo Documentation & Proof Service

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Delivery of project materials to a customer's construction site or home, especially for B2B/trade accounts and staged deliveries |
| **Frequency** | ~8,000–12,000 project deliveries/month requiring photo documentation (primarily B2B/trade, high-value, and staged deliveries) |
| **Volume** | 5–15 photos per delivery |
| **Owner** | Delivery Driver / Logistics Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Delivery Driver, Customer (Site Representative), Logistics Coordinator, Trade Account Manager |

### Background

BuildRight Depot delivers materials to construction sites and homes for both B2C (home delivery per W19) and B2B (project deliveries per W164, W980) customers. Material deliveries to construction sites carry unique risks that standard delivery proof (signature on delivery receipt) does not adequately address: (a) **Dispute risk**: Customer claims not all items were delivered ("I ordered 500 bags of cement but only received 450") — without photographic evidence, BuildRight must absorb the shortage cost; (b) **Damage in transit**: Materials damaged during unloading at site (cement bags torn, tiles chipped, lumber scratched) — without before/after photos, it's unclear whether damage occurred during BuildRight's handling or the customer's; (c) **Staged delivery reconciliation**: For project accounts with 5–10 staged deliveries over months (W164), photographic proof of each stage provides an auditable trail for milestone billing (W165); (d) **Site condition documentation**: Photos of the delivery location (driveway accessibility, unloading area condition, existing structures) protect BuildRight from claims of property damage during delivery; (e) **Insurance claims**: If materials are damaged by weather or theft after delivery but before installation, delivery photos prove the materials were delivered in good condition, supporting the customer's insurance claim (W858). This workflow establishes a systematic photo documentation protocol for project material deliveries, complementing W811 (wholesale delivery proof & POD reconciliation) and W592 (ecommerce delivery tracking & POD management).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pre-Delivery Preparation & Delivery Order Assignment**: (a) Logistics Coordinator assigns delivery to driver with delivery order details: (i) customer name, trade account number (if applicable); (ii) delivery address and site contact person/number; (iii) itemized material list with quantities; (iv) special delivery instructions (e.g., "Enter through gate on south side," "Unload inside garage," "Forklift delivery — ensure site has compacted surface"); (v) whether photo documentation is required: mandatory for all B2B/trade deliveries, B2C deliveries > PHP 20,000, and all staged project deliveries; (b) driver reviews delivery order on handheld tablet and plans route | Logistics Coordinator / Driver | Logistics Coordinator | 10–15 min |

| 5 | **Post-Delivery Photo Archive & Analytics**: (a) all delivery photos archived in cloud storage: (i) organized by delivery order number, date, and customer account; (ii) retained for 7 years (BIR requirement for transaction documentation); (iii) accessible to: Logistics Coordinator (operations), Trade Account Manager (customer service), Finance (billing dispute resolution), Legal (liability claims); (b) monthly delivery documentation compliance report: (i) % of required deliveries with complete photo documentation (target: ≥ 98%); (ii) common photo quality issues (blurry, missing categories, no GPS tag); (iii) dispute resolution effectiveness: % of delivery disputes resolved using photo evidence vs. requiring further investigation; (iv) average photos per delivery and average file size (for storage planning); (c) photo analytics used for: (i) driver performance scoring (documentation completeness); (ii) site risk assessment (difficult access sites flagged for future delivery planning); (iii) damage trend analysis (specific routes or handling methods causing more damage) | System / Logistics Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 1–2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Delivery order management module (W19) — order assignment, item list
- Driver handheld tablet — camera, GPS, timestamping
- Cloud photo storage — real-time upload, 7-year retention
- Digital POD module — signature capture, receipt generation
- Document management (W255) — delivery receipt archival
- Delivery tracking module (W592) — real-time delivery status
- Customer notification module — email delivery confirmation with photos
- Shortage/damage investigation module (W91, W500) — discrepancy handling
- Driver performance module — documentation compliance scoring
- BI analytics (W880) — compliance reporting

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Driver photo compliance**: Drivers may rush or skip photo documentation under time pressure; mandatory photo categories must be enforced by the tablet app (cannot complete delivery without all required photos)
- **Photo quality**: Poor lighting (early morning/late evening deliveries), rain (camera lens drops), or shaky hands produce unusable photos; photo quality check before submission is recommended
- **Storage cost**: 8,000–12,000 deliveries × 5–15 photos × 3–5 MB per photo = ~120–900 GB/month of photo storage; cloud storage cost must be budgeted (~PHP 50,000–150,000/month)
- **Customer privacy concerns**: Photographing a customer's home or construction site may raise privacy concerns; photo policy should be communicated in delivery terms and conditions, and photos should focus on materials, not residential interiors
- **Network connectivity**: Remote Philippine construction sites may have poor 4G/LTE signal; tablet must cache photos for upload when connectivity is restored

### Staffing Implication
