## W196. Route Planning & Dispatch Optimization

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Released Home Delivery orders (W19) or Store Replenishment orders (W4) |
| **Frequency** | Daily (Morning and Evening waves) |
| **Volume** | ~115 home deliveries/DC/day; ~33 store replenishments/DC/day |
| **Owner** | Logistics Planner |
| **Participants** | Logistics Planner, DC Dispatch, Drivers, 3PL Partners |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Batching**: System aggregates all pending deliveries by zone, weight, and volume (CBM) | System | Logistics Planner | Automated |
| 2 | **Vehicle Allocation**: System recommends vehicle type (10-wheeler vs. 6-wheeler vs. small van) based on load and delivery access restrictions (e.g., "Truck Ban" zones) | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | **Optimization**: Route optimization engine calculates most efficient sequence to minimize kilometers and fuel; accounts for traffic patterns and delivery windows | System | Logistics Planner | 5 min |
| 4 | **Manifest Generation**: Finalize route; system generates Trip Manifest and Load List; assigns to Driver/Vehicle | Logistics Planner | DC Manager | 10 min |
| 5 | **Driver Briefing**: Driver receives digital manifest on mobile app; verifies load; starts trip | Driver | DC Dispatch | 15 min |

### System Touchpoints
- Route Optimization Engine integration (Step 3)
- Driver Mobile App — manifest delivery, POD capture, route navigation
- Vehicle Master (ERP) — vehicle type, capacity, availability

### Pain Points / Risks
- MMDA truck ban hours in Metro Manila restrict delivery windows and force suboptimal route timing
- LTO driver license compliance — expired licenses halt dispatch
- Philippine fuel price volatility (PHP/USD + VAT impact) erodes route cost estimates

### Staffing Implication
Daily across 4 DCs. ~115 home deliveries + ~33 store replenishments per DC per day. 1 Logistics Planner per DC (5 FTE), ~2 hours/day on routing.

### Time Estimate
~30 min/DC/day for batching, optimization, and manifest generation; driver briefing 15 min/trip.

## W431. LGU-Specific "Truck Ban" & Route Governance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily route planning (W196) for deliveries in Metro Manila or provincial cities with active truck bans |
| **Frequency** | Daily |
| **Volume** | ~150 delivery trips/day (DC to Store and DC to Customer) |
| **Owner** | Logistics Planner |
| **Participants** | DC Dispatch, Driver, Regional Manager, MMDA / LGU Traffic Enforcers |

### Background

Managing large-format deliveries in the Philippines involves navigating a complex web of "Truck Ban" hours. Metro Manila (MMDA) and various provincial LGUs (e.g., Cebu City, Davao City) enforce different ban hours, restricted routes, and "window hours" (e.g., 10 AM to 5 PM). Violating a truck ban results in heavy fines and vehicle impounding, which disrupts the entire supply chain.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Truck Ban Database Maintenance**: Logistics Planner updates the ERP "Truck Ban Master" with current ban hours per city, vehicle type (e.g., 6-wheeler vs. 10-wheeler), and exemption status (e.g., refrigerated or essential goods) | Logistics Planner | — | 1 hour/week |
| 2 | **Route Constraint Input**: Route optimization engine (W196) pulls active ban hours from the Master; system blocks delivery windows that fall within ban periods for specific zones | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | **Dispatch Timing**: DC Dispatch adjusts the "Release Time" for trucks to ensure they reach their destination before the morning ban starts (typically 6:00 AM) or after the afternoon ban ends | DC Dispatch | DC Manager | 10 min/trip |
| 4 | **Exemption Management**: For "Essential Relief" goods (W428), Regional Manager applies for special LGU "Truck Ban Exemptions"; system tags specific vehicles with the exemption permit number | Regional Manager | Logistics Mgr | 1 day |
| 5 | **Real-Time Diversion**: If a truck is delayed (traffic/accident) and risks being caught in the ban, DC Dispatch sends an "Immediate Stop" alert via the Driver App; Driver parks at a pre-designated "Safe Zone" until the ban lifts | DC Dispatch | Driver | 5 min |
| 6 | **Fine & Impound Handling**: In case of apprehension, Driver logs the incident in the Fleet App; Logistics Manager coordinates with the LGU/MMDA for fine payment and vehicle release | Driver / Logistics Mgr | — | 4–8 hours |

### System Touchpoints

- Truck Ban Master: repository of LGU-specific traffic rules and restricted zones (W431.1)
- Route Optimization Engine: integrates time-based road constraints into routing logic (W431.2)
- Integration with W196 (Route Planning), W199 (Telematics), and W428 (Disaster Relief)

### Pain Points / Risks

- **Ad-hoc Ban Changes**: LGUs often change ban hours or restricted routes with little notice (e.g., during "ASEAN Summit" or "Pista"), causing immediate routing failures
- **"Window Hour" Congestion**: The 10 AM to 5 PM window in Metro Manila creates a massive surge in truck traffic, often leading to gridlock that prevents trucks from finishing their deliveries before the 5 PM ban resumes
- **Impounding Delays**: If a truck carrying high-value materials (appliances/electronics) is impounded, the customer delivery is delayed by days, leading to high CSR volume and potential damage to goods

### Staffing Implication

Managed by the Logistics Planner and DC Dispatch as part of daily operations. No incremental headcount.

### Time Estimate

**Total**: Database maintenance — 1 hour/week; Route adjustment — 10 min per affected trip; **Total: ~2 hours/day cumulative effort per DC**

## W1167. Reverse Logistics & Vendor Return Shipment Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor return authorization created in W88 (RTV Processing) or product recall per W622; or DC-initiated return for damaged/defective/overstock goods |
| **Frequency** | ~200–300 vendor return shipments/month across all DCs |
| **Volume** | ~50–75 return shipments per DC per month; average 8–12 lines per return |
| **Owner** | DC Returns Coordinator |
| **Participants** | DC Returns Coordinator, Category Manager (return authorization), DC Dispatch, Logistics Planner, AP Clerk |

### Background

BuildRight processes vendor returns for multiple reasons: defective merchandise (per W91 damaged goods disposition), vendor-authorized overstock returns (per W88 RTV processing), product recalls (per W622 recall readiness), and consignment stock returns (per W23 when consignment agreements are terminated). The logistics of moving goods from 200 stores back to DCs, consolidating them, and shipping them to vendors is a distinct reverse supply chain that requires dedicated coordination. Unlike forward logistics where routes and schedules are predictable, reverse logistics is ad-hoc, fragmented (items come from individual stores), and has lower priority on DC dock scheduling (per W585). Delays in reverse logistics tie up warehouse space and delay vendor credit processing.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Return consolidation at DC**: Store-returned goods (per W91, W12) arrive at DC via backhaul on regular store replenishment trucks; DC Returns Coordinator segregates return items into designated "Return Staging Area" by vendor and return reason; scan each item into return staging location in WMS | DC Returns Coordinator | DC Supervisor | 30 min/truck |
| 2 | **Return shipment planning**: Weekly, DC Returns Coordinator reviews accumulated return inventory by vendor; group returns into full-pallet or LTL shipments based on volume and vendor location; coordinate with Logistics Planner to schedule pickup during DC dock off-peak hours per W585 | DC Returns Coordinator | DC Manager | 2 hours/week |
| 3 | **Documentation & authorization**: For each return shipment: (a) generate Return Shipment Advance Shipping Notice (ASN) with item list, quantities, and return reason codes per W88 authorization; (b) attach vendor Return Merchandise Authorization (RMA) number; (c) for recalled items per W622, include recall reference and safety compliance documentation; (d) for import returns, prepare customs re-export documentation if duty recovery applies | DC Returns Coordinator | Category Manager | 30 min/shipment |
| 4 | **Carrier booking & dispatch**: Logistics Planner books carrier for return shipment pickup; for local vendors, arrange vendor's own pickup or BuildRight's backhaul truck; for import vendors, book freight forwarder for return shipment; generate Return Manifest; DC Dispatch coordinates dock appointment per W585 | Logistics Planner / DC Dispatch | DC Manager | 30 min/shipment |
| 5 | **Loading & dispatch**: DC loaders stage return pallets on dock; verify items against Return Manifest; photograph pallet condition for proof; carrier signs Bill of Lading; system updates return order status to "Shipped to Vendor"; inventory relieved from DC | DC Returns Coordinator / Loader | DC Supervisor | 30 min/shipment |
| 6 | **Vendor credit follow-up**: AP Clerk tracks return shipment delivery to vendor; if vendor does not issue credit within 30 days of receipt, escalate to Category Manager per W244 invoice dispute; for recalled items, track vendor reimbursement per W1151 recall cost recovery | AP Clerk | Category Manager | 15 min/return |
| 7 | **Monthly reverse logistics analytics**: DC Returns Coordinator compiles monthly report: return volume by reason (defective, overstock, recall, consignment), return-to-vendor cycle time, carrier cost per return shipment, DC staging area utilization, vendor credit realization rate | DC Returns Coordinator | VP Supply Chain | 2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Return order management linked to W88 RTV authorization
- WMS return staging area and location management
- DC dock scheduling per W585 for pickup coordination
- Carrier booking and manifest generation per W196
- AP vendor credit tracking per W244
- Reverse logistics BI dashboard

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Low priority on DC docks**: reverse logistics competes with high-priority inbound receiving and outbound dispatch; return shipments often delayed, extending vendor credit cycle time
- **Fragmented store returns**: items returned from 200 stores in small quantities make consolidation inefficient; minimum shipment thresholds needed per vendor
- **Vendor credit delays**: some vendors dispute return condition or quantities; AP must track and escalate per W244, tying up working capital
- **Seasonal spikes**: post-holiday and post-season returns (Christmas décor, flood control items) create volume surges that overwhelm return staging area

### Staffing Implication
- **DC Returns Coordinator**: 1 FTE per DC (~4 FTE total), handling return consolidation, planning, and documentation ~6–8 hours/day
- **Logistics Planner**: return carrier booking absorbed into existing role (~2 hours/week)
- **AP Clerk**: vendor credit follow-up absorbed into existing role (~4 hours/week)

### Time Estimate
- Return consolidation: 30 min/truck × ~10 trucks/DC/week = 5 hours/week/DC
- Weekly planning: 2 hours/DC/week
- Per-shipment documentation and loading: 1 hour/shipment × ~15 shipments/DC/month = 15 hours/month/DC
- **Total: ~30–40 hours/DC/month**

## W1168. Direct Store Delivery (DSD) Receiving, Verification & Vendor Compliance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | DSD vendor truck arrives at store receiving dock with purchase order delivery |
| **Frequency** | ~500–600 DSD receipts/month across all stores (~2–3 DSD deliveries per store per week) |
| **Volume** | ~3 DSD deliveries/store/week × 200 stores = ~600 receipts/month; higher volume at stores near cement plants and lumber mills |
| **Owner** | Receiving Clerk / Stock Associate |
| **Participants** | Stock Associate (receiving), Department Supervisor, AP Clerk (3-way match), Category Manager (vendor compliance) |

### Background

Approximately 30% of BuildRight's goods by value are delivered direct-to-store (DSD), bypassing DCs entirely. These are primarily bulky, heavy, or locally sourced items where double-handling through a DC is uneconomical: cement, sand, gravel, lumber, structural steel, and large appliances from local distributors. DSD deliveries represent ~8–10% of total receipt count but include high-value items. Unlike DC-managed receipts (per W3 and W109), DSD receiving happens at the store level by Stock Associates who must verify quantities, check quality, and reconcile against the PO — all while managing their primary replenishment and customer-facing duties. DSD compliance (delivery windows, documentation quality, quantity accuracy) is historically lower than DC-managed deliveries because vendors interact directly with store staff who have less receiving training and less leverage than DC receiving teams.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pre-delivery coordination**: System sends DSD delivery schedule to store 24 hours in advance based on vendor ASN; Stock Associate reviews expected deliveries for the day; confirms dock/receiving area availability; coordinates with Department Supervisor for heavy/bulky items requiring forklift or additional labor | Stock Associate / System | Department Supervisor | 15 min/day |
| 2 | **Vendor arrival & check-in**: DSD vendor truck arrives at store receiving dock; Stock Associate verifies: (a) delivery against scheduled DSD PO — vendor name, PO number, expected items; (b) driver identity and vehicle condition; (c) for cement/lumber/sand: verify truck weight ticket matches PO quantity; (d) direct vendor to designated unloading area | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 10 min |
| 3 | **Unloading & quantity verification**: (a) Stock Associate supervises unloading; counts pieces/units as they come off the truck; (b) for bulk items (sand, gravel): verify volume against weight ticket conversion; (c) for lumber: verify board feet per piece against PO specification; (d) for cement: count bags and check for torn/wet bags; (e) for appliances: verify model numbers and serial numbers against PO; (f) flag any quantity discrepancy (> 2% tolerance) immediately | Stock Associate / Vendor Driver | Department Supervisor | 30–60 min |
| 4 | **Quality inspection**: Stock Associate inspects DSD goods: (a) cement bags: check for moisture damage, clumping, or expired manufacturing date (cement has 3-month shelf life from production); (b) lumber: check for warping, cracks, and grade stamps; (c) structural steel: verify dimensions and grade markings; (d) reject non-conforming items — document with photos and note on delivery receipt | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 15–30 min |
| 5 | **Goods receipt processing**: Stock Associate processes goods receipt on handheld tablet: (a) scan PO barcode or enter PO number; (b) enter actual received quantities per line; (c) note any discrepancies (short shipment, over-shipment, quality rejects); (d) capture electronic signature from vendor driver acknowledging receipt; (e) system updates store inventory and creates AP voucher for 3-way match per W244 | Stock Associate / System | Department Supervisor | 10 min |
| 6 | **Putaway to sales floor**: Stock Associate moves DSD goods directly to sales floor or designated outdoor storage area (lumber yard, cement staging); no backroom storage for DSD items — sell-from-shelf model; update bin location in system | Stock Associate | Department Supervisor | 20–45 min |
| 7 | **DSD vendor compliance tracking**: System captures DSD delivery performance data: (a) on-time delivery % (delivered within scheduled window), (b) quantity accuracy % (shipped vs. ordered), (c) quality rejection rate, (d) documentation completeness; feeds vendor scorecard per W44 and SCP-020; Category Manager reviews DSD vendor compliance monthly | System / Category Manager | VP Merchandising | 15 min/month/vendor |

### System Touchpoints
- DSD PO management and ASN processing
- Store receiving module on handheld tablet
- Weight ticket capture for bulk items (cement, sand, gravel)
- Inventory update with real-time ATP adjustment
- AP 3-way match per W244
- Vendor scorecard per W44 (SCP-020)
- DSD delivery scheduling module

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Store receiving skill gaps**: Stock Associates are less trained in receiving procedures than DC receiving clerks; quantity verification errors are more frequent, leading to inventory discrepancies
- **Vendor short-shipment**: DSD vendors delivering cement and lumber may short-ship by 5–10%, knowing store staff rarely count every bag or board; contributes to inventory shrinkage
- **Weather-dependent deliveries**: DSD items (cement, sand, lumber) are often unloaded outdoors; rain during unloading damages cement and lumber, creating quality disputes
- **Receiving dock congestion**: DSD deliveries may arrive simultaneously with DC replenishment trucks; store has only 1 receiving dock, creating scheduling conflicts
- **Bulk quantity verification difficulty**: counting 200+ cement bags or verifying cubic meters of sand is time-consuming and inaccurate; weight-based verification helps but requires calibrated truck scales

### Staffing Implication
- **Stock Associate (Receiving)**: ~2–3 DSD deliveries/week × 1.5 hours each = ~3–5 hours/week of receiving duty; absorbed by existing Stock Associate role
- **Department Supervisor**: quality escalation and heavy-item coordination ~1 hour/week; absorbed by existing role

### Time Estimate
- Per DSD delivery: pre-delivery 15 min + check-in 10 min + unloading/verification 30–60 min + quality inspection 15–30 min + GR processing 10 min + putaway 20–45 min = **1.5–3 hours per delivery**
- **Chain-wide: ~1,200–1,800 hours/month**

## W1169. Import Container Inbound Logistics, Port Drayage & DC Delivery

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Import container cleared by customs per W464 and released from port/terminal for delivery to DC |
| **Frequency** | ~400–600 TEUs/month; ~20–30 containers per DC per week |
| **Volume** | ~15–25 container drayage moves per DC per week; peak during pre-Ber months (July–August) and pre-summer (February–March) build-ups |
| **Owner** | Inbound Logistics Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Inbound Logistics Coordinator, Customs Broker, Drayage Carrier, DC Receiving Supervisor, Import Coordinator, Finance (duty payment) |

### Background

BuildRight imports ~40% of its COGS from international vendors (China, Taiwan, Indonesia, Malaysia, Japan, Europe), moving ~400–600 TEUs (twenty-foot equivalent units) per month. After customs clearance per W464, containers must be moved from Philippine ports (Manila International Container Terminal, Manila South Harbor, Batangas Port, Cebu Port, Davao Sasa Wharf) to the respective DCs. This "last mile of import logistics" — port drayage — is a critical operation because Philippine ports impose demurrage charges for containers not picked up within free time (typically 5–7 days per W249). Container drayage requires booking trucking capacity, coordinating with container yard operations, managing chassis availability, and scheduling DC receiving appointments. Port congestion, truck bans (per W431), and limited truck availability create delays that directly increase demurrage costs and delay DC receiving.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Container release tracking**: Customs Broker confirms container release from Bureau of Customs per W464; Inbound Logistics Coordinator receives release notification; verifies: (a) container number, (b) bill of lading, (c) free time expiry date (demurrage risk per W249), (d) destination DC; enters container into "Drayage Queue" in ERP with priority based on free time remaining | Customs Broker / Inbound Logistics Coordinator | Import Coordinator | 30 min/container |
| 2 | **Drayage carrier booking**: Inbound Logistics Coordinator books drayage carrier: (a) select from approved drayage carriers based on port location, chassis availability, and DC destination; (b) confirm truck availability within 24 hours of release for containers with < 3 days free time remaining; (c) provide container details, port pickup location, and DC delivery address; (d) for overweight containers (tiles, cement, steel): ensure carrier has appropriate truck capacity (10-wheeler with 20ft container chassis or 40ft chassis for FCL) | Inbound Logistics Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 30 min/container |
| 3 | **Port pickup & inspection**: Drayage carrier picks up container from port terminal; driver conducts visual inspection: (a) verify container seal number matches bill of lading; (b) check for visible damage (dents, holes, water intrusion); (c) photograph container condition and seal at port gate; (d) flag discrepancies immediately to Inbound Logistics Coordinator | Drayage Carrier Driver | Inbound Logistics Coordinator | 2–4 hours (port processing) |
| 4 | **Inland transit & truck ban management**: Carrier transports container from port to DC; system tracks via telematics per W199; route planning accounts for: (a) LGU truck ban restrictions per W431; (b) port-to-DC route typically 2–4 hours for Metro Manila ports to Laguna/Clark DCs; (c) Cebu port to Mandaue DC: 1 hour; (d) Davao Sasa Wharf to Davao DC: 30 min; (e) system provides live ETA to DC Receiving Supervisor | Drayage Carrier / System | DC Dispatch | 1–4 hours transit |
| 5 | **DC receiving appointment & unloading**: Container arrives at DC; DC Receiving Supervisor verifies: (a) container seal number, (b) external condition; schedules unloading per W585 dock appointment; DC team unloads container: (a) full container load: 2–4 hours for manual unloading of 20ft container; (b) verify item counts and SKUs against import PO and packing list per W3; (c) flag any damage or shortage discovered during unloading; (d) process goods receipt in WMS | DC Receiving Supervisor / DC Team | DC Manager | 4–6 hours/container |
| 6 | **Empty container return**: After unloading, drayage carrier must return empty container to port/depot within carrier's free time (typically 48–72 hours); Inbound Logistics Coordinator tracks empty return to avoid per diem charges; confirm return with carrier; close drayage order in system | Drayage Carrier / Inbound Logistics Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 4–8 hours |
| 7 | **Demurrage & detention monitoring**: System tracks container free time countdown: (a) port demurrage: free time starts at vessel discharge; standard rate PHP 5,000–8,000/day after free time; (b) carrier detention: free time starts at container gate-out; standard rate PHP 2,000–4,000/day; (c) weekly report of containers at risk of demurrage; escalate at-risk containers to VP Supply Chain for expedited drayage; (d) demurrage costs tracked per W249 and reported monthly | System / Inbound Logistics Coordinator | VP Supply Chain | 2 hours/week |
| 8 | **Monthly drayage analytics**: Inbound Logistics Coordinator compiles drayage performance: (a) average port-to-DC cycle time by port, (b) demurrage incidence and cost, (c) carrier on-time pickup rate, (d) container damage rate, (e) cost per TEU by lane; benchmark against prior period | Inbound Logistics Coordinator | VP Supply Chain | 3 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Container tracking module linked to W464 customs clearance and W144 import operations
- Drayage carrier booking and dispatch system
- DC dock scheduling per W585
- Telematics per W199 for in-transit visibility
- Port terminal API for container release status
- Demurrage monitoring dashboard per W249
- Goods receipt per W3

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Manila port congestion**: Manila International Container Terminal (MICT) and South Harbor are chronically congested; container pickup can take 2–3 days from release, eating into free time; Batangas Port alternative has limited capacity
- **Chassis availability**: Philippine ports have chronic chassis shortages; carriers may not have equipment available for container pickup even when released by customs
- **Demurrage cost exposure**: at PHP 5,000–8,000/day/container and 400–600 TEUs/month, even a 10% demurrage incidence rate represents PHP 2–5M/month in avoidable costs
- **Container damage in transit**: Philippine road conditions (potholes, flooding) cause container damage and cargo shifting; especially critical for fragile items (tiles, appliances, glass)
- **DC receiving bottleneck**: container unloading requires 2–4 hours of dock time and labor; 15–25 containers/week per DC creates scheduling pressure on docks also serving vendor deliveries and outbound dispatch

### Staffing Implication
- **Inbound Logistics Coordinator**: 1 FTE per DC region (~2–3 FTE total), managing ~20–25 container drayage moves/week; ~6–8 hours/day on coordination
- **DC Receiving Supervisor**: container receiving absorbed into existing role
- **Drayage carrier coordination**: ~30 min per container × 100 containers/week = 50 hours/week chain-wide

### Time Estimate
- Per container: booking 30 min + port pickup 2–4 hours + transit 1–4 hours + DC unloading 4–6 hours + empty return 4–8 hours = **12–24 hours total cycle time**
- Weekly monitoring: 2 hours/DC
- Monthly analytics: 3 hours
