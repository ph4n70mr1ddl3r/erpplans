## W1163. 3PL & Delivery Partner Daily Operations Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Daily operations cycle; partner performance threshold breach |
| **Frequency** | Daily monitoring; monthly formal review |
| **Volume** | ~80% of outbound deliveries handled by 3PL partners (~8–10 active partners) |
| **Owner** | Logistics Manager |
| **Participants** | Logistics Manager, 3PL Dispatch Coordinator, DC Dispatch, AP Clerk |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Daily Partner Availability Check**: Logistics Manager confirms 3PL partner vehicle availability for the day via partner portal or messaging; updates dispatch planning with confirmed truck count | Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 30 min |
| 2 | **Dispatch Coordination**: DC Dispatch assigns deliveries to 3PL partners alongside owned fleet per W196; system generates partner-specific trip manifests; partner driver receives manifest via partner mobile app | DC Dispatch | Logistics Manager | 30 min |
| 3 | **Real-Time Tracking**: System ingests 3PL partner GPS/telematics data via API; DC Dispatch monitors partner vehicle locations alongside owned fleet on unified dashboard; delays flagged automatically | System / DC Dispatch | Logistics Manager | Continuous |
| 4 | **Delivery Confirmation**: Partner driver captures POD (signature + photo) via partner app; system updates order status in ERP; customer notification triggered per W708 | Partner Driver | DC Dispatch | 5 min/delivery |
| 5 | **Exception Handling**: For failed deliveries (customer unavailable, access denied, vehicle breakdown): DC Dispatch coordinates with partner for same-day reattempt or next-day reschedule; customer notified per W708 | DC Dispatch | Logistics Manager | 15 min/exception |
| 6 | **Daily Settlement Reconciliation**: System matches partner delivery records against ERP dispatch manifest; flags unmatched deliveries for investigation; feeds weekly partner scorecard per W242 | System / AP Clerk | Logistics Manager | 30 min |

### System Touchpoints
- 3PL partner API integration (vehicle availability, GPS tracking, POD capture)
- Unified dispatch dashboard (owned + 3PL fleet)
- ERP order management (delivery status, customer notification per W708)
- AP settlement (partner invoice reconciliation per W7)
- Partner scorecard module (W242, SCP-020)

### Pain Points / Risks
- Partner vehicle no-shows during peak season (Ber months) — must activate backup partners or own fleet overtime
- API integration reliability varies by partner technology maturity — some partners require manual manifest communication
- Partner driver quality inconsistency — POD photo quality, customer interaction, cargo handling standards
- Philippine traffic unpredictability makes SLA commitments difficult for partners in Metro Manila

### Staffing Implication
- **Logistics Manager**: ~1 hour/day on partner coordination; absorbed by existing role
- **3PL Dispatch Coordinator**: ~4 hours/day on manifest distribution and exception handling; 1 FTE per DC region

### Time Estimate
~2 hours/day for daily operations; monthly formal review ~4 hours.

## W1164. Inter-Island Logistics Coordination & Shipping Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | DC replenishment order requiring inter-island transfer; or vendor DSD to island store bypassing nearest DC |
| **Frequency** | ~30–40 inter-island shipments/month |
| **Volume** | ~5–10 ferry/ro-ro shipments/week across 3 island groups (Luzon, Visayas, Mindanao) |
| **Owner** | Inter-Island Logistics Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Supply Planner, DC Dispatch, Shipping Line Coordinator, Customs (if applicable), Receiving Store |

### Background

Operating across the Philippine archipelago requires managing inter-island logistics through roll-on/roll-off (ro-ro) ferries and containerized shipping. The 4-DC network (Davao, Cebu, Laguna, Clark) covers primary demand, but store-to-DC replenishment, emergency stock transfers, and seasonal surges require inter-island coordination. Key ferry routes include: Mindanao–Visayas (Davao–Cebu), Visayas–Luzon (Cebu–Manila/Batangas), and intra-Visayas connections.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Shipment Planning**: Supply Planner identifies inter-island transfer need from W22 or W214; creates transfer order in ERP with origin DC, destination store/DC, and required delivery date | Supply Planner | Logistics Manager | 30 min |
| 2 | **Route & Carrier Selection**: Inter-Island Logistics Coordinator selects optimal route (direct ferry vs. multi-hop) and shipping line based on: transit time, schedule frequency, cargo type compatibility, cost per CBM, and historical reliability per W727 | Logistics Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 30 min |
| 3 | **Booking & Documentation**: Coordinator books cargo space with shipping line; prepares shipping documents: bill of lading, cargo manifest, packing list; for hazmat shipments per W699, obtains Dangerous Goods Declaration | Logistics Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 1 hour |
| 4 | **Loading & Dispatch**: DC loads goods onto truck; truck driven to port; cargo loaded onto ferry/ship (ro-ro for truck-on-board or containerized); system updates transfer order status to "In Transit — At Sea" | DC Dispatch / Driver | Logistics Coordinator | 4–8 hours |
| 5 | **In-Transit Monitoring**: System tracks shipment via shipping line API or manual status updates; Coordinator monitors weather advisories (PAGASA) for potential delays; provides ETA updates to receiving location | Logistics Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 15 min/day per shipment |
| 6 | **Port Arrival & Delivery**: Truck offloads at destination port; drives to receiving store or DC; Receiving Clerk processes goods receipt per W109 or W3; system updates inventory and closes transfer order | Driver / Receiving Clerk | DC Manager / Store Manager | 4–8 hours |
| 7 | **Transit Damage & Loss Handling**: Receiving location documents any damage or shortage with photos; Coordinator files claim with shipping line per W500; tracks claim to resolution | Logistics Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 1–2 weeks |
| 8 | **Monthly Route Analytics**: Coordinator compiles inter-island logistics report: transit time by route, on-time delivery rate, cost per CBM by lane, damage/loss rate, carrier reliability ranking | Logistics Coordinator | VP Supply Chain | 4 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Transfer order management (W22)
- Shipping line booking API or portal integration
- In-transit inventory visibility per INV-014
- PAGASA weather advisory integration for route risk assessment
- Goods receipt processing (W109, W3)
- Damage claim management (W500)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Ferry schedule disruptions**: Philippine ferry services frequently delayed or cancelled during typhoon season (June–November), rough seas, and mechanical breakdowns; 24–48 hour delays common
- **Port congestion**: Manila port congestion can delay container pickup by days; Batangas port as alternative has limited roll-on/roll-off capacity
- **Cargo damage at sea**: Saltwater exposure, shifting cargo, and rough handling at ports cause higher damage rates than land transport; insurance claims per W610 are more frequent for inter-island shipments
- **Limited refrigerated transport**: Perishable or temperature-sensitive items (paint, chemicals per W492) require climate-controlled containers with limited availability on inter-island routes

### Staffing Implication
- **Inter-Island Logistics Coordinator**: ~30–40 shipments/month × ~2 hours/shipment for planning, booking, and monitoring = ~60–80 hours/month; 1 dedicated FTE
- **Logistics Manager**: monthly review and escalation ~4 hours/month; absorbed by existing role

### Time Estimate
Per shipment: planning 30 min, booking 1 hour, loading 4–8 hours, transit 12–48 hours, delivery 4–8 hours; monthly analytics: 4 hours.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | **Carrier Assignment**: System assigns orders to delivery partners based on: item type (standard vs. bulky vs. hazmat per W237), delivery zone, partner capacity, and SLA tier (same-day, next-day, 3-day provincial) | System | Dispatch Coordinator | 5 min |
| 3 | **Driver/Rider Dispatch**: Partner or own-fleet driver receives delivery manifest via mobile app; confirms pickup from fulfillment location; system sends customer notification "Out for Delivery" per W708 | Driver / Partner | Dispatch Coordinator | 10 min |

| 7 | **SLA Monitoring**: System calculates SLA compliance: order-to-delivery time vs. target (same-day < 8 hours, next-day metro < 24 hours, 3-day provincial < 72 hours); breaches auto-escalated per severity per W591 | System | Ecommerce Operations | Automated |
| 8 | **Daily SLA Report**: Overnight SLA performance calculation per W591; daily dashboard by partner, zone, and SLA tier; weekly partner performance feed to W242 and SCP-020 | System / Dispatch Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 30 min |

### System Touchpoints
- Order management system (W19, POS-023)
- Route optimization engine (W196)
- Delivery partner API (Lalamove, Transportify, Grab, own fleet)
- Customer notification engine (W708)
- Real-time tracking with customer-facing map
- POD capture system (W592)
- SLA monitoring dashboard (W591, ECOM-023)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Philippine address quality**: Many customer addresses lack standardization (barangay-only, landmark-based, no postal code); requires driver phone confirmation adding 3–5 min per delivery
- **Traffic unpredictability**: Metro Manila and Cebu City traffic makes same-day SLA unreliable; weather (typhoons, flooding) causes widespread delivery failures
- **High-value item security**: Deliveries of power tools (PHP 5K–50K) and appliances risk theft during last-mile; requires partner insurance verification per W610
- **Customer not-at-home rate**: ~15–20% failed first delivery attempts in Philippine ecommerce; reschedule costs erode delivery margin

### Staffing Implication
- **Last-Mile Dispatch Coordinator**: ~17,200 ecommerce deliveries/month ÷ 30 days = ~573/day; 2–3 coordinators per shift at DC; ~6 FTE across 4 DCs
- **Ecommerce Operations**: SLA monitoring absorbed into existing role

### Time Estimate
Dispatch coordination: ~2–3 hours/DC/day; SLA monitoring: 30 min/day; weekly review: 2 hours.

## W1166. Carrier Rate Management, Freight Audit & Cost Optimization

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Monthly carrier rate review cycle; or significant rate change from carrier; or quarterly benchmarking |
| **Frequency** | Monthly rate review; quarterly benchmarking; annual contract renewal |
| **Volume** | ~20–30 active carriers and freight forwarders; ~PHP 200–300M annual freight spend |
| **Owner** | Logistics Manager |
| **Participants** | Logistics Manager, Procurement, Finance, VP Supply Chain |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rate Card Maintenance**: Logistics Manager maintains carrier rate cards in ERP: per-km rate by vehicle type, per-CBM rate for inter-island, per-delivery rate for last-mile, accessorial charges (waiting time, redelivery, hazmat surcharge per W237); updates on rate change notification from carrier | Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2 hours/month |
| 2 | **Freight Invoice Audit**: System performs automated freight audit: matches carrier invoice to dispatch manifest × contracted rate card; flags discrepancies > 5%; AP Clerk investigates and resolves per W244 | System / AP Clerk | Logistics Manager | 4 hours/month |
| 3 | **Monthly Cost Analysis**: Logistics Manager compiles monthly freight cost report: total spend by carrier, cost-per-delivery by zone, cost-per-CBM by lane, cost-per-km by vehicle type; compares to budget per W489 | Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 4 hours/month |
| 4 | **Quarterly Rate Benchmarking**: Procurement and Logistics Manager benchmark carrier rates against Philippine market: (a) solicit spot rates from alternative carriers, (b) compare to DTI-referenced transport rates, (c) analyze rate trends per SCP-019; identify lanes where BuildRight is above market | Procurement / Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 8 hours/quarter |
| 5 | **Rate Negotiation**: For lanes above market benchmark: Logistics Manager negotiates rate reduction with incumbent carrier; if unsuccessful, Procurement issues RFQ per W224 to alternative carriers; evaluation criteria: rate 40%, reliability 30%, coverage 20%, technology 10% | Logistics Manager / Procurement | VP Supply Chain | Per carrier |
| 6 | **Annual Contract Renewal**: For top-10 carriers by spend: annual contract renewal per W688 with updated rate schedules, SLA targets per SCP-020, insurance requirements per W610, and penalty clauses for repeated SLA breaches | Procurement / Legal | VP Supply Chain | Per W688 |

### System Touchpoints
- Carrier rate card master in ERP
- Freight audit engine (invoice matching per W244)
- AP processing (W7)
- Carrier scorecard (W44, SCP-020)
- Cost analytics (SCP-019)
- Contract management (W688)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Fuel price pass-through**: Most carrier contracts include fuel surcharge adjustments tied to Philippine petroleum prices; monthly rate volatility of 3–5% complicates budgeting
- **Carrier rate opacity**: Philippine trucking rates are relationship-based and lack published tariffs; benchmarking relies on competitive quotes which may not reflect actual market rates
- **Hidden accessorial charges**: Waiting time, detention, redelivery, and documentation fees often unlisted in rate cards; freight audit catches these but adds AP processing time
- **Port demurrage and storage**: Import container demurrage at Philippine ports per W249 adds unplanned freight cost; needs tighter coordination with W2B import timeline

### Staffing Implication
- **Logistics Manager**: ~6 hours/month on rate management and cost analysis; absorbed by existing role
- **AP Clerk**: ~4 hours/month on freight invoice audit; absorbed by existing role
- **Procurement**: ~8 hours/quarter on benchmarking and negotiation; absorbed by existing procurement function

### Time Estimate
Monthly: rate maintenance 2 hours, freight audit 4 hours, cost analysis 4 hours; quarterly: benchmarking 8 hours; annual: contract renewal per carrier.

## W1191. Construction Site Delivery Coordination, Access Assessment & Crane/Boom Truck Scheduling

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer places delivery order for heavy/bulky construction materials (cement, steel rebar, lumber, tiles, sand, gravel) to a construction site address |
| **Frequency** | ~3,000–4,000 construction site deliveries/month across all channels (B2B trade, B2C retail, ecommerce) |
| **Volume** | ~15% of total deliveries involve construction site destinations (vs. residential/commercial addresses) |
| **Owner** | Delivery Dispatch Coordinator |
| **Participants** | Delivery Dispatch Coordinator, 3PL Partner Driver, Store Manager, Customer (site contact), Category Manager (heavy materials) |

### Background

Delivering construction materials to active construction sites presents unique logistics challenges not found in standard residential delivery: unpaved access roads, narrow gates, absence of receiving docks, multi-story delivery requirements (materials needed on upper floors), crane/boom truck requirements for heavy loads, site safety protocols, and coordination with site foremen who may have limited availability. This workflow manages the specialized coordination needed to successfully deliver heavy and bulky construction materials to active job sites.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Site access assessment at order entry**: When customer provides a construction site delivery address: (a) system checks delivery address against "Site Access Profile" database (pre-assessed sites with known constraints); (b) if site is new or unprofiled, Delivery Dispatch Coordinator calls customer/site foreman to assess: (i) road accessibility (paved/unpaved, bridge weight limits), (ii) gate width (minimum 3 meters for 10-wheeler), (iii) unloading area availability, (iv) floor-level delivery requirement, (v) crane/boom truck requirement for multi-story or heavy items; (c) creates Site Access Profile in system for future reference | Delivery Dispatch Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 15 min/new site |
| 2 | **Vehicle assignment based on site profile**: System recommends vehicle type based on site access profile: (a) standard 10-wheeler wing van for paved-road sites with dock/ground-level unloading, (b) 6-wheeler truck for narrow-access sites, (c) boom truck/crane truck for multi-story delivery, (d) mini-dump truck for sand/gravel delivery; Logistics Manager confirms vehicle assignment and selects appropriate 3PL partner from approved heavy-haul partners | System / Delivery Dispatch Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 15 min/delivery |
| 3 | **Delivery scheduling with site contact**: Delivery Dispatch Coordinator schedules delivery date/time with site foreman; confirms: (a) site will be accessible on delivery date (no concrete pouring or excavation blocking access), (b) unloading crew available at site, (c) site safety requirements (PPE required, prohibited areas); sends SMS/email confirmation with delivery window and vehicle type | Delivery Dispatch Coordinator | — | 10 min/delivery |
| 4 | **Load securing for construction materials**: At dispatch, driver and warehouse crew follow special load-securing protocols for construction materials: (a) cement bags stacked on pallets, wrapped in plastic (moisture protection), (b) rebar bundles secured with steel strapping, (c) lumber loaded lengthwise with end flags for overhang, (d) tiles in original cartons, no more than 4 pallets high; weight distribution verified | DC Warehouse Crew / Driver | DC Supervisor | 30 min/load |
| 5 | **On-site delivery execution**: Driver arrives at construction site; (a) dons required PPE (hard hat, safety vest — mandatory on construction sites per DOLE), (b) coordinates with site foreman for unloading location, (c) if boom truck: operates boom crane to deliver materials to specified floor; (d) if ground-level: unloads with site crew assistance; (e) captures POD (signature + photo of delivered materials at site) | Driver / Site Foreman | Delivery Dispatch Coordinator | 30–90 min/delivery |
| 6 | **Shortage and damage resolution**: If delivery is short-loaded or materials are damaged during transit/unloading: (a) driver notes discrepancy on delivery receipt, (b) Delivery Dispatch Coordinator notifies Store Manager and customer within 2 hours, (c) replacement delivery scheduled within 48 hours for critical materials (cement, rebar — construction timeline dependent), (d) damage claim filed with 3PL partner if applicable per W1166 | Delivery Dispatch Coordinator | Logistics Manager | 15 min/exception |
| 7 | **Site Access Profile update**: After delivery, Delivery Dispatch Coordinator updates Site Access Profile with delivery notes: (a) actual access conditions encountered, (b) any issues or delays, (c) recommended vehicle type for future deliveries, (d) site contact updates; profile available for future orders to the same site | Delivery Dispatch Coordinator | — | 5 min/delivery |

### System Touchpoints
- Site Access Profile database with geo-tagged site records (W1191.1)
- Vehicle recommendation engine based on site constraints and load characteristics (W1191.2)
- Delivery scheduling with site contact confirmation (W1191.3)
- GPS tracking of heavy-haul vehicles with ETA notification to site foreman (W1191.5)
- POD capture with photo documentation at construction site (W1191.5)
- Exception management workflow linking to carrier claims (W1166) (W1191.6)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Site access changes between order and delivery**: active construction sites change daily; a road accessible during order placement may be blocked by excavation or material staging by delivery date; mitigated by re-confirming access 24 hours before delivery
- **Site safety liability**: delivery personnel entering active construction sites face injury risks from falling objects, heavy equipment, and open excavations; mitigated by mandatory PPE, site safety briefing from foreman, and company insurance coverage for delivery personnel
- **Boom truck/crane availability**: specialized vehicles (boom trucks) are limited in the 3PL fleet; peak demand periods (post-disaster, construction boom) may exceed capacity; mitigated by pre-contracting multiple heavy-haul partners and scheduling boom truck deliveries 3–5 days ahead
- **Multi-drop inefficiency**: construction site deliveries are typically single-drop (one site per trip) due to heavy loads and specialized unloading requirements; this increases per-delivery cost vs. multi-drop residential routes

### Staffing Implication
- **Delivery Dispatch Coordinator**: ~20–30 hours/week on construction site delivery coordination (60–70% of delivery dispatch capacity during peak construction months). Absorbed within existing role.
- **3PL Partner heavy-haul drivers**: engaged on per-trip basis per existing 3PL contracts.
- **No permanent incremental headcount**.

## W1208. Customer Home Delivery White-Glove Service & Installation Add-On Dispatch

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer purchases large/bulky item (appliance, aircon, water heater, bathroom set) with delivery + optional installation add-on at POS or ecommerce |
| **Frequency** | ~2,000–3,000 white-glove deliveries per month across 200 stores |
| **Volume** | ~30–40 per store per month; higher in Metro Manila and urban stores |
| **Owner** | Logistics Dispatcher |
| **Participants** | Logistics Dispatcher, Sales Associate, Installation Technician (per W600 accredited), Customer, Delivery Driver, 3PL Provider |

### Background

BuildRight Depot sells bulky home improvement items (appliances, aircons, water heaters, bathroom fixtures, kitchen cabinets) that require professional home delivery and often installation. The "white-glove" service includes scheduled delivery window, in-home placement, unpacking, debris removal, and optional installation by an accredited technician (per W600). This service is a competitive differentiator against pure-play ecommerce (Lazada/Shopee) that cannot provide professional installation for building materials and appliances. With ~30% of revenue from professional/trade customers and ~55% from walk-in retail, the white-glove service targets the retail DIY homeowner segment that needs help completing projects. This workflow complements W1165 (last-mile delivery dispatch) by adding the installation coordination layer and W138 (home installation services) by covering the delivery-to-installation handoff.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Customer purchases bulky item at POS or ecommerce with delivery selected; Sales Associate or system captures: (a) delivery address, (b) preferred delivery date and time window (morning 8–12 / afternoon 1–5), (c) floor level and elevator access, (d) installation add-on selection (Yes/No), (e) site preparation status (plumbing rough-in complete? electrical outlet ready?), (f) contact number and alternate contact | Sales Associate / Customer | — | 5 min |
| 2 | System validates delivery address against serviceable coverage areas; estimates delivery fee based on distance zone and item weight/dimensions; if installation add-on selected: system checks technician availability for requested date using technician scheduling calendar; system confirms or proposes alternative date | System | — | Automated |
| 3 | Logistics Dispatcher reviews delivery queue for the day; groups white-glove deliveries by geographic zone to optimize routing; assigns deliveries to drivers and 3PL partners with appropriate vehicle type (6-wheeler for appliances, wing van for construction materials) | Logistics Dispatcher | Logistics Manager | 30 min/day |
| 4 | If installation add-on: Logistics Dispatcher assigns accredited Installation Technician from scheduling pool; system sends technician a job notification with: order details, item specifications, site access notes, customer contact, estimated arrival window | Logistics Dispatcher / System | Installation Manager | 15 min |
| 5 | System sends customer automated delivery confirmation via SMS/email: delivery date, time window, driver name and contact, technician name (if installation), preparation checklist (clear pathway, measure doorways, verify electrical/plumbing readiness) | System | — | Automated |
| 6 | Delivery driver and technician arrive at customer site; driver unloads and carries item to specified room (white-glove: in-home placement, not curbside); technician inspects site readiness: (a) measurements match product dimensions, (b) electrical/plumbing connections accessible, (c) wall/floor conditions suitable for installation | Driver / Technician | Logistics Dispatcher | 15–30 min |
| 7 | If site is NOT ready (missing electrical outlet, plumbing not roughed-in, access too narrow): technician documents issue with photos; communicates to customer what preparation is needed; Logistics Dispatcher reschedules delivery/installation; delivery fee may still apply per service terms | Technician / Logistics Dispatcher | Installation Manager | 10 min |
| 8 | If site is ready: technician proceeds with installation per W138; upon completion: demonstrates product operation to customer; customer signs acceptance form on technician's tablet; system records installation completion with photo documentation | Technician / Customer | Installation Manager | 1–3 hours |
| 9 | Driver collects payment if COD; system processes payment if prepaid; system triggers delivery confirmation to customer via SMS/email with: digital receipt, installation warranty card, customer satisfaction survey link | Driver / System | — | 5 min |
| 10 | Post-delivery: system sends follow-up SMS/email at 48 hours: satisfaction check, product care tips, and offer for related products (e.g., aircon maintenance kit, water filter replacement) | System | CX Manager | Automated |

### System Touchpoints
- POS/ecommerce delivery scheduling module with coverage area validation and fee calculation (W1208.2)
- Technician scheduling calendar integrated with W600 accredited installer database
- Delivery route optimization grouped with standard replenishment routes (W196)
- SMS/email notification engine for customer communications
- Technician tablet app for job details, site inspection, installation documentation, and customer acceptance
- Integration with W138 (installation services), W600 (technician accreditation), W1165 (last-mile dispatch), W196 (route planning)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Failed deliveries due to site unreadiness** — customers underestimate preparation requirements (no electrical outlet, plumbing not installed); wasted technician and driver time; rescheduling cost and customer frustration
- **Technician no-shows** — freelance installation technicians may fail to arrive, leaving driver and customer waiting; backup technician pool needed per region
- **Damage during in-home transport** — tight stairwells, narrow doorways in Philippine homes risk damaging both the product and customer's property; insurance coverage required
- **Scheduling conflicts** — installation technicians often serve multiple retailers; scheduling conflicts arise during peak periods (summer aircon installation season, holiday appliance purchases)
- **Customer not home** — estimated 5–10% no-show rate; driver waits then returns item to store/DC; re-delivery fee applies but customer disputes are common

### Staffing Implication
- 1 Logistics Dispatcher per DC (4) for white-glove delivery coordination: ~4 hours/day on delivery planning + technician scheduling
- Installation Technicians: per W600 accreditation program; ~50–80 technicians nationwide (mix of BuildRight employed and accredited freelancers)
- No incremental logistics headcount — dispatcher role absorbed by existing Logistics Coordinator

### Time Estimate
- Per delivery scheduling: ~5 min customer intake + automated validation
- Per delivery day planning: ~30 min dispatcher time for 30–40 deliveries
- Per white-glove delivery: 15–30 min in-home placement + 1–3 hours installation (varies by product)
- **Chain-wide: ~2,000–3,000 deliveries/month × 1–3 hours each = 2,000–9,000 technician hours/month**

## W1371. 3PL Carrier Onboarding, Qualification Audit & Service Level Agreement Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New 3PL carrier needed due to geographic expansion, capacity shortfall, or carrier termination; or annual carrier requalification cycle |
| **Frequency** | 5–10 new carrier onboardings/year; all carriers requalified annually |
| **Volume** | ~30–40 active 3PL carrier contracts chain-wide (80% of fleet capacity); each carrier operates 2–10 trucks |
| **Owner** | Logistics Manager (HQ) |
| **Participants** | Logistics Manager, VP Supply Chain, Legal Counsel, Finance, Safety Officer, Insurance Provider |

### Background

BuildRight operates with 80% of its truck fleet provided by third-party carriers — a cost-efficient model common in Philippine retail where owning and maintaining a large fleet is capital-intensive. With ~30–40 active 3PL carrier contracts, carrier quality directly impacts store replenishment timeliness, merchandise condition upon delivery, and BuildRight's brand reputation. A poorly qualified carrier that delivers damaged goods or misses delivery windows cascades into stockouts, customer complaints, and DC congestion. This workflow manages the end-to-end carrier lifecycle: onboarding, qualification audit, SLA negotiation, and annual requalification — ensuring every carrier handling BuildRight inventory meets operational, safety, and financial standards.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|

- Carrier master record with full qualification documentation, rate cards, and zone assignments (W1371.8)
- Carrier portal with self-service application, document upload, order acceptance, POD upload, and invoice submission (W1371.2–3, 8)
- Carrier SLA performance dashboard tracking on-time rate, damage rate, POD completion, and incident count (W1371.10)
- Automated SLA breach alerting and penalty calculation (W1371.6d)
- Carrier requalification scheduling with document expiry tracking (W1371.10)
- Integration with W1163 (3PL daily operations), W1166 (carrier rate management & freight audit), W1165 (last-mile dispatch & tracking)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Small carrier operational risk** — many Philippine 3PL carriers are small operators (2–5 trucks) with limited financial reserves; a single truck breakdown or insurance lapse can disrupt BuildRight's delivery schedule for an entire region
- **Insurance adequacy** — cargo insurance coverage of PHP 500K–2M per truckload may be insufficient for high-value loads (power tools, appliances); BuildRight's own goods-in-transit insurance per W863 provides backstop but creates claims processing overhead
- **Driver turnover at 3PL carriers** — carrier drivers change frequently; each new driver must be trained on BuildRight's delivery standards and carrier portal, but 3PL carriers bear this responsibility inconsistently
- **Rate card renegotiation** — fuel price volatility in the Philippines (diesel prices fluctuate PHP 10–20/liter over a year) triggers frequent rate adjustment requests from carriers; fuel surcharge clauses require disciplined index tracking

### Staffing Implication
- Logistics Manager: ~20% of time on carrier management (onboarding, qualification, requalification); absorbed into existing role
- Safety Officer: ~5 days/year for carrier vehicle inspections (on-site visits)
- Legal Counsel: ~5–10 days/year on carrier contract drafting and review
- No incremental headcount

### Time Estimate
- Per carrier onboarding: ~3–4 weeks (sourcing 2–3 days + document collection 1 week + audit 1–2 days + contract 1 week + orientation half day)
- Annual requalification: ~1–2 days/carrier × 30–40 carriers = ~30–80 days/year
- **Total carrier management effort: ~150–200 days/year**

## W1372. Customer Delivery Damage, Loss & Shortage Claim Processing & Carrier Liability Settlement

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Customer reports delivery damage, loss, or shortage upon receipt of ecommerce home delivery (W19); or store reports inbound shipment damage during receiving (W109/W666); or customer reports damage from in-store delivery (W5D) |
| **Frequency** | ~100–200 delivery incidents/month chain-wide (damage, loss, or shortage across all delivery channels) |
| **Volume** | Damage rate target < 0.5% of shipments; at ~5,000 store replenishment + ~42,900 ecommerce deliveries/month, expected incidents ~240–350/month |
| **Owner** | DC Dispatch Coordinator (store deliveries); Ecommerce Operations (ecommerce deliveries) |
| **Participants** | Store Receiving Clerk, Store Manager, Carrier, Customer, Ecommerce Team, Finance, Insurance Provider |

### Background

With ~5,000 store replenishment shipments and ~42,900 ecommerce deliveries per month, delivery incidents are inevitable. BuildRight merchandise ranges from durable items (cement, hardware) to fragile products (tiles, glass, appliances) to high-value goods (power tools worth PHP 5,000–50,000 each). When merchandise arrives damaged, short-shipped, or lost in transit, BuildRight must: (a) resolve the customer's issue immediately (replacement, refund, or redelivery), (b) determine carrier liability, (c) recover costs from the carrier or insurance, and (d) identify patterns indicating systemic carrier performance issues. The financial exposure is significant — a single truckload of tiles or appliances can represent PHP 1–3M in merchandise value.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incident reported: (a) store delivery: Store Receiving Clerk identifies damage/shortage during receiving per W109, records on handheld with photos and variance quantities; (b) ecommerce delivery: Customer reports damage via call center, chat, or website with photos; (c) in-store delivery: Customer contacts store CSR per W5D | Store Receiving Clerk / Customer / CSR | Store Manager / Ecommerce Team | 5–10 min |
| 2 | System creates delivery incident record: (a) linked to shipment ID (W1370); (b) carrier name, driver, vehicle plate; (c) incident type: damage, shortage, loss, wrong item; (d) affected items with quantities and value; (e) customer photos (if applicable) or Store Receiving Clerk photos uploaded; (f) POD status (signed, unsigned, signed with notation) | System / Reporter | DC Dispatch Coordinator | 5 min |
| 3 | For customer-facing deliveries (ecommerce, in-store delivery): immediate customer resolution takes priority — (a) if damage: Ecommerce Team/CSR offers replacement shipment (expedited per W19) or refund per W12; (b) if shortage: system checks if shorted items are available at nearest store or DC for immediate re-shipment; (c) if total loss: full refund or reorder processed; (d) customer resolution must occur within 24 hours of incident report | Ecommerce Team / CSR | Store Manager | 15 min |
| 4 | DC Dispatch Coordinator (or Ecommerce Ops) determines carrier liability: (a) reviews POD documentation — if carrier driver signed clean POD (no damage notation), carrier is liable; if POD noted damage at pickup, liability shifts to DC (poor loading per W836); if no POD captured, carrier is presumed liable per contract terms; (b) for shortage: carrier liable if dispatch quantities (W1370.1) match DC pick confirmation (W1352) but receipt is short — indicates pilferage or loss in transit; (c) for loss (entire shipment): carrier liable unless force majeure (typhoon, accident with police report) | DC Dispatch Coordinator / Ecommerce Ops | Logistics Manager | 30 min |
| 5 | If carrier liable: (a) system generates carrier claim with supporting documentation (shipment record, POD, photos, item values); (b) claim submitted to carrier via carrier portal; (c) carrier has 5 business days to accept or dispute claim; (d) if accepted: carrier pays deduction amount or BuildRight offsets against next payment per W7; (e) if disputed: Logistics Manager mediates with carrier using evidence from POD, GPS tracking, and photos | Logistics Manager / System | VP Supply Chain | 30 min/claim |
| 6 | If claim exceeds carrier's cargo insurance deductible or carrier disputes liability: (a) Finance files claim with BuildRight's goods-in-transit insurance per W863; (b) insurance adjuster may inspect damaged goods; (c) insurance payout processed per policy terms; (d) BuildRight retains right to pursue carrier for insurer's subrogation | Finance / Insurance Provider | CFO | 1–2 weeks |
| 7 | Damaged goods disposition per W91: (a) if resalable at discount: reprocessed and marked down per W93; (b) if repairable: sent to vendor for repair or disposed; (c) if total loss: written off per W92 with carrier/insurance recovery tracked separately; (d) damaged goods must be physically segregated and documented for carrier/insurance inspection before disposition | Store Receiving Clerk / DC Team | DC Manager | Per W91 |
| 8 | Monthly: Logistics Manager reviews delivery incident analytics: (a) incident rate by carrier, route, and merchandise category; (b) average claim value and recovery rate; (c) carriers exceeding damage rate threshold (> 0.5%) flagged for improvement notice or contract review per W1371; (d) top damaged categories (tiles, glass, appliances) reviewed for packaging improvement with Merchandising | Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Delivery incident record creation with shipment linkage, photo upload, and variance tracking (W1372.2)
- Carrier liability determination workflow with POD-based rules engine (W1372.4)
- Carrier claim generation via carrier portal with documentation package (W1372.5)
- Claim offset against carrier payment per W7 (W1372.5d)
- Insurance claim integration with goods-in-transit policy per W863 (W1372.6)
- Damaged goods disposition tracking per W91 (W1372.7)
- Delivery incident analytics dashboard by carrier, route, and category (W1372.8)
- Integration with W109 (store receiving), W666 (receiving QC), W19 (ecommerce delivery), W5D (in-store delivery), W1370 (shipment tracking), W91 (damaged goods), W92 (inventory adjustment), W93 (markdown), W1163 (3PL operations), W1371 (carrier management)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Customer satisfaction impact** — damaged delivery is the #1 driver of negative ecommerce reviews in Philippine retail; slow claim resolution (> 48 hours) leads to social media complaints and chargebacks
- **Carrier claim recovery rate** — small 3PL carriers may dispute claims or lack financial capacity to pay; estimated recovery rate of 60–70% from carriers; insurance recovery covers remainder but adds processing time (2–4 weeks)
- **Tile and glass damage rate** — fragile categories (tiles, glass panels, mirrors) have damage rates of 2–3% vs. 0.5% average, requiring specialized packaging and handling instructions that carriers may not follow
- **Evidence quality** — customer photos taken with phone cameras in poor lighting may not clearly show damage; Store Receiving Clerk photos are more reliable but may not capture all damage before goods are moved from receiving dock

### Staffing Implication
- DC Dispatch Coordinator: ~30 min/day on carrier claim management; absorbed
- Ecommerce Team: ~2 hours/day on customer-facing delivery issue resolution; absorbed
- Logistics Manager: ~2 hours/month on incident analytics; absorbed
- Finance: ~4 hours/month on insurance claim filing; absorbed
- No incremental headcount

### Time Estimate
- Per incident (store delivery): ~45 min (5 min report + 5 min system entry + 30 min carrier liability + 5 min disposition)
- Per incident (ecommerce delivery): ~60 min (10 min customer report + 15 min customer resolution + 30 min carrier liability + 5 min documentation)
- Monthly analytics review: 2 hours
- **Chain-wide: ~200–350 incidents/month × 45–60 min = ~150–350 hours/month**

## W1373. 3PL Carrier Driver Accreditation, Vehicle Inspection & Compliance Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New 3PL driver assigned to BuildRight routes by carrier; or annual driver re-accreditation cycle; or driver involved in delivery incident per W1372 |
| **Frequency** | Ongoing as drivers rotate; estimated 50–80 new driver accreditations/year across all carriers; annual re-accreditation for all active drivers |
| **Volume** | ~150–200 active 3PL drivers handling BuildRight deliveries chain-wide |
| **Owner** | Logistics Manager (HQ) |
| **Participants** | Logistics Manager, Carrier Fleet Manager, Safety Officer, HR (training records) |

### Background

With 80% of BuildRight's fleet operated by 3PL carriers employing ~150–200 drivers, BuildRight does not directly employ these drivers but relies on them to represent the brand during store deliveries and customer home deliveries. In the Philippine trucking industry, many 3PL operators are small companies with informal safety practices, high driver turnover, and minimal training programs. A 3PL driver arriving at a BuildRight store is the face of the company — their professionalism, safety compliance, and delivery handling directly affect store operations and customer perception. This workflow ensures that every driver handling BuildRight cargo meets minimum accreditation standards, operates roadworthy vehicles, and understands BuildRight's delivery protocols.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3PL carrier notifies Logistics Manager of new driver assignment: submits driver's (a) professional driver's license (restriction code 2 for 6-wheeler, code 3 for 10-wheeler); (b) LTO (Land Transportation Office) driving record clearance; (c) NBI/Police clearance; (d) drug test result (per DOLE Department Order 89-09 for drivers of heavy vehicles); (e) basic literacy certification (read/write English or Filipino) | Carrier Fleet Manager / Logistics Manager | — | 1 day |
| 2 | Logistics Manager verifies driver credentials: (a) checks LTO license validity online; (b) verifies restriction codes match assigned vehicle type; (c) reviews NBI clearance; (d) confirms drug test is within 6 months; (e) checks driver's age (minimum 21 years per Philippine trucking standards) | Logistics Manager | — | 30 min/driver |
| 3 | Carrier conducts vehicle inspection and submits Vehicle Condition Report: (a) vehicle registration (current LTO registration); (b) annual vehicle inspection result (LTO Motor Vehicle Inspection); (c) tire condition (minimum tread depth, no recapped tires on front axle); (d) brake system condition; (e) lights, reflectors, and warning devices; (f) cargo securing equipment (tie-down straps, chains, tarpaulins); (g) fire extinguisher (required for cargo vehicles per LTO); (h) for refrigerated trucks (if applicable): reefer unit temperature maintenance verification | Carrier Fleet Manager | Logistics Manager | 1 day |
| 4 | If driver credentials and vehicle condition are satisfactory: Logistics Manager activates driver in system: (a) creates driver accreditation record linked to carrier master; (b) assigns driver to eligible routes/zones; (c) issues BuildRight carrier driver ID badge with photo; (d) grants carrier app access for route plans, POD capture, and delivery confirmations | Logistics Manager | — | 15 min/driver |
| 5 | Driver completes BuildRight delivery orientation (can be done by carrier with BuildRight-provided materials): (a) BuildRight store receiving protocol (check in with Store Receiving Clerk, wait for unloading, obtain POD signature); (b) cargo securing standards (tarpaulin for weather protection, strap tension requirements for tiles and lumber); (c) carrier app usage (route navigation, stop check-in, POD capture, incident reporting); (d) emergency procedures (accident, breakdown, cargo shift during transit); (e) prohibited behaviors (unauthorized stops, drinking, using phone while driving, soliciting tips from stores) | Carrier Fleet Manager / Logistics Manager | Safety Officer | 2 hours/driver |
| 6 | System tracks driver performance: (a) on-time delivery rate per driver; (b) POD completion rate per driver; (c) delivery incident count (damage, shortage) per driver per W1372; (d) driver ratings from Store Receiving Clerks (optional feedback via handheld after each delivery); (e) drivers with > 2 incidents in 30 days flagged for review | System | Logistics Manager | Automated |
| 7 | Annual re-accreditation: (a) driver submits updated license, NBI clearance, drug test; (b) carrier submits updated Vehicle Condition Report; (c) Logistics Manager reviews 12-month driver performance; (d) drivers with satisfactory performance and clean records renewed; (e) drivers with persistent issues not renewed — carrier must assign replacement driver | Logistics Manager / Carrier Fleet Manager | VP Supply Chain | 30 min/driver |
| 8 | If driver involved in delivery incident per W1372: (a) Logistics Manager reviews incident severity; (b) for minor incidents (first occurrence, minor damage): driver receives written warning and mandatory re-orientation per Step 5; (c) for major incidents (high-value damage, theft suspicion, safety violation): driver accreditation suspended pending investigation; carrier must assign replacement driver within 48 hours; (d) for confirmed theft or gross negligence: driver accreditation permanently revoked; carrier notified; incident reported to LTO if warranted | Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | Per incident |

### System Touchpoints
- Driver accreditation record linked to carrier master with credential tracking and expiry dates (W1373.2, 4)
- Vehicle Condition Record per driver/vehicle with inspection date and findings (W1373.3)
- BuildRight carrier driver ID badge issuance tracking (W1373.4)
- Driver performance dashboard with on-time rate, POD completion, incident count, and store ratings (W1373.6)
- Annual re-accreditation scheduling with credential expiry alerts (W1373.7)
- Incident-linked driver review and accreditation suspension workflow (W1373.8)
- Integration with W1163 (3PL daily ops), W1165 (last-mile dispatch), W1371 (carrier onboarding), W1372 (delivery incidents), W1370 (shipment tracking)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Driver substitution without notification** — 3PL carriers may substitute drivers without informing BuildRight, sending unaccredited drivers who don't know BuildRight's delivery protocols; Store Receiving Clerks must check driver ID badge at receiving dock
- **High driver turnover in Philippine trucking** — annual turnover rates of 30–50% among 3PL drivers mean frequent re-accreditation processing; carrier responsibility to notify BuildRight of driver changes is inconsistently followed
- **Fake or expired credentials** — LTO license verification is available online but some carriers submit expired or fake licenses; periodic spot-checks with LTO database mitigate
- **Drug test validity** — one-time drug test at onboarding does not guarantee ongoing compliance; BuildRight cannot mandate random drug testing for 3PL drivers (not their employer) but can require it as a contract condition per W1371

### Staffing Implication
- Logistics Manager: ~10% of time on driver accreditation (~50 days/year); absorbed
- Safety Officer: assists with vehicle inspections during carrier onboarding per W1371 (~5 days/year); absorbed
- No incremental headcount

### Time Estimate
- Per new driver accreditation: ~2.5 hours (30 min verification + 15 min system setup + 2 hours orientation)
- Per re-accreditation: 30 min desk review
- Per incident review: 30–60 min
- Annual effort: ~150–200 hours/year
