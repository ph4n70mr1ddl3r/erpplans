## W1348. Fleet Vehicle Preventive Maintenance Scheduling, Work Order & Parts Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vehicle odometer or engine-hours threshold reached; or calendar-based service interval due |
| **Frequency** | Per-vehicle: every 10,000 km or 6 months (whichever comes first); ~20% owned fleet (40 trucks) + 80% third-party (160 trucks, third-party maintenance outside scope) |
| **Volume** | ~40 owned trucks × 2–3 services/year = ~80–120 preventive maintenance (PM) events/year; ~7–10 PM events/month |
| **Owner** | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor, Mechanic, Parts Coordinator, Logistics Planner, Finance (capex approval for major services) |

### Background

BuildRight's owned fleet (~40 trucks, a mix of 10-wheeler wing vans and 6-wheeler trucks) transports merchandise from 4 DCs to 200 stores. Vehicle breakdowns during delivery routes cause missed replenishment windows, spoiled perishable/seasonal shipments, and emergency 3PL spot-hire costs at premium rates. Preventive maintenance scheduling must balance vehicle availability against service needs, especially during peak seasons when all trucks are fully deployed.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System tracks vehicle odometer and engine-hours via telematics (W199); auto-generates PM alert when threshold is within 500 km or 2 weeks of due date | System | — | Automated |
| 2 | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor reviews upcoming PM schedule against delivery dispatch plan; selects service window that minimizes fleet impact (typically between dispatch waves or on low-demand days) | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | Logistics Manager | 30 min/week |
| 3 | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor creates digital PM work order: standard service checklist (oil change, brake inspection, tire rotation, fluid top-up, belt/hose inspection, electrical system check) | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | — | 15 min/work order |
| 4 | Parts Coordinator checks parts availability for scheduled PM items; orders parts if not in stock from approved automotive suppliers (lead time: 2–5 days for common parts) | Parts Coordinator | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | 30 min/work order |
| 5 | Mechanic performs PM per work order checklist; records findings, parts used, and labor hours in system; flags any issues requiring follow-up repair | Mechanic | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | 4–8 hours/truck |
| 6 | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor reviews completed work order; approves or schedules follow-up repair for flagged issues; updates vehicle maintenance history | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | — | 15 min/work order |
| 7 | Finance receives completed work order with parts and labor costs; processes vendor payment for externally sourced parts; records maintenance cost against vehicle asset | Finance (AP) | Controller | Per W7 |
| 8 | System updates vehicle availability status back to "active" in dispatch planning module | System | — | Automated |

### System Touchpoints
- Telematics integration (W199) for odometer/engine-hours tracking
- Automated PM alert generation with threshold configuration
- Digital work order creation, assignment, and completion tracking
- Parts inventory management for commonly stocked maintenance items (filters, belts, brake pads, fluids)
- Vehicle maintenance history log with cost accumulation per vehicle
- Fleet availability status feed to dispatch planning (W196)
- Finance integration for maintenance cost recording against vehicle fixed asset

### Time Estimate
- PM scheduling: 30 min/week for Fleet Maintenance Supervisor
- Work order creation: 15 min × 7–10 PM events/month = ~2 hours/month
- Parts coordination: 30 min × 7–10 PM events/month = ~4 hours/month
- Mechanic labor: 4–8 hours × 7–10 PM events/month = ~28–80 hours/month (1–2 full-time mechanics)
- Work order review: 15 min × 7–10 events/month = ~2 hours/month
- Total: ~35–90 hours/month depending on service complexity

### Pain Points / Risks
- PM scheduling conflicts with peak delivery periods: during Q4 ber-months and bi-monthly sale events, taking a truck offline for PM reduces delivery capacity when demand is highest; Supervisors may defer PM, increasing breakdown risk
- Parts availability for older trucks: BuildRight's fleet includes trucks of varying age; parts for older models may have longer lead times or be discontinued, forcing emergency sourcing at premium prices
- Inconsistent mechanic findings documentation: if mechanics do not thoroughly document wear patterns during PM inspections, emerging issues (e.g., brake lining approaching minimum thickness) are missed, leading to on-road failures

### Staffing Implication
- 1 Fleet Maintenance Supervisor (dedicated role) manages PM scheduling, work orders, and parts coordination across all 4 DC locations; ~6–8 hours/week on PM activities
- 2 Mechanics (full-time, split across DC3 Laguna and DC1 Davao, the two largest operations) handle 7–10 PM events/month plus ad-hoc repairs; each mechanic can handle ~3–4 PM events/week
- 1 Parts Coordinator (shared with general DC supplies procurement) dedicates ~4 hours/month to PM parts management

## W1349. Fleet Tire Lifecycle Management, Tread Monitoring & Replacement Scheduling

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Tire tread depth reaches minimum threshold (3 mm for steer axles, 2 mm for drive/trailer axles per Philippine LTO regulations); or tire age exceeds 5 years; or tire damage detected during pre-trip inspection |
| **Frequency** | ~40 owned trucks × 10–18 tires per truck = ~400–720 tires under management; average replacement every 60,000–80,000 km or 2–3 years per tire; ~150–200 tire replacements/year |
| **Volume** | ~12–17 tire replacements/month across owned fleet |
| **Owner** | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor |
| **Participants** | Mechanic, Driver (pre-trip inspection), Parts Coordinator, Tire Supplier, Finance (AP) |

### Background

Tires represent a significant operating cost for BuildRight's owned fleet of 40 trucks navigating Philippine provincial roads, which include rough concrete, unpaved construction site access roads, and occasional flood-damaged surfaces. Tire failures during delivery cause route delays, potential cargo damage, and safety hazards. Systematic tread monitoring and proactive replacement scheduling prevents roadside failures and optimizes tire spend by replacing tires at the optimal point in their wear lifecycle — not too early (wasting remaining tread life) and not too late (risking blowout).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Driver performs pre-trip inspection: visually checks tire condition, notes any visible damage (cuts, bulges, uneven wear); reports concerns via driver mobile app | Driver | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | 5 min/pre-trip |
| 2 | During scheduled PM (W1348), Mechanic measures tread depth on all tires using calibrated depth gauge; records readings per tire position (steer left, steer right, drive inner, drive outer, trailer positions) | Mechanic | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | 15 min/truck |
| 3 | System logs tread depth readings by tire ID; calculates remaining useful life based on wear rate trends; auto-generates replacement alert when tread approaches minimum threshold | System | — | Automated |
| 4 | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor reviews tire replacement forecast monthly: groups replacement orders by DC to consolidate supplier deliveries; considers retreading for drive/trailer positions where casing integrity is good | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | Logistics Manager | 2 hours/month |
| 5 | Parts Coordinator places tire order with approved tire supplier (Philippine distributors: Yokohama, Goodyear, Continental); arranges delivery to respective DC maintenance bay | Parts Coordinator | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | 30 min/order |
| 6 | Mechanic performs tire replacement or retreading; records old tire condition (tread depth, damage type, reason for removal) and new tire serial number/position | Mechanic | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | 1–2 hours/truck (2–4 tires) |
| 7 | Old tires evaluated for retreading potential, recycling, or disposal; serviceable casings sent to retreading partner; unserviceable tires disposed per DENR waste tire guidelines | Mechanic / Parts Coordinator | Fleet Maintenance Supervisor | 15 min/set |
| 8 | Finance records tire replacement cost; capitalizes tires above PHP 5,000 per unit or expenses as maintenance consumable below threshold | Finance (AP) | Controller | Per W7 |

### System Touchpoints
- Tire inventory register with serial number, position assignment, purchase date, and mileage accumulation
- Tread depth logging per tire ID with trend analysis and remaining-life calculation
- Automated replacement alert generation based on tread depth, age, or mileage thresholds
- Integration with PM work order system (W1348) for scheduled tread measurement
- Supplier purchase order integration for tire procurement
- Tire cost tracking per vehicle for total cost of ownership analysis

### Time Estimate
- Pre-trip tire inspection by driver: 5 min/truck/trip (absorbed in existing pre-trip routine)
- PM tread measurement: 15 min/truck × 40 trucks × 2–3 times/year = ~20–30 hours/year of mechanic time
- Monthly replacement forecast review: 2 hours/month × Fleet Maintenance Supervisor
- Tire replacement labor: 1–2 hours/truck × 12–17 replacements/month = ~12–34 hours/month
- Old tire disposition: 15 min/set × 12–17 sets/month = ~3–4 hours/month
- Total incremental: ~40–60 hours/month across all roles

### Pain Points / Risks
- Premature tire wear from provincial road conditions: roads to construction sites and provincial stores accelerate tire degradation beyond standard wear models, making remaining-life predictions inaccurate
- Retreading quality varies among Philippine retreading shops; a poorly retreaded tire may fail within weeks, negating the ~40–50% cost savings vs. new tire and creating safety liability
- Tire theft or substitution: with ~400–720 tires in circulation across 4 DCs and multiple maintenance bays, tracking individual tire assets requires disciplined serial number management; discrepancies between system records and physical inventory indicate potential theft or unrecorded replacements

### Staffing Implication
- Fleet Maintenance Supervisor: ~2 hours/month on tire forecast review; absorbed within existing fleet management duties
- Mechanics: tire replacement and measurement absorbed within ~28–80 hours/month PM capacity
- Parts Coordinator: ~30 min/month on tire procurement; minimal incremental effort

## W653. Fleet Accident & Incident Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vehicle accident, cargo damage incident, or third-party property damage during delivery operations |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 incidents/month across fleet |
| **Volume** | ~2–4 per week |
| **Owner** | Fleet Safety Officer |
| **Participants** | Driver, Fleet Manager, Insurance Claims Coordinator, LP Analyst, HR Business Partner (if driver injury) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Driver activates incident protocol: stops vehicle, secures scene, checks for injuries, calls emergency services if needed, notifies Fleet Manager via mobile app within 15 minutes | Driver | Fleet Safety Officer | Immediate |
| 2 | Driver collects evidence: photographs of vehicle damage, third-party vehicle/property damage, road conditions, cargo condition; obtains third-party information (name, contact, vehicle details, insurance); identifies witnesses; does NOT admit fault | Driver | Fleet Safety Officer | 30 min |
| 3 | For accidents with injuries or significant property damage: Driver files police report at nearest PNP station; obtains police blotter reference number | Driver | Fleet Safety Officer | 1–2 hours |
| 4 | Fleet Safety Officer receives incident report; classifies severity (Minor: cosmetic damage only, Moderate: functional damage, cargo affected, Major: injury, total loss, third-party claim); activates appropriate response level | Fleet Safety Officer | Fleet Manager | 30 min |
| 5 | Fleet Manager arranges: vehicle recovery/towing if undrivable, cargo salvage/transfer to backup vehicle for delivery completion, driver medical assessment if injured | Fleet Manager | Fleet Safety Officer | 2–4 hours |
| 6 | Insurance Claims Coordinator initiates insurance claim per W610: submits incident report with evidence, obtains repair estimate from accredited shop, tracks claim to settlement | Insurance Claims Coordinator | Fleet Manager | 1–2 weeks |
| 7 | Fleet Safety Officer conducts investigation: reviews telematics data (speed, braking, location), driver history, route conditions; determines root cause (driver error, vehicle failure, third-party fault, road hazard); classifies preventability | Fleet Safety Officer | Fleet Manager | 3 days |
| 8 | For preventable accidents: Fleet Manager applies progressive discipline per driver safety program (W197); assigns remedial training; adjusts driver safety score | Fleet Manager | VP Supply Chain | Per W197 |
| 9 | Monthly: Fleet Safety Officer produces incident analytics: incident rate per 100,000 km, preventability rate, cost per incident, trend by driver/route/vehicle/time; feeds driver performance dashboard (W197) | Fleet Safety Officer | Fleet Manager | 2 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- Fleet telematics (W199), incident reporting mobile app, insurance claims module (W610), driver management (W197), repair work order system

### Time Estimate
- Scene management: 1–2 hours; investigation: 3 days; claim resolution: 1–2 weeks

### Pain Points / Risks
- Driver panic or flight from accident scene; third-party fraud (staged accidents); evidence collection quality varies by driver composure; insurance claim processing delays in Philippine insurance market; cargo damage claims with customer impact during peak season

### Staffing Implication
- **Fleet Safety Officer**: ~2 hours/month on incident analytics + ad hoc investigation time (varies by incident frequency). Absorbed within existing safety role.
- **Fleet Manager**: per-incident response time varies; ~2–4 hours per moderate/major incident for recovery coordination. Absorbed within existing role.
- **Insurance Claims Coordinator**: ~1–2 weeks per claim tracking. Absorbed within existing insurance/finance role.

## W799. Vehicle Acquisition, Registration, Insurance & Disposal Lifecycle Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Fleet expansion need (new store openings); vehicle end-of-life (mileage/age threshold); insurance renewal; annual registration |
| **Frequency** | Vehicle acquisition: 5-10 per year; registration: annual per vehicle; insurance: annual renewal; disposal: 3-5 per year |
| **Volume** | ~40 owned vehicles (20% of fleet); mixed fleet of owned and 3PL per model-company profile |
| **Owner** | Fleet Manager |
| **Participants** | VP Supply Chain, Finance, Procurement, Insurance Provider, LTO, External Dealer |

### Background

BuildRight operates approximately 40 owned trucks (20% of total fleet, per model-company profile) for DC-to-store delivery and inter-DC transfers. The remaining 80% of transport is handled by 3PL partners per W242. Each owned vehicle has a lifecycle from acquisition through registration, insurance, maintenance, and eventual disposal. Philippine vehicles require annual registration with the Land Transportation Office (LTO), emission testing per Clean Air Act, and comprehensive insurance including third-party liability. This workflow governs the full vehicle asset lifecycle.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Vehicle Requirement & Acquisition**: When fleet expansion is needed: (a) Fleet Manager assesses vehicle type needed (6-wheeler, 10-wheeler, wing van) based on route characteristics and volume per W196; (b) Procurement issues RFP per W224 to authorized truck dealers (Isuzu, Hino, Mitsubishi Fuso); (c) evaluate bids on: purchase price, fuel efficiency, after-sales support, parts availability in Philippines, resale value; (d) Finance approves CAPEX per W21; (e) register new vehicle in Fleet Master per W401 with all specifications, purchase date, warranty terms | Fleet Manager / Procurement | VP Supply Chain | 4-6 weeks per vehicle |
| 2 | **LTO Registration & Compliance**: All owned vehicles must be registered annually with LTO: (a) emission testing per Clean Air Act (RA 8749) at LTO-accredited testing center; (b) vehicle inspection at LTO; (c) compulsory third-party liability (CTPL) insurance; (d) LTO registration fee payment; (e) registration sticker and plate renewal; (f) system tracks registration expiry dates with 60-day advance alerts per W506; (g) vehicle cannot be operated with expired registration per Philippine law | Fleet Admin | Fleet Manager | 1-2 days per vehicle |
| 3 | **Insurance Management**: Annual fleet insurance renewal: (a) Fleet Manager reviews current coverage: comprehensive, third-party liability, cargo insurance, acts of God (typhoon, flood); (b) obtain competitive quotes from insurance providers; (c) negotiate fleet discount based on claim history per W610; (d) ensure coverage meets BuildRight cargo liability requirements; (e) register insurance policy in system per W59 with expiry alerts; (f) add new vehicles to policy; remove disposed vehicles | Fleet Manager | VP Supply Chain / Finance | 1-2 weeks annual renewal |
| 4 | **Preventive Maintenance Scheduling**: Fleet Manager schedules PM per W188 based on: (a) manufacturer-recommended intervals (mileage or time-based); (b) telematics data per W199 (actual odometer vs. PM threshold); (c) seasonal requirements (pre-typhoon inspection, post-typhoon undercarriage check); (d) safety-critical items: brakes, tires, steering, lights inspected every 10,000 km; (e) system auto-generates PM work orders when mileage threshold approaches; (f) vehicle grounded if PM overdue by >10% | Fleet Manager / Mechanic | VP Supply Chain | Per W188 schedule |
| 5 | **Vehicle Disposal**: When vehicle reaches end-of-life (typically 8-10 years or 500,000 km): (a) Fleet Manager recommends disposal based on: maintenance cost trend, reliability history, resale value projection, safety risk; (b) Finance approves disposal per W39 fixed asset disposal; (c) remove vehicle registration from LTO; (d) cancel insurance coverage; (e) sell via competitive bid to used truck dealers; (f) or trade-in with dealer for new vehicle acquisition per Step 1; (g) remove from Fleet Master per W401; (h) book gain/loss on disposal per W39 | Fleet Manager / Finance | VP Supply Chain | 2-4 weeks per vehicle |

### System Touchpoints

- Fleet Master per W401 with vehicle specifications, lifecycle dates, and cost history
- LTO registration tracking with expiry alerts per W506
- Insurance policy management per W59 with renewal alerts
- Preventive maintenance scheduling per W188 with telematics integration per W199
- Fixed Asset module per W39 for depreciation, CAPEX, and disposal accounting
- Procurement integration per W224 for vehicle acquisition bidding
- Fuel management integration per W198 for total cost of ownership tracking
- Telematics integration per W199 for odometer-based PM triggers

### Pain Points / Risks

- **LTO registration backlogs**: Philippine LTO offices often have processing backlogs; vehicles with pending registration cannot legally operate, disrupting delivery schedules
- **Emission testing failures**: older vehicles may fail emission testing, requiring engine overhaul before registration can proceed; unplanned cost and downtime
- **Insurance premium increases after claims**: fleet accident rate per W653 directly impacts insurance premiums; high claim frequency can increase premiums by 20-40%
- **Vehicle resale value depreciation**: Philippine truck resale values drop significantly after 8 years; early disposal (at 6-7 years) maximizes resale value but increases annual CAPEX
- **Parts availability for older vehicles**: manufacturers may discontinue parts for older models; maintenance costs and downtime increase as vehicles age past parts support
- **Driver abuse and vehicle damage**: driver behavior directly impacts vehicle lifespan; telematics per W199 and driver scorecard per W197 are critical for vehicle longevity

### Staffing Implication

- **Fleet Manager**: ~8-12 hours/month on vehicle lifecycle management; absorbed by existing role
- **Fleet Admin**: ~4-6 hours/month on registration and insurance paperwork; absorbed by existing admin role
- **No incremental headcount**.

### Time Estimate

- Vehicle acquisition: 4-6 weeks per vehicle
- Annual registration: 1-2 days per vehicle × 40 vehicles = 40-80 days/year
- Insurance renewal: 1-2 weeks/year (all vehicles)
- PM scheduling: per W188 (ongoing)
- Vehicle disposal: 2-4 weeks per vehicle

## W1207. Third-Party Logistics (3PL) Provider Quarterly Performance Review & Contract Renewal

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Quarterly (Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4) per contract SLA review schedule; or triggered by persistent service failures exceeding threshold |
| **Frequency** | Quarterly performance reviews; annual contract renewal evaluation |
| **Volume** | ~15–20 active 3PL contracts (80% of fleet is third-party per company profile) |
| **Owner** | Logistics Manager |
| **Participants** | Logistics Manager, 3PL Account Manager, Finance Manager, Procurement Manager, Fleet Supervisor |

### Background

BuildRight Depot operates a mixed fleet model: 20% owned and 80% third-party trucks. Third-party logistics providers handle the majority of outbound distribution (DC → store replenishment) and last-mile customer deliveries. With ~5,000 store replenishment orders and ~42,900 ecommerce deliveries per month, 3PL performance directly impacts store replenishment on-time rates (target ≥ 95%) and customer delivery SLA compliance. Quarterly performance reviews ensure carriers maintain service standards, identify improvement opportunities, and inform contract renewal or rebidding decisions. This workflow complements W197 (driver performance — owned fleet), W198 (fuel management), W653 (fleet accidents), and W1163 (3PL daily operations) by governing the strategic vendor relationship and contract lifecycle.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | System compiles **quarterly 3PL scorecard** for each active provider: (a) on-time delivery rate (target ≥ 95% for store replenishment; ≥ 90% for customer delivery), (b) delivery damage rate (target < 0.5% of shipments), (c) invoice accuracy rate (target ≥ 98%), (d) vehicle utilization rate, (e) driver compliance with LGU truck ban hours per W431, (f) safety incident count, (g) POD (proof of delivery) return timeliness, (h) cost per delivery vs. contract rate, (i) escalation response time | System / Logistics Analyst | Logistics Manager | Automated |
| 2 | Logistics Manager reviews each 3PL scorecard; highlights providers below SLA thresholds; prepares quarterly review presentation with: scorecard summary, trend analysis (quarter-over-quarter), top issues, and recommended actions (continue, improvement plan, rebid) | Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2–3 hours |
| 3 | Logistics Manager conducts **quarterly business review meeting** with each 3PL Account Manager: (a) present scorecard results, (b) discuss root causes for below-threshold metrics, (c) agree on corrective actions and timelines, (d) review upcoming volume projections and capacity requirements, (e) discuss rate adjustments if triggered by contract escalation/de-escalation clauses | Logistics Manager / 3PL Account Manager | VP Supply Chain | 1–2 hours per provider |
| 4 | Finance Manager reviews 3PL cost performance: (a) actual freight cost per ton-km vs. contract rate, (b) fuel surcharge reconciliation, (c) demurrage and detention charges per W249, (d) invoice discrepancy history; Finance Manager flags cost anomalies for Logistics Manager investigation | Finance Manager | CFO | 1 hour |
| 5 | If 3PL provider fails to meet minimum SLA for 2 consecutive quarters: Logistics Manager initiates **improvement plan** with: (a) specific corrective actions, (b) 30-day remediation timeline, (c) weekly check-in calls, (d) consequence clause (contract termination if not remediated within 60 days) | Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2 hours |
| 6 | **Annual contract renewal**: for contracts expiring within 90 days: (a) Procurement Manager issues RFQ to current provider and 2–3 alternative providers, (b) Logistics Manager provides volume forecast and service requirements, (c) Procurement Manager evaluates bids on: cost (40%), service quality track record (30%), capacity and coverage (20%), safety and compliance (10%), (d) VP Supply Chain approves award or re-award | Procurement Manager / Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 1–2 weeks |
| 7 | System updates 3PL vendor master with renewed contract terms: new rates, SLA targets, capacity commitments, effective dates; deactivates expired contracts; Logistics Manager communicates any provider changes to DC Supervisors and Store Managers | Logistics Manager / System | Procurement Manager | 30 min |
| 8 | If contract awarded to new provider: Logistics Manager executes 3PL onboarding per W1163: system integration testing, driver badge issuance, route familiarization, and pilot runs before full cutover | Logistics Manager | VP Supply Chain | 2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- 3PL scorecard automated compilation from: delivery tracking system (W199 telematics), POD return tracking, invoice matching (W7), damage claim records (W653), truck ban compliance (W431)
- Vendor master with contract terms, rate tables, SLA targets, and renewal dates
- Procurement RFQ module for annual rebidding
- Integration with W1163 (3PL daily operations), W198 (fuel monitoring), W653 (accident management), W249 (demurrage), W431 (truck ban)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **3PL capacity crunch during peak seasons** (Q4 holiday, typhoon recovery) — carriers prioritize larger clients; BuildRight's regional coverage may be deprioritized by national 3PLs
- **Rate escalation disputes** — fuel surcharges, toll fee increases, and wage adjustments create contract friction; clear escalation clauses in contracts mitigate
- **Service degradation during transition** — switching 3PL providers introduces risk of missed deliveries during onboarding; parallel running with old and new provider recommended
- **Data visibility gaps** — smaller provincial 3PLs may lack GPS/telematics, limiting real-time tracking and scorecard accuracy
- **Single-provider dependency** — concentration of volume with 1–2 dominant 3PLs creates leverage risk during renewal negotiations

### Staffing Implication
- Logistics Manager: ~40–60 hours/quarter for all 3PL reviews (15–20 providers × 2–3 hours each for preparation + meeting)
- Logistics Analyst: ~8 hours/quarter for scorecard compilation (automated with analyst validation)
- Finance Manager: ~1 hour/quarter for cost review
- No incremental headcount — absorbed by existing roles

### Time Estimate
- Per provider quarterly review: 2–3 hours preparation + 1–2 hours meeting = ~3–5 hours
- All providers per quarter: 15–20 × 3–5 hours = 45–100 hours
- Annual contract renewals: ~1–2 weeks per renewal event (typically 3–5 renewals per year)
- **Annual estimate: ~300–500 person-hours/year**

## W1256. Returnable Transport Packaging (RTP) Pool Management, Tracking & Reconciliation

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — weekly RTP inventory reconciliation; monthly pool health review; ad-hoc triggered by RTP shortage at DC or store accumulation alert |
| **Frequency** | Weekly reconciliation; monthly pool review; annual procurement cycle for RTP replenishment |
| **Volume** | ~2,000 RTP units (pallets, plastic totes, wire baskets) in circulation across 4 DCs and 200 stores; ~5,000 DC-to-store shipments per month using RTP |
| **Owner** | Logistics Planner / DC Operations Manager |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Receiving Clerk, 3PL Transport Coordinator, Procurement Specialist, Finance Controller |

### Background

BuildRight Depot's DC-to-store distribution network moves ~5,000 replenishment shipments per month across 200 stores. Each shipment uses returnable transport packaging (RTP) — standardized plastic pallets, stackable totes for hardware/fasteners, wire baskets for lumber, and insulated containers for temperature-sensitive items (paint, chemicals). RTP represents a significant capital investment (~PHP 2,000–5,000 per unit) and is designed to be returned empty to the DC after each delivery cycle, reducing single-use cardboard and shrink-wrap costs by an estimated PHP 15–20M per year. However, RTP pool management is a persistent challenge in Philippine retail: (a) stores accumulate RTP and fail to return it promptly, creating DC shortages; (b) RTP is repurposed by store staff for in-store display or storage rather than returned; (c) 3PL transport partners may not load empty RTP on return trips, prioritizing loaded deliveries; (d) RTP damage rate is estimated at 5–8% per year due to rough handling, forklift damage, and UV degradation in outdoor lumber yards. This workflow manages the RTP pool lifecycle — tracking, reconciliation, return logistics, and replenishment procurement — to maintain adequate pool levels and minimize loss.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Weekly RTP inventory reconciliation**: DC Operations team reconciles RTP pool inventory: (a) count RTP units at each DC (by type: pallets, totes, wire baskets); (b) query store-level RTP on-hand from system (stores scan RTP on receiving and log returns); (c) count RTP on 3PL trucks (in-transit); (d) calculate pool variance: total acquired – (DC on-hand + store on-hand + in-transit) = unaccounted loss; (e) investigate variances > 2% of total pool | DC Shift Supervisor | DC Operations Manager | 2 hours/week |
| 2 | **Store RTP return enforcement**: When DC-to-store delivery is made, driver collects empty RTP from previous delivery: (a) driver scans returning RTP units with handheld — system matches to store's outstanding RTP balance; (b) store Receiving Clerk confirms RTP return count and condition; (c) system updates store RTP on-hand and DC RTP on-hand; (d) for stores with > 20 outstanding RTP units (accumulation threshold): system alerts Store Manager for mandatory return on next delivery; (e) stores with > 50 outstanding units for > 30 days: flagged to Regional Director for action | Receiving Clerk / Driver | Store Manager | 10 min per delivery |
| 3 | **Damaged RTP disposition**: Stores and DCs inspect RTP condition at each return: (a) **Grade A** (serviceable): clean, no cracks, all features functional — return to pool; (b) **Grade B** (repairable): cracked but structurally sound — send to DC for repair (plastic welding, wire straightening); (c) **Grade C** (unserviceable): broken, warped, contaminated — disposition as scrap per W443; (d) system tracks damage rate by store, DC, and 3PL partner; (e) stores with > 10% damage rate receive handling retraining | Receiving Clerk / DC Quality Inspector | DC Operations Manager | 5 min per RTP return |
| 4 | **Monthly pool health review**: Logistics Planner reviews RTP pool health: (a) total pool size vs. target (minimum 2,500 units for current volume); (b) utilization rate — % of pool in active circulation vs. idle at stores; (c) loss rate trend — unaccounted losses as % of total pool per month; (d) damage rate trend; (e) average RTP turnaround time (DC → store → DC return); (f) calculate replacement procurement need: estimated losses + estimated damage = new RTP procurement quantity | Logistics Planner | DC Operations Manager | 3 hours/month |
| 5 | **Annual RTP procurement**: Procurement Specialist executes annual RTP replenishment purchase: (a) determine procurement quantity based on pool health review; (b) obtain competitive quotes from RTP suppliers (minimum 3 quotes for > PHP 500,000 purchase); (c) submit capex/expense request per approval matrix (W21); (d) manage delivery of new RTP units to DCs; (e) update pool master with new unit count and supplier warranty terms | Procurement Specialist | Finance Controller | 8 hours/year |

### System Touchpoints
- **WMS** — RTP tracking at DC level, inbound/outbound RTP count
- **Inventory Module** — store-level RTP on-hand tracking, return logging
- **Transport Planning Module** — driver RTP collection assignment, return trip loading
- **Procurement Module** — annual RTP purchase order, supplier management
- **BI Platform** — RTP pool dashboard, loss rate analytics, turnaround time tracking
- **Barcode/RFID** — RTP unit identification (barcode labels or RFID tags for automated counting)

### Pain Points / Risks
- RTP tracking accuracy depends on store scanning discipline — busy stores may skip RTP return scanning during peak hours
- 3PL drivers may not have space for empty RTP on return trips if truck is loaded with DSD vendor goods
- Plastic pallets degrade in Philippine heat and UV exposure — outdoor lumber yard storage accelerates degradation
- Stores may resist returning RTP if repurposed for in-store use (display risers, storage bins)
- Initial RTP capital investment is significant (~PHP 5–10M for 2,000 units) — ROI depends on loss rate staying below 10% per year

### Staffing Implication
- DC Shift Supervisor: 2 hours/week on reconciliation
- Logistics Planner: 3 hours/month on pool health review
- Receiving Clerks: 10 min additional per delivery for RTP scanning

### Time Estimate
- Weekly reconciliation: 2 hours/week = 104 hours/year
- Monthly review: 3 hours/month = 36 hours/year
- Store RTP handling: 10 min × 5,000 deliveries/month = 50,000 min/month (~833 hours/month)
- **Annual estimate: ~10,100 person-hours/year** (mostly distributed across store receiving staff)
