## W838. CCTV & Surveillance System Daily Operations & Incident Review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — daily operations review; ad-hoc triggered by exception report (W837) or incident report |
| **Frequency** | Daily (continuous monitoring during store hours; incident review within 24 hours) |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × average 2–3 incident reviews per day = ~400–600 reviews; 24/7 continuous monitoring at 4 DCs |
| **Owner** | LP Officer (Store-Level) / Security Operations Center (SOC) Lead |
| **Participants** | LP Analyst, Store Manager, IT Security, External Security Agency (contracted) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Verify all store CCTV cameras online and recording — automated health check at store open; flag any offline cameras to IT Security for immediate remediation | IT Security | SOC Lead | 20 min |
| 2 | Monitor real-time camera feeds at SOC for 4 DCs and 10 highest-shrinkage stores (rotating selection monthly) | SOC Operator | SOC Lead | Continuous (shift-based) |
| 3 | Review flagged incidents from exception report (W837) — retrieve footage for timestamp window, document observation, tag for investigation if warranted | LP Analyst | LP Manager (Regional) | 60 min |
| 4 | Conduct spot-check surveillance review — random selection of 5 stores per day for cashier area, entrance/exit, and high-value merchandise zones | LP Analyst | LP Manager (Regional) | 45 min |
| 5 | Archive incident footage per retention policy — 30 days routine, 1 year for active investigation, 7 years for prosecuted cases (aligned with BIR/legal requirements) | LP Analyst | SOC Lead | 15 min |
| 6 | Generate daily CCTV operational status report — camera uptime %, incident captures, response time to offline alerts | SOC Operator | SOC Lead | 15 min |

### System Touchpoints
- **CCTV/DVR/NVR System** — camera health monitoring, footage retrieval, archival
- **ERP LP Module** — incident tagging, case linking, evidence management
- **IT Operations Monitoring** — camera uptime alerts, storage capacity tracking
- **Access Control System** — integration with entrance/exit camera feeds
- **AI Video Analytics Platform** (future) — automated behavior detection, person tracking

### Pain Points / Risks
- Camera uptime target is 98%; actual may be lower in provincial stores with poor connectivity
- Storage capacity for 30-day retention across 200 stores requires ~500 TB
- SOC staffing for 24/7 monitoring is expensive; currently only 4 DCs and 10 stores covered
- Privacy concerns under RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act) — CCTV signage and notice requirements

### Staffing Implication
- SOC: 3 operators × 3 shifts = 9 operators + 1 SOC Lead (national)
- LP Analysts include CCTV review in daily exception workflow (W837)
- IT Security: 1 FTE dedicated to CCTV infrastructure maintenance

### Time Estimate
- Daily health check: 20 min automated
- Incident review: 60 min per analyst per day
- Spot checks: 45 min per analyst per day
- Archival and reporting: 30 min per analyst per day
- **Total daily effort: ~6 analyst-hours + 9 SOC operator shifts**

## W844. Store Entrance/Exit Audit & Electronic Article Surveillance (EAS) Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — monthly per store; or triggered by EAS alarm frequency spike or shrinkage result |
| **Frequency** | Monthly per store (200 stores = ~200 audits/month); daily EAS alarm response |
| **Volume** | ~200 entrance/exit audits/month; ~500–800 EAS alarm responses per day chain-wide |
| **Owner** | LP Officer (Store-Level) / Security Guard (contracted) |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, LP Analyst, Security Guard Agency Supervisor, IT Security |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | EAS alarm response — security guard approaches customer, requests to verify purchase, checks receipt against bag contents, tags/detags merchandise, logs alarm in incident register | Security Guard | Store Manager | 5 min per alarm |
| 2 | Daily EAS alarm log review — Store Manager reviews incident register; flag repeat alarm locations (may indicate tag deactivation failure), repeat individuals, or alarm-to-apprehension ratio | Store Manager | LP Manager (Regional) | 15 min daily |
| 3 | Monthly entrance/exit audit — verify EAS gate functionality, tag application compliance at checkout, source-tagging compliance for high-risk SKUs, security guard presence and procedure adherence | LP Analyst | LP Manager (Regional) | 60 min per store |
| 4 | EAS tag inventory management — reconcile EAS tag stock (hard tags, soft labels), order replenishment, ensure tag availability at all POS terminals and receiving dock | Store Manager | LP Manager (Regional) | 30 min monthly |
| 5 | Source-tagging compliance verification — confirm vendor-applied EAS labels on high-risk categories (power tools, electrical, plumbing fittings, adhesives) per vendor agreement | LP Analyst | Procurement Manager | 2 hours monthly |
| 6 | Generate monthly EAS effectiveness report — alarm rate, apprehension rate, tag application compliance %, shrinkage correlation by store | LP Analyst | LP Manager (Regional) | 2 hours |

### System Touchpoints
- **EAS System** — gate alarm logging, tag/deactivation counter, system health monitoring
- **POS System** — tag deactivation confirmation at checkout
- **ERP LP Module** — alarm incident register, audit checklist, tag inventory tracking
- **Procurement Module** — source-tagging vendor compliance tracking
- **BI Dashboard** — EAS effectiveness analytics, store comparison

### Pain Points / Risks
- EAS false alarm rate can be high (> 30%) due to tag deactivation failures, causing customer frustration
- Security guard agencies have high turnover (~50% annually); continuous training required
- Source-tagging vendor compliance is inconsistent; some vendors resist cost of tag application
- EAS system maintenance (gate calibration, deactivator pad replacement) requires specialized vendor support
- Cultural sensitivity: customers may feel accused when EAS alarm triggers; staff must handle with courtesy

### Staffing Implication
- Security guards: 2 per store × 200 stores = 400 guards (contracted through agency)
- LP Analysts: monthly audit rotation (each analyst covers ~40 stores)
- 1 FTE EAS System Administrator (national, under IT Operations)

### Time Estimate
- Daily EAS response: 5 min × ~3 alarms/store = 15 min/store/day
- Monthly audit: 60 min per store
- **Annual estimate: ~18,000 alarm response hours + ~400 audit hours + ~200 admin hours = 18,600 person-hours/year**

## W1211. Store-Level Vendor & Delivery Driver Access Control & Yard Security Protocol

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Vendor delivery truck arrives at store receiving dock; or 3PL driver arrives for customer delivery pickup; or service contractor arrives for maintenance |
| **Frequency** | 2–3 vendor deliveries per store per day + 3PL driver visits + occasional service contractors = ~5–8 external personnel access events per store per day |
| **Volume** | ~1,000–1,600 external access events per day chain-wide (200 stores × 5–8) |
| **Owner** | Receiving Clerk / LP Officer |
| **Participants** | Receiving Clerk, LP Officer, Vendor Driver, Store Manager, Security Guard |

### Background

BuildRight Depot stores receive 2–3 vendor deliveries and multiple 3PL driver visits daily. Each external person entering the store premises — particularly the receiving dock, backroom, and lumber yard — represents a security and safety risk. External personnel may: (a) steal merchandise from unsecured backroom areas, (b) introduce safety hazards by operating equipment (forklifts, pallet jacks) in the yard, (c) access restricted areas (cash office, IT server room), (d) conduct unauthorized deliveries (phantom deliveries). The lumber yard and outdoor materials area is particularly vulnerable — large, open-access areas with high-value items (lumber, rebar, cement) where distinguishing vendor activity from theft is difficult. This workflow governs the check-in, escort, monitoring, and check-out of all external personnel at store premises, complementing W843 (vendor & delivery fraud detection) and W838 (CCTV operations).

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | External personnel (vendor driver, 3PL driver, service contractor) arrives at store entrance or receiving gate; Security Guard greets and requests: (a) government-issued ID, (b) delivery documents (DR, PO reference) or service work order, (c) vehicle plate number and details | Security Guard | LP Officer | 2 min |
| 2 | Security Guard verifies identity and purpose: (a) checks delivery documents against expected deliveries in system (Receiving Clerk's daily delivery schedule from W109), (b) for service contractors: verifies against approved contractor list per W600, (c) for unexpected/unlisted deliveries: contacts Receiving Clerk for authorization | Security Guard | Store Manager | 2 min |
| 3 | Security Guard logs visitor in access control system: (a) visitor name, company, ID number, vehicle plate, purpose, time-in, assigned escort; (b) issues color-coded visitor badge (green = vendor delivery, yellow = 3PL driver, orange = service contractor) with time-limited validity (max 2 hours for deliveries; max 8 hours for contractors); (c) retains visitor's government ID at guard station | Security Guard / System | LP Officer | 3 min |
| 4 | Receiving Clerk receives notification and escorts vendor driver to receiving dock; Receiving Clerk remains present throughout unloading; driver is NOT permitted in: (a) sales floor, (b) cash office, (c) stockroom beyond receiving staging area, (d) IT/server area | Receiving Clerk | LP Officer | Duration of unloading |
| 5 | For lumber yard / outdoor materials deliveries: Security Guard or LP Officer monitors via CCTV; driver must remain within designated unloading zone; yard gate closed after entry | LP Officer / Security Guard | Store Manager | Duration of unloading |
| 6 | Upon completion: Receiving Clerk verifies delivered goods match DR (W109 goods receipt); signs delivery receipt; escorts driver back to exit; Security Guard checks: (a) vehicle cargo area visually inspected for unauthorized merchandise, (b) driver returns visitor badge, (c) Security Guard returns government ID, (d) logs time-out in access control system | Receiving Clerk / Security Guard | LP Officer | 5 min |
| 7 | If visitor badge not returned within validity period: system alerts LP Officer; LP Officer locates visitor; if visitor cannot be located: Store Manager notified; LP Officer reviews CCTV footage for visitor's last known location | System / LP Officer | Store Manager | 10 min |
| 8 | Weekly: LP Officer reviews access control log for anomalies: (a) visitors exceeding time limit, (b) frequent visits by same vendor driver outside scheduled deliveries, (c) vehicles without proper documentation, (d) after-hours access events; escalates patterns to LP Director | LP Officer | LP Director | 30 min/week |

### System Touchpoints
- Visitor access control system: visitor registration, badge issuance, time tracking, auto-expiry alerts
- Daily delivery schedule from W109 (goods receiving) for pre-authorized visitor validation
- Approved contractor database from W600 (service contractor accreditation)
- CCTV system integration (W838) for visitor movement tracking
- LP analytics dashboard for access pattern analysis and anomaly detection

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Tailgating** — vendor drivers holding gates open for unauthorized persons to enter yard/backroom; anti-tailgating turnstiles or mantrap gates mitigate at high-risk stores
- **Visitor badge sharing** — drivers exchanging badges to extend access; photo-on-badge and guard verification mitigates
- **Receiving Clerk distraction** — Clerk called away during unloading leaves driver unattended in backroom; merchandise vulnerability during unattended periods
- **After-hours deliveries** — emergency vendor deliveries outside store hours lack same security controls; Store Manager must authorize and be present
- **Driver familiarity** — regular drivers known to staff may bypass check-in procedures over time; LP Officer spot-checks mitigate

### Staffing Implication
- Security Guards: already deployed at each store (1–2 per store); visitor management adds ~2–5 min per visitor = ~25–40 min/day
- Receiving Clerks: escort duty is part of receiving process; no incremental time
- LP Officer: weekly access log review adds ~30 min/week; absorbed by existing regional LP team
- No incremental headcount

### Time Estimate
- Per visitor check-in: 5 min (2 min ID verification + 3 min system registration)
- Per visitor check-out: 5 min (vehicle inspection + badge return + system log)
- Weekly access review: 30 min per LP Officer per region
- **Chain-wide: ~1,000–1,600 visitors/day × 10 min = 167–267 person-hours/day**

## W1247. Outdoor Lumber Yard & Building Material Yard Theft Prevention & Surveillance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — daily surveillance protocol; ad-hoc triggered by exception report (W837), inventory variance alert, or incident report |
| **Frequency** | Daily (every operating day at all 200 stores with lumber yards) |
| **Volume** | 200 stores × daily surveillance protocol; estimated 15–25 lumber yard theft incidents per month chain-wide |
| **Owner** | LP Officer (Store-Level) / Store Manager |
| **Participants** | LP Analyst, Lumber Yard Sales Associate, Department Supervisor (Lumber & Building Materials), LP Manager (Regional) |

### Background

BuildRight Depot's outdoor lumber yards and building material display areas are among the highest shrinkage risk zones in the store network. Lumber (4,900 SKUs, 14% of assortment), cement, rebar, hollow blocks, steel pipes, and other bulky construction materials are stored in partially enclosed or open-air areas that are difficult to secure with conventional EAS gates. Unlike the controlled main sales floor, the lumber yard has multiple access points: customer vehicle loading lanes, forklift operating zones, delivery truck gates, and pedestrian walkways. Construction materials represent high-value, easily resold goods — a single bundle of premium plywood (PHP 3,500–5,000) or a pallet of cement (PHP 8,000–12,000) can be loaded into a pickup truck within minutes. Philippine construction material theft is exacerbated by: (a) active informal construction markets where stolen materials are resold at 40–60% of SRP, (b) contractor crews who may have legitimate access to the yard but remove more than purchased, (c) after-hours access via perimeter fencing. This workflow governs the daily surveillance, theft prevention, and incident response procedures specifically for outdoor lumber yards and building material display areas.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Opening yard security check**: Before customer access, Lumber Yard Sales Associate conducts perimeter walk: (a) verify fencing integrity — no cuts, breaches, or lifted sections; (b) check all gates locked and chain intact; (c) verify CCTV camera coverage of yard entry/exit points (minimum 4 cameras per yard: main gate, vehicle exit, pedestrian entry, high-value display); (d) confirm yard lighting operational for evening hours; (e) report any security deficiencies to Store Manager before yard opens | Lumber Yard Sales Associate | Store Manager | 15 min |
| 2 | **High-value material inventory spot-check**: Verify physical count of top 20 highest-value lumber and building material SKUs against system on-hand: (a) premium plywood bundles, (b) structural lumber (2×4, 2×6 per board), (c) rebar bundles, (d) cement pallets, (e) steel pipe lengths; flag variance > 2 units per SKU for investigation | Lumber Yard Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | 20 min |
| 3 | **Customer vehicle loading monitoring**: During operating hours, Sales Associate monitors customer vehicle loading zone: (a) verify customer presents sales receipt before loading bulky items into vehicle; (b) match receipt line items to physical goods being loaded; (c) for large purchases (> PHP 10,000 in lumber/CM), verify payment completed at POS before release; (d) log vehicle plate number for purchases > PHP 20,000 | Lumber Yard Sales Associate | Department Supervisor | Continuous |
| 4 | **Contractor crew access control**: For registered trade account customers purchasing in bulk: (a) verify trade account ID and authorized purchaser list; (b) issue yard access badge that logs entry/exit time; (c) limit yard access to 2 crew members per account visit; (d) verify total loaded quantity matches invoice before yard exit | Department Supervisor | Store Manager | 5 min per crew |
| 5 | **CCTV real-time monitoring review**: LP Officer reviews CCTV footage for lumber yard during high-risk windows (opening hour, lunch rush, closing hour): (a) flag vehicles entering loading zone without stopping at POS; (b) identify individuals loitering near high-value stacks; (c) detect concealment of smaller items (tools, fittings) within lumber bundles; (d) cross-reference vehicle plates with previous incident database | LP Officer / LP Analyst | LP Manager (Regional) | 30 min/day |
| 6 | **Incident response for yard theft**: If theft detected: (a) approach subject with minimum 2 staff present; (b) if customer has unpaid goods in vehicle, request return to POS for payment; (c) if subject refuses or flees, record vehicle description, plate number, and CCTV timestamp — do not physically pursue; (d) file incident report within 1 hour; (e) for repeat offenders or organized groups, escalate to LP Manager (Regional) for investigation per W840 | LP Officer / Department Supervisor | Store Manager | 30–60 min per incident |
| 7 | **Closing yard security protocol**: After customer hours: (a) final yard walk to verify no customers or unauthorized persons remain; (b) conduct closing count of top 10 high-value SKUs; (c) secure all gates with padlock; (d) verify CCTV recording continues in night-vision mode; (e) report any closing variance to Store Manager via system alert | Lumber Yard Sales Associate | Store Manager | 20 min |

### System Touchpoints
- **CCTV System** — yard-specific camera feeds with vehicle license plate recognition (LPR) at exit gate
- **POS System** — receipt verification for goods release, vehicle plate logging for high-value purchases
- **Inventory Module** — daily spot-check count entry, variance alert generation
- **LP Module** — incident reporting, vehicle plate blacklist, contractor access badge logging
- **Trade Account Module** — authorized purchaser verification, crew access logging

### Pain Points / Risks
- Multiple yard access points make complete perimeter security impractical without significant capital investment
- Customer self-loading culture in Philippine hardware retail conflicts with controlled goods release procedures
- After-hours theft via perimeter breach is difficult to prevent without 24/7 security guard presence
- Weather exposure damages electronic security equipment (CCTV, sensors) in outdoor yards
- Balancing customer experience with security — excessive checks drive contractors to competitors

### Staffing Implication
- Lumber Yard Sales Associate: yard security duties add ~1 hour/day per store to existing role
- Department Supervisor: crew access control adds ~30 min/day during peak hours
- LP Officer: 30 min/day CCTV review per store (can be centralized at regional level)

### Time Estimate
- Per store per day: ~85 min for yard security activities (opening 15 + spot-check 20 + CCTV 30 + closing 20)
- **Annual estimate: 200 stores × 365 days × 85 min = ~103,000 staff-minutes/year (~1,720 staff-hours/year)**

## W1248. BOPIS Staging Area Security & Omnichannel Fulfillment Fraud Prevention

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — daily BOPIS staging area audit; ad-hoc triggered by customer complaint of order tampering, inventory variance at staging, or LP exception alert |
| **Frequency** | Daily monitoring at all 200 stores; investigation as needed |
| **Volume** | ~25,700 BOPIS orders/month; estimated 50–80 staging-related incidents/month chain-wide |
| **Owner** | Customer Service Representative / LP Analyst |
| **Participants** | Store Manager, Stock Associate, LP Analyst, Ecommerce Operations Manager |

### Background

BuildRight Depot processes ~25,700 BOPIS (Buy Online, Pick Up In Store) orders per month across 200 stores, staged at the customer service counter for customer pickup within 4 hours. Each BOPIS order involves: (a) store receives pick list from ecommerce platform, (b) Stock Associate picks items from shelves and stages at designated BOPIS holding area, (c) customer presents ID and order confirmation for release. The BOPIS staging area creates a unique security vulnerability: items are picked from inventory but not yet sold, creating a "limbo" state where goods are reserved but unattended for up to 5 days. Risks include: (i) employee theft from staged orders (high-value power tools, electrical fixtures), (ii) customer impersonation — presenting fake order confirmation to collect another customer's goods, (iii) staged order tampering — swapping higher-value items with lower-value substitutes before customer pickup, (iv) order abandonment — customer never picks up and staged items are not returned to shelf promptly. This workflow governs security and fraud prevention for BOPIS and omnichannel fulfillment staging areas.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Daily staging area opening audit**: Customer Service Representative verifies staging area readiness: (a) confirm BOPIS holding shelves/lockers are empty from previous day or contain only active orders within hold period; (b) verify CCTV coverage of staging area; (c) check that staged orders are in sealed bags/containers with order number label; (d) report any unsealed or unlabeled staged goods to Store Manager | Customer Service Representative | Store Manager | 15 min |
| 2 | **BOPIS pick verification**: When Stock Associate completes BOPIS pick, system requires: (a) scan each item barcode to confirm correct SKU and quantity; (b) photograph staged order (mobile app) as pick verification evidence; (c) system generates tamper-evident bag seal number or locker code; (d) Stock Associate and Customer Service Representative both verify staged order count matches pick list | Stock Associate / Customer Service Representative | Store Manager | 5 min per order |
| 3 | **Customer pickup identity verification**: At BOPIS pickup: (a) customer presents government-issued ID matching order name; (b) Customer Service Representative scans order QR code and verifies customer identity; (c) system displays order contents — representative opens sealed bag in customer presence; (d) customer confirms item count and condition; (e) system records pickup timestamp and customer acknowledgment | Customer Service Representative | Store Manager | 5 min per pickup |
| 4 | **Anti-impersonation controls**: For high-value BOPIS orders (> PHP 10,000): (a) system sends SMS OTP to order phone number at pickup — customer must provide OTP; (b) if third-party pickup authorized, system requires pickup authorization code from order owner; (c) flag attempts to collect multiple orders under different names from same ID; (d) flag pickup attempts within 30 min of order placement (insufficient time for legitimate pick) | Customer Service Representative | LP Manager (Regional) | 3 min per high-value pickup |
| 5 | **Staged order tampering detection**: Daily reconciliation of staged orders: (a) system compares pick-scan log to current staged inventory; (b) flag any staged order with item count discrepancy; (c) for sealed bags: verify seal number matches system record; (d) for open-shelf staging: spot-check item SKUs against pick list; (e) investigate any tampered seals or mismatched items as potential theft per W839 | LP Analyst / Customer Service Representative | Store Manager | 30 min/day |
| 6 | **Abandoned order return-to-shelf**: For orders exceeding 5-day hold period: (a) system auto-generates cancellation and refund; (b) Stock Associate returns items to shelf and scans each item for inventory reconciliation; (c) system verifies return-to-shelf count matches original pick count; (d) any items missing from abandoned order are flagged as potential shrinkage | Stock Associate | Store Manager | 10 min per abandoned order |

### System Touchpoints
- **Ecommerce Platform** — BOPIS order lifecycle, pickup confirmation, cancellation trigger
- **POS / Customer Service Module** — ID verification, QR code scanning, OTP validation
- **Inventory Module** — staged order inventory status, return-to-shelf reconciliation
- **LP Module** — tampering incident logging, impersonation flag tracking
- **CCTV System** — staging area coverage, pickup counter footage
- **SMS Gateway** — OTP delivery for high-value pickups

### Pain Points / Risks
- BOPIS staging areas compete with limited backroom space in big-box format; secure lockers may not be feasible for bulky items (lumber, appliances)
- Customer impersonation is difficult to detect without OTP for all orders, but OTP adds friction to pickup experience
- Employee collusion risk — Stock Associate stages order, accomplice picks up with fake ID
- Abandoned order processing creates additional labor; ~5% abandonment rate means ~1,285 returns-to-shelf/month

### Staffing Implication
- Customer Service Representative: BOPIS security duties add ~45 min/day to existing role
- LP Analyst: staging tampering detection adds 30 min/day to existing monitoring duties
- Stock Associate: pick verification adds 5 min per order (~2,140 min/day chain-wide for ~25,700 orders/month)

### Time Estimate
- Daily monitoring: 45 min per store (opening audit + tampering detection)
- Per order: 8 min additional security overhead (pick verification + pickup verification)
- **Annual estimate: 200 stores × 365 × 45 min + 308,000 orders × 8 min = ~79,600 person-hours/year**
