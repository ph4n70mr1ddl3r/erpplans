## W846. Loss Prevention Training, Awareness & Compliance Program

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — onboarding (new hire), annual refresher (all store employees), quarterly update (Store Managers and LP-sensitive roles) |
| **Frequency** | Onboarding: per new hire (~200 new hires/month given ~30% annual turnover); Annual refresher: all 4,500+ store employees; Quarterly update: 200 Store Managers + 400 key holders |
| **Volume** | ~2,400 training completions per year (onboarding) + 4,500 annual refresher completions + 800 quarterly update completions |
| **Owner** | LP Training Coordinator / HR Training Manager |
| **Participants** | LP Manager (Regional), Store Manager, New Hire Employee, HR Training Manager, External Training Provider (for specialized topics) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | New hire LP orientation (Day 2 of onboarding) — cover cash handling policy, return/refund procedure, receiving verification, EAS tagging, incident reporting, whistleblower channel, and consequences of theft | Store Manager / HR Training | LP Manager (Regional) | 2 hours per new hire batch |
| 2 | Annual LP refresher training (e-learning + in-store practical) — update on new fraud schemes, policy changes, exception report awareness, and shrinkage results; includes quiz (minimum 80% pass rate) | LP Training Coordinator | National LP Director | 1 hour per employee (e-learning) + 30 min practical |
| 3 | Quarterly LP awareness update for Store Managers — review chain-wide LP metrics, share anonymized case studies, introduce new detection tools or policy changes, set quarterly LP targets | LP Manager (Regional) | National LP Director | 2 hours per session (regional grouping) |
| 4 | Specialized LP training for receiving clerks — vendor fraud indicators, dock security procedures, weight verification, short-shipment detection; certification required before solo receiving shifts | LP Training Coordinator | LP Manager (Regional) | 4 hours per clerk (initial) + 2 hours annual refresh |
| 5 | LP awareness campaign execution — monthly LP tip of the month (digital poster + store huddle talking point), quarterly LP awareness week (in-store displays, reward for LP tips), annual LP excellence awards | LP Training Coordinator | National LP Director | 4 hours monthly |
| 6 | Training effectiveness measurement — track shrinkage correlation with training completion rates, quiz scores, and time-to-incident for new hires; adjust curriculum based on findings | LP Analyst | National LP Director | 4 hours quarterly |

### System Touchpoints
- **LMS (Learning Management System)** — e-learning delivery, quiz administration, completion tracking
- **HR System** — training record, certification status, onboarding checklist
- **ERP LP Module** — shrinkage data for training effectiveness correlation
- **Communication Platform** — digital poster distribution, awareness campaign delivery
- **BI Dashboard** — training completion rates by store, quiz pass rates, correlation analytics

### Pain Points / Risks
- High employee turnover (30–40%) means ~30–40% of trained staff leave within 12 months — continuous retraining required
- E-learning completion rates in provincial stores may be low due to limited computer access
- Training is perceived as non-revenue-generating and may be deprioritized during peak seasons
- Filipino cultural norm of "pakikisama" (getting along) can discourage reporting of colleague theft
- Whistleblower channel awareness is critical but utilization is typically low without trust-building

### Staffing Implication
- 1 FTE LP Training Coordinator (national)
- HR Training Manager: 20% of time allocated to LP training
- Store Managers: 2 hours per new hire batch for LP orientation
- External provider: for annual specialized training (investigation techniques, interview skills)

### Time Estimate
- New hire orientation: 2 hours × 200 batches/year = 400 hours
- Annual refresher: 1.5 hours × 4,500 employees = 6,750 hours (mostly self-service e-learning)
- Quarterly updates: 2 hours × 5 sessions × 4 quarters = 40 hours
- Specialized training: 4 hours × ~100 clerks/year = 400 hours
- **Annual estimate: ~1,000 live instruction hours + 6,750 e-learning hours = 7,750 total hours/year**

## W1173. High-Risk SKU Protection Plan & Product Security Fixture Deployment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual shrinkage analysis identifies high-theft categories; or new high-value product category launch; or store remodel/refresh |
| **Frequency** | Annual plan review; quarterly deployment execution; ad-hoc for new categories |
| **Volume** | ~1,500–2,000 high-risk SKUs across chain (out of 35,000 active); ~200 stores requiring security fixture maintenance |
| **Owner** | LP Manager (Regional) |
| **Participants** | LP Analyst, Category Manager, Store Manager, LP Training Coordinator, Security Equipment Vendor |

### Background

BuildRight's product assortment includes categories with high theft appeal: power tools (PHP 3,000–50,000), electrical wiring and breakers (portable, resellable), plumbing fittings (brass, copper — high scrap value), adhesives and sealants (compact, expensive), and safety equipment. These high-risk SKUs represent ~4–5% of active items but account for ~25–35% of shrinkage value. Standard EAS tagging per W844 provides basic deterrence, but professional thieves defeat EAS tags, and internal theft by employees is harder to detect with physical security alone. This workflow establishes a tiered protection plan: Spider Wrap/Alpha Keepers for mid-range items, locked display cases for high-value tools, and secured behind-counter storage for premium products. Deployment must balance theft prevention against customer experience — customers want to touch and compare tools before purchasing, so over-securing merchandise reduces sales conversion.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **High-risk SKU identification**: Annual shrinkage analysis per W845 identifies top-theft SKUs by: (a) shrinkage value (quantity × cost), (b) shrinkage rate (shrinkage quantity ÷ sales quantity), (c) theft pattern (external shoplifting vs. internal employee theft); LP Analyst classifies SKUs into protection tiers: Tier A (locked case, behind counter): items > PHP 10,000 or top-50 shrinkage value; Tier B (Spider Wrap / Alpha Keeper): items PHP 2,000–10,000 with high shrinkage rate; Tier C (EAS tag + shelf positioning): items < PHP 2,000 with moderate shrinkage | LP Analyst | National LP Director | 2 days |
| 2 | **Protection plan per category**: For each Tier A/B category, LP Manager designs protection plan with Category Manager: (a) power tools: locked glass display case with sample tools on display; full inventory behind counter; customer requests specific model, staff retrieves from secure storage; (b) electrical fittings: Spider Wrap on multi-packs; individual fittings in Alpha Keepers; high-value breakers in locked peg hook area; (c) plumbing fittings (brass/copper): secured bin display with employee-access-only backing; (d) adhesives/sealants: EAS tag + shelf positioning near high-traffic aisle; (e) Category Manager approves plan ensuring sales conversion impact is acceptable | LP Manager (Regional) + Category Manager | National LP Director | 1 day/category |
| 3 | **Fixture procurement & deployment**: For new security fixtures: (a) LP Manager orders locked display cases, Spider Wraps, Alpha Keepers, secured peg hooks from security equipment vendor; (b) schedule installation during store remodel or low-traffic period; (c) Store Manager coordinates installation with vendor; (d) train staff on fixture access procedures (key management, Spider Wrap removal at POS); (e) system updates high-risk SKU protection tier assignment for POS deactivation procedures | LP Manager / Store Manager / Vendor | National LP Director | 1–2 weeks/store |
| 4 | **POS integration for security deactivation**: System ensures: (a) Tier A items: POS requires manager key swipe to authorize sale; (b) Tier B items: POS prompts cashier to remove Spider Wrap/Alpha Keeper and scan deactivation barcode; (c) all high-risk items: POS enforces quantity threshold alert — if customer purchases > 3 units of a Tier A item, alert LP Analyst for potential ORC per W840 | System / IT | LP Manager | Per IT sprint |
| 5 | **Quarterly compliance verification**: LP Analyst conducts quarterly store visit to verify: (a) security fixtures intact and functional, (b) high-risk SKUs correctly placed in designated protection fixtures, (c) Spider Wraps/Alpha Keepers applied to all Tier B items, (d) EAS tags present on all Tier C items, (e) locked case keys secured and access logged; compliance score feeds store LP scorecard | LP Analyst | LP Manager (Regional) | 60 min/store |
| 6 | **Annual plan refresh**: LP Manager reviews annual shrinkage data vs. protection plan: (a) did protected categories show shrinkage reduction? (b) are new categories emerging as high-theft? (c) should any categories be re-tiered? (d) fixture maintenance/replacement needs; update plan for next fiscal year | LP Manager (Regional) | National LP Director | 1 day |

### System Touchpoints
- Shrinkage analytics per W845 for high-risk SKU identification
- ERP item master with protection tier classification
- POS security deactivation prompts and quantity alerts
- Security fixture asset tracking per store
- LP store compliance scoring module
- Integration with W844 EAS management and W840 ORC detection

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Sales conversion impact**: locked display cases reduce impulse purchases; customers may leave if they cannot access product without staff assistance, especially during peak hours when staff availability is limited
- **Customer experience friction**: Spider Wraps and Alpha Keepers make it harder for customers to handle and compare products; trade-off between security and shopping experience
- **Key management risk**: locked case keys held by multiple staff members increase risk of internal theft; electronic key card access with audit trail preferred but more expensive
- **Fixture maintenance**: Spider Wraps break, Alpha Keepers crack, locked case hinges jam; unmaintained security fixtures are worse than no fixtures (false sense of security)
- **Category boundary disputes**: LP wants maximum protection; Category Manager wants maximum accessibility; balanced approach requires executive arbitration

### Staffing Implication
- **LP Analyst**: quarterly compliance visits — 200 stores × 60 min × 4 visits/year = 800 hours/year; ~0.5 FTE equivalent
- **LP Manager (Regional)**: plan design and deployment coordination ~10–15 days/year; absorbed by existing role
- **Store staff**: locked case access adds ~2–3 min per customer interaction for Tier A items

### Time Estimate
- Annual plan design: 2–3 days
- Per-category protection design: 1 day
- Per-store fixture deployment: 1–2 weeks
- Quarterly compliance visit: 60 min/store
- **Annual estimate: ~1,200 compliance hours + ~20 planning days**

## W1174. Loss Prevention Store Compliance Audit Program & Scoring

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Scheduled — monthly per store; or triggered by shrinkage result spike, exception report anomaly (W837), or anonymous tip |
| **Frequency** | Monthly per store (200 stores = ~200 audits/month); each store receives 1 comprehensive + 1 spot-check audit per quarter |
| **Volume** | ~400 audits/quarter (200 comprehensive + 200 spot-checks); ~1,600 audits/year |
| **Owner** | LP Auditor |
| **Participants** | LP Auditor, Store Manager, Department Supervisor, LP Manager (Regional), National LP Director |

### Background

Systematic LP compliance auditing ensures that loss prevention policies and procedures are consistently applied across all 200 stores. Without regular auditing, LP compliance degrades — cash handling procedures loosen, receiving verification becomes cursory, EAS tagging rates drop, and high-risk SKU security fixtures fall into disrepair. The audit program provides a standardized scoring framework that enables: (a) store-to-store comparison, (b) trend tracking over time, (c) identification of systemic compliance gaps, and (d) targeted remediation for underperforming stores. Audit scores are incorporated into Store Manager performance evaluation and incentive compensation, creating accountability for LP results.

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Audit schedule & preparation**: LP Auditor receives monthly audit schedule from LP Manager (Regional); each auditor covers ~40–50 stores; for comprehensive audits: pre-load audit checklist on tablet with store-specific data: current shrinkage rate, exception report trends per W837, previous audit findings and remediation status, high-risk SKU list per W1173 | LP Auditor | LP Manager (Regional) | 30 min/store prep |
| 2 | **Comprehensive audit execution** (quarterly per store): LP Auditor conducts in-store assessment covering 10 LP domains: (a) **Cash handling**: cash drawer procedures, safe access log, deposit preparation, dual-control compliance; (b) **Receiving**: DSD verification per W1168, goods receipt accuracy, dock door security; (c) **EAS compliance**: tag application rate per W844, deactivator functionality, gate alarm response; (d) **High-risk SKU protection**: fixture integrity per W1173, correct tiering, key management; (e) **Refund/return controls**: authorization compliance, ID verification, receipt validation per W841; (f) **CCTV & access control**: camera uptime per W838, recording integrity, restricted area access; (g) **Backroom security**: receiving dock access, stock room access, trash compactor area (concealment point); (h) **LP awareness**: staff knowledge of reporting procedures, whistleblower channel, LP policy; (i) **Vendor compliance**: vendor ID verification, delivery appointment adherence, dock security per W843; (j) **Documentation**: LP log maintenance, incident reporting accuracy, corrective action closure | LP Auditor | Store Manager | 3–4 hours |
| 3 | **Spot-check audit execution** (alternate quarters): Focused 60-min audit targeting 1–2 domains based on: (a) previous comprehensive audit deficiencies, (b) current shrinkage trend, (c) exception report alerts per W837; faster format allows more frequent store visits without disrupting operations | LP Auditor | Store Manager | 60 min |
| 4 | **Scoring & rating**: System scores each domain on 1–5 scale: 1 = Non-compliant (critical gap), 2 = Below standard, 3 = Partially compliant, 4 = Compliant, 5 = Best practice; overall store score calculated as weighted average: cash handling (20%), receiving (15%), EAS (15%), high-risk SKU (15%), refund controls (10%), CCTV (5%), backroom (5%), LP awareness (5%), vendor compliance (5%), documentation (5%); overall rating: A (≥ 4.5), B (3.5–4.4), C (2.5–3.4), D (< 2.5 — requires immediate action plan) | System / LP Auditor | LP Manager (Regional) | 15 min |
| 5 | **Findings debrief**: LP Auditor debriefs Store Manager on findings: (a) review each domain score with specific observations, (b) highlight critical findings requiring immediate remediation, (c) acknowledge areas of excellence, (d) agree on remediation actions with deadlines, (e) Store Manager signs acknowledgment | LP Auditor | Store Manager | 30 min |
| 6 | **Remediation tracking**: Store Manager executes corrective actions; system tracks remediation progress with deadlines; LP Auditor follows up on critical findings within 30 days; unresolved critical findings escalated to Regional Manager; repeated D ratings escalated to VP Store Operations with potential Store Manager performance action | Store Manager / LP Auditor | LP Manager (Regional) | 30 min/follow-up |
| 7 | **Monthly reporting & analytics**: LP Manager (Regional) compiles monthly audit analytics: (a) average store score by region, (b) most common deficiencies, (c) remediation closure rate, (d) score trend by store, (e) correlation between audit score and shrinkage rate; present to National LP Director for strategic decisions | LP Manager (Regional) | National LP Director | 4 hours/month |

### System Touchpoints
- LP audit checklist application on tablet with photo capture
- Domain scoring engine with weighted calculation
- Store LP compliance dashboard with trend tracking
- Remediation tracking module with deadline alerts
- Integration with W837 exception reporting, W844 EAS, W838 CCTV, W1173 high-risk SKU
- Store Manager performance evaluation linkage

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Audit fatigue**: monthly audits can feel punitive rather than supportive; auditor tone and approach must be coaching-oriented
- **Auditor consistency**: different auditors may score the same observation differently; calibration sessions needed quarterly
- **"Show time" effect**: stores may temporarily improve compliance when auditor is present but revert after; unannounced spot-checks per step 3 mitigate
- **Remediation resource constraints**: Store Managers may agree to corrective actions but lack staff time to execute; realistic remediation plans required
- **Data overload**: 200 stores × monthly audits = 2,400 audits/year; analytics must identify actionable patterns, not just generate reports

### Staffing Implication
- **LP Auditors**: 1 auditor per 40–50 stores = ~4–5 FTE national; each conducts ~40–50 comprehensive audits/quarter + 40–50 spot-checks/quarter = ~160–200 audits/year per auditor
- **LP Manager (Regional)**: 4 hours/month on reporting; absorbed by existing role

### Time Estimate
- Per comprehensive audit: prep 30 min + execution 3–4 hours + scoring 15 min + debrief 30 min = **4.5–5.5 hours**
- Per spot-check: prep 15 min + execution 60 min + scoring 10 min + debrief 15 min = **1.5–2 hours**
- **Annual: ~2,400 audits × 3 hours average = ~7,200 auditor-hours/year**
