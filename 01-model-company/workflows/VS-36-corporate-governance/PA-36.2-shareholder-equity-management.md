## W1722. Stock & Transfer Ledger Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Stock issuance, transfer, or periodic reconciliation |
| **Frequency** | Continuous for transactions; monthly reconciliation |
| **Volume** | ~10–20 stock transactions/year (family corporation with limited transfers) |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, Stockholder, CFO, Legal Counsel |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Corporate Secretary maintains stock and transfer ledger for each of the 5 entities: (a) stockholder name, address, TIN, (b) number of shares held, certificate numbers, (c) date of acquisition, (d) par value and total subscribed/paid-in capital, (e) pledges, liens, or encumbrances, (f) transfer history | Corporate Secretary | Legal Counsel | Continuous |
| 2 | System maintains electronic stock ledger with: (a) automatic totaling of shares outstanding per entity, (b) ownership percentage calculation, (c) link to SEC GIS filing data, (d) audit trail of all changes, (e) segregation by share class (if multiple classes exist) | System | Corporate Secretary | Automated |
| 3 | Monthly: Corporate Secretary reconciles stock ledger with: (a) equity accounts in GL per entity (total shares outstanding × par value = issued capital stock in GL), (b) SEC registration records, (c) physical stock certificates on hand (if certificated shares) | Corporate Secretary | CFO | 1 hour/month |
| 4 | Annual: Stock ledger extract provided to External Auditor for confirmation of equity balances and ownership as part of annual audit | Corporate Secretary | External Auditor | During annual audit |


### System Touchpoints
- Stock & transfer ledger per entity — stockholder, TIN, shares, certificate numbers (step 1)
- Electronic ledger — automatic totals, ownership percentage, certificate linkage (step 2)
- Monthly GL reconciliation — shares outstanding × par value = issued capital (step 3)
- Annual auditor confirmation of equity and ownership (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Ledger-GL divergence risk**: the share register disagrees with the equity accounts; mitigated by monthly reconciliation to each entity's GL (step 3)
- **Duplicate-certificate risk**: new certificates issued without cancelling originals; mitigated by the cancel-before-issue discipline linked to W1724 processing (step 1)

### Automation Opportunity
- System continuous audit of "audit trail of all changes," (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "records" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-extract of "extract provided to External Auditor for confirmation of equity balances and…" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-350 (ensure controlled execution — Shareholder & Equity Management (PA-36.2).)
- operational: periodic reconciliation against source documents

### Time Estimate
Ledger maintenance continuous on ~10–20 annual stock transactions; monthly ledger-to-GL reconciliation 1 hour ≈ 12 hours/year (electronic ledger totaling automated); annual auditor extract at the annual audit.

## W1723. Annual Stockholders' Meeting Preparation & Conduct

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual requirement per Revised Corporation Code |
| **Frequency** | Annual (5 entities × 1 meeting = 5 meetings; typically held consecutively on the same day) |
| **Volume** | 5 meetings/year |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, Board Chair, CEO, CFO, Stockholders, External Auditor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Corporate Secretary prepares meeting notice: (a) date, time, venue (or virtual meeting platform), (b) agenda items per Revised Corporation Code requirements: (i) election of directors, (ii) approval of previous year's financial statements, (iii) appointment of external auditor, (iv) declaration of dividends (if any), (v) other matters requiring stockholder approval, (c) notice distributed to all stockholders minimum 21 days before meeting per by-laws (or per Revised Corporation Code) | Corporate Secretary | Board Chair | 1 week |
| 2 | CFO and External Auditor prepare financial statement package for stockholder review: (a) audited balance sheet, income statement, cash flow statement, statement of changes in equity, (b) notes to financial statements, (c) external auditor's report (opinion) | CFO / External Auditor | Board | Part of annual close |
| 3 | Stockholders' meeting conducted: (a) Corporate Secretary confirms quorum (majority of outstanding shares per by-laws), (b) each agenda item presented and voted upon, (c) election of directors (if applicable), (d) stockholder questions addressed, (e) minutes recorded per W1716 process | Corporate Secretary | Board Chair | 1–3 hours |
| 4 | Post-meeting: Corporate Secretary files SEC GIS within 30 days, processes any stockholder-approved actions (dividend payment, director elections, auditor appointment) | Corporate Secretary | CEO | 1 week |

### System Touchpoints
- Meeting notice generation per Revised Corporation Code requirements — agenda, venue/virtual platform, notice period (step 1)
- Audited financial statement package for stockholder review (step 2)
- Quorum confirmation and voting record (step 3)
- Post-meeting SEC GIS filing within 30 days and approved-action processing (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Quorum risk**: Family corporations typically have few stockholders; absence of one significant stockholder may prevent quorum; mitigated by the Corporate Secretary review and approval gate
- **Virtual meetings**: Revised Corporation Code allows remote participation; ensure platform provides adequate security and identity verification
- **Multi-entity coordination**: 5 annual meetings can be efficiently conducted back-to-back on the same day with common stockholders

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-generate of "prepares meeting notice" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-review of "CFO and External Auditor prepare financial statement package for" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "recorded per W1716 process" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-350 (ensure controlled execution — Shareholder & Equity Management (PA-36.2).)
- operational: Corporate Secretary review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Per annual cycle ~10 days (meeting notice preparation 1 week + 5 consecutive stockholders' meetings 1–3 hours + SEC GIS filing and post-meeting processing within 30 days) ≈ 80 hours/year across the 5 entities.

## W1724. Stock Transfer & Ownership Change Processing

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Stockholder wishes to sell, gift, or otherwise transfer shares |
| **Frequency** | ~5–10 transfers/year (family corporation with limited activity) |
| **Volume** | Low volume but legally significant |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, Transferor, Transferee, Legal Counsel, Board |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Transferor notifies Corporate Secretary of intent to transfer shares: (a) provides stock certificate(s) for cancellation, (b) identifies transferee, (c) provides transfer documentation (deed of sale, donation, or inheritance documents) | Transferor | Corporate Secretary | 1 day |
| 2 | Corporate Secretary verifies: (a) transferor is registered stockholder of the shares, (b) shares are not subject to lien, pledge, or lock-up, (c) transfer complies with by-laws (right of first refusal, board approval requirements), (d) transferee is qualified to hold shares (if by-laws impose restrictions) | Corporate Secretary | Legal Counsel | 2–3 days |
| 3 | Board approval (if required by by-laws): Corporate Secretary presents transfer request to board; board approves or exercises right of first refusal (existing stockholders may have preemptive right to purchase shares before external transfer) | Corporate Secretary | Board | At next board meeting |
| 4 | Corporate Secretary processes transfer: (a) cancels old stock certificate, (b) issues new certificate to transferee, (c) updates stock ledger (removes from transferor, adds to transferee), (d) records transfer date, consideration (if disclosed), and supporting document reference | Corporate Secretary | Legal Counsel | 1 day |
| 5 | Tax compliance: (a) transfer subject to documentary stamp tax (DST) per NIRC — Corporate Secretary ensures DST payment to BIR, (b) if transfer is sale: capital gains tax implications for transferor, (c) if transfer is donation: donor's tax may apply, (d) Corporate Secretary maintains DST-proof documentation | Corporate Secretary / Tax Accountant | CFO | 1–2 days |


### System Touchpoints
- Transfer verification — registered holder, lien/pledge/lock-up check, transferee eligibility (step 2)
- Board approval gate with right-of-first-refusal handling per by-laws (step 3)
- Certificate cancel-and-issue processing (step 4)
- Stock ledger and GL equity update with auditor-visible trail (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Unauthorized-transfer risk**: shares move without board approval or ROFR compliance; mitigated by the verification and board gates (steps 2–3)
- **Chain-of-title error**: ledger updates break the ownership audit trail; mitigated by the cancel-issue-reconcile sequence with annual auditor confirmation (step 4; W1722)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-document of "Transferor notifies Corporate Secretary of intent to transfer shares" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-register of "registered stockholder of the shares" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approves or exercises right of first refusal" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-350 (ensure controlled execution — Shareholder & Equity Management (PA-36.2).)
- operational: Corporate Secretary review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
~2–3 days of Corporate Secretary effort per transfer (verification 2–3 days elapsed + processing and certificate reissuance 1 day + documentary stamp tax compliance 1–2 days; board approval at next meeting) × ~5–10 transfers/year ≈ 10–30 days/year.

## W1725. Dividend Declaration & Distribution

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Board decision to declare dividend based on retained earnings and cash availability |
| **Frequency** | Annual or semi-annual (board discretion) |
| **Volume** | 1–2 dividend declarations/year |
| **Owner** | CFO |
| **Participants** | CFO, Corporate Secretary, Treasury, Stockholders |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CFO prepares dividend proposal for board: (a) available retained earnings per latest audited financial statements, (b) cash position and liquidity assessment, (c) proposed dividend rate (per share or as percentage of par value), (d) total dividend amount, (e) record date (determines which stockholders receive dividend), (f) payment date, (g) withholding tax implications (10% final withholding tax on cash dividends per Philippine tax law) | CFO | CEO | 2–3 days |
| 2 | Board approves dividend declaration; Corporate Secretary records resolution per W1717 | Board | Board Chair | At board meeting |
| 3 | Corporate Secretary determines stockholders entitled to dividend as of record date; calculates dividend per stockholder based on shares held | Corporate Secretary | CFO | 1 day |
| 4 | Treasury processes dividend payment: (a) bank transfer to each stockholder, (b) 10% withholding tax remitted to BIR (BIR Form 1601-FQ, quarterly remittance of final withholding taxes), (c) stockholder receives net dividend and final tax withheld certificate (BIR Form 2306), (d) system records dividend payment in equity accounts: debit retained earnings, credit cash (net of WHT) | Treasury / CFO | CFO | 1–2 days |
| 5 | Tax filing: CFO includes dividend withholding in the quarterly BIR Form 1601-FQ filing; provides stockholders with final tax withheld certificates (BIR Form 2306) for their individual income tax returns | CFO / Tax Accountant | — | Part of quarterly filing |

### System Touchpoints
- Dividend proposal model — available retained earnings from audited FS, liquidity assessment (step 1)
- Board declaration resolution per W1717 (step 2)
- Record-date entitlement calculation per stockholder (step 3)
- Payment processing with 10% withholding remittance (BIR Form 1601-FQ) (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Insolvency test**: Philippine law prohibits dividend declaration if company is insolvent or would become insolvent after payment; CFO must confirm financial viability
- **Cash availability**: BuildRight's expansion (10–15 new stores/year) consumes significant cash; dividend must be balanced with reinvestment needs
- **Multi-entity dividends**: Each of the 5 entities may declare dividends independently; holding company (BuildRight Holdings) receives dividends from subsidiaries and may declare its own dividend to ultimate stockholders

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "CFO prepares dividend proposal for board" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approves dividend declaration" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-calculate of "calculates dividend per stockholder based on shares held" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-350 (ensure controlled execution — Shareholder & Equity Management (PA-36.2).)
- operational: CFO review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
~4–6 days per dividend declaration (CFO proposal 2–3 days + record-date entitlement calculation 1 day + Treasury payment processing 1–2 days; 10% withholding within quarterly 1601-FQ filings) × 1–2 declarations/year ≈ 4–12 days/year.

## W1726. Capital Increase & Stock Issuance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Board decision to increase authorized capital stock (for business expansion, debt-to-equity conversion, or new investor) |
| **Frequency** | Rare — every 2–5 years depending on growth needs |
| **Volume** | Low volume but legally and financially significant |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, CFO, Legal Counsel, Board, Stockholders, SEC |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Board approves increase in authorized capital stock: (a) current authorized capital and utilization, (b) proposed new authorized capital, (c) number of new shares and par value, (d) purpose of increase (store expansion, debt repayment, new entity capitalization), (e) pre-emptive rights of existing stockholders | Board | Board Chair | At board meeting |
| 2 | Stockholder approval: (a) 2/3 vote of outstanding shares required per Revised Corporation Code, (b) stockholders' meeting called or written assent obtained, (c) stockholders may subscribe to new shares in proportion to existing holdings (pre-emptive right) | Stockholders | Board Chair | 2–4 weeks |
| 3 | SEC filing: (a) amended articles of incorporation filed with SEC, (b) includes board resolution, stockholder resolution, and capital increase details, (c) SEC approval obtained (typically 2–4 weeks), (d) filing fees paid | Corporate Secretary | Legal Counsel | 4–6 weeks |
| 4 | Stock issuance: (a) new shares issued to subscribing stockholders, (b) stock certificates issued per W1724 process, (c) stock ledger updated, (d) payment received from stockholders (cash or property contribution), (e) GL updated: debit cash/receivable, credit issued capital stock and additional paid-in capital | Corporate Secretary / CFO | — | 1–2 weeks |
| 5 | Post-issuance: Corporate Secretary updates SEC GIS; CFO includes capital change in next financial statement; new capital structure reflected in consolidated equity reporting | Corporate Secretary / CFO | — | At next reporting |


### System Touchpoints
- Board approval of authorized-capital increase with utilization analysis (step 1)
- Stockholder approval — 2/3 of outstanding shares per Revised Corporation Code (step 2)
- SEC amended-articles filing with resolutions (step 3)
- Subscription, issuance, and ledger/payment updates (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Approval-defect risk**: an increase without the required 2/3 stockholder vote voids the issuance; mitigated by vote-threshold verification before SEC filing (steps 2–3)
- **Subscription-shortfall risk**: increased capital authorized but unsubscribed, incurring fees without benefit; mitigated by subscription commitments ahead of filing (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approves increase in authorized capital stock" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-file of "filed with SEC" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-update of "updated" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-350 (ensure controlled execution — Shareholder & Equity Management (PA-36.2).)
- operational: Corporate Secretary review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
Per capital-increase event: board approval + stockholder 2/3 vote over 2–4 weeks + SEC amended-articles filing over 4–6 weeks + stock issuance 1–2 weeks ≈ ~2–3 days of concentrated Corporate Secretary/CFO effort per event at every 2–5 years; no recurring annual figure.

## W1727. Officer & Director Appointment & Changes

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Board decision, resignation, retirement, or removal of officer or director |
| **Frequency** | ~5–10 changes/year across 5 entities |
| **Volume** | Low volume but legally significant |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, Board Chair, CEO, affected individual, Legal Counsel |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Officer change triggered by: (a) board appointment (new hire or promotion), (b) resignation (written notice), (c) removal (board action with cause), (d) death or incapacity | Corporate Secretary | Board Chair / CEO | Event-driven |
| 2 | Board passes resolution appointing/removing officer per W1717; for directors: stockholder election or board action to fill vacancy per by-laws | Board | Board Chair | At board meeting or written consent |
| 3 | Corporate Secretary updates records: (a) officer/director register, (b) stock ledger (if officer is also stockholder), (c) bank signatory cards (for officer changes affecting banking authority), (d) SEC GIS (updated at next filing), (e) BIR registration (for authorized representatives), (f) internal authorization matrices (system access, approval limits) | Corporate Secretary | Legal Counsel | 1–3 days |
| 4 | Incoming officer: (a) receives orientation on corporate governance responsibilities, (b) signs acceptance letter, (c) updates bio on file, (d) added to bank signatory panel (if applicable), (e) system access configured per role | Corporate Secretary / IT / Treasury | CEO | 1 week |


### System Touchpoints
- Officer/director change triggers — appointment, resignation, removal, retirement (step 1)
- Board resolution per W1717; stockholder election for directors (step 2)
- Record updates — officer/director register, stock ledger, bank signatory cards (step 3)
- Incoming officer orientation and acceptance letters (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Lingering-signatory risk**: departed officers retain bank signing power; mitigated by bank signatory card updates on every change (step 3)
- **Vacancy-limbo risk**: a resignation sits unactioned, leaving statutory officer roles vacant; mitigated by register tracking with board-action follow-through (steps 1–2)

### Automation Opportunity
- System auto-record of "Corporate Secretary updates records" (replaces manual Step 3).
- System auto-file of "file" (replaces manual Step 4).

### Controls
- CTL-350 (ensure controlled execution — Shareholder & Equity Management (PA-36.2).)
- operational: Corporate Secretary review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
~2–6 days per officer/director change (register updates 1–3 days + incoming-officer orientation over a 1-week window) × ~5–10 changes/year ≈ 10–60 days/year, event-driven.

## W1728. Corporate Entity Registration & Amendment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New entity creation (e.g., new subsidiary), amendment to existing entity (name change, address change, purpose change), or entity dissolution |
| **Frequency** | Rare — every 1–3 years |
| **Volume** | Very low — 5 entities currently; new entity creation is exceptional |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, Legal Counsel, CFO, Board, SEC |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | For new entity creation: (a) Board approves creation of new subsidiary (e.g., if BuildRight expands into a new business line), (b) Legal Counsel drafts articles of incorporation and by-laws, (c) identifies incorporators (minimum 2 per Revised Corporation Code, reduced from previous 5), (d) determines principal office, corporate purpose, authorized capital, term of existence | Legal Counsel / Corporate Secretary | Board | 2–3 weeks |
| 2 | SEC registration: (a) file articles of incorporation with SEC, (b) pay filing fees, (c) SEC issues certificate of incorporation, (d) obtain tax identification number (TIN) from BIR, (e) register with local LGU for business permit, (f) register with SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG as employer (if entity will have employees) | Corporate Secretary / Legal Counsel | CEO | 4–8 weeks |
| 3 | For amendments: (a) board and stockholder approval per Revised Corporation Code, (b) file amended articles with SEC, (c) update BIR, LGU, bank records, (d) update corporate records per W1719 | Corporate Secretary | Legal Counsel | 4–6 weeks |
| 4 | Post-registration: CFO opens bank accounts for new entity; IT configures ERP entity; Accounting Manager establishes GL and COA for new entity; intercompany relationships documented | CFO / IT / Accounting Manager | — | 1–2 weeks |


### System Touchpoints
- Entity creation workflow — board approval, articles drafting, SEC registration, TIN/LGU/bank setup (steps 1–2)
- Amendment filings — board/stockholder approvals and amended articles with SEC (step 3)
- Post-registration setup — ERP entity configuration, GL/COA creation, intercompany agreements (step 4)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Registration-gap risk**: a new entity transacts before SEC/BIR registration completes; mitigated by sequencing registration ahead of bank and ERP go-live (steps 2, 4)
- **IC-setup omission**: the new entity trades without intercompany agreements or transfer pricing; mitigated by IC agreement setup as a required post-registration step (step 4)

### Automation Opportunity
- System rule-based auto-approval of "approves creation of new subsidiary" (replaces manual Step 1).
- System auto-file of "file articles of incorporation with SEC" (replaces manual Step 2).
- System auto-record of "records" (replaces manual Step 3).

### Controls
- CTL-350 (ensure controlled execution — Shareholder & Equity Management (PA-36.2).)
- operational: Corporate Secretary review and approval gate

### Time Estimate
7–13 weeks elapsed per new-entity occurrence (2–3 + 4–8 + 1–2 weeks, steps 1–2 and 4); amendments run 4–6 weeks (step 3, the alternative branch) — at Rare — every 1–3 years ≈ 0.3–1 occurrence/year.
