## W1729. Corporate Policy Development & Approval

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New regulation, operational need, audit finding, or periodic policy review |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 policy updates/year + 3–5 new policies/year |
| **Volume** | ~40–50 active corporate policies |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, Policy Owner (department head), Legal Counsel, CHRO, Board |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Policy owner identifies need for new or revised policy: (a) regulatory change (e.g., new BIR regulation, NPC circular), (b) operational incident revealing policy gap, (c) internal audit recommendation, (d) industry best practice adoption, (e) periodic review (all policies reviewed at least every 2 years) | Policy Owner | Corporate Secretary | Event-driven |
| 2 | Policy owner drafts policy using standard template: (a) purpose and scope, (b) definitions, (c) policy statement (clear, actionable rules), (d) procedures for implementation, (e) roles and responsibilities, (f) exceptions and approval process, (g) monitoring and enforcement, (h) related documents, (i) version history | Policy Owner | Department Head | 1–2 weeks |
| 3 | Review and consultation: (a) Legal Counsel reviews for legal compliance, (b) CHRO reviews for employee impact and communication plan, (c) affected departments provide feedback, (d) Corporate Secretary reviews for consistency with existing policies | Legal Counsel / CHRO / Corporate Secretary | Policy Owner | 1 week |
| 4 | Approval: (a) operational policies: CEO approval, (b) financial policies (procurement, expense, capex): CFO approval, (c) HR policies: CHRO + CEO approval, (d) governance policies: Board or Audit Committee approval, (e) critical policies (code of conduct, anti-fraud, whistleblower): Board approval | Approver per policy type | Board / CEO | 1–2 weeks |
| 5 | Communication and implementation: (a) CHRO distributes to all employees via company portal, (b) training sessions for significant policy changes, (c) acknowledgment collected from all employees (digital signature), (d) effective date clearly stated, (e) previous version archived | CHRO / Policy Owner | Corporate Secretary | 1–2 weeks |

### System Touchpoints
- Policy management module with version control and approval workflow (W1729.2–4)
- Company-wide policy portal with search and acknowledgment tracking (W1729.5)
- Policy review calendar with automated reminders for 2-year review cycle (W1729.1)

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Policy proliferation**: 50+ policies can overwhelm employees; ensure policies are concise and accessible (not buried in dense documents)
- **Acknowledgment fatigue**: Employees signing 50 policies without reading them; focus training on the 10–15 most critical policies
- **Enforcement gap**: Policies without monitoring and consequences are ignored; internal audit should include policy compliance in audit program

## W1730. Annual Corporate Governance Self-Assessment

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual governance review cycle (Q4) |
| **Frequency** | Annual |
| **Volume** | 1 assessment/year |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, Board Chair, CEO, all directors |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Corporate Secretary distributes governance self-assessment questionnaire to all directors: (a) board composition and independence, (b) board meeting effectiveness, (c) information quality and timeliness, (d) committee functioning, (e) risk oversight, (f) management-board relationship, (g) corporate culture and ethics, (h) director time commitment, (i) board succession planning | Corporate Secretary | Board Chair | 1 week |
| 2 | Directors complete assessment individually (confidential); Corporate Secretary aggregates results | Directors | — | 1 week |
| 3 | Corporate Secretary presents aggregate results to Board (no individual attribution): (a) strengths and positive practices, (b) areas for improvement, (c) comparison to prior year (trend), (d) specific recommendations | Corporate Secretary | Board Chair | 1 hour at board meeting |
| 4 | Board discusses results and agrees on improvement actions: (a) assign owners and timelines for each action, (b) track implementation at subsequent meetings, (c) report to stockholders in annual report (if voluntary disclosure desired) | Board | Board Chair | 1–2 hours |

## W1731. Intercompany Service Agreement Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | New service relationship between BuildRight entities; annual renewal |
| **Frequency** | ~10–15 agreements maintained; annual review |
| **Volume** | Core intercompany services: (1) Depot → Logistics: warehousing fees, (2) Depot → Digital Commerce: ecommerce fulfillment fees, (3) Depot → Property Mgmt: lease payments, (4) Holdings → all subsidiaries: management fees |
| **Owner** | CFO |
| **Participants** | CFO, Corporate Secretary, Legal Counsel, Transfer Pricing Advisor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | CFO and Legal Counsel maintain intercompany service agreements: (a) warehousing and distribution services (Logistics → Depot), (b) ecommerce platform services (Digital Commerce → Depot), (c) property lease agreements (Property Management → Depot/Logistics/Digital Commerce), (d) management and shared services (Holdings → all subsidiaries), (e) IT services (Holdings or Depot → all entities), (f) HR and payroll services (Holdings → all entities) | CFO / Legal Counsel | CEO | Annual review |
| 2 | Transfer pricing documentation: (a) engage external transfer pricing advisor for annual study, (b) benchmark intercompany pricing against comparable market rates (arm's length principle), (c) document pricing methodology, comparables, and economic analysis, (d) maintain documentation per BIR Revenue Regulations No. 34-2022 (Philippine TP rules) | External TP Advisor / CFO | CFO | 2–4 weeks/year |
| 3 | Agreements updated with approved pricing; board resolutions authorizing intercompany arrangements per W1717; signed agreements filed per W1719 | Corporate Secretary / CFO | Board | 1 week |
| 4 | Monthly: Accounting Manager ensures intercompany transactions are recorded per agreed pricing; quarterly reconciliation per PA-17.2 | Accounting Manager | CFO | Monthly |

### Pain Points / Risks
- **Transfer pricing audit risk**: BIR increasingly scrutinizes intercompany pricing for family-owned corporate groups; documentation must be robust
- **Service allocation**: Allocating shared services cost (HQ payroll, IT, finance) across 5 entities requires a fair and consistent allocation key (revenue, headcount, or transaction volume)
- **Billing regularity**: Intercompany fees should be billed monthly (not in lump sums) to avoid period-end distortion

## W1732. Corporate Signing Authority & Delegation of Authority Matrix

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Organizational change, policy review, or new officer appointment |
| **Frequency** | Annual review; ad-hoc updates for organizational changes |
| **Volume** | 1 master authority matrix maintained |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, CEO, CFO, all department heads, IT (system configuration) |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Corporate Secretary maintains the Delegation of Authority (DOA) matrix: (a) financial approvals: purchase orders, vendor payments, expense reimbursements, capex requests, (b) contractual authority: contract execution, lease signing, vendor agreements, (c) banking authority: account opening, fund transfers, check signing, (d) HR authority: hiring, salary changes, termination, (e) operational authority: inventory adjustments, pricing overrides, markdown approvals | Corporate Secretary | CEO / CFO | Continuously |
| 2 | DOA tiers by value: (a) < PHP 50K: Department Manager, (b) PHP 50K–200K: Department Head / VP, (c) PHP 200K–1M: C-Suite member, (d) PHP 1M–10M: CEO, (e) > PHP 10M: Board approval, (f) dual signature for bank transactions > PHP 500K | Corporate Secretary / CFO | Board | Annual approval |
| 3 | System configuration: IT configures approval workflows in ERP per DOA matrix: (a) PO approval limits per user role, (b) capex approval workflow per W1811, (c) payment approval limits, (d) contract execution limits, (e) system enforces limits (cannot approve beyond authority) | IT | CFO | 1–2 days |
| 4 | Annual review: CFO and Corporate Secretary review DOA matrix for appropriateness: (a) adjustments for inflation (thresholds may need increase), (b) changes for new roles or organizational restructuring, (c) board approval for changes to Board-level thresholds | CFO / Corporate Secretary | Board | 1 day |
| 5 | DOA matrix distributed to all managers; training on approval limits; Corporate Secretary maintains signed acknowledgment copies | Corporate Secretary / CHRO | CEO | 1 week |

## W1733. Whistleblower & Ethics Reporting Channel Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual policy review; report received through ethics channel |
| **Frequency** | Continuous channel availability; ~20–40 reports/year expected |
| **Volume** | ~20–40 ethics reports/year |
| **Owner** | VP Internal Audit |
| **Participants** | VP Internal Audit, Corporate Secretary, Legal Counsel, HR, accused party's supervisor |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Maintain confidential ethics reporting channel: (a) toll-free hotline (outsourced to third-party provider for anonymity), (b) web-based reporting portal (encrypted, accessible to all 6,715 employees), (c) email to dedicated ethics mailbox, (d) physical drop boxes at each store/DC/HQ, (e) option for anonymous reporting in all channels | VP Internal Audit | CEO / Audit Committee | Continuous |
| 2 | Report received and triaged by VP Internal Audit: (a) categorize: (i) fraud/theft, (ii) harassment/discrimination, (iii) safety violation, (iv) policy violation, (v) conflict of interest, (vi) other, (b) assess severity: high (fraud > PHP 100K, safety risk, senior management involved), medium (policy violation, smaller amounts), low (minor concern, suggestion), (c) assign investigator | VP Internal Audit | CEO (high severity) | 1 day |
| 3 | Investigation: (a) high severity: dedicated investigator, may engage external forensic team, report to Audit Committee, (b) medium: Internal Audit investigator, 30-day target resolution, (c) low: referred to department head for resolution, (d) all investigations documented, evidence preserved | Investigator | VP Internal Audit | 1–30 days |
| 4 | Resolution and action: (a) substantiated: disciplinary action per HR policy (warning, suspension, termination, legal referral), (b) unsubstantiated: case closed with documentation, (c) reporter protected from retaliation per whistleblower policy, (d) accused party informed of outcome and appeal rights | VP Internal Audit / HR / Legal Counsel | CEO / Audit Committee | Variable |
| 5 | Quarterly report to Audit Committee: (a) number of reports by category and severity, (b) investigation outcomes, (c) action taken, (d) average resolution time, (e) trend analysis, (f) policy or process improvement recommendations | VP Internal Audit | Audit Committee | Quarterly |

## W1734. Conflict of Interest Disclosure & Management

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual disclosure requirement; new hire; triggered by specific situation |
| **Frequency** | Annual disclosure (~6,715 employees for senior staff; ~315 HQ employees for full disclosure) |
| **Volume** | ~315 detailed disclosures/year from managers and above; ~6,715 simplified for all employees |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, CHRO, all managers and above, Board |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Annual conflict of interest disclosure: (a) all managers and above (Store Managers, DC Supervisors, Department Heads, C-Suite, Directors): complete detailed COI questionnaire, (b) all other employees: acknowledge COI policy and report any actual conflicts, (c) questionnaire covers: (i) financial interests in vendors/suppliers/competitors, (ii) employment by or consulting for related entities, (iii) family members in competing or supplying businesses, (iv) outside business interests that may conflict, (v) gifts and entertainment received beyond policy limits | Corporate Secretary / CHRO | CEO | Annual (January) |
| 2 | Corporate Secretary reviews disclosures: (a) identified conflicts assessed for materiality, (b) potential mitigation: recusal from related decisions, divestiture of conflicting interest, reassignment of duties, (c) senior management and director conflicts escalated to Board Chair or Audit Committee | Corporate Secretary | CEO / Board Chair | 2–3 weeks |
| 3 | Conflict management plan documented and tracked: (a) agreed mitigation measures, (b) monitoring responsibilities, (c) annual reassessment | Corporate Secretary | — | As needed |
| 4 | Non-disclosure consequences: failure to disclose a material conflict of interest is a disciplinary offense per employee handbook; substantiated undisclosed conflicts may result in termination | CHRO / Legal Counsel | CEO | As needed |

## W1735. Data Privacy Governance & NPC Compliance

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) compliance requirement; new data processing activity; NPC audit; data breach |
| **Frequency** | Continuous governance; annual review; event-driven for breaches |
| **Volume** | Ongoing compliance for 600,000 customer records + 6,715 employee records + vendor data |
| **Owner** | Data Protection Officer (DPO) |
| **Participants** | DPO, CIO, Legal Counsel, Corporate Secretary, all department heads |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | DPO maintains data privacy governance framework: (a) privacy policy (external — for customers), (b) data protection policy (internal — for employees), (c) data processing agreements with all third-party service providers, (d) privacy impact assessments for new systems/processes, (e) data retention and disposal schedule, (f) breach notification procedure, (g) consent management for customer data collection | DPO | CEO | Annual review |
| 2 | NPC registration: (a) DPO registered with National Privacy Commission (mandatory per RA 10173), (b) data processing systems registered with NPC, (c) annual renewal of registration | DPO | Corporate Secretary | Annual |
| 3 | Consent management: (a) loyalty program members: consent for data collection, marketing communications, profiling, (b) ecommerce customers: privacy notice at checkout, cookie consent, data sharing consent, (c) employees: consent for processing SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG data, CCTV monitoring, biometric data, (d) vendors: consent for financial data processing | DPO / Marketing / HR / Procurement | DPO | Continuous |
| 4 | Data breach response (if triggered): (a) within 72 hours: initial assessment, containment, NPC notification (if involving sensitive personal information affecting 100+ data subjects), (b) within 5 days: full breach report to NPC, (c) affected data subjects notified, (d) remedial measures implemented, (e) post-incident review | DPO / IT / Legal Counsel | CEO | Event-driven |
| 5 | Annual privacy audit: DPO conducts internal review of data processing practices; findings reported to Audit Committee; corrective actions tracked | DPO | Audit Committee | 1–2 weeks/year |

## W1736. Corporate Social Responsibility Oversight

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Annual CSR planning (Q4); community engagement events; disaster response needs |
| **Frequency** | Annual planning; quarterly activities; event-driven for disaster response |
| **Volume** | ~20–30 CSR activities/year |
| **Owner** | Corporate Secretary |
| **Participants** | Corporate Secretary, CEO, CHRO, CMO, affected communities |

### Steps

| # | Activity | Role (R) | Role (A) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Annual CSR plan development: (a) aligned to BuildRight's community presence in 200+ locations across the Philippines, (b) focus areas: (i) housing/building assistance (core competency as hardware retailer), (ii) community safety and disaster preparedness, (iii) skills training (construction, DIY workshops for communities), (iv) environmental initiatives (tree planting, waste reduction), (v) employee volunteerism, (c) budget: typically 1–2% of net income | Corporate Secretary / CMO | CEO | 1 week (Q4) |
| 2 | CSR activity execution: (a) community build projects (partner with LGUs for housing materials donation), (b) disaster response (rapid mobilization of building materials for affected communities — Philippines experiences ~20 typhoons/year), (c) skills training workshops (free community DIY clinics at stores), (d) employee volunteer days (1 paid volunteer day per employee per year) | CMO / CHRO / Store Managers | Corporate Secretary | Quarterly activities |
| 3 | CSR reporting: (a) annual CSR report (may be part of ESG reporting per VS-25), (b) activity tracking: number of beneficiaries, volunteer hours, donations made, (c) budget vs. actual tracking, (d) communication to stakeholders (internal newsletter, social media, community acknowledgment) | Corporate Secretary / CMO | CEO | Quarterly updates |
| 4 | Board oversight: CSR activities and budget presented to Board annually; significant disaster response commitments (> PHP 1M) approved by CEO; > PHP 5M reported to Board | Corporate Secretary | Board | Annual |
