# VS-152: Corporate Social Responsibility, Foundation & Community Investment

> **Governance & Assurance** · [Value Stream Index](../value-stream-index.md)

---

## Overview

Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR), Foundation & Community Investment workflows for BuildRight
Depot Corp. — owning the enterprise **social-impact and community-investment discipline** that
operates alongside commercial activity: the BuildRight Foundation, community livelihood programs,
disaster relief, employee volunteerism, and the impact measurement/reporting that makes it
accountable to the board, the SEC, and the communities BuildRight serves across 200 store
communities.

Today this discipline is **unowned as a program**. "CSR" appears across ~58 PA files and
"foundation" ~24, but as incidental *references* (an ESG report topic in VS-25.2, a sourcing note
in VS-94, a govt-affairs activity in VS-104, a marketing/PR lever in VS-14.3) — the defining terms
"CSR", "social responsibility", "foundation", and "community investment" appear in **zero** PA
files as dedicated workflow headers and in **zero** value-stream directory names. No value stream
owns the end-to-end **CSR/Foundation operating discipline**: CSR strategy and governance, the
Foundation's legal/financial/operating setup (SEC registration, PCNC accreditation, BIR
donee-qualified status), the community-investment and livelihood portfolio, disaster response &
community resilience (distinct from VS-69 corporate disaster ops), employee volunteerism and
payroll-giving, partnerships with NGOs/LGUs/cooperatives, and the impact measurement (logic model,
theory of change, SROI), reporting (GRI/IRIS+/SEC), and stakeholder engagement.

This is distinct from **VS-25 (ESG & Sustainability)** which owns the *environmental & governance
reporting* of the corporate footprint — this value stream owns the *social/community-investment
programs and the Foundation*. It is distinct from **VS-94 (Cooperative & Community Procurement)**
which is a *sourcing channel* — this value stream owns the *community-development outcomes* (a
cooperative can be a VS-94 supplier and a VS-152 livelihood partner). It is distinct from **VS-104
(Government Affairs)** which manages *policy/advocacy relationships* — this value stream manages
*community-level social investment*. It is distinct from **VS-69 (Typhoon & Disaster Response)**
which runs *corporate operational recovery* — this value stream runs *community-facing disaster
relief* (the BuildRight Foundation's relief operations in BuildRight's store communities). It is
distinct from **VS-14.3 (Brand/PR)** which communicates — this value stream *does* the social
programs the communications team can then tell.

BuildRight's exposure is structural: as a PHP 62.3B retailer operating across 200 Philippine
communities (profile §3.1) with a stated ESG posture (VS-25) and a cooperative/community sourcing
program (VS-94), social license to operate is a material asset; a structured CSR/Foundation program
is expected by the board (profile §11.1, VS-36 governance), by SEC/large-customer B2B due
diligence, and by the communities themselves. CSR spend benchmarks 0.5–2% of net profit
(PHP ~0.2B–0.8B/yr at BuildRight's scale) and ungoverned community spend leaks value, duplicates,
or fails to produce measurable impact — the discipline that converts that spend into structured,
measurable, accountable social outcomes is genuinely unowned.
